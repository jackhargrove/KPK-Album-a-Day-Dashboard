# Write-up

Andrew Hill was a jazz pianist born in 1931 and raised in Chicago. He began playing piano at age 13 and first played with Charlie Parker and Miles Davis as a teenager. He became a full-time professional musician in 1953 at age 22 and spent the 50s paying his dues as a sideman before moving to NYC in 1961. Hill released *Point of Departure*, his fourth album as a bandleader, in 1965. In addition to Hill’s piano, it features Eric Dolphy on alto sax/bass clarinet/flute, Kenny Dorham on trumpet, Joe Henderson on tenor sax/flute, Richard Davis on double bass, and Tony Williams on drums. We previously listened to Eric Dolphy’s album *Out to Lunch*, which also featured Davis and Williams on bass and drums. Henderson appeared on Grant Green’s *Idle Moments*, Lee Morgan’s *The Sidewinder*, and Freddie Hubbard’s *Red Clay*. Hill composed every song on the album. While it did not receive much commercial attention, jazz critics enjoyed it. Later in life, Hill became a tenure track professor at Portland State University in Oregon in 1989. He spent the last two decades of his life conducting jazz workshops at prestigious universities across North America. Hill had a small late career renaissance with his 2001 album *Dusk*, which received rave reviews in the jazz press. Hill died of lung cancer in his Jersey City home in 2007. He was 75 years old. Shortly after, he received a posthumous honorary doctorate from Berklee College of Music.

# Hargrove's review

I really hated this one upon first listen because it reminded me so much of Eric Dolphy’s *Out to Lunch*. It was that same style of mid-60s-avant-garde-but-certainly-not-free jazz that’s basically like hard bop but with only the solos and where the rhythm instruments are allowed to get weird. Imagine the vindication I felt when I then read that Eric Dolphy plays on this record! While I hated this style with Dolphy, this record started growing on me for whatever reason. It’s actually bizarre. I really cannot figure out why things started to work for me. Usually I hate when a jazz record never settles into any kind of groove, but it all feels nice and vibey here. Usually I hate the bass clarinet, but it sounds just find to me on the last two tracks. Usually I hate when a jazz record gets sparse and slow at the end, but I actually really liked “Dedication”. Usually I hate overly staccato piano parts, but I never once mind Hill’s playing. I don’t know what it is, all the rules for me fail with this album. Now, it’s not like I’m in love with this record or anything. It’s still opaque, almost entirely improvised jazz that I’m going to stick in C Tier. But compared to the low D Tier of its most similar album on the list? That’s a positively glowing review. Even as I type this, I can barely keep any specifics of the album in my head. Usually, that would be a major ding. But I don’t know, man. I had a good time while listening.

I appreciate how “Refuge” just jumps right into things with no intro or anything. It starts with a great melody before going into a series of solos. All the while, I have no clue what the FUCK the bass is doing, but I honestly love it. All of the solos are good, but particularly the bass solo and the tenor sax solo, and there’s an incredible rush when all the instruments come back together at the end. “New Monastery” shocks me with a great solo from Dolphy on the alto sax. There’s a good tenor sax solo too; I’ve been quite impressed with Henderson so far. I think “Spectrum” is a super cool track. I just love how dynamic it is, constantly shifting between sections. A minute-and-a-half in, there’s a drumroll, and it just kicks into a completely different gear. The highlight for me is a bass mini solo that briefly goes into 5/4. And then, it goes into something slow and soulful right after. It ends by starting anew in the final 20 seconds after a complete drum breakdown. It’s a pretty cool track, honestly. “Flight 19” is my least favorite, but that doesn’t mean much. The music on this album is remarkably consistent from track to track. Every instrument comes in together at once on “Dedication”, nice and slow. They all sound out of sync but still together, which might be the story of this album. The midsection features a gorgeous piano solo, easily my favorite solo on the album. It’s a super solid closer.

I don’t know. It shouldn’t work. But it kinda does. I can actually see some scenarios where I would throw this album on. I was not expecting that.

Favorite tracks: Refuge, Spectrum, Dedication

Least favorite track: Flight 19
