# Write-up

Animal Collective is an experimental music group formed in Baltimore in 1999 consisting of vocalist and multi-instrumentalist Avey Tare (David Portner), vocalist and multi-instrumentalist Panda Bear (Noah Lennox), multi-instrumentalist Deakin (Josh Dibb), and multi-instrumentalist Geologist (Brian Weitz). See above for the write-up I did over a year ago when we listened to the group’s debut album for information on their origins, but here’s a quick summary of their early albums: Avey Tare and Panda Bear made the experimental but delightful *Spirit They’re Gone, Spirit They’ve Vanished* in 2000, added Geologist for the hellishly noisy and unstructured *Danse Manatee* in 2001, and swapped in Deakin for Geologist on the warm acoustic folk excursion *Campfire Songs* in 2003.

The first record released under the Animal Collective name, and the first to feature all four members together, was 2003’s *Here Comes the Indian*. The Animal Collective name was originally coined by journalists, referring to the fact that the four of them loosely recorded music as a collective on their independent Animal record label (now named Paw Tracks). Over these four albums, the group’s music had gotten more and more complex. In reaction to this, Avey Tare and Panda Bear recorded the group’s fifth album alone with acoustic instrumentation in rural Colorado. The resulting album, 2004’s *Sung Tongs*, was not only a defining excursion into the freak folk movement, but also in retrospect the start of AnCo’s golden age. The track “Who Could Win a Rabbit” is a BANGER. Geologist and Deakin returned for the group’s sixth album, 2005’s *Feels*. This fan favorite album abandons much of the folk pretext for pure, bizarre pop made with the help of Sun City Girls (AnCo’s chief inspiration) collaborator Scott Coburn. “Grass” and “Banshee Beat” are my recommendations from this album. All four again appeared on their seventh album, 2007’s *Strawberry Jam*; I can’t give too many details because it’s also on our list, but this was the group’s commercial breakthrough, hitting the Billboard 200 and earning an appearance on Conan, and my personal favorite AnCo album.

AnCo’s commercial and artistic peak came with their eighth album, *Merriweather Post Pavilion*, in 2009. The album takes its name from a massive outdoor concert venue halfway between Baltimore and DC. Deakin sat this record out, but the other three all appear on it. Avey Tare had always been the group’s de facto leader, but Panda Bear arguably dominates *Merriweather Post Pavilion*. Inspired by the 2007 Panda Bear solo record *Person Pitch*, AnCo largely abandoned guitars in favor of samplers. Like the Beatles, a song’s lead vocalist is typically also its writer; Avey Tare wrote and sang “In the Flowers”, “Also Frightened”, “Summertime Clothes”, “Bluish”, “Taste”, “Lion in a Coma”, and “No More Runnin”, while Panda Bear wrote and sang “My Girls”, “Daily Routine”, “Guys Eyes”, and “Brother Sport”. The album’s first single was “My Girls”, a Panda Bear written and sung track based off of “Your Love”, one of the original Chicago house tracks. It has become the group’s most famous song by far; Beyoncé even interpolated its melody on “6 Inch” from her 2016 album *Lemonade*. *Merriweather Post Pavilion* reached an unbelievable #13 on the Billboard 200 and sold over 200,000 copies.

Sadly, AnCo’s magic vanished as quickly as it arrived. Since 2009, the group has released six albums, none of them very good. Panda Bear is the only member to have done anything of importance since, appearing on the popular single “Doin’ It Right” by Daft Punk in 2013 and releasing some decent solo albums (though none so good as 2007’s *Person Pitch*). But with five certified classics under their belt and the dominance of their creative, reverb-drenched, psychedelic style on both 2010s indie and mainstream music, AnCo’s legacy is secure.

# Hargrove's review

I can’t believe it took us over a year to get to a second AnCo album! As I’m sure I mentioned in my review of their “debut” over a year ago, it was one of the first “indie” bands I got into in early 2017 when I decided I wanted to get more into music. As I’m sure is the case for very many people, *Merriweather Post Pavilion* was my first AnCo album, and “My Girls” the first track to really catch my ear. In the nine years since, I have worked my way through every album that came before MPP, working backwards until *Sung Tongs* (including Panda Bear’s solo *Person Pitch*, a personal favorite), going to the very beginning with STGSTV, and then giving a listen or two to their extremely, unlistenably experimental albums between STGSTV and *Sung Tongs*. I’ve settled on *Strawberry Jam* as my favorite, the album that most perfectly balances creativity and sonic experimentation with perfect pop songwriting. Still, *Merriweather Post Pavilion* holds a certain allure; as much as I love “April and the Phantom” and “Who Could Win a Rabbit” and “The Purple Bottle” and “Fireworks”, there’s a certain sleek, untouchable quality to a “My Girls” or a “Summertime Clothes”. To stop myself from rambling, I think this album is very, very good. Now that that’s been firmly established, I’m going to spend this review outlining my issues with it. Rest assured, this will be ranked in my S tier. I am not shitting on the album. But I think I can distill what it is I like about AnCo better by outlining what I dislike about MPP instead of what I like about it.

Not to diminish the contributions of Geologist or Deakin, who I imagine are vital contributors technically and behind the scenes, but AnCo’s finished product is really a collaborative project between Avey Tare and Panda Bear. More specifically, it’s primarily an Avey Tare venture with Panda Bear as a secondary creative, at least in my view. To grossly oversimplify, I almost see the dynamic as Avey being Lennon-McCartney and Panda being Harrison (Geologist is Rin and Deakin is Go). Avey’s innate knack for melody is the group’s greatest strength. Seriously, I do not know how he does it. He consistently pulls out these simple, catchy melodies that somehow every other musician in history missed. Orpheus speaks through Avey Tare as he did McCartney and Mozart. Avey’s other strength, however, is a double-edged sword: his manic, frantic, unpredictable delivery. The jumps from one melody to the next, the screams, the instant switches from quiet to loud. If left unchecked, this can easily become self-indulgent or grating. When properly accompanied, however, Avey’s style perfectly complements the winding nature of his melodies and gives AnCo its unfettered creative charm. That’s where Panda Bear shines. In contrast to Avey’s God-given spark from the ether, Panda’s style is methodical, repetitive, and heavily layered. He finds pretty but conventional melodies, using elaborate harmony to turn them into something majestic. Instrumentally, Panda’s contribution of “perfect percussion” created an uncommonly solid backbone around which Avey’s innovative songwriting could flourish, and he’s played that role since then. Additionally, his relaxed and mellow vibe create the perfect refuge from Avey’s excesses when Panda started getting his own songs on later albums. His 2.5 songs on *Strawberry Jam* is just the perfect amount.

But on MPP, they Panda my Avey. My Avey got Panda’d. And I really can’t blame them. *Person Pitch* is an amazing, amazing album. I would also want to replicate that song if I were Avey. On top of that, Panda wrote “My Girls”. I would also give him a four songs on the album to the person who wrote “My Girls”. But it throws off the balance for me ever so slightly. As I outlined in the pervious paragraph, Panda Bear’s style works great solo. It doesn’t really need any balancing. Avey, by contrast, is not an artist I want to listen to solo. I need his genius filtered through Animal Collective. For that reason, it’s not ideal to me for an AnCo album to be this Panda-heavy. It’s a comparative disadvantage. Of Panda’s four tracks, three range from good to phenomenal, but “Daily Routine” is a drag I could do without. I would love if it were replaced by an energetic Avey track, but instead it halts the insane momentum after “Summertime Clothes”. But that’s fine. Avey has his own total loser track on this album in the form of “Lion in a Coma”. The deeper issue is that they Panda’d my Avey. On tracks like “No More Runnin”, “Also Frightened”, “Bluish”, and “Taste”, Avey takes a much slower, calmer, hazier, dreamier, vibier approach to songwriting than I’ve come to expect from him. And the thing is, individually, these are all still phenomenal tracks; the latter three all make my favorites list. But in aggregate, it lowers the ceiling of this album as a whole for me by just the tiniest bit. When the album still rocks this hard, that’s really not a serious issue. That being said, I think this small opportunity cost explains what it is I prefer about *Strawberry Jam* and probably *Feels*.

I really blabbered on there, so I’m going to try to make my album and song specific review quick. I absolutely adore this album’s warmth. It’s a perfect summer playlist all on its own. Not only does the music envelop you like sunshine as you’re chilling on the Beach, but the concept album-level focus on familial love makes it that much warmer. “In the Flowers” is a perfect Avey track, jaunting between haunting finger-picked arpeggios and electronic noise explosions as he paints a vivid picture of his wife through an unforgettable melody. Panda contributes his magnum opus in “My Girls”, a dynamic and progressively layered music quilt sewn from his strongest melody. The final minute when everything comes together must be one of the most satisfying moments in all of music. “Also Frightened” strikes a haunting balance between the joy and fear of rearing a child as Avey and Panda combine vocals beautifully in the chorus. Avey’s peak on this album comes in “Summertime Clothes”, a ridiculously catchy melody meeting with the infectious delight of a bouncing synths to create one of the warmest pieces of music I’ve ever heard. “Daily Routine” is the first of this album’s two misses, Panda’s mechanical delivery and the white noise synth drowning out its positive qualities. “Bluish” brings things right back with another crazy Avey melody over a supremely relaxing instrumental. “Guys Eyes” is Panda’s third best contribution but still quite good; I particularly like the combination of electronic and acoustic percussion. “Taste” is the perfect Avey deep cut, a slow but freaky track buoyed by a floating synth, strong bass line, and beautiful backing vocals from Panda. I do not care for “Lion in a Coma” one bit. While I usually like off-kilter rhythms, the melody sounds like it’s spilling off the side of a table, jammed in in a decidedly unpleasant fashion. Plus, the jaw harp sounds stupid. “No More Runnin” suffers the most from the Pandafication of my Avey, lacking even a strong melody to save it. The album ends with the opposite: they Avey’d my Panda??? “Brother Sport” finds Panda at his most energetic and manic, though at its core it keeps the same repetitive and layered style. It’s another banger from Panda’s strongest showing on any AnCo album.

That took forever. Good album. Can’t wait for *Strawberry Jam*.

Favorite tracks: In the Flowers, My Girls, Also Frightened, Summertime Clothes, Bluish, Guys Eyes, Taste, Brother Sport

Least favorite track: Lion in a Coma
