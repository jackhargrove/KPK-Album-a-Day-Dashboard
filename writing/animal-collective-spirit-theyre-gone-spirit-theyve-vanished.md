# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Avey Tare (David Portner), Panda Bear (Noah Lennox), Geologist (Brian Weitz), and Deakin (Josh Dibb) were four childhood friends from Baltimore who made music on their own and together during their time in high school. In 1998, they all went off to college, with Panda Bear and Deakin in Boston and Avey Tare and Geologist in New York. In 1999, Panda Bear released his debut solo studio album with contributions from Deakin. Avey Tare decided to record his own solo album in 1999 as a way to channel the frustration and isolation he felt after moving to New York. He recorded it while home in Baltimore for the Summer, and he asked Panda Bear to do the drums for the album. Avey Tare was so impressed with Panda Bear’s drums that he chose to give Panda Bear equal credit for the album, *Spirit They’re Gone, Spirit They’ve Vanished* by Avey Tare and Panda Bear.

Avey Tare, Panda Bear, Geologist, and Deakin continued to work together on various projects with different combinations of people until 2003, when they finally decided to form a permanent band named Animal Collective. Every project they had worked on collaboratively over the previous 3 years was reclassified as an Animal Collective album, with *Spirit They’re Gone, Spirit They’ve Vanished* retroactively considered Animal Collective’s debut album. Animal Collective went on to be one of the most legendary indie rock bands of the 2000s decade, smoothing out some of the rougher edges from their early work to make frantic, psychedelic folk music that would achieve mainstream success, and even be interpolated by Beyoncé on *Lemonade*. There are two more Animal Collective albums later on the list, *Strawberry Jam* (2007) and *Merriweather Post Pavilion* (2009), as well as a Panda Bear solo record, *Person Pitch* (2007).

Animal Collective’s early work, like *Spirit They’re Gone, Spirit They’ve Vanished* is often seen as part of the New Weird America movement, which was a re-contextualization of 60s psychedelic and folk music. Animal Collective were also founding fathers of the freak folk movement, which would ultimately produce my favorite artist of all time, Joanna Newsom.

# Hargrove's review

“The two sides of my brain need to have a meeting” - Jack White

The album that birthed Animal Collective. It sounds like Tinker Bell, Peter Pan, Hansel, and Gretel all met up in the forest and decided to make an album. And for some reason decided to overdub a sustained fire alarm over two of the tracks. A deep exploration of the nature and purpose of dissonance, juxtaposing heavenly vocal melodies and twinkling instrumentation with screeching feedback, yells, and extremely lo-fi production. An album that truly makes noise fans question why they like noise. And unfortunately, in pursuit of this goal, Avey Tare and Panda Bear made some decisions that make the album sound worse. My heart wants to forgive them, to point out that it leads to *Strawberry Jam* and “My Girls” in just a few years, to say that the beauty completely justifies the pain. My brain knows that this album is deeply flawed. My heart wants my brain to shut the fuck up.

I don’t want to say negative things about this album but, since I have to, I’m going to get them out of the way in this paragraph. While I will defend the use of the high pitched noises on “Spirit They’ve Vanished” and “Alvin Row”, as well as the wall of static at the beginning of “April and the Phantom”, as interesting and not pointless, I will not claim that they are enjoyable. “Untitled”, however, does little to justify its nonstop ear rape, and I think the album would be completely better off without it. And though I’m a fan of plenty of lo-fi albums, the mix on this album is just dreadful. I’ve heard the 2023 remaster is somewhat better in this regard, but as it is, the vocals are muddy and muted. Finally, even some of the best tracks overstay their welcome, particularly in the middle of the album.

But oh my God is there so much to love about this album, starting with Panda Bear’s percussion. The drums throughout are nothing short of PERFECT, complimenting every last idea that Avey Tare has in the rest of the music. The cymbal crashes in “La Rapet”, their crispness in “Chocolate Girl”, the syncopated bass drum, the jittery snare and hi-hat in “April and the Phantom” and “Bat You’ll Fly”. When I learned today while researching the write-up that Panda Bear wasn’t originally going to be credited until Avey Tare heard how good the drums were, I was not at all surprised.

Another aspect of this album that you gotta love is the vocal melodies. I mentioned in my review of the last album that I thought one melody in “Long Season” was very novel sounding to me, and that I found that rare with melodies. I don’t find that rare with Avey Tare vocals, however. Just about every song introduces a melody in the vocals unlike any I’ve ever heard before. My favorite on this album is the melody that begins at 5:15 in “Chocolate Girl”, but the “she ran out of nature” part in “April and the Phantom” is a close second. I also enjoy the competing vocal melodies in “Bat You’ll Fly”.

The last major aspect of this album that I greatly admire is the atmosphere it creates and maintains, and also how it subverts that atmosphere. “Someday I’ll Grow to Be as Tall as the Giant” is a great example, a driving waltz with shiny, twinkling synths. Every song makes me feel as if I’m lost in a wondrous forest in medieval Germany, my every move being narrated by the Brothers Grimm. But, much like the stories of the Brothers Grimm, there’s a dark underbelly. “Alvin Row” achieves this juxtaposition with 2 minutes of chaos being followed with 10 minutes of beauty and lilting piano. “Spirit They’ve Vanished” does it by playing the beauty and the pain simultaneously. To me, it really paints a picture of innocence, with both the wonder and the fear it brings.

This album is a story of the beautiful and the unpleasant. The wondrous and the terrifying. The sparkle and the darkness of the night sky. I’m not going to pretend they didn’t take the dissonance to excess, but the beauty is excessive too. A flawed record, but a record with flaws I’m willing to look past.

Favorite tracks: April and the Phantom, Penny Dreadfuls, Chocolate Girl, La Rapet, Bat You’ll Fly, Alvin Row

Least favorite track: Untitled

7/10 - my brain

10/10 - my heart

So let’s call it an 8/10
