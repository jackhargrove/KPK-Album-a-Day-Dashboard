# Write-up

See above for my write-ups on *Spirit They’re Gone, Spirit They’ve Vanished* and *Merriweather Post Pavilion*, which cover the broad strokes of AnCo’s career. This write-up will focus narrowly on the group’s seventh studio album, 2007’s *Strawberry Jam*. After the underground favorites *Sung Tongs* and *Feels*, the members of AnCo decided they wanted to make a “desert record”, so they worked at a studio in Tucson, Arizona. AnCo had recently signed to a new independent label, Britain’s Domino, and felt some pressure and writer’s block during the creation of this album. Panda Bear came up with the album’s name after being served a packet of strawberry jam on a flight to Greece and being enraptured by the look of the jam; he wanted the album to sound the way the jam looked, synthetic and sharp but also tangy and sweet. The first single “Peacebone” uses a now-iconic sample of the word “bonefish” that was taken from the online media archive of the Cornell Lab of Ornithology. “Fireworks”, the second single, became AnCo’s most commercially recognizable song up to that point, as well as an enduring fan favorite. Panda Bear sings lead vocals on “Chores” and “Derek”, songs he is also strongly suspected of being the main writer of. Panda and Avey split vocal duties on “. Otherwise, Avey was the primary songwriter and lead vocalist for every track. The album received critical praise upon release. Perhaps more importantly, it was AnCo’s first album to chart on the Billboard 200, peaking at number 72.

# Hargrove's review

More than a year into this project, we finish our three Animal Collective records (four if you include Panda Bear’s *Person Pitch*) with my personal favorite record of theirs, *Strawberry Jam*. I have a habit of liking transitional records, even entirely transitional artists in the case of David Bowie. When an artist goes from being big in avant-garde circles to big in mainstream circles, I almost always appreciate most the record that comes right in the middle of that swing, a perfect medium between the two realms. *Strawberry Jam* demonstrates this even better than most, striking a balance between the “experimental” and the “pop” better than any other “experimental pop” album I know. Are you an early AnCo fan who loves the unbridled, often abrasive creativity? Check out “Cuckoo Cuckoo” or “#1”. Are you a *Merriweather Post Pavilion* fan who loves the hypnotic, anthemic sing-alongs? You’re in luck! We’ve got “Fireworks”, “For Reverend Green”, and “Peacebone” doing it just as well as “Summertime Clothes” and “My Girls”. The balance between Avey and Panda is also just about perfect here, with Panda taking lead on “Chores” and “Derek”, two very good tracks that I didn’t need any more of. More importantly, Panda returns to the drumset à la *Spirit They’re Gone, Spirit They’ve Vanished* and it fucking RULES. Panda Bear is a generationally talented drummer that can make any song 10x better. At this point, I think I’ve made clear what it is I love about Animal Collective, so I won’t belabor the point beyond saying that *Strawberry Jam* does those things better than any of their other albums. Unfortunately, there is exactly one thing holding me back from putting this album in my tippy-top SS Tier (and giving it the requisite 5/5 stars on RYM): I really dislike the track “#1”. I might have been happy to look the other way were I only nonplussed, but I do not care for it one bit. Maybe that’s fitting, though. Very few legendary bands of the 60s had any albums that were unblemished. Those are AnCo’s peers.

“Peacebone” begins with hilariously infectious “bonefish” sample before throwing out a writhing soup of synths. Slowly, they coalesce into a bouncing beat that backs an absolute home run melody from Avey in both the verses and the chorus. Much of the album lyrically laments the loss of childhood magic, but Avey prefaces it all here by admonishing that toxic nostalgic instinct within himself: “An obsession with the past is like a dead fly/And just a few things are related to the old times/When we did believe in magic and we did die”. More than anything else, though, I love the screaming. I love the screaming all across this album. It’s the number one thing that was missing from *Merriweather Post Pavilion*. There’s nothing better than a scream in the middle of one of the most beautiful melodies you’ve ever heard. It had been a while since I listened to this album in full, and I was a little down on “Unsolved Mysteries” while readjusting. In my last few listens, however, I came back around to it hard. I really like the back-and-forth between the strumming and the staccato stabbing. Avey gets slow, but he keeps that winding, grand quality about himself. I’m a tiny bit torn on “Chores” because I love the first section and the final section, but the middle section gets a little too hazy and unstructured for me. The first part is the most energetic and frantic I’ve ever heard Panda sound. It still hast that rigidity I expect from a Panda piece, but it’s fast enough to give you whiplash. I like this part of the track enough to make it a favorite despite its shortcomings.

“For Reverend Green” is my all-time favorite AnCo song. It’s just perfect. The blurry synth line in the background contains so much potential energy that the exquisite pacing of the song structure then exploits. The non-lyrical song parts also provide temporary pressure valves, while the yelling generates explosive release. It’s truly masterful how well Avey manipulates tension and energy in this track. And who doesn’t love singing along to “Now I think that’s a riot”. “Fireworks” is much of the same and very nearly as good. The pacing and control makes these two tracks as good as they are. You can’t listen to “And I can't lift you up 'cause my mind is tired/It's family beaches that I desire/Sacred nights where we watch the fireworks/They frighten the babies and you know/They've got two!/Flashing eyes and if they’re color blind/They make me feel/That I'm only all I see sometimes” without belting it out! “#1” is the number one thing holding me back from giving this record a perfect score. I don’t really jive with the concept at all, and the rest of it then falls apart and overstays its welcome. The short and sweet “Winter Wonder Land” brings back all the lost momentum, thankfully. I love the fast drumming that resembles a metal skank beat. Panda is just a miracle worker with the drums. It’s catchy, it’s fast, it’s short. I love it. “Cuckoo Cuckoo” takes a while to find its footing, but once it gets going, I find it immensely powerful. The utter calamity of the chorus matches the intensity of the screamed melodic choruses on other track. This is a track with a very strong center of gravity. It’s an experiment that works. “Derek” starts off on a slightly weak note, but the second half develops into vintage Panda, a proto-“Brother Sport”. It’s maybe not as strong as some of the best tracks, but I like how it fits in here at the end.

My final AnCo ranking on this list is *Strawberry Jam* > *Merriweather Post Pavilion* > *Person Pitch* > *Spirit They’re Gone, Spirit They’ve Vanished*. Three S Tier albums and a B Tier is a hell of a run.

Favorite tracks: Peacebone, Unsolved Mysteries, Chores, For Reverend Green, Fireworks, Winter Wonder Land, Cuckoo Cuckoo, Derek

Least favorite track: #1
