# Write-up

Aphex Twin, real name Richard D James (who would have guessed?) is a British electronic musician who ranks among the most acclaimed electronic artists of all time. James is from Cornwall and frequently references the Cornish language and slang in his work. He is known for making insane claims about his life, such as independently inventing techno before he even heard it and sleeping 2 hours a night. He got his start doing free parties on beaches in Cornwall, and eventually was convinced to sign a record contract while tripping on acid. His first full release, Selected Ambient Works 85-92 was a huge critical success, and is widely considered among the greatest records ever made. He followed it up with the even more ambient Selected Ambient Works II, then the more beat-driven I Care Because You Do. Richard D. James Album was his fourth full length release and the first to venture into the subgenera known as Drill 'N Bass, which is characterized by taking the breakbeats used in jungle music and speeding them up to the point where they become impossible to dance to. Squarepusher, whose album we did recently, is another major artist who worked in the genre. The genre mostly burned out after the late 90s, but Richard D James Album is considered one of the core albums. James had a few drill 'n bass releases under different names after Plug, a friend and local DJ, put him onto the genre, including Hangable Audio Bulb (put out under the name AFX), which was also very influential into the genre.

The title and cover art of Richard D James Album was intended as a reaction to the techno trend of total anonymity and letting the art speak for itself. The album features samples of James's own voice as well as a violin that he bought at a local market and taught himself to play single notes, which he then sampled. Still, the lion's share of the sounds on the album are entirely electronic. James would go on to release 2 more albums, Drukqs and Syro. He also has an extensive catalog of releases under other names. Every album he has released as Aphex Twin with the exception of Drukqs has been universally acclaimed.

# Hargrove's review

Eh. It’s fine. There are some good tracks on this thing. There are some real misses. This was unfortunately still not the album to break me through on electronic music. I still feel like an outsider looking in. I want to feel the computer music as though I was born to it. I wish to take to it as I’ve taken to metal. Please, techno Jesus, please, take me in. I wasn’t crazy enamored with the drill and bass style with Squarepusher, and this didn’t change things a ton. It is interesting how different the vibe is between this album and *Hard Normal Daddy* though given both belong to a narrow, hyper-specific subgenre. Where Squarepusher used the blistering, abrasive drum beats to construct a whimsical video game world, Aphex Twin plays them off the gentle, timeless melodies that mark his real talent. Whether it’s a techno motif looped for 10 minutes or a blink-and-you-miss-it piano piece, Aphex Twin is at his best when he’s creating beauty through melody. Unsurprisingly, the best songs on this album are the ones where he finds that beauty. On the other hand, the album really falters when the melodies don’t work. I’m known to like music that’s plenty abrasive, but tracks like “Peek 824545201” and “Logan Rock Witch” have little in the way of redeeming qualities, and “To Cure a Weakling Child” is just insidious. And because the drill and bass style doesn’t resonate with me naturally, the mid songs just pass me by. What I do have to admit, however, is that where there are moments of beauty, those moments are intense. I will keep plugging away, and one day I’ll be a fan of electronic.

“4” sets a high bar for drill and bass right at the beginning. The drums are tasteful, and I love the pretty violin fighting with the beep boop melody. Gorgeous track. “Cornish Acid” is also quite good, if insignificant. The spacey chords underneath make the track for me, but it’s over before you know it. “Peek 824545201” has an awfully annoying intro and continues with a cluttered, busy sound in the foreground throughout. It is not a song I want to listen to again. “Fingerbib” is definitely the best track. The violin glides and soars to perfection while the rhythmic and melodic bits combine to power an electric carousel in the foreground. The entire track careens around and around again in a circle. “Carn Marth” kicks the aggression up a notch, and it’s one of the mid tracks. The drum rhythm is genuinely impressive, admirable even, but it’s so loud as to become overbearing. It chafes as I listen. I cannot stand “To Cure a Weakling Child”. The vocal bit is so grating to my ear and it just goes the entire time. And the rest of the track isn’t even that good! The drums are weak sauce! “Goon Gumpas” is a nice little melodic piece. The staccato strings are gorgeous. It is unfortunately fleeting, but pretty nonetheless. “Yellow Calx” is another mid. It’s decent, but I barely remember it once it’s gone. “Girl/Boy Song” is comparable in quality to “4”. It’s like the strings from “Goon Gumbas” set over some of the best drums on the entire album. The cymbals just go CRAZY, and then you have the soaring violin and the xylophone thing. It’s a very well-constructed track, if nothing else. “Logan Rock Watch” ends things on a dogshit note. Not once did I ask myself “You know what this album needs? 3 minutes of a Jew’s harp”.

I want so badly to compare it driectly to *Hard Normal Daddy* for formal reasons, but I’m having difficulty because they feel so different. I’m gonna put this one just above because it’s the tighter album. I respect that it gave me about the same amount of material that I liked in *Hard Normal Daddy* but in about half the time.

Favorite tracks: 4, Cornish Acid, Fingerbib, Goon Gumpas, Girl/Boy Song

Least favorite track: To Cure a Weakling Child

# Aidan's review

I wouldn't call this the most purely enjoyable album to listen to at any individual moment, but it's incredibly listenable as a whole. I've listened to it nearly 10 times now and it just never gets dull. There's a bit of whiplash in transitioning between the first few tracks, but it's never dull, never overwhelming. And it just sounds so damn good, the scraping drums, the violin samples, the electronic strings. It's just a joy to listen to. The melodies are simple yet ethereal, the beats are so fast they serve as texture more than anything, it's a fascinating combo. The highs of the album are when it all becomes simple, a single instrument plays, the chaos fades away and you're left with a sense of beauty. And then it's gone, subsumed in the chaos of the breakneck breakbeats. But James has such a good sense of how often to evoke that feeling that it's an incredibly rewarding album. My favorite tracks are the ones that maintain this the longest, like 4, Peek 8245401 and Goon Gumpas, but I have become convinced that these songs wouldn’t sound nearly as good in isolation if not paired with their slightly harsher companions. Great album, one I started out very high on and only got more positive towards.
