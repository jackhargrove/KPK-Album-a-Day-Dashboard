# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Richard D. James, better known as Aphex Twin, is a Welsh-Irish electronic muscian based in London. He grew up in Cornwall, England, where he began to DJ at outdoor parties as a teenager in the late 80s. As his reputation as a techno and acid house DJ grew, he was approached by record executives, who finally convinced him to release his first single while tripping on acid.

After releasing a few EPs, Aphex Twin released his debut studio album Selected Ambient Works 85-92 in 1992. It contained his backlog of music that he had made going back to when he was 14 years old. It received critical and commercial acclaim at the time, and is today considered one of the best albums of the 90s. Aphex Twin went on to release more albums, one more of which is on this list.

Aphex Twin, and this album in particular, defined a movement known as Intelligent Dance Music (IDM). IDM was a branch of electronic dance music that was more ambient and relaxing than typical EDM. IDM has become the electronic genre of choice for hipster douchebags such as myself, and there are plenty of examples throughout this list. Notably, IDM artists hate the term IDM because it makes them look pretentious, but it is still widely used by music critics.

# Hargrove's review

When we originally did this album all the way back in February, I was visiting New York and never actually got the chance to write a full review. We had just started (this was album 14) and everything was different then: we were actually doing an album every single day of the week, we weren’t taking breaks when I was busy, and I didn’t have a rigid process for reviewing albums like I do now. Anyway, it fell through the cracks and it’s been nagging at me for a long time. i figured I should just get it done before the year is out. Part of why I’ve been putting it off for so long is that I just don’t much care for it. Though I didn’t review it, I did rank it; at the time we initially listened, I ranked it 11th out of 14, and in the interceding time it’s fallen to 162nd out of 184. It’s in D Tier. I know everyone and their mother loves it, even in this chat, but it’s just really not my thing. That being said, I don’t usually have that much trouble listening to albums I don’t love. Hell, I went and listened to *Trout Mask Replica* again last week voluntarily because we were listening to a Captain Beefheart album. No, I think the reason I was putting this off is deeper, and illustrates why I’m not crazy for it: I just don’t have that much to say about it! I’ve tried to sit down and do this review before a couple of time over the last few months, but each time after I finish a listen I just don’t have anything I want to write about. Though I’ve found some records I really like through this list, I am a complete stranger to EDM and EDM-related genres and thus find those records harder to relate to. Additionally, I am not the style of listener who generally prefers true ambient music. My favorite albums are all more up-front emotionally. So this is just a confluence of styles that leaves me alienated. I do admit there is some nice music on here. I especially find many of the synth timbres gorgeous, and Aphex Twin has a real knack for melody. Overall, though, my most common thought when this album is playing is “how much longer is this?” I don’t think it’s an attention span issue; I have no trouble enjoying the hour-long, largely static track “Dopesmoker”. It’s just not my thing.

“Xtal” is a good proof of concept for the album. It’s certainly both ambient and techno, and has some heavenly timbres. It feels like a true update to the music on *Ambient 1: Music for Airports*. I like the polyrhythms on “Tha” and the texture of the drone, but it just goes on way too long. “Pulsewidth” has kind of a horn-sounding synth, which is interesting, I guess. “Ageispolis” maybe has an interesting drum beat but was also the first time I wished the album would end soon on my final listen. “I” is a short interlude. I like “Green Calx” because it finally shakes things up. I’m also just a sucker for that squelching acid sound. It’s also more upbeat than the rest of the album, plus it has a sick breakdown that sounds like blasters errantly firing. “Heliosphan” is also good because it has a super memorable melody, which coincidentally reminds me of the song Tame Impala did for *Barbie*. I despise the vocal sample and the incessant melody on “We Are the Music Makers”. I really don’t like that track. After two pretty good tracks and a bad track, “Schottkey 7th Path” brings us back to meh. “Ptolemy” is one of the better tracks. I like the squelching bass, the fast drums, and the nice melody. “Hedphelym” is cool too because it creates this hellish, apocalyptic atmosphere that contrasts totally with the airy breeziness of everything else. “Delphium” is admittedly kind of electro groovy but I’m just so ready for this album to end at this point. “Actium” is one last 7-fucking-minute hurrah.

If it is your thing, I totally get why. I wish it resonated with me too!

Favorite tracks: Xtal, Green Calx, Heliosphan, Ptolemy, Hedphelym

Least favorite track: We Are the Music Makers
