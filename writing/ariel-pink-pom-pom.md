# Write-up

Ariel Pink is the stage name of Ariel Rosenberg, an outsider musician from LA who originally released music as Ariel Pink’s Haunted Graffiti. He was the primary creator of hypnagogic pop, a truly experimental style of music he developed in the late 90s and early 2000s making an unbelievable number of unreleased tapes by himself in his rich dad’s garage in Beverly Hills. He lived an unorthodox childhood (including losing his virginity to a prostitute in Mexico City at age 13) and was a very weird kid. His parents wanted him to be an artist of some sort, but he only ever received training in painting. He was obsessed with music though, and not the typical cool underground music weird artsy kids are usually into. He loved the bubblegum pop of the 60s, the massive arena rock bands of the 70s, the huge hair metal and New Wave groups of the 80s, and the grunge and alternative explosion of the 90s. He loved mainstream, commercial culture, and the artsier musicians he loved (Nirvana, The Cure, Lou Reed) were all massively successful too. He believed Nirvana was the last great rock band, and all music afterward sucked. He began to record extremely lo-fi tapes in high school, and, because he had no musical training, they often contained simple guitar chords and drum beats recorded on separate tracks, with his dreamy, melodic singing laid on top. His vocal melodies were the main focus, and defined his style; they all sounded deeply familiar, as if you’d heard them somewhere before, but if you tried to place them, you’d never be successful. He attended the California Institute of the Arts in the late 90s, where he further solidified his style and met John Maus, who became a close collaborator. From 1996-2003, Pink recorded 200 cassette tapes worth of music, which he would slowly release over the next decade. He had been self-releasing, and pretty much only is friends and family had ever heard his music. This changed in 2003, when a mutual friend gave his tape titled *Worn Copy* to Animal Collective. They were blown away by how weird it was, and signed him to their new label. For the rest of the 00s, they released 5 of his tapes, and he built up a reputation underground as a truly unique artist.

In 2010, Pink was signed by 4AD, a legendary indie label. This allowed him to record in a real studio with a real producer for the first time. That year, he released *Before Today*, which was a studio re-working of various unreleased songs from his early tapes. It was his breakout record, with “Round and Round” being ranked by Pitchfork as the best song of 2010 and the second best song of 2010-2014. He followed it up in 2012 with *Mature Themes*, an intentionally difficult record meant to alienate the new fans he had gained. In 2014, he released his first record as just Ariel Pink (instead of Ariel Pink’s Haunted Graffiti), *pom pom*. It was his first to contain newly written songs, and his first to have other writers (including Azealia Banks, who co-wrote “Nude Beach A Go-Go” and recorded her own version). It’s often considered to be his best work by fans. His new reputation allowed him to do work with major artists like Charli XCX, Miley Cyrus, and MGMT. His 2017 album *Dedicated to Bobby Jameson* was also well-received. In 2021, tragedy struck: Pink attended the January 6 Capitol riot, and not long after was sued for sexual assault. His life and career have spiraled out of control since, seeing him get dropped by his label, get more involved in far-right politics, and as of 2025 he’s dating a woman who is self-avowed Nazi. The 2020s have seen the sad end of the career of one of the most interesting and influential artists of the 21st century.

Ariel Pink’s hypnagogic pop, a style pulled completely out of his brain and not belonging to any real musical tradition, had unbelievable influence of the music of the 2010s. His early tapes are super cool, and I highly recommend checking out *The Doldrums* or *Worn Copy* if only for the experience. The term “hypnagogic” refers to the liminal phase between being awake and falling asleep, and that’s the exact feeling his music evokes. It feels like you’re about to fall asleep, and various unconnected melodies from songs you heard at the grocery store earlier that day are reverberating around your head. It’s been described as “a memory of a memory”, and I think that’s apt. It’s a landfill of the popular styles of the 20th century, and yet there’s something so sweet about it: the melodies are catchy, pretty, and dreamy. What I find most interesting is that none of this was done intentionally or academically; it’s a naïve reflection of Pink’s own brain. The nostalgic and dreamy style was very influential on Panda Bear’s *Person Pitch*, which in turn inspired the chillwave genre of Toro y Moi and Neon Indian. Chillwave and hypnagogic pop more broadly caught on like wildfire in the early 2010s internet culture, and this produced vaporwave and everything that came from that. As the 2010s continued, these genres eventually inspired genres as broad as bedroom pop (Mac DeMarco, Rex Orange County, Clairo), hyperpop (Charli XCX, SOPHIE, A. G. Cook), and cloud rap (Playboi Carti, Yung Lean, Bladee). Some artists still make music in the original hypnagogic pop style, with Cindy Lee’s *Diamond Jubilee* in 2024 receiving substantial critical acclaim. Although Pink’s more famous 2010s output like *pom pom* saw a fusion of his hypnagogic pop style with a more conventional indie rock and neo-psychedelia sound, his early tapes are a singular oddity with no real antecedents. More than anything else, Pink’s sound is a skeleton key to understanding the music of the 2010s and beyond.

# Hargrove's review

I like this album a lot and have for a long time. I discovered Ariel Pink in 2017 or 2018 when I was first reading Pitchfork and “Round and Round” was number 2 on their list of Best Songs of the Decade So Far (2010-2014) (Grimes’ “Oblivion” was number 1, so this list was also partially responsible for me listening to Grimes for the first time). “Round and Round” is an amazing song and I would encourage anyone to listen to it. It’s more or less a basic pop song, nice melody and pretty singing, but there’s something simmering under the surface that’s so surreal. It’s really hard to place and it fucks with me to this day. So ethereal and dreamlike, it puts me into a trance. I wanted to know what else to listen to by Ariel Pink, so I turned to my other chief source of recommendations at the time, Anthony Fantano. He had given *pom pom* a 9, so I gave it a listen as well. It was much weirder and abrasive than “Round and Round”, but I was floored all the same. “Not Enough Violence” to this day among my favorite songs of all time, definitely in my top 50 at least. It left a huge impression on me. I still don’t understand how he can make songs with such standard instrumentation sound so bizarre, so off-putting. I love it. It just has to be the melodies. I often downplay the importance of melody because it’s so rare that music will have a truly new melody. As a result, I usually look more for unique atmospheres, or powerful songwriting, or interesting personalities, or complex rhythms. When music does have new and unusual melodies, though, it’s so amazingly exciting. Animal Collective is great for this too. Beyond “Not Enough Violence”, one of the coolest songs ever made, these are the others that have stood out to me over the years. “White Freckles” has a catchy and all-over-the-place guitar line that complements the frantic singing so perfectly. “Lipstick” has an awesome, sleazy bass synth that I just love, and the flutes remind me of some cool sonic textures Kero Kero Bonito would explore in the future. “Put Your Number in My Phone” is a gorgeously pretty 60s pop ditty with an unbelievably catchy chorus. “Nude Beach A Go-Go” is a song that I find kind of annoying, but I’ve found myself revisiting it more than any song other than “Not Enough Violence”. I just love juxtaposition of such a standard early 60s Beach Boys vocal surf track with the highly sexual lyrics. “Negativ Ed” is the best of the weird bouncy commercial jingle section in the middle of the record. Speaking of weird bouncy commercial jingles, “Jell-o” has begrudgingly earned my respect with just how unabashedly irritating it is. “Black Ballerina” has my favorite chorus (“Elevators, manufacturers”) and the bass line is positively infectious. I like “Exile on Frog Street” a lot, and it surprises me that it’s the least streamed song on the album; it’s really soft and nice to listen to, and creates such a pretty, wistful atmosphere. “Dayzed Inn Daydreams” is a stunning closer that saves some of the most beautiful melodies for last. This one absolutely kills me in that I SWEAR I have heard the verse and chorus melodies before but I cannot for the life of me find them anywhere else. It drives me CRAZY. This is not a perfect album by any means. It’s really scattered, really all over the place. The section from “Nude Beach A Go-Go” to “Jell-o” really tries your patience. It just explores every random idea Pink had, for better or for worse. Sometimes worse. But overall, very much for the better. This is a super weird album that I really love. It is unfortunate what Pink has become in real life, but it really doesn’t bother me in the way that some artists have (Mark Kozelek being a piece of shit, for example, is unfathomably heartbreaking to me). He’s a weirdo, very likely autistic, who has the supreme gift of translating exactly what’s in his brain into music. That music is often vapid, simple, naïve. It’s only fitting that he’s a manchild in real life. It’s what it is, and I certainly will never stop listening to his music, which I think is some of the most inventive shit ever put on wax. He never made an album that was consistently great all the way through, which is why my ranking might feel a little low after all this praise, but that was part of the charm.

Favorite tracks: White Freckles, Lipstick, Not Enough Violence, Put Your Number in My Phone, Nude Beach A Go-Go, Negativ Ed, Jell-o, Black Ballerina, Exile on Frog Street, Dayzed Inn Daydreams

Least favorite track: Dinosaur Carebears

# Aidan's review

Super unique sound and some amazing songs. I really wish that the album as a whole was more consistent because the gems are so damn good. Lipstick, Not Enough Violence, White Freckles and Dazed Inn Daydream are enough to make this album absolutely worth a listen. There's a contingent of songs that are good enough to satisfy as well, but there's also a few downright stinkers on here. Stuff like Nude Beach A Go-Go and Sexual Athletics could have worked in a different context (I could see Beat Happening pulling it off) but juxtaposed with the most sinister songs they just don't work. And because of how simple they are, them working is largely a factor of how much you buy into the simplicity. I prefer the slick synths of the great songs, and I can't find it in me to reconcile the sleekness of the good songs with the child-like whimsy of the bad songs. I don't think this record could really have been made another way, Ariel Pink makes such good synth pop because he truly never grew up, but I don't have to take the baby with the bathwater. Still a good album, but one I would enjoy a lot more with judicious use of the skip button.
