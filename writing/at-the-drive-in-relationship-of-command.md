# Write-up

At The Drive In was a post hardcore quintet from El Paso, Texas. The band started as a duo with Jim Ward on guitar and Cedric Bixler-Zavala singing. Their early lineup was tumultuous, but they eventually stabilized, adding Omar Rodríguez-López on guitar, Tony Hajar on drums and Paul Hinijos on bass. The group toured at a breakneck pace, their first tour merely spanned the entire state of Texas, while their sophomore trip saw them taking 42 days to travel 10,000 miles (4x the NY-LA distance!). The band would later go on 11,000 and 24,000 mile tours. This touring also allowed them to land their first record contract, after a few members of the tiny punk label Flipside were among the 9 people who came to one of their shows. Their reputation grew from there, and after releasing their first album, Acrobatic Tenement, the band was drawing triple digit crowds, a remarkable feat for an underground hardcore punk band.

Unfortunately, the kind of label that would pick you up from a 9 person show was not the kind of label to remain solvent, and the band was forced to move on to the largely pop-punk Fearless Records for their follow-up, In/Casino/Out. The band decided to record live in order to better capture their energy. The band's star continued to rise, leading to them touring with big names like Jimmy Eat World and Rage Against the Machine. Relationship of Command was their third and final album, and recorded right at the peak of their success. Iggy Pop was brought into the studio and features on vocals for two tracks, including the ransom note on "Enfilade". The band went on Letterman, then Conan, and the lead single, One Armed Scissor, got airtime on MTV.

Despite being at the peak of their artistic success, the band was plagued by issues. Two months after releasing the album, the band's van flipped over, leaving them with minor injuries. The band walked off a set in Sydney after the audience refused to stop moshing. Internally, Rodríguez-López and Bixler-Zavala had hard drug habits and desires for a different direction. Faced all of this combined with the newfound expectation from hitting it big, the band decided to quit at the top. Rodríguez-López and Bixler-Zavala formed the Mars Volta, and pursued a more prog rock inspired sound. The rest of band formed Sparta, which continued playing post-hardcore. Jim Ward later jumped ship to the Mars Volta, and recorded their debut album, De-Loused in the Comatorium (featured on prior versions of this list). His cousin, Jeremy was also a member before dying of a heroin overdose. This inspired Rodríguez-López and Bixler-Zavala to get clean themselves, and for Rodríguez-López to join Scientology, which would in turn lead to Danny Masterton allegedly sexually assaulting his wife. The band has reunited twice and released one album since breaking up.

# Hargrove's review

As I’ve said before, I have a real soft-spot for emo. It’s debatable whether At the Drive-In qualify as emo or are just post-hardcore, but it’s undeniable that they have a very similar sound and were extremely influential on the development and breakthrough of third wave emo. I think what I find so gratifying about good emo is that it’s loud, fast, high-octane, adrenaline rush music that doesn’t have the meat-headed dullness of so many other genres that share those qualities. The person screaming in emo always seems well-read, articulate, and clean. Much like me. You certainly get that in spades here on *Relationship of Command*. Bixler-Zavala spends nearly the entire album screaming at the top of his lungs while the guitars scrape a jagged edge through your eardrums. But they use big words with unclear meanings, and Rodríguez-López’ guitar is doing weird-ass shit, and the cover art is historical. It makes it feel like it’s the way I would be expressing my emotions. On top of that general feel, this album is just full of great songwriting. The first six songs, more than half of the album, are just banger after banger after banger. It’s crazy impressive. The band gets more experimental on the back half to mixed results, but there’s still a lot great to be found there. In a lot of ways, this feels like the culmination of the entire 90s post-hardcore scene. This sound is what things were building to, and it does not surprise me at all that a song like “One Armed Scissor” was the breakthrough of emo into the mainstream. Stellar songwriting brought to a sound that had been honed by many other great bands. It’s a recipe for success.

“Arcarsenal” starts off a little bit weak, but it finds its footing as soon as the vocals come in. Those vocals will be delivered with unrelenting energy for the entirety of the album, a constant that creates a pretty high floor for each song. The guitar also finds a pretty interesting melody, though the “cause I’d really like to meet him” part reigns supreme. “Pattern Against User” is perhaps the poppiest track, and it sounds damn good. Starting here, it feels like Bixler-Zavala’s vocal delivery is at least a little bit influenced by rap. I don’t know how exactly to explain it, but he comes off as an MC sometimes more than a singer. The vocals and the guitar are just divine on the chorus, and I like the quieter interlude section in the middle. Banger. “One Armed Scissor” is just ridiculously good. Those guitars slice into you right from the beginning, Then, during the verses, there’s a little repeated high-pitched two-note guitar part that plays every few bars and it reminds me so much of Slint. Super catchy chorus too. An amazing song. I absolutely LOVE that “Sleepwalk Capsules” starts with its foot all the way down on the gas after the quiet ending of the previous track. The vocals hit me in the chest like a ton of bricks. There’s a really nice call-and-response in the vocals here and the guitar plays around them perfectly. Absolutely zero energy has been lost so far, and that even continues into the lower tempo of “Invalid Litter Dept.” The energy of the intense anger has just been converted into a seething, smoldering exasperation. Just listen to the tension in the main guitar riff. Bixler-Zavala sounds a ton like Travis Morrison from The D Plan here, which is unsurprising since Morrison was very influenced by rap too. This song is maybe a bit too long, but it’s strong enough that it’s fine. “Mannequin Republic” is a little weak in the verses, but the chorus is intense enough that I love it and think it continues the energy. The lightning fast bass line is also a huge plus.

“Enfilade” is the first point at which the album falters, which honestly is pretty impressive. It’s where they start to experiment a bit, which I think is just not what this album is about. This is a pristine packaging of a sound that has already been experimented on for over a decade. Or, at least, it has been up to this point. The watery effects on the vocals are pretty annoying, and the guitar feels masturbatory at points. “Rolodex Propaganda” is a tight short banger though, which prevents the Iggy Pop cameo from being for naught. Bixler-Zavala sounds deranged on hi sportion of the chorus. Good track. “Quarantined” slows things down again and, though there’s an admirable vocal performance, I don’t think it pulls it off like “Invalid Litter Dept.” “Cosmonaut” is just as much of a banger as anything on the first half though. Man this song rules. One last hurrah of the amazing sound this album has produced. “Non-Zero Possibility” is intriguing at first and sounds unlike anything else on the album. It’s so hazy and directionless. And then the chorus is awesome. Unfortunately, it overstays its welcome and noodles off into nothingness. A really disappointing way to end the album.

As you can see, I have my complaints here and there, particularly on the back half. But man is there so much good. The 7 best songs on this album are better than the 7 best songs on so many other albums. It’s surprisingly deep for the level of quality. I really enjoyed it. About on Brand New level. And I really like Brand New.

Favorite tracks: Arcarsenal, Pattern Against User, One Armed Scissor, Sleepwalk Capsules, Invalid Litter Dept., Mannequin Republic, Rolodex Propaganda, Cosmonaut

Least favorite track: Enfilade

# Aidan's review

90s or 00s last album from a post hardcore band that randomly becomes critically acclaimed is its own micro genre at this point, and Relationship of Command is probably the best example of it. It's got a bit of emo, the name it halfway between the bluntness of Slint and the ornateness of And You Will Know Us By The Trail Of The Dead, it has the spoken word deliveries of D-Plan at sections and the experimentation of Unwound at points. My opinion on this album would shift every time I would listen to it, sometimes I'd really dig it from start to finish, and other I'd find it losing momentum towards the middle on songs like Invalid Litter Dept and Mannequin Republic. The ransom note bit at the beginning of Enfilade is annoying, but it serves as a nice palette cleanser into a latter half that I found constantly good, not great. Still, the first few songs come in with a jaw dropping amount of energy, and when I found myself getting into the groove, I ended up really liking this album. You hear segments of stuff that other bands would do later all over, and that alone is worth listening to.
