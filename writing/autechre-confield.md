# Write-up

Autechre (pronounced "aw-tek-er") is an English electronics duo formed in Manchester in 1987. Its two members, Rob Brown and Sean Booth, were both members of the graffiti and breakdancing scene in Manchester at the time. The two chose their name from typing random letters until they found something that sounded nice. In 1992, they signed to local Warp Records. They were put on the map along with other local artists when Warp Records' compilation album, Artificial Intelligence, garnered critical attention, becoming a cornerstone for the growing genre of  "Intelligent Dance Music", a label, like most of the genre, Autechre thinks is stupid. Also featured on Artificial Intelligence was "The Dice Man", an alias for Aphex Twin. The two released their debut album, "Incunabula", a year later, which was mostly just a compilation of old material. Their second album and third album each shifted them further away from the melodic sound of their debut album, moving more towards experimental and ambient techno.

Confield was their sixth studio album, and among their most abstract. The duo moved even further away from melodies towards more abstract sounds. Despite their roots in making club music, this album's music mostly came from the ideas that they had been unable to make work in a club environment. The duo also incorporated techniques from process music, using the music programming language Max. They called their method "the system". The album remains one of their best liked works. The duo are still making music to this day, with their most recent release coming in 2020. The band is also known for their pitch black shows, which the duo finds more immersive. They have also been involved with a collective known as "Gescom" that has released 10 EPs and two albums. Not much is known about the collective besides the involvement of Autechre, as the members are anonymous. Autechre cites Coil as one of their biggest influences, and in turn Charli XCX has shouted them out as an influence on her.

# Hargrove's review

Over the last few weeks, we’ve primarily listened to records that I had pre-existing strong feelings on, new (to me) records that I found decent, and new (to me) records that I found uninteresting. All three of those archetypes are super easy reviews for me to write. Autechre’s *Confield* is the first record to truly challenge me in some time. As I’m writing this, I’m finishing up my seventh and final listen and still haven’t worked out which tracks are my favorites and which is my least favorite. How can I? This record sounds like absolutely nothing else in terms of timbre, arrangement, atmosphere, and even emotional arc across the album. Some of it I really like, while some of it I really don’t. Though the schizophrenic percussion in the foreground of every track sonically resembles drum/drill and bass, the music conceptually takes after techno in its cold, mechanical non-melodies. Seriously, I don’t know if I can think of any other music this uninvitingly, bitterly frigid. Maybe some post-punk. Autechre achieves this temperature through the use of what I can only describe as those click-clacking metal balls you get as a prize for selling the 15th most chocolate bars in your fifth grade class. Autechre spends the entirety of the opener “VI Scose Poise” toying around with the novel sound before putting it into work in various mind-bending rhythms across the rest of the album. Often, these clacking ball sounds feel as if they’re allowed to roam free, akin to the aleatoric music of John Cage. These rhythms are paired with haunting synth atmospheres that somehow get creepier and creepier the deeper you get into the album. Maddeningly, this already chaotic mishmash of ideas has to wildly swing in quality throughout the album. The first three tracks are great, then the next three suck, and the final three are all over the place. I hope you can now see my predicament in reviewing this album. I will say this: love it or hate it, I was rarely bored.

“VI Scose Poise” finds Autechre spending seven whole minutes figuring out how to use a new musical toy. It reminds me a lot of Morton Feldman’s *Rothko Chapel + Why Patterns?*, particularly the “Why Patterns?” part. I enjoy listening to this creativity enough to name it a favorite. After a long demonstration of whatever weird-ass sound generation technique Autechre has discovered, “Cfern” shows us how it can make a hard fucking beat. The little bouncy balls make the same tone every time, so they mimic the snare on 2 and 4 just by slamming down as hard as possible. The synth atmosphere in the background sounds deeply foreboding, reminiscent of the cinematics of *Murmuüre*. I love the balls build from mimicking a basic techno beat to mimicking a drum and bass beat. “Pen Expers” starts much busier and more skittery than the last track. I mean that in a good way; it’s got so much going on that it’s genuinely hypnotic. It reminds me of drill and bass, but the atmosphere’s so minimal and barren that I don’t have to deal with the questionable aesthetic sensibilities of Squarepusher or Aphex Twin. Everything Autechre builds with this amazing track comes crashing down in the final minute. It’s quite the experience. The clacking balls take it easier in “Sim Gishel”, which instead leads with Ikeda-esque crackling noises. It doesn’t keep me as enthralled as the last two. It felt like all of the first three tracks kept building on top of each other, picking up where the last one left off, but this is a clear break in the chain. After the last one lost me, “Parhelic Triangle” drives me further away. There’s no groove to be found, nothing hypnotic, nothing to get lost in. Instead, multiple rhythms are overlaid that should not under any circumstances by overlaid. On the plus side, the synth atmosphere keeps getting bleaker. If nothing else, I have to give it credit for making me deeply anxious. “Bine” spikes my cortisol even further, upping the glitch factor and taking the synth atmosphere to a darker place still. I can’t stand the first half of this track, but when it settles into the sound of cultish chanting in a cave as the percussion overstimulates me near the end, I get back on board. “Eidetic Casein” has a funky slap bass feel to it, but then at the same time, there’s a crazy dissonant synth line that sounds like a carousel engine dying. I was not expecting this album to go in such a terrifying direction when it started. It’s another one that makes my hair stand on end for the first half but that I settle into by the end. “Uviol” is notably based around a shuffle rhythm. It’s minimal and repetitive otherwise, but the swing certainly makes it stand out. It keeps going in and out of swing, too. There are so many interesting little details in this album. It would be a great track except all the synths in the second half sound like flatulence to me. “Lentic Catachresis” brings the clacking balls pounding back with an intensity not seen since the first third. It backs the sound of short-circuiting, what I can only describe as a mass murder of robots. It ends things on an insane, and quality, note.

Now that we’ve completed the IDM on the list, I went back to re-listen and make sure I had my order set. I still don’t get *Selected Ambient Work 85-92* at all. Today must have been my 15th listen to that album and it has never once made me feel anything. Above that, Aphex Twin’s *Richard D. James Album* and Squarepusher’s *Hard Normal Daddy* remain roughly comparable. The drum patterns are similar in both style and quality. The main difference is the urban video game aesthetic of Squarepusher vs. the creepy classical aesthetic of Aphex Twin. Neither particularly appeals to me, but both are fine. Next up is Boards of Canada’s *Geogaddi*. I actually liked it better today than I did when we listened for the list, but it’s still too long and not as good as *Music Has the Right to Children*, which would be an easy A Tier for me were it on the list. I will be placing Autechre above all of these, but still in C Tier. At the end of the day, I guess IDM just isn’t really my genre (except *Music Has the Right to Children*, I love you).

Favorite tracks: VI Scose Poise, Cfern, Pen Expers, Lentic Catachresis

Least favorite track: Parhelic Triangle

# Aidan's review

What a weird album. It took me a few listens to get my head around it, but the album it reminded me the most of was Metal Machine Music. Both albums were interested in the shapes and textures they could evoke rather than sound. Confield has melodies too, and the best songs are where you can hear the extended melodies juxtaposed the fascinating repetitive textures. The opener VI Scose Poise does it pretty well, but where it shines the most is on Pen Expers, Sim Gishel and Lentic Catachresis. At times, the melodies aren't as compelling, causing the songs feel too repetitive, like on Parhelic Triangle and Bine. It's an acquired taste of an album, and it's certainly cool, but the main draw was the novelty of the sounds. Cool enough, and I'm happy I listened to it, but not something that I have a burning urge to come back to.
