# Write-up

Bathory was a Swedish black metal band who, at the time of this record, consisted of guitarist and vocalist Quorthon (real name Thomas Forsberg), drummer Vvornth (real name ?????), and bassist Kothaar (real name ?????). They were one of the most important bands to the first wave of black metal in pioneering the black metal sound. Bathory was formed in 1983, and its original drummer was Jonas Åkerlund, who has gone on to be a prolific music video director, working with Lady Gaga, Madonna, and Britney Spears among others. The name comes from 16th century Hungarian Countess Elizabeth Báthory, who was a serial killer with over 300 victims. Quorthon, who was the primary creative in the group, came from an oi punk (British subgenre of hardcore) background. In the early to mid 1980s, the dominant form of extreme metal (as opposed to mainstream metal) was thrash, with bands like Metallica, Slayer, and Megadeth becoming very successful. Thrash took the sound of late 70s NWOBHM (New Wave of British Heavy Metal) bands like Iron Maiden and combined it with hardcore punk. In 1982, speed metal (a precursor to thrash) band Venom released the album *Black Metal*, which combined speed an early thrash metal with Satanic lyrics. Though it was not yet black metal in sound, it would give the genre its name. In the mid 80s, bands like Hellhammer (Switzerland), Mayhem (Norway), and Bathory (Sweden) formed what is now called the “first wave” of black metal. Bathory would become the single most important band in defining the style, with their third album, 1987’s *Under the Sign of the Black Mark*, now considered the first truly black metal album. Black metal’s style consolidated as very similar to thrash metal, but with an even harsher and more aggressive sound, rawer production, and despicable lyrics. In 1988, Bathory released its fourth album, *Blood Fire Death*, which is today the most well-regarded Bathory album. Most of the record falls under the black metal sound as described above, but the tracks “A Fine Day to Die” and “Blood Fire Death” are long epics with a much slower pace and lyrics concerning vikings and pre-Christian Scandinavia. Their fifth album, 1990’s *Hammerheart*, would be entirely in this style, marking the first record in the viking metal genre. # Hargrove's review

Pretty solid album honestly. The two viking metal tracks (“A Fine Day to Die” and “Blood Fire Death”) are certainly the standouts, but overall this is a fine exhibition of the sound I think of when I think of metal. The drums are fast and consistent, the guitar is fuzzy and icy, the vocals are incomprehensible, and everything feels epic in scope. Just a solid album from front to back. Not transcendent, not middling, but damn solid. Will definitely be revisiting this and listening to the album they made after this one.

“A Fine Day to Die” and “Blood Fire Death” are the two best tracks, but I very very much preferred the latter to the former. “Blood Fire Death” is the masterpiece of this album, and the closest it comes to transcendence. I love the chanting that they bring back from the opening track, but I especially adore the background vocals through the main sections. They just make everything feel grand and important, as if the fate of the world was hanging in the balance. As if I’m Link in the Temple of Time. The drums are quite dynamic, which is refreshing after how much every other song relies on blast beats (not that that’s a bad thing, it was just a nice change of pace). The slow and heavy sections in the beginning have some really good guitar work, but my favorite guitar part is the mechanical line during the chorus. Speaking of the chorus, I’m a big fan of the wailed vocals there too. The acoustic bits at the end feel great and very earned, adding a reflective feel. This song is grand, ever-changing, and eminently enjoyable. By far my favorite. “A Fine Day to Die” is also quite good, just in a different league. I appreciate the restraint it shows in the opening, giving a minute and a half of buildup before things get metal. It’s similarly shapeshifting to “Blood Fire Death”, especially in terms of tempo. This is the first song with vocals and they’re jarring at first, but you get used to them. The lyrics aren’t anything special, but they do a good job of setting the tone and atmosphere. I am a little disappointed by how the song fizzles out at the end. Overall, two really unique and really good tracks.

In terms of the more mainline black metal songs, my two favorites were “The Golden Walls of Heaven” and “Holocaust”. “The Golden Walls of Heaven” has the best of the martial drum lines in the album. It’s so consistent and driving. It also works well with the lyrics about invading heaven and scalping God. The lyrics on this album are hit or miss, but they just work for me in this one, and the “Satan” acrostic is kind of cute. The tremolo guitar is also great. “Holocaust” also has great lyrics. The topic of nuclear war is kind of tired at this point, but I thought the lyrics were very evocative of the terror, and the sexual metaphors fit perfectly. This song is just a sonic assault from beginning to end and I love it. The drums are strong without being overbearing like they are in some other tracks. The guitar is grinding nonstop, complementing the drums perfectly. These two are really good.

“Oden Ride over Nordland” and “Dies Irae” are both good, but in a separate category from the two above. The opener “Oden Ride over Nordland” is really great at setting the atmosphere, establishing the Medieval setting, the martial themes, and the epic scope. However, it being an ambient intro makes me uncomfortable comparing it with the other tracks. “Dies Irae” has a lot to love, but it’s a flawed track. The “Christ the Bastard Son of Heaven” acrostic is uneven, unlike the consistent 5 letters in “Satan”, resulting in the verses being rhythmically and melodically awkward. However, I think the guitar work is standout, and the drums are just right. So it’s a win in my book.

“Pace Till Death” and “For All Those Who Died” are my two least favorites, particularly the latter. “Pace Till Death” has an interesting lyrical idea with the persona on the verge of death, but the execution does very little for me. It’s also far too drum-driven overall, with everything else lacking. That being said, I do really like the chord changes at the end of each vocal phrase. “For All Those Who Died” has super basic drums and the vocal performance is dry and boring. However, the line delivery of the title refrain is good. For both of these tracks, I don’t love ‘em, I don’t hate ‘em. The album outro is insignificant and wasn’t even supposed to be on this, so I’ll leave it be.

Overall, just a solid album. Great representative of the metal genre. Some good tracks, and something I would recommend to someone trying to get into more underground metal.

Favorite tracks: Oden Ride over Nordland, A Fine Day to Die, The Golden Walls of Heaven, Holocaust, Blood Fire Death

Least favorite track: For All Those Who Died
