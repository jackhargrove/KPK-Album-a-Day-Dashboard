# Hargrove's review

This is a record that my dad holds near and dear to his heart, and that I’ve been fascinated by since I was a little kid. The cover is just so vulgar when you’re 10. There’s a bad word and a lady in heat! But I never actually listened to it. First I was too young, and then when I started getting into music on my own I really steered clear of the overly harsh punk stuff. I did listen to Big Black’s version of “The Model” for the first time earlier this year and I was quite impressed. I genuinely think that Kraftwerk’s original “Das Model” is a top 20 if not top 10 song of all-time, and though Big Black’s version doesn’t do it better, it certainly does it justice. I also had a general familiarity with Albini as the guy who produced Joanna Newsom’s and Nirvana’s best albums, and also just as a generally important guy in the scene. I broke the news to my dad when he died last year, and I found myself surprised how distraught I was despite never having listened to any of his work as a musician. He just felt important to music as a scene, artistically and spiritually. Now I’ve finally listened to the vulgar album that so intrigued me as a kid. Many times. It’s so short! And I’ve found myself really liking it, more and more with each listen. There is nothing I *hate* more than agreeing with an artist’s take on his own work, but I have to agree with Albini that side 1 of this album is killer killer killer. Side 2 is much spottier, but overall this is a damn good record. The drum programming is phenomenal, it gives a cold, industrial edge that foreshadowed Nine Inch Nails (of whom I’m a huge fan). The bass lines are surprisingly funky. The vocals are wild, and the domination of the instrumental in the mix gives them a dark, creepy quality. And the guitars slice through your skin like a hot knife through butter. It’s unbelievable how close they sound to chainsaws at points. The biggest thing holding this album back is the weak songwriting on side 2. If side 2 were as good as side 1, this would be rank around XTC, Ween, and Weezer for me in high S Tier (my new highest tier is SS Tier). Unfortunately, if “ifs” and “buts” were candies and nuts, we’d all have a merry Christmas. Still, this album is good enough to rank quite highly. Certainly much higher than I thought it would going in.

“The Power of Independent Trucking” is a fiery and loud opener to this album that I thought was kind of insignificant at first but that I grew to really like. I enjoy how the vocals are so low in the mix as to be nearly inaudible, I love how fast the drum programming is, and the guitars feel like they’re flying right into my face. “The Model” is kind of cheating because it’s a cover of a perfect song, but I’m really impressed with how well they pulled it off. The jagged guitars work nearly as well as the peppy synths of the original, especially in the parts between the verses. The instrumental outro is pure sex. My only complaint is that they didn’t do verse 3. I really like the dissonance of the guitars in the opening of “Bad Penny” and the repeated riff is quite strong. I also find the lyrics quite funny; in the spoken word part in the middle, Albini kind of sounds like the “calc stands for calculator” guy and it reminds me that he’s just kind of a dork saying all this shit. This song scorches me from start to finish. “L. Dopa” slaps you in the face with its bass and drum programming straight from the get-go. The drums are so relentless that it’s almost a metal song. I also looked into the story behind the lyrics and it’s genuinely existentially terrifying. Good track. “Precious Thing” has a drum beat that makes it sound like a new wave song, albeit a really fucked up new wave song, and I think that’s funny. The bass line is crazy good too. I’m only so-so on the vocals and guitars, but the rhythm section makes it one of my favorite tracks. “Colombian Necktie” ends side 1 as the least good song, but it’s still good enough to be one of my favorite tracks. It has the energy and intensity of the songs that precede but without the memorability or stickiness. Thankfully, it has enough of the former to outweigh the latter, mostly because the guitar fucks so hard.

“Kitty Empire” starts side 2 as the first real miss, which is unfortunate as I really like it conceptually. Albini including an entire track imagining his pet cat as a king is both funny and endearing. It also has a rumbling, ominous atmosphere that feels oppressively heavy (in a good way). But the issue is that it’s the longest song on the album by a wide margin, and that combined with its slow tempo just grinds the momentum of the album to a halt. I think if they capped it at 3 minutes it would have been great, but it’s just left on a bit too long. “Ergot” though tops it as my least favorite track. The super fast hi-hats are cool, but the song is a chaotic mess. Still, I could overlook that if everything wasn’t so muddled. It dulls the razor-sharp edge that has defined the album up to this point. “Kasimir S. Pulaski Day” is fine but entirely unremarkable. “Fish Fry” starts to pick things back up again. It does the quiet/loud thing pretty well, the guitar is feedbacky as hell, the drum programming is back to breakneck pace, and the vocals are even kind of catchy. Not quite a favorite, but the best on this side so far. “Pavement Saw” is in a similar league, with one caveat: this is the only song where I think the drum programming is overdone. It jsut barely crosses the line into being overbearing and detracting from the rest of the track. “Tiny, King Of the Jews” is by far the best rack on side 2, and is as good as anything on side 1. It randomly sounds very 80s synthpop, and it’s, like, good 80s synthpop. It builds tension amazingly well, feeling like it’s constantly growing into something massive and unwieldy. It’s also got a great melody. One of the best tracks period. “Bombastic Intro” feels like the stellar drum programming is taking a quick bow, and I like that enough to include it on my favorite tracks list as well.

This is just a really cool album. It’s much better than it has any right to be. When things work, they work so well. I’m sure it no longer sounds as transgressive or abrasive as it did back in 1987, but man does it still sound sharp and unique. It’s inconsistent (how could it not be?), but I see myself coming back to it very frequently.

Favorite tracks: The Power of Independent Trucking, The Model, Bad Penny, L. Dopa, Precious Thing, Colombian Necktie, Tiny, King Of the Jews, Bombastic Intro

Least favorite track: Ergot
