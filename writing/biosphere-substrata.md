# Write-up

Biosphere, real name Geir Aule Jenssen, is very familiar with the cold. Born above in Tromsø, Norway, the most northern city with >30,000 people, he climbs mountains as a hobby and studied archeology, specifically the Ice Age. Prior to releasing music as Biosphere beginning in 1991, he was part of Bel Canto, a synth pop trio which continued after he left and released solo works as Bleep. His initial solo works are typically classified as house music, but with Substrata, he moved towards an ambient sound, with samples from the David Lynch directed TV show Twin Peaks and a ABC documentary on Russian psychics. The interest in Russia and several other foreign countries is a continued theme throughout his work, his second album was titled "Patashnik" after Russian slang for a "goner", and he has also drawn inspiration from Japan and France. He has also created soundtracks for films, the most notable of which is was made for a festival to accompany Dziga Vertov's groundbreaking 1929 film, Man with Movie Camera.

# Hargrove's review

Ambient electronica is really not my genre, so I was pretty apprehensive going in. I was pretty down on this the first few listens, but it did grow on me. I think everything from “Kobresia” onward is pretty damn good, but the first half of the record still leaves much to be desired for me. When this album is trying to be atmospheric, more often than not it just doesn’t work. I see a lot of people describe this record as very cold, but I really don’t get that at all. On the other hand, I think this record has a lot of cool textures, and I found I preferred it when it leaned into that aspect of things. The first track that really explores textures is “Chukhung”, with that echoing sound that feels almost 3-dimensional. It runs counter to this jangling sound line, and I think the effect works quite well. I’m a huge fan of the guitar on “Times When I Know You’ll Be Sad”, especially the final strum of each repeated line. The drowning out of the guitar at the end is well-executed as well. “Kobresia” is the best track on the album, with this sweeping, soaring instrumental. I found the *Twin Peaks* spoken word samples in other tracks to be kind of annoying, but the Russian sample here is great. I also like how much louder this track is than the others. There’s also relaxed guitar strumming that pops up occasionally, which I love. “Antennaria” is nice too, the main instrumental line feels so off-kilter and sharp while the droning back and forth in the background sets the atmosphere. This track balances texture and atmosphere better than any other. “Uva-Ursi” rounds out the best three track run of the album with an actual rhythm. The guitar sounds out of tune (in a good way) and it pulsates like one of the visions Alice sees as she’s falling through the rabbit hole into Wonderland. “Sphere of No Form” is a step down but still I appreciate the rough foghorn texture that contrasts with everything else. “Silene” drops back into a glacial pace which worried me, but the mechanical chugging main instrumental line is enjoyable, and I love the droning undercurrent of darkness. Unfortunately, quite a few of the tracks on the front half didn’t work for me at all. I really didn’t enjoy any of the *Twin Peaks* samples. The opener “As the Sun Kissed the Horizon” is a complete nothingburger. People seem to really like “Poa Alpina” but I really only cared for the voices; otherwise, the atmosphere isn’t enough to carry the track given how static it is. My least favorite track is a competition between “The Things I Tell You” and “Hyperborea”. The former is half uninteresting stereo shudders, half mechanical oblivion. The latter is just “talking talking talking talking BEEP BOOP BEEP BOOP” that does nothing for me. Overall though, I found enough that I liked to make this an enjoyable and rewarding listen. Certainly worse than Oneohtrix Point Never and Tim Hecker though.

Favorite tracks: Chukhung, Times When i Know You’ll Be Sad, Kobresia, Antennaria, Uva-Ursi, Silene

Least favorite track: Hyperborea

# Aidan's review

Absolutely adored The Winter Ray, the last ambient album I listened to and this just didn’t match up. Samples are so so and twin peaks definitely has become trite by now to sample. Agree that I didn’t really get a cold vibe from this, although the soundscapes are the highlight of the album. The tactile feeling in some of the tracks with dripping water, the sound of the Russian psyching swallowing and breathing, they’re all amazing. The actual music is a lot less inspiring, it’s completely fine, but rarely does anything really notable, just keeps to the ambient playbook. Overall there just wasn’t enough interesting things vs annoying things for me to really enjoy it. I think my bar for ambient albums is a little higher because variety matters a lot less so you can replay your favorites a lot, but this was not one of my favorites.
