# Write-up

Björk Guðmundsdóttir is an Icelandic art pop musician born in 1965. One thing to keep in mind while reading this: the population of Iceland is currently 400,000 people (a little less than Staten Island), and it was 200,000 around 1970 (about the same as Yonkers right now). Iceland is tiny. When Björk was 6, her parents put her in school to study piano and flute. When she was 11, she sang at a school talent show, which earned her airplay on Iceland’s only radio station, which in turn earned her a record contract. She released the album *Björk* as a tween in 1977, but it is not usually considered part of her discography. After this experience, she became a part of the budding punk scene, forming a band called Spit and Snot. In the early 80s, she formed a jazz group. Around 1982, she entered the avant-garde art scene. In 1983, she joined a post-punk and gothic rock group. All of this is to say, Björk had experimented with an astonishing variety of styles by the time she was 18. In 1986, Björk founded and became lead vocalist for the Sugarcubes, becoming part of the growing underground “alternative rock” movement. The Sugarcubes released the single “Birthday” in 1987, which became surprise hit in the English-speaking world. After three albums and a performance on SNL, the Sugarcubes broke up in 1992. The year prior, Nirvana’s *Nevermind* suddenly made the alternative rock scene wildly popular worldwide. The stage was set for Björk to begin a solo career.

Björk moved to London in 1992. Her first single, 1993’s “Human Behaviour”, was a hit. But it wasn’t alternative rock at all; since moving to London, Björk became obsessed with the house music scene, and began making music in that style. She was also part of the early trip hop scene, and the single was produced by a Massive Attack affiliate. Her 1993 debut solo album, aptly titled *Debut*, contained more artsy house music. It went platinum in the US, rocketing her to stardom. She released her second album *Post* in 1995, pivoting to more confrontational techno and industrial inspired art pop. *Post* also went platinum and received even better reviews than *Debut*. In 1996, while working on her third album in London, a crazed stalker attempted to kill her with a sulfuric acid mail bomb and then filmed himself committing suicide. While she avoided the attack after police found the video, it affected her deeply and caused her to move to Spain. There, she completed her third album, 1997’s *Homogenic*. Björk’s goal was to make an album that explored only one sound instead of her diverse genre explorations on her first two records, hence the title’s play on “homogeneous”. Additionally, she wanted to make music that was more personal. She settled on a sound that combined cold electronic beats with lush string orchestras to reflect the dualities of Iceland: green meadows and active volcanoes, traditional people and advanced technology. The album produced 5 music videos, including two of the most acclaimed in history for “Hunter” and “All Is Full of Love”. It only went gold commercially but is regarded by critics as her best album.

Björk released *Vespertine* in 2001, going in a softer and more intimate direction. It is often seen as Björk’s best by fans and the internet. Since then, she has released six more albums, most recently *Fossora* in 2022. Her style has gotten increasingly experimental and eclectic, though 2015’s *Vulnicura* notably saw decent success. She has continued to collaborate with artists on the cutting edge of popular music, including Death Grips, The Knife, Arca, and Rosalía. She is frequently cited by other musicians as a major influence. Thom Yorke considers “Unravel” his favorite song credits Radiohead’s decision to shift to electronic music on *Kid A* to *Homogenic*. Kanye West claims she was one of the biggest inspirations for *808s and Heartbreak*. And, of course, an entire generation female of alt-pop musicians in the 2010s aspire to her uniqueness and artistry.

# Hargrove's review

This is such a good album. I don’t listen to Björk nearly often enough. In recent times, I mostly revisit stuff like “Army of Me” and “It’s Oh So Quiet”, but the last few days of keeping *Homogenic* on repeat has kicked my ass into gear. Juxtaposition for the sake of juxtaposition can often ring hollow, but the combination of the electronic beats and the gorgeous strings works so damn well. Remarkably, beautifully, magically well. I think they each complement a different component of Björk’s sound and songwriting and that’s why it feels so natural. Those glitchy electronics set the baseline with her drive, her focus, and her aggression, while the strings add her flare for theatrics and unabashed humanity. It’s a gimmick that’s more than enough to carry a 40 minute album. I’ve always been partial to unusual female singing voices, and Björk’s is no exception. Not only do I like how distinctive she sounds, but in particular I respect how willing she is to stretch and strain her voice. This is a quality I’ve always admired in Fiona Apple and Joanna Newsom. There’s a time and a place for detached coolness, but passion always wins me over in the end. Lyrically, it’s mixed. There are some truly standout lines on a few tracks (I’ll mention them later), but others leave me feeling pretty cold. Björk’s emotive singing and melodramatic instrumentals lend themselves to moments of pure ecstasy when the words match the music, so I do wish there was a little more consistency here. There are also a few songs that I don’t think are all that strong, even if stylistically they’re fantastic. But still, this is a very good album. I feel comfortable calling it my favorite Björk album as a whole.

“Hunter” is so, so, so good. What an insane opener. The glitchy, fluttering drum beat sets such an ominous tone. I love how it plays with stereo, unpredictably going from one ear to the other. The ooooooohs in the background help build that intensely foreboding atmosphere, and then the strings come in to bring it over the top with melodrama. The lyrics are as focused and, frankly, terrifying as the instrumental, making this one fantastic and coherent song. The best part of “Jóga” is definitely its chorus. The extra beat inserted every three bars makes everything feel unbalanced and off-kilter. The strings play a beautifully complex melody. I don’t agree with the assessments online that it’s her best song (or even the best on this album), but it’s pretty good. I unfortunately don’t see what Thom Yorke sees in “Unravel”. I think it’s one of the weaker tracks. It’s stripped back, but without the intensity of something like “Hunter”. I think this album excels when it leans into its aggression (“Hunter”, “5 Years”), its grandiosity (“Bachelorette”, “Jóga”), or both (“Pluto”). “Unravel” just doesn’t follow any of those paths. “Bachelorette”, on the other hand, is a BANGER. Holy SHIT what a good song. Its strings are no doubt the best on the whole album. They just build and build and build and build, creating nonstop tension. I don’t know how I haven’t made this connection before, but Magdalena Bay’s “Cry for Me” reminds me a lot of “Bachelorette”. “I’m a fountain of blood/In the shape of a girl” is one of my favorite lines from the album.

“All Neon Like” slows down the pace significantly, but it’s placed really well on the album so it works. It’s a decent song with a really playful drum line but it maybe goes on a bit too long and isn’t as memorable as some of the others. I really like “5 Years”. It’s got great lyrics and even better delivery. I appreciate that she seems to be someone who’s unafraid to try her hardest and care about things and it’s cathartic to hear her excoriate someone who doesn’t. “I’m so bored of cowards”! I also like the electronic instrumental kind of swirling into and beating against itself like a wave. “Immature” is quite simple. It’s not bad, but it feels undeveloped in both the vocal line and the instrumental. I think it’s the track that could most easily be left off the album. I love “Alarm Clock” though. It’s the song that sounds the least like the rest of the album, but i really don’t mind that at all weirdly. I think it’s just how joyful Björk sounds. It’s so smooth, so hip hop, and you can just vibe out to it. “Pluto” goes pretty hard. It’s got great aggression and harshness but still those oooooohs are so beautiful. It’s just cathartic. And super industrial. You know me, I like abrasive noises and weird female vocalizations. This is that. “All Is Full of Love” actually reminds me a bit of *Hosianna Mantra*. It’s ambient in a new age kind of way. It’s pretty, introduces new textures, and kind of abandons rhythm in favor of flow. It’s a nice way to end things.

This album has been a pleasure to listen to and I’m sad I have to put it down for now. God, I just love strings. Have I mentioned that I love strings? Have I said “melodrama” yet? Have I said “strings inject melodrama”? God, I love strings.

Favorite tracks: Hunter, Jóga, Bachelorette, 5 Years, Alarm Call, Pluto, All Is Full of Love

Least favorite track: Immature
