# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Blank Banshee is the alias of Patrick Driscoll, a Canadian musician and visual artist. A similar name was originally used for Blank Banshees, a band consisting of Driscoll and Curtis “Cormorant” Ferguson in 2010. The duo performed at the Canadian East Coast Music Awards in 2011. Ferguson left the group in 2012, Driscoll moved to Vancouver, and he released his debut solo album *Blank Banshee 0* under the name Blank Banshee. The album was released as a “pay what you want” digital download, the same way that Radiohead’s *In Rainbows* was released in 2007. The album was released as part of the vaporwave and seapunk aesthetics/scenes, both of which I will explain in the next paragraph. It combined vaporwave sounds with trap (a hip hop subgenre, if you’re unaware) beats, pioneering the microgenre-within-a-microgenre of vaportrap. Blank Banshee has continued to release well-received albums since this one, developing a more mature IDM sound, but his early work is still remembered as one of the finest examples of vaporwave. The song “Teen Pregnancy” eventually became associated with Simpsonwave (a trend in 2016 where clips from The Simpsons would be played over vaporwave songs) and was also a TikTok song in 2020.

# Hargrove's review

I will admit it up front: I had a vaporwave phase Freshman Winter. Early 2020 was probably the nadir of vaporwave’s influence, with it being 8 years since its heyday and immediately before its rennaissance during the pandemic. I thought the aesthetic was super interesting, but like a typical poser I only listened to two vaporwave albums, *Floral Shoppe* and Eccojams. I liked both quite a bit though, if more intellectually than viscerally. Dead mall videos are super eery and I highly recommend you guys check them out. Anyway, I was excited that we got to listen to a vaporwave album that I hadn’t listened to. Overall, I thought it was alright. The trap-flavored beats™️ are an interesting touch that make this, for better or for worse, the most early 2010s piece of art in existence. I particularly liked the stretch of songs early in the record that had that guitar that sounds like the Home Depot commercial. I also really liked the chords in some songs. There were a few tracks that I didn’t love though, and a few more that felt kind of superfluous (which isn’t great for an album already so short). Overall, I liked it less than Eccojams but more than *Floral Shoppe*.

By far my favorite track is “Venus Death Trap”. This has the Home Depot guitar I referenced, and it uses it at its most dynamic and frenetic. The rhythm is off-kilter in the best way, it kind of reminds me of “Idioteque”. I also like the weird-ass vocal sample. The preceding track “Ammonia Clouds” is the first Home Depot guitar song and also very much to my liking. The guitar solos are great, as are the synth crescendos. This track does the best job for me at evoking that corporate nostalgia feeling that I want to mainline into my veins. “Hyper Object” turns the Home Depot guitar funky, as if Nile Rodgers were being trapped in that giant white space Squidward gets banished to. The guitar is genuinely hypnotic, and the vocals are nice and high. The last of this group is “Photosynthesis”, which demotes the guitar to the “verse” sections instead of the “chorus” sections, but I like it all the same.

Another song I really like is the closer, “B:/ Shut Down”. It has this nocturnal, achingly beautiful feeling to it that really evokes longing to me. I think it’s one of the best tracks in spite of its brevity. The opener “B:/ Startup” is also quite good; the samples going back and forth between two notes (my guess is the dominant and the tonic, Carms am I correct?) basically pares down tension build and release into their most basic elements. I’m biased on “Wavestep” because it samples from *The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time* and I’m a Zelda fanboy, but the watery synths are cool. It’s also very relaxing until the revving noises near the end. “Teen Pregnancy” is also nice with the watery synths, and the spoken word sample is quite funny.

There are unfortunately some pretty forgettable tracks on this project. My least favorite by far is “Bathsalts”, it’s just so in-your-face when everything else is relaxing. “Deep Space” is a little too spare for my liking. “Visualization” is an interlude in an album that’s way too short for interludes. “World Vision” is a little too similar to the opener. Probably some others that didn’t leave a huge impression on me.

Overall, this leaves me at the same place as other vaporwave albums: really cool on a conceptual level, and a few bangers that are nice to listen to, but for the most part doesn’t sustain an entire album. I feel like it’s telling that I enjoyed writing the write-up much more than this review. The aesthetic is great though/

Favorite tracks: B:/ Startup, Wavestep, Ammonia Clouds, Venus Death Trap, Hyper Object, Photosythesis, Teen Pregnancy, B:/ Shut Down

Least favorite track: Bathsalts
