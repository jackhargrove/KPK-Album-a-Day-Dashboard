# Write-up

Blasphemy is a black metal band formed in Vancouver, Canada, in 1984 that at the time of this record consisted of vocalist and bassist Nocturnal Grave Desecrator and Black Winds (real name Gerry Bull, hereafter known as “Nocturnal”), guitarist Caller of the Storms (real name Geoff Drakes, hereafter known as “Storms”), rhythm guitarist and backing vocalist Traditional Sodomizer of the Goddess of Perversity (real name Marco Banco, hereafter known as “Sodomizer”), and drummer 3 Black Hearts of Damnation and Impurity (real name Sean Stone, hereafter known as “Black Hearts”). The band pioneered a subgenre of black metal known as war metal. Nocturnal and Black Hearts founded Blasphemy in 1984, with Storms joining next. The band has also previously been known as Antichrist, Desaster, and Thrash Hammer. Blasphemy released its first demo in 1989. *Fallen Angel of Doom….*, the band’s full-length debut album, came out in 1990. It is known for its brutal heaviness and for being the first true war metal album. Blasphemy followed it up with another record, *Gods of War*, in 1993. The band was inactive for most of the 90s and 00s, but the band has continued performing since 2009. Nocturnal, Caller, and Black Hearts are all still members, while Sodomizer has since been replaced by Deathlord of Abomination and War Apocalypse. The members are all kind of delinquent criminals; most of them have served time in prison, including one who attempted to smuggle drugs and his girlfriend onto a flight to Amsterdam.

War metal, the genre Blasphemy is credited with creating, is heavily based off of the first-wave of black metal. This makes it a cousin genre to Norwegian (second-wave) black metal instead of a descendant genre. First-wave black metal bands like Bathory often based their sound off of thrash metal. War metal takes that brutal, thrashy, first-wave black metal base and adds elements of death metal like abrupt tempo shifts and growled vocals. The Canadian war metal scene, led by Blasphemy, would often hang around the Ross Bay Cemetery in Victoria, British Columbia, where they would play metal music and perform “Satanic rituals” as part of the Ross Bay Cult.

# Hargrove's review

We’ve had such a good run with metal albums. 255 albums in, 22 of which were metal, and I had only one metal album below C Tier (and even that one, the Type O Negative album, is in high D, and very much due for a revisit after over a year). Unfortunately, Blasphemy’s *Fallen Angel of Doom....* suuuuuuuuuuuuucks. On my first two listens, I genuinely thought this record was going to be a competitor for the very bottom spot on my ranking. It sounded like complete nonsense, indistinguishable noise without any of the things I like about metal. Eventually, I was able to break through the lo-fi production and actually get a handle on how things sounded (this process may or may not have been aided by lifting weights). The issue is, it takes a decent amount of effort to start parsing this record, and the reward is…still pretty bad? The 80% of the riffs on this record are so Goddamn flat. Metal is a genre built around the primacy of the guitar riff. If you want to be an essential metal record, you need to have better riffs than this! The songs on the second half do generally have better, more interesting riffs than the first half, but even the best are only okay. On top of that, the guitar solos are fucking dogshit. They appear out of nowhere, play nonsense for four bars, and then disappear. Outside of the guitar, the vocals on this thing just sound hilarious. I never felt any genuine menace or desolation as I do on all of the other black metal albums we’ve listened to, even the ones I don’t care for. The quasi-death growls and “yeah” interjections sound straight out of a cartoon. The drums are less bad than the other elements, but I find the insane tempo a serious detriment given the album’s other problems. When everything already sounds so flat and lifeless, super fast drumming only serves to make it less distinguishable. Overall, I think what I heard on this record is akin to what Aidan hears when he listens to fast metal albums that I like but he doesn’t. I don’t mean to compare Blasphemy to any number of bands that use blast beats better, as I still think they’re great. But I think now I can hear what Aidan hears.

I don’t hate the airy, spacious intro that is “Winds of the Black Godz (Intro)”. I could see it on an indie or shoegaze or emo album. “Fallen Angel of Doom” starts real sudden, and God does the voice sound stupid. The death growls are peppered in all wrong. The drums guide the track more than the tepid, indistinct riff. There’s a decent chugging section near the end, but that’s the only saving grace. The next three tracks all sound almost identical to the first track. “Hording of Evil Vengeance” at least starts with a recognizable riff, but then it goes back into grinding sludge. Cool intros followed by bullshit becomes a recognizable pattern on this record. Another pattern is switching between extremely fast and midtempo sections. The midtempo sections are 10x better, but only take up 20% of the run time. “Darkness Prevails” begins with cool chanting, then goes back into the same crunched drumming, indistinguishable riffs, and Sisyphean solos. “Desecration” gets the nod for my least favorite track for two reasons. Firstly, the midtempo section on this one sucks just as bad as the fast section. Secondly, it comes at the end of the first half’s run of awfulness. “Ritual” is the first track to sound like a real song. The drums are a little bit crisper, and there’s an actual tension-building, structural guitar riff in the fast part. If I were the label, I would choose this as the single. “Weltering Blood” is not as good as “Ritual”, but still much better than the first half. The vocals being so low in the mix is a marked improvement, and there’s some real intensity building in the chorus. “Demoniac” is about on par with “Weltering Blood”, though the voice at the end is kind of annoying. “Goddess of Perversity” has the highest highs of any track on the album. There’s a cool intro followed up by a passable riff that continues into fast drumming with a gong sound effect. Then, there’s an actually good, bouncy rhythmic part. Things get worse once the vocals start, so it’s less consistent than “Ritual”, but everything outside of the verse parts is decent. “The Desolate One (Outro)” goes back to the abysmal quality of the first half, with the caveat that there’s a decent breakdown in the middle that actually gets kind of sludgy. The outro is cool too; peaceful but creepy, more elevated than I would have expected to end this album.

On the bright side, we were due for a truly awful record. We’ve had a pretty good hot streak. I’ll take the recalibration.

Favorite tracks: Ritual, Goddess of Perversity

Least favorite track: Desecration
