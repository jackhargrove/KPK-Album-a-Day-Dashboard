# Write-up

Boris is a Japanese experimental rock and metal band formed in 1992 consisting of lead guitarist Wata (woman 😍), bassist and rhythm guitarist Takeshi, and drummer Atsuo. All three provide vocals. Though they’ve never found much success in their native Japan, they have released multiple albums that have gotten huge in the US underground scene. They are obsessed with early Seattle sludge metal group Melvins, who were friends with Kurt Cobain and predecessors of grunge. They took their name from a Melvins song and think they’re the greatest band of all time. Boris’ sound is difficult to classify, but for the most part they play doom, sludge, and drone metal; they experiment all over the place though, into post-rock, hardcore punk, shoegaze, you name it. They released their debut album in 1996 and their second album in 1998. Their third album, *Flood*, was released in 2000, and we listened to it a couple months ago. After releasing two more albums, they released *Boris at Last -Feedbacker-* in 2003. After three more albums, they released *Pink* in 2005, which was their commercial breakthrough and received rave reviews from American indie outlets like Pitchfork. In 2008, they went on tour with Nine Inch Nails, further bolstering their popularity. They are prolific, with 28 solo albums (most recent in 2022) and numerous collaborations, including 7 with Japanese harsh noise pioneer Merzbow (has an album on the list) and American drone metal artist Sunn O))) (has an album on the list). There’s not a ton of information out there about Boris, but the coolest fact I can find is that their bassist and rhythm guitarist, Takeshi, plays a double necked bass guitar so he can play bass and guitar at the same time. Also, Wata and Atsuo are married with children. *Boris at Last -Feedbacker-* is one of their three most celebrated albums, alongside *Flood* and *Pink*. Much like *Flood*, the album is one long composition broken down into sections. They have played the composition in its entirety live at All Tomorrow’s Parties festival in 2009 and 2010. # Hargrove's review

This album is super similar to *Flood* except with harsher sounds. The structure is more or less the same, with a long ambient opening, post-rock build, a banging climax halfway through, and then looooooooong falling action. I can respect this structure. I think it’s pretty interesting, and there are some cool things you can do with falling action. Unfortunately, in both cases that aspect doesn’t really work for me. The build in the beginning is fine because you know something amazing is going to come, made all the better by your patience. But then you get to, for instance, “Feedbacker, Pt. 4”. It’s 9 minutes of straight up harsh noise wall and practically nothing by way of structure. Now, I could see myself finding this satisfying, cathartic even, if I were a little bit more drawn into the album. I really tried to be. “Feedbacker, Pt. 2” and “Feedbacker, Pt. 3” are both quite good. I particularly like the former; I found “Feedbacker, Pt. 1” to be too long for how static it was, but once the drums come in at the beginning of “Feedbacker, Pt. 2” that same slow-moving sound becomes a vibe. And then that psychedelic guitar solo that kicks in halfway through, it’s heaven. The build in this section is truly great, better than the build in the second part of “Flood”. “Feedbacker, Pt. 3” is 5 minutes of sustained climax, and it’s executed well. The bass work in particular stands out to me, and I like how the last 3 minutes or so are a rock song à la Queens of the Stone Age. It doesn’t reach the highs of the third part of “Flood” (when the vocals kick in heavy), but the core of this record, that is, parts 2 and 3, are rock solid. But the beginning is aimless and formless, and the falling action is a slog. Still, the middle is good enough to keep this in B Tier, but it’s below *Flood* simply because that album has a higher ratio of kick-ass to noodling.

Favorite tracks: Feedbacker, Pt. 2, Feedbacker, Pt. 3

Least favorite track: Feedbacker, Pt. 4
