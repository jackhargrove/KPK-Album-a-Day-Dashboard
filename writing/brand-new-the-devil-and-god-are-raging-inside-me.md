# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Brand New is an emo band that formed in 2000 in Long Island, with Jesse Lacey as lead singer and songwriter, Vincent Accardi on lead guitar and a secondary songwriter, Brian Lane on drums, and Garrett Tierney on the bass. Various members of the band had played together in the late 90s in the Long Island post-hardcore scene, but they officially formed the band in 2000, choosing the name Brand New as a joke because their sound wasn’t particularly revolutionary. They released their debut album in 2001, a pretty standard and angsty emo album from the time when emo was at its peak popularity. Also around this time, Brand New got into a massive beef with fellow emo band Taking Back Sunday. In 2003, Brand New put out their second album, *Deja Entendu*. This record was a huge step up over their debut, keeping the aesthetics of emo but using significantly more complex and interesting instrumentation. To this day, *Deja Entendu* is considered one of the best emo albums of all time.

After their second album, Brand New took a short break from making music and they moved to a major record label. Despite the move to a major label, the band began making the least commercial music of their careers; the early demos of *The Devil and God Are Raging Inside Me* are heavily, HEAVILY influenced by *Spiderland*. However, in early 2006, the demos for the album leaked online. This forced Brand New to re-record the songs for the official album, giving them more care and enhancing the sound. The end result still had that Slint influence, but Brand New had truly carved out their own niche. Emo was well past its prime commercially, but what Brand New was doing was well past the basic emo of the early 2000s anyway. Many have argued that *The Devil and God Are Raging Inside Me* is more of an indie rock album with emo aesthetics. It is commonly considered the group’s signature albums, and one of the greatest emo records of all time.

I’m going to use this third paragraph to give a brief history of emo. The Velvet Underground established garage rock in the 60s as a do-it-yourself version of rock music with loud noises and a chaotic spirit. This ended up being the basis for the punk music of the early 70s, where barely-trained musicians channeled their anger into the few chords they knew on the guitar. In the early 80s, hardcore punk gained popularity, with better musicians but louder guitars and lots and lots and lots of screaming. Hardcore punk led to post-hardcore, which took the aesthetics of hardcore punk but made it a little bit artsier and sophisticated. Post-hardcore became the most popular genre in Washington DC in the late 80s. DC group Rites of Spring developed a niche within post-hardcore with very sad and emotional performance and are often considered the first emo band. The genre was still very harsh, but in 1991 the post-hardcore group Slint released their seminal album *Spiderland* which contributed a chiller sound to emo. In the mid-90s, a group of emo bands from the middle of the country became popular, and this established second-wave or Midwest emo. Emo broke into the national consciousness in the early 2000s and became one of the most popular genres for a time, with groups like Jimmy Eat World and My Chemical Romance. By the time of this record, emo was waning in popularity, allowing Brand New to take it to more interesting new places. There was an emo revival in the 2010s, which was more influenced by Brand New than any other group.

# Hargrove's review

I think it’s no secret at this point that I have a special place in my heart for emo music (looking at you “Misery Business”). While my favorite emo bands typically come out of the 90s, *The Devil and God Are Raging Inside Me* is the shining example of good 2000s emo. Emo as an aesthetic had gotten really trash by 2006, and one thing that I really respect about Brand New is that no matter how good their music got, they refused to abandon the ridiculous emo persona. I mean on “Degausser”, amid the swirling guitars and strangely melodic screams, we get such terrible, angsty lines as “Life is a test and I get bad marks”. Just fucking incredible, I don’t mean this as a joke, I genuinely enjoy the clash of the genre’s amateurish sensibilities with the heaviness of the music on the album. The one hallmark of the genre that Brand New is truly missing is a terrible vocalist; Jesse Lacey actually sounds pretty damn good throughout. A little sad that I had already heard the first random album, but I do think it’s a banger.

“Sowing Season” is my favorite type of album opener: it tricks you into thinking this is a quiet album, you turn your volume up to compensate, and then BAM your eardrums rupture. I sometimes get sick of “quiet/loud” stuff, but I think it’s done really well here. The guitar work is overbearing, and it sounds like if Slint turned their amps up to 11. It immediately hooked me the first time I listened. “Millstone” is probably the most conventional emo song on the album, but it does it so well. Just burning angst, “I’m a bad person and I can’t help it” lyrics, a catchy pop chorus, melodic yelps in the background, distorted guitars; if you had never heard an emo song before, I might use this as a prime example of the genre. “Jesus Christ” starts quiet, with an angular guitar riff in the background the whole time. This one gives me more Midwest vibes than any other song on the album. I really like the vocal build, even though the instruments never catch up, and the vocal counterpoint before the pregnant pause is really cool.

I already talked about “Degausser” a bit, but I really think it’s the masterpiece of the album. The vocal line is so creepy, it sounds like a horror movie. The tension is palpable; you can just tell things are going to explode. Then you get the heavy metal guitar that is positively hypnotic, and the screams come in almost anthemically. Great, great track. The beginning of “Limousine” kinda reminds me of the beginning of “Exit Music for a Film” by Radiohead. The vocal melody and the acoustic guitar chords just have such a dark sound about them. And then you get to the repeated “Well I love you so much/But do me a favor, baby, don’t reply/‘Cause I can dish it out, but I can’t take it” counting up to 7. The music builds around it, the vocals become more and more unhinged, really a highlight of the album for me. “You Won’t Know” is a cool concept because it really stretches the quiet and loud sections, only having three: quiet, loud, quiet. And matching that, the quietis very quiet, and the loud is very kickass, in-your-face loud.

“Welcome to Bangkok” is the first weak track on the album (NOTE: Aidan pre-empted me here, this is what I get for being slow with my review). It’s nice as a largely instrumental piece, but it doesn’t do much for me. I like “Not The Sun” quite a bit though, one of the screamiest tracks and the “be my babe” melody is catchier than it is annoying for me. I also love the jittery bass line. The loud instrumentals of “Luca” are some of the strongest on the album, and I like that almost Violent Femmes-y acoustic bits. “Untitled is unfortunately the weakest track on the album, and between it and “Welcome to Bangkok”, the second half is definitely weaker than the first. “Archers” has kind of a pop-punk lead guitar with a My Chemical Romance vocal line, more of a gothic vibe than anywhere else on the album. The chorus is pop perfection. “Handcuffs” is a great closer, and an interesting atmospheric piece in its own right. It’s consistently heavy throughout, but it really builds, and then it falls to end the album with a whimper.

The ideal specimen of third-wave emo. A lot of times, groups that are artsier than their peers feel like they have to put on an act, pose as if they’re above it all. I love that Brand New uses these completely base aesthetics no matter how much their music puts their fellows to shame. The loud parts are in your face and deeply satisfying, the quiet parts tender and thoughtful. Only two subpar tracks. Was really happy when we drew this one last night.

Favorite tracks: Sowing Season (Yeah), Millstone, Jesus Christ, Degausser, Limousine, Not the Sun, Archers, Handcuffs

Least favorite track: Untitled
