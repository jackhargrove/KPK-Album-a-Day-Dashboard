# Write-up

The first album to call itself “ambient” as well as one of the best works of the genre, Ambient 1 was first started and intended to be an art instillation during the recording session for David Bowie’s Low, on which Eno worked as a producer. Musically, the album is child-like, all featuring loops with minimalistic notes and murmuring wordless vocals. The album is simple as well, being true to its name as music intended to capture and enhance the feeling of being at an airport. For Eno, a "nervous flyer", this mean the feeling of possible death, the sense of forward movement through hustle and bustle and a world entirely created by humans. One of my favorite short stories, "Tlon, Uqbar, Orbius Tetrius" by Jorge Luis Borges, which tells of the creation of an encyclopedia of a fictional world, was cited by Eno as an inspiration for this album.

The ambient genre, which was at least named for this album, broadly encompasses music which is designed to be listened to along with other music. The video that I linked to earlier, which has this album being performed live in a working airport, is a great example of how the soundscapes of an airport enhance the simplicity of ambient music.

# Hargrove's review

I was fully ready to write a lukewarm review of this album. I had listened to it the requisite three times and wasn’t really hearing what I had hoped to hear. I was familiar with Eno’s work, and had heard of this album, and had listened to many ambient albums inspired by it, but I had never listened to this. I expected something more concrete, more coherent, more *musical*. Instead, though it all sounds nice, it never really coheres, never sticks together in the way that it feels like it should. “1/1” was really the only track that I “got”.

But then I sat down to write this review. I put on the album one last time and began to take notes, began to outline, began to look up stupid things I was curious about like where it was recorded and where in England Brian Eno was from. And I started to get it. It really was a beautiful backing to the other things I was doing. It functions as ambient music in the most literal sense of the word. After a while, I began focusing on the music again and I enjoyed it more. A little bit. Like, I wouldn’t call this my favorite or even close to it, but I finally felt like I had gained something from having listened to this album.

“1/1” is the best and meatiest track, and the piano work is really stellar. It has more going on than any other track, and it had the most for me to grab on to. I also thought the synths were great, especially in the bass. And it ambles along at just the right speed, sometimes speeding up, sometimes slowing down, but always just right. It was the longest track, but the only one where I never found myself wondering when it would end. “2/1” is still my least favorite track, although it grew on me from the first few listens. On my loud listen where I really cranked the volume in my airpods, the voices became super grating, but otherwise I didn’t think they were terrible. It felt like I was in the entrance to The Good Place, or at a spa, or joining a cult. But it would have been nice if there was something more than just the voices.

That leads to “1/2”, which combines the voices with a piano. I do rather like the piano in this track; it comes in fits and starts, little bursts that drive the track forward (if it’s going anywhere. It used the voices in a more interesting way than “2/1”, but the piano isn’t quite as good as “1/1”, so it’s a wash. And I really grew to like “2/2”. Something about those synths sound so regal. Like I’m in a medieval castle about to meet the king, except I’m inside a computer. It also reminded me of *A Clockwork Orange*. Probably my second favorite track.

All in all, another important album that I’ve meant to check off my list for years. I’m glad that I was able to walk away having felt something, because it was looking rough for most of the day. Would definitely consider listening to this while I’m working, or if I want to calm down.

Favorite tracks: 1/1, 2/2

Least favorite track: 2/1

# Aidan's review

Ambient music is really hard to get used to in a day. I’ve listened to this album a lot and it just sometimes clicks so perfectly with the audio environment, but other times it doesn’t. I found the Bang on a Can performance when doing to summary for this album and it significantly raised my estimation of it, sounded like a track out of Lift Your Skinny Fists. I also appreciate the short track listing, I think a lot of ambient really relies on length to establish itself, while Eno does it with simplicity. As with any Eno project, the individual notes just sound amazing, and with an album so focused on individual notes, it works fantastically
