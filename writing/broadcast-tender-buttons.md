# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

Though I developed my taste on 00s and 10s indie when I was a teenager, I’ve been very down on the genre as of late. The bands and aesthetics have just dragged on for too long and often just feel tired to me. So when this album was chosen and I saw that it was aughts indie, my expectations for enjoyment were not particularly high. However, I *really* enjoyed *Tender Buttons*. Not only that, but I’ve found it much easier to describe exactly what I like about it than most other albums. My first listen was a bit off-putting, but quickly on the second and third I began having the theses and epiphanies that usually only come to me on my last one or two listens before writing the review. This album is basically noise pop but using an entirely different kind of noise than anything else does. That staticky, wriggling, electronic hiss makes for a stellar timbre on this record. Its interplay with Television/Strokes/Interpol guitar is even better. Keenan’s vocals are dreamy but with an eerie undercurrent, I absolutely love them. The songwriting is consistently great, with multiple choruses and instrumental loops stuck in my head as I’m writing this. If that’s not enough, multiple tracks even have some great lyrics that have kept me thinking all day. And though they’re never a focal point, the drums and bass provide the perfect rhythm section needed. Broadcast remind me a lot of Stereolab, a group that I like in abstract but whose music often leaves me cold. But Broadcast trades Stereolab’s erudite art school vibe for something much warmer and more personal. In that sense, I think Keenan’s vocals just completely make this album. I feel drawn into her world, but as a visitor and not a guest; I feel like we’re simultaneously face to face and a million miles apart. It’s alluring, enticing, uncanny, and a little frightening. I also feel like St. Vincent gets so much of her sound from these guys, but I can’t find anyone else online who agrees with me. *Tender Buttons* is both very good and very accessible, I highly recommend it to anyone.

Broadcast sets the bar high with “I Found the F”. Keenan’s delivery permanently etched “Water, water, water, waaaaaaaater” into my brain. The descending guitar line sounds great and has such a bizarre distortion effect. Dare I say the guitar line sounds Baroque? Great song. “Black Cat” has even more catchy tidbits, namely “Curiouser and curiouser” and “Awkwardness happening to someone you love”. The latter line also makes me think. What does it mean for awkwardness to happen to someone? The instrumental is buoyed by a super bouncy electronic noise line that reminds me a ton of Grimes’ “Oblivion” (well, vice versa). It just keeps going and going and going like a river. “Tender Buttons” is unfortunately my least favorite track. Just a bit too unstructured, unfocused, slow, and not all that memorable. I love the instrumental on “America’s Boy” but at the same time it has one of my least favorite vocal melodies, preventing it from being one of my favorite tracks. “Tears in the Typing Pool” is just gorgeous though. What a tender little ballad with a strummed acoustic guitar, beautiful vocal melody, and electronic noise somehow fashioned to sound like a pan flute. So, so, so pretty. I love the way the lyrics on “Corporeal” use “autonomy” and “anatomy”. The guitar sounds kind of folksy while an electric eel of noise wriggles all over everything. And the vocals are stunning. “Bit 35” is an interlude, but a nice one. The drum machine is done well.

“Arc of a Journey” is the harshest track sonically. This noise just doesn’t sound like anything I’ve heard before in music. The songwriting and instrumentation are so dreamy here. I just melt into it. I positively ADORE the lyrics, particularly “Constellation of Orion/A picture with a past, a future so vast/A mnemonic game on the arc of a journey” and “I report there once were cities/Open academies, avenues, and trees/Human law, automatic oracles/Calendar, the years, verbal hemispheres”. “Michael a Grammar” is just a banger. Super fun and super catchy song. It reminds me of video game music a bit. The little guitar stabs on the chorus also remind me of the Strokes (and, ultimately, Television). Keenan delivers “My feet are dancing so much and I hate that” perfectly. “Subject to the Ladder” rounds out a stellar three track run. The guitar feels a little thin and spindly thanks to the dissonance, reminding me of “Lullaby” by the Cure. The piercing noise forms a beautiful harmonic structure. It’s hypnotic. I’m also a sucker for the Greek myth-inspired lyrics. “Minus 3” is another decent interlude like “Bit 35”. “Goodbye Girls” is a bit weaker than my other favorite tracks, but I’m still including it because I like the melody and because it’s so well-written. There’s some tonal dissonance between the oddly uplifting instrumental and the chorus of “Vacantly they listen/Giving no emotion/Leaving no impression/Love you all night”. It’s off-putting in the best way. “You and Me in Time” builds an impressive atmosphere given its simplicity, but it’s a little unsatisfying, leaving something to be desired in structure and writing. “I Found the End” isn’t a remarkable track on its own, but it’s a suitably dark and meditative closer for the album.

I suspect I’ll be revisiting this a lot going forward. It just feels so refreshing to hear a take on aughts indie I’ve never heard before. RIP Trish Keenan.

Favorite tracks: I Found the F, Black Cut, Tears in the Typing Pool, Corporeal, Arc of a Journey, Michael a Grammar, Goodbye Girls

Least favorite track: Tender Buttons
