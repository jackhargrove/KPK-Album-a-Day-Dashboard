# Hargrove's review

Built to Spill is a conservatively a top 10 band of all time for both of my parents. Realistically, it’s probably top 5. My family once took a weekend trip to Boise just to see Built to Spill play with Modest Mouse. I saw Built to Spill live for a second time last year, when Aidan came to DC to see them play with, again, Modest Mouse. That puts Built to Spill in a very small group of non-Sacramento local musical artists that I’ve seen more than once in concert (the others are Weird Al Yankovic, Modest Mouse, Kendrick Lamar, and Baby Keem). Yet, somehow, I had never actually listened to a Built to Spill album. It really comes down to me being a contrarian, I guess, but it would just have made my parents too happy if I got into Built to Spill. I’ve always instead preferred Modest Mouse, their PNW slacker rock contemporaries. Now that I’ve sat down and listened to *Perfect From Now On* nine times or so, I’ve come to the conclusion that I obviously always was going to: it’s pretty damn good. I mean, there’s no way I wasn’t going to enjoy this album. It’s a style that I’ve had mainlined into my veins since I was a baby. Martsch is a fucking wizard on the guitar, playing some of the most expressive solos I’ve heard this side of Hendrix, as well as some fiery riffs. Anyway, I liked this record, and I think it’s obvious what about it appealed to me. Instead, I’d like to focus on why it still doesn’t resonate with me like I feel it should. *Perfect From Now* is easily a high B/low A Tier album for me, which is a great result for any record, but I have multiple comparable records sitting in my SS Tier. For one, I’m just not sold on Martsch’s voice. I understand the Neil Young comparisons, but in my opinion, he takes the whine of Young’s voice without the power. It’s not a huge deal, easily the least important of my reservations, but it dampens the amount of power that can be put into a chorus or vocal explosion. And that ties directly into my second point: this album lacks the sharply defined, cathartic peaks of a record like *The Lonesome Crowded West*. In fairness, *Perfect from Now On* deserves significant praise for just how consistent it is. Every track is good, an impressive feat given the extended run times. I’ve added five of them to my liked songs playlist. But though most every track builds, and there are some gorgeous moments, there’s just no “Can’t do it not even if sober”. There’s no breakdown from “Doin’ the Cockroach”. Those are both examples from the same album which I’ve chosen because of the direct comparability, but I don’t mean to only compare to Modest Mouse here. Built to Spill’s climaxes just end up a little too hazy, blurry, and indistinct for me. And that’s okay. A lot of people have a preference for that. I just prefer something sharper, more concentrated, something I can scream along too. And that leads into my third point, which is that the haziness does get a little overbearing for me at times. Slacker rock has a loose, amorphous sound. Psychedelic rock has a loose, amorphous sound. *Perfect from Now On* distills that quality from both genres. I can appreciate it, and I’m being honest when I say I do. I like this record! It just doesn’t tickle me in the way that peer albums do. Anyway, I hope I’ve sufficiently shielded myself from Aidan’s wrath. Read the paragraph below for mostly praise!

I have long been terrified by the very concept of infinity, so the lyrics to “Randy Described Eternity” fucked with me in that regard. I’m always reminded of a torture device in the *Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy* series that forces its victim to genuinely understand the size of the universe, which invariably drives him insane. I like both the quiet and loud sections, but I prefer the quiet sections. They remind me of Red House Painters. “I Would Hurt a Fly” is a track that didn’t much catch my attention in earlier listens, but that I became enamored with on my final focused listen before this review. I think that’s because the song itself isn’t all that impressive, but the effects around it are killer. I love the squirming wah of the guitar, the quasi-breakbeat in the drums, and the foreboding strings. “Stop the Show” eventually becomes a fiery banger, but unfortunately you have to wade through three minutes of lethargic intro to get there. That’s enough to prevent it from being a favorite track, but once you do get to the meat of it, you’re treated to an emo banger a la Unwound. “Made-Up Dreams” is the fourth song in a row to start slow, a pattern that wears a little thin. I find the production on this track tinny, the synth sounds completely out of place, and Martsch sounds particularly whiny, so it’s my least favorite track. Much like many of Modest Mouse’s songs, “Velvet Waltz” proves that 6/8 just works well for this kind of music. Martsch’s voice is whiny here, sure, but man does he stretch it to an emotional maximum on this track. The outro of this track is simply orgasmic, building to pure ecstasy with cymbals on the downbeat in a similar manner to Swans’ “Helpless Child”. “Out of Site” is my favorite track. Without fail, it sucked in my attention on every listen if I was a little zoned out. Who knew Martsch could play the guitar so funky? The transitions are a tad bit clunky, but it’s one the shortest track so that keeps things tight. I’m not a huge fan of “Kicked It in the Sun”. It never picks up much steam at all. Plus, why does he say “So come on over, yeeeeeeeeaaaaaaaah” like that? The lyrics in the first part of “Untrustable/Part 2 (About Someone Else)” seriously resonated with me. “You can't trust anyone/‘Cause you're untrustable/How can you trust someone you know can't trust you?” and “You don't like anything/‘Cause you're unlikable/All because you're not interested in you anymore/It's so unthinkable/‘Cause you don't think of it/How many ideas can fit in your reality?“ both sincerely fucked me up. The first part also has a fantastic chorus, then the track barrels into a gorgeous second section to close out the album.

I agonized about whether I should put this in the Alt Classics™️ wall at the top of B Tier. Ultimately, I’m sticking it just above that in the bottom of A Tier for now. I’d like to have some time to sit with this record, and I can always move it around later, but that’s what I’m feeling right now.

Favorite tracks: Randy Described Eternity, I Would Hurt a Fly, Velvet Waltz, Out of Site, Untrustable/Part 2 (About Someone Else)

Least favorite track: Made-Up Dreams
