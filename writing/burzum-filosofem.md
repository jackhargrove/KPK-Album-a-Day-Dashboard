# Write-up

Burzum is the solo project of Norwegian black metal artist Varg Vikernes that began in 1991. Vikernes was a part of the “Black Metal Inner Circle” in Norway in the late 1980s and early 1990s; ***I urge you to read the third paragraph of this write-up because the stories are fucking insane.*** Vikernes began listening to heavy metal when he was 12 after discovering Iron Maiden and he was also a big fan of classical music, in particular Tchaikovsky. He began to play guitar at age 14 and joined Norwegian death metal band Old Funeral at age 17 in 1990. However, in 1991 Vikernes became part of the burgeoning black metal scene in Oslo, so he ditched his lame death metal band and started his own black metal project, Burzum. “Burzum” means “darkness” in Black Speech, a language from *Lord of the Rings* In 1992, Vikernes joined the band Mayhem as bassist using the name Count Grishnackh; Mayhem was the center of the black metal scene in Norway, and guitarist Euronymous was the leader of it all. Vikernes was bassist for Mayhem’s 1994 album *De mysteriis Dom Sathanas*, which was the template for all black metal since. As Burzum, Vikernes released one album each in 1992, 1993, and 1994. Vikernes pioneered an ambient form of black metal that differed from much of the rest of the scene. Here is where we get to the not-so-fun part: Vikernes (as well as the rest of the Black Metal Inner Circle) is a really, really bad person. He first gained notoriety as an arsonist in the early 90s, successfully burning down numerous historic churches in Norway. Then, in 1993, he murdered his Mayhem bandmate Euronymous for reasons that are still disputed. He was sentenced to 21 years in prison (the maximum sentence in Norway), but he was released in 2009 after 15 years. While in prison, Vikernes developed his political philosophy: he is a proud white supremacist and Nazi (he’s been noted as one of Kanye’s favorite artists for a while now lol). *Filosofem*, the final album Burzum recorded before Vikernes’ murder of Euronymous, was released in 1996 while Vikernes was in prison. Since his release, Vikernes has moved to France and released 7 more albums as Burzum.

*Filosofem* is perhaps the most acclaimed, most experimental, and most influential album in black metal. “Filosofem” is Norwegian for “philosopheme”, a type of statement in philosophy. The first track recorded for the album was the opener “Dunkelheit”, which was in fact the first song ever recorded for the Burzum project in 1992. The production was terrible though, so it went unused. However, when Vikernes was recording *Filosofem* in 1993, he decided he wanted to make an extremely lo-fi record, so he used the track. Instead of using an amplifier, Vikernes plugged his guitar directly into an old stereo. Instead of using a real microphone for his vocals, Vikernes used a helicopter headset. Unlike other black metal music of the time, most of the tracks on *Filosofem* take significant influence from ambient music, particularly in terms of repetition and minimalism. The fifth track is genuinely ambient, with a single melody used for 25 minutes. Not long after recording the album, Vikernes was arrested for murdering Euronymous. The album was produced by Pytten while Vikernes was in prison, and released 3 years after recording in 1996. Many musicians since have cited Burzum as an influence, including Thurston Moore of Sonic Youth, Phil Elverum of The Microphones and Mount Eerie, and, of course, Kanye West.

Okay, now for the history of black metal. Black metal came out of the thrash metal scene of the early 80s, with British band Venom being particularly influential; their 1982 album *Black Metal* gave the genre its name. This inspired the “first wave” of black metal, which was known for extremely high tempos, tremolo guitar riffs, high-pitched shrieking vocals, lo-fi production, and (importantly) Satanic lyrical themes. In 1984, Mayhem was founded in Oslo, Norway by guitarist Euronymous; he would become the central figure of Norway’s black metal scene. In 1988, Swedish artist Dead joined Mayhem as vocalist, and introduced “corpse paint”, black and white makeup intended to make the wearer look dead, to black metal. Mayhem became known for their disgusting live performances where severed pig’s heads would be thrown at the audience and Dead would mutilate himself on stage. Dead was severely mentally ill, and he killed himself with a shotgun in the home he shared with Euronymous at age 22 in 1991. When Euronymous discovered Dead’s dead body, he took a photo and used it as the cover of Mayhem’s next album. He also took bits of Dead’s skull and turned them into necklaces he would give to musicians he liked. Euronymous founded the record store Helvete (Norwegian for “hell”) in 1991, and it became the local gathering place for a group known as the “Black Metal Inner Circle” (BMIC), which included Vikernes, Hellhammer, Faust, Necrobutcher, and others. Euronymous rejected Christianity and became an unironic theistic Satanist, convincing the others of the BMIC to do the same (Euronymous’ general ideology was “bad things are good”). The BMIC began a campaign of church burnings in the early 90s that terrorized Norway; they successfully burned down 17 churches from 1992-1995. In 1992, BMIC member Faust murdered a man on the street and went to prison. All of this gained them significant bad press and police attention, causing Euronymous to close Helvete in early 1993. For reasons that are still not entirely clear, Euronymous and Vikernes began to have an incredibly strained relationship in 1993. In August of 1993, Vikernes went to Euronymous’ apartment and stabbed him 23 times, killing him. Vikernes claims it was in self-defense and that Euronymous planned to kidnap and torture him to death, but this is unproven. Vikernes was sentenced to 21 years in prison in 1994. Between Dead’s suicide, the church burnings, and Euronymous’ murder, black metal was getting a lot of attention in Norway. This led to an explosion in the number of black metal bands, and led to the “second wave” of black metal in the mid-to-late 1990s. Black metal as a genre is still popular in metal scenes today, particularly as a fusion genre; for example, the band Deafheaven combines black metal with shoegaze to form “blackgaze”.

# Hargrove's review

This album was unfortunately quite good. Varg is an actual piece of shit, but I definitely see why he was super influential in spite of that. I’m genuinely impressed with how he keeps the aggression and distortion and speed I expect from metal but he makes it truly ambient through repetition. I historically really like repetition (*Remain in Light*, *Dopesmoker*, *Songs for the Deaf* are all A tier or above for me), it just entrances me, and the same happens here. This album is also just so completely cold. *Pornography* by The Cure created a similarly dark and crushing atmosphere, but *Filosofem* just completely strips away all the passion of that record and all we’re left with is icy despondency. This record is as riff-forward as any other metal record, but the fuzziness of the lo-fi recording creates a different kind of dominance; instead of grabbing attention through volume and timbre, it invades and oppresses your eardrums leaving no room for escape. Really, really cool effect. When the drums are there, they’re driving but somehow don’t command your attention and instead fade into a dull, mechanical vessel for the song to move forward. My biggest gripe is the vocals, which kind of just go over the edge of lo-fi into unpleasant, and really just add very little to any song outside of “Gebrechlichkeit I” for me. We’re two for two on the metal albums so far, I’m excited to hear more.

“Dunkelheit” is a great entry into this pitiful, sorrowful world, with the synth work being a highlight of the album for me. The synths are just so cold, so lifeless, it’s a really brilliant use of the soullessness that can be found in computers to enhance the mood. It’s also nice, if simple, melodically. “Jesus’ Tod” is the fastest and most traditional metal song of the bunch, and the guitar riff is the most complex. I’m a big fan of the riff. The drums are pretty insane too, moving along at a blistering pace but not inserting any energy or passion into the vibe. “Erblocket Die Tochter Des Firmaments” has my favorite bass work on the album, and the guitar is just nonstop brutally crushingly oppressive. The vocals have grown on me somewhat on that track, but not entirely. “Gebrechlichkeit I” slows things down, strips away the drums, and lets us know that, somehow, this album is becoming even more of a downer. The vocals are haunting, rasping out the last thoughts of a man freezing to death, and this is the only song where the vocal style truly clicks for me. The guitar is really atmospheric, and the synth is always there, reminding you of its presence but never doing anything to save you. This song makes me feel genuinely kind of sick. “Gebrechlichkeit II” is like a remix, but it builds in a way that isn’t seen elsewhere in the album. Just truly grows into this ominous, seething whirlpool of hate. The 25 minute ambient piece “Rundgang Um Die Transzendentale Saule Der Singularitat” really tests the listener’s patience, but I do like the core melody. It’s more dynamic than you think at first listen, with everything going on around the melody being in flux, but it requires some endurance. think this could sound really cool in the right setting, but as it is it’s a bit of a hill to climb on every listen.

Similar to The Cure, this album is monolithic and terrifying. It applies the tenets of metal perfectly to instill a complete and total emptiness. A genuine black hole, a void of an album. I think you can only understand it if you’ve experienced a Norwegian winter. Will definitely keep listening and checking out similar stuff.

Favorite tracks: Dunkelheit, Jesus Tod, Erblocket Die Tochter Des Firmaments, Gebrechlichkeit I, Gebrechlichkeit II

Least favorite track: Rundgang Um Die Transzendentale Saule Der Singularitat
