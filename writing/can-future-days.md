# Write-up

Future Days is our second and final album by CAN. We left off with them after they released Tago Mago, which was critically well liked but didn't exactly fly off the shelves. The band moved out of the castle where they had recorded, and moved into an former movie theater, noise proofing their makeshift studio with army surplus mattresses and installing custom synths.  Their first single from the album, "Spoon", was a smash hit, rising the sixth on the German charts and making their label eager to capitalize on their popularity. They recorded their next album, Ege Bamyasi (Turkish for "Aegean Okra", a can of which graces the cover) with a strict six month deadline and ended up recording the last track (chronologically) in a single rushed 10 minute improvisational setting. The album got the band enough publicity to play on BBC and in France. Critically, it was and still is beloved. Steven Malkmus of Pavement and Thurston Moore of Sonic Youth both listened to the album constantly. Geoff Barrow of Portishead called it one of his favorite albums and the Austin indie rock band Spoon took their name from the lead single. "Vitamin C", the second from the album, has become Spoon's most popular song in modern times.

After the success of Ege Bamyasi, CAN went on vacation. Bassist Hogler Czukay went to Southeast Asia, lead singer Damo Suzuki went back home to Japan, guitarist Michael Karoli went to Portugal and keyboardist Irmin Schmidt went to South France. There is no record of where their drummer, Jaki Liebezit went. Still in the former movie theater, they all reunited in a calm and peaceful state of mind and set out to make music that reflected this mood. The first track they recorded, "Bel Air", was dedicated to Hedy Lamarr (for no other reason than Schmidt admired her) and the studio has a gas leak during the one and only session they needed to record it. The record saw them go even further into the direction of ambient music, a theme they had explored on the second half of Tago Mago. However, the sound they sought out was much softer than the jarring back half of Tago Mago, instead creating lush soundscapes that evoked oceans and outer space. The album, like the previous two, was well received, and Suzuki retrospectively called it his favorite that he made with CAN. In fact, he was so happy with the album that he didn't think he could do anything better, so, at the behest of his girlfriend, he left the band and became a Jehovah's Witness. The band put out one more good album (per Schmidt), Soon Over Babaluma, with Karoli taking over most vocal duties in Suzuki's absence, before abandoning their experimental roots to make more conventional music after signing with Harvest Records, including disco and reggae influenced songs. Czukay left the band in 1977 after another bassist joined, limiting his contributions to the purely experimental side, and the band fully split in 1979.

The band reunited three times briefly, putting out their final album in 1986, making a song for a Wim Wenders film in 1991 and contributing to a compilation in 1999. Suzuki was not part of any of these reunions, although the original lead singer, Malcolm Mooney, joined the 1986 album. Karoli died in 2001, Leibezit and Czukay in 2017 and Suzuki died in 2024.

# Hargrove's review

Well, this one is certainly a lot tighter than *Tago Mago* was. On the flip side, I don’t think any of the tracks are as good as “Halleluwah” or even some of the tracks on side 1. Still, this is a very solid record. The drums may not feel as rigid and regimented as they do in the more motorik songs of previous albums, but the dull, throbbing kick drum hypnotizes all the same. I admire Damo’s restraint on this album, delivering some really beautiful vocal melodies without losing his distinctive vibe. The ambient synth pads are quite nice as well, giving many of the tracks depth and a sense of space. My biggest issue is that none of the songs are particularly memorable, nor do they much distinguish themselves from the other tracks. The exception is “Moonshake”, which has a pop structure that would make it feel at home on *Ege Bamyası*. This is probably my least favorite of the three CAN albums I’ve listened to, never reaching the jaw-dropping highs of *Tago Mago* or the consistent bangers of *Ege Bamyası*. Still, it has to be one of the most consistent three album runs I’ve ever heard from any artist. All of these albums are really on just about the same level. When I’m listening to *Future Days*, I enjoy every second, even if I don’t come away from it with as much as I do their other records. It’s a damn solid record.

“Future Days” keeps the same drum rhythm for nearly all of its 10 minutes, and it’s certainly an interesting one. It’s kind of funky, almost Latin in its particular syncopation. Damo delivers the most angelic vocal melody I’ve ever heard from him. The studio effects near the end are interesting, but I’m not crazy impressed by them. Overall, this is a decent track. “Spray” has more exotic percussion and a pounding kick drum that make the first part of it quite fun. However, it drifts off into aimlessness after a few minutes, making it my least favorite track. “Moonshake” is a krautpop banger. I love the way Damo says “whoa, whoa, whoa, whoa” as well as the guitar that comes right after. The drums are classic krautrock. I love the effect created by the drumming on “Bel Air”, that rapid fire alternating between the kick drum and the hi-hat. The chord changes driven by the bass are sublime as well. The song gets really good when it picks up around 4:30. The pounding bass drum is the best part of the track, but I love the spacey guitar breakdown sections as well. The song just keeps going and going and going and going until it falls apart with about three minutes left, picking up again right before the ending.

As I said, this is a rock solid record. Still, it’s notable to me that this is one of the shorter reviews I’ve written in a while. I just don’t have all that much to say about it!

Favorite tracks: Moonshake, Bel Air

Least favorite track: Spray

# Aidan's review

My long time favorite CAN album, here we see a perfect union between krautrock's trance inducing instrumentals and Suzuki's glossolalic vocals. "Future Days" is a neat opener, but the real highlight of the album is in "Moonshake", with Spray building up and "Bel Air" building down from the 3 minutes of ecstasy. When listening to this, I can understand how speaking in tongues was seen as holy across cultures. There's a decent amount of dawdling on this album, but it's all in service of achieving that zen state from which the intensity is raised. The good parts of Tago Mago are nearly as good as this, but this is an album without the same messing around on the back half. Great album, "Moonshake" rivals "Hallogallo" for my favorite krautrock song.
