# Write-up

After listening to Radiohead's most krautrock inspired album, we now arrive at CAN, the third krautrock band we'll cover (we have 1 more CAN album and an album by Faust remaining from the krautrock scene). CAN was formed as a quartet, with Holger Czukay on bass, Irmin Schmidt on keyboard, Jaki Liebezeit on drums and Micheal Karoli on guitar. Czukay and Schmidt had studied music under modernist classical composer Karlheinz Stockhausen and used the band as a way to incorporate his ideas into rock music. The early music of the band was mostly soundtracks for German films. Their first vocalist was Malcolm Mooney, an American who had become friends with Schmidt after hiking with his wife. The band was originally called Inner Space, but Mooney convinced the Germans to change it to CAN, due to it having some positive meaning in languages ranging from English to Turkish to Japanese. Mooney would leave the band after the first album after their chaotic music began to contribute to a decline in his mental health. One disputed rumor is during one performance, he shouted "upstairs, downstairs" for 3 hours straight, going on long after the rest of the band stopped playing.

Bereft of a vocalist, the band picked up the Japanese Damo Suzuki off the streets (literally, they found him busking and had him sing the same night). Suzuki sung in many languages or no language at all, depending on who you ask, calling it "The Language of the Stone Age". In his first concert, he managed to drive out nearly all of the audience with his singing, and the band was sold on him. It was with Suzuki that CAN would release the three albums that they are most well known for, Tago Mago, Ege Bamyasi and Future Days. For Tago Mago, the band cashed in a promise for a year's free stay in a castle in Western Germany and recorded for 3 months straight. The band was extremely hard working, averaging 13 hours and going up to 16 hours of playing daily. The entire album was recorded in incredibly low tech, using two two track tape recorders, with no mixing board and only three microphones. Most of the music was played in the entrance hall of the castle. The only control the band had over their volume on the track was how loud they played and how far they were from the microphones. The band improvised heavily, often not listening to one another in a belief if they all had the same spiritual intention, the music would fallow. Czukay also incorporated secret recordings of the band jamming, the dog of their host at the castle and a child who stumbled in, all of which were incorporated into the album. The album was named after the Illa de Tagomago, an island near Ibiza where Liebezeit thought had magic connections and had tried to commit suicide on.

Tago Mago was acclaimed on release, and while the experimental of the album, especially the second half, didn't make it fly off the shelves, bands from Talk Talk to T.Rex to Radiohead have cited it as an influence. The band Spoon (a favorite of me and Beto O'Rourke) took their name from a single off of Ege Bamyasi. Along with New!, CAN is one of the leading bands of the Krautrock movement, a loose grouping of experimental music coming from Western Germany around 1970 that were characterized by their usage of trance-inducing rhythms and musique-concrete influences. The name was originally controversial (I really wish Gotterdamer rock, named after the end of the world in Wagner's ring cycle, caught on) and rejected by the bands, especially because it was bestowed upon them by British music journalists, but it's become the accepted term today. We will explore the remaining story of CAN when we get to Future Days.

# Hargrove's review

This is going to be a really hard album to rank because of its awesome first disc and…interesting second disc, to say the least. I’ve been a big fan of Can’s “Vitamin C” for a while, and I’ve listened to the full album that’s on, *Ege Bamyası*. Instead of that album, however, this list has *Tago Mago* (the one before it) and *Future Days* (the one after it). I liked *Ege Bamyası*, but it left me with a lot of questions about krautrock. It really didn’t sound the way the genre had been described to me. When we listened to *NEU!*, it helped clear things up for me, as that album represents all the stereotypes: driving motorik drum beat, psychedelic instrumental textures, avant-garde tape manipulation. After listening to *Tago Mago*, it’s helped me both connect Can to that stereotypical krautrock sound, as well as open my mind to the ways in which krautrock was musically progressive. My impression of motorik was that it had to be a driving, unsyncopated beat with hi-hats on the eighth notes and snares on 2 and 4. On *Tago Mago* though, and especially on “Halleluwah”, Liebezeit gets that same entrancing effect despite the crazy bass drum syncopation work he does. It’s nuts. This album has some really impressive drumming. Damo Suzuki’s vocals are a little polarizing; I think they’re absolutely stellar on disc 1, but I get a little tired of the screaming gibberish on side 2. I was surprised by how bluesy Karoli’s guitar work was, especially given that krautrock is one of the (numerous) genres that people claim finally stripped the blues out of rock and made it European. Overall, the first half of this album is just a fun, hypnotic groove. The songwriting is a little lackluster, but it makes up for it with just 35 minutes of enrapturing vibes. The second half though really holds it back when considered as a whole album. Many other albums on this list, like *NEU!* or the Henry Cow album, do the same thing where half the album is real music and the other half is unlistenable experimentation. Those albums were at least single albums though. It’s tough to rate this when there’s over half an hour of bullshit, though “Bring Me Coffee or Tea” is pretty nice. Still, this is a super cool album. I see myself listening to “Halleluwah” a lot.

“Paperhouse” kicks things off with a very nice melody from Damo. It’s got a super sick breakdown in the middle of the track and some inventive use of the bass drum from Liebezeit. “Mushroom” continues the same general vibes, but this time Damo’s vocals are even catchier. “When I saw skies were red/I was born, and I was dead” is completely stuck in my head. “Oh Yeah” also continues like a single composition. I’m even more awestruck by Liebezeit’s drums here. I just don’t understand how he can be as propulsive as “Hallogallo” with the bass drum all over the place like this. “Halleluwah” is a masterpiece. Those drums rain straight thunder. It’s genuinely hypnotic. This track feels like a real proof-of-concept for krautrock. You can really feel the funk in the middle of the song too. The swirling build from 15:00ish to 16:30ish is my favorite part of the track. “Aumgn” definitely sounds a lot like a Karlheinz Stockhausen composition, which makes it much more bearable when you’re familiar with that. The beginning section is the best part but this track loses my interest quickly and then goes on for almost 20 minutes. “Peking O”, despite being much shorter, is also much harder to listen to. You can only put up with Damo speaking in tongues over a jackhammer for so long. The primitive drum machine is cool though. “Bring Me Coffee or Tea” provides a nice reprieve after almost half an hour of some serious bullshit. The guitar work sounds so pretty. There are nice cymbals in the second half, some good melodies, it’s a good track.

How does one weigh grooviness and cool factor against the realities of an album listening experience? Tough to say. I think I like this less than Henry Cow, but not by a ton. I’ll put them right next to each other. I reserve the right to change this significantly though if I find myself coming back to “Halleluwah” a ton. I could see this eventually moving up to A Tier.

Favorite tracks: Paperhouse, Mushroom, Oh Yeah, Halleluwah, Bring Me Coffee or Tea

Least favorite track: Peking O

# Aidan's review

Our second Krautrock record with half amazing songs and half experiments that would have been best left out. At least CAN have the good sense to leave the bad stuff until the second half so you don't have to skip around to get at the meat of the album. And the first half is absolutely worth it, the drumming and Damo Suzuki's manic vocals work together to make something absolutely hypnotic. It's nearly 40 minutes of some of the most enjoyable drumming I've heard from an album. And then there's the second half. At least Bring Me Coffee Or Tea is somewhat listenable, but Aumgn and Peking O are just drek and it's insane that a band that delivered something as tight as the first half would put this on an album. I really like CAN, and I typically just pretend like the second half of this album doesn't exist, but it's hard to give it full marks, especially when I know that their next two albums are nearly or as good as the first half of Tago Mago and don't have the regrettable forays into the avant garde.
