# Hargrove's review

This is going to be a really hard album to rank because of its awesome first disc and…interesting second disc, to say the least. I’ve been a big fan of Can’s “Vitamin C” for a while, and I’ve listened to the full album that’s on, *Ege Bamyası*. Instead of that album, however, this list has *Tago Mago* (the one before it) and *Future Days* (the one after it). I liked *Ege Bamyası*, but it left me with a lot of questions about krautrock. It really didn’t sound the way the genre had been described to me. When we listened to *NEU!*, it helped clear things up for me, as that album represents all the stereotypes: driving motorik drum beat, psychedelic instrumental textures, avant-garde tape manipulation. After listening to *Tago Mago*, it’s helped me both connect Can to that stereotypical krautrock sound, as well as open my mind to the ways in which krautrock was musically progressive. My impression of motorik was that it had to be a driving, unsyncopated beat with hi-hats on the eighth notes and snares on 2 and 4. On *Tago Mago* though, and especially on “Halleluwah”, Liebezeit gets that same entrancing effect despite the crazy bass drum syncopation work he does. It’s nuts. This album has some really impressive drumming. Damo Suzuki’s vocals are a little polarizing; I think they’re absolutely stellar on disc 1, but I get a little tired of the screaming gibberish on side 2. I was surprised by how bluesy Karoli’s guitar work was, especially given that krautrock is one of the (numerous) genres that people claim finally stripped the blues out of rock and made it European. Overall, the first half of this album is just a fun, hypnotic groove. The songwriting is a little lackluster, but it makes up for it with just 35 minutes of enrapturing vibes. The second half though really holds it back when considered as a whole album. Many other albums on this list, like *NEU!* or the Henry Cow album, do the same thing where half the album is real music and the other half is unlistenable experimentation. Those albums were at least single albums though. It’s tough to rate this when there’s over half an hour of bullshit, though “Bring Me Coffee or Tea” is pretty nice. Still, this is a super cool album. I see myself listening to “Halleluwah” a lot.

“Paperhouse” kicks things off with a very nice melody from Damo. It’s got a super sick breakdown in the middle of the track and some inventive use of the bass drum from Liebezeit. “Mushroom” continues the same general vibes, but this time Damo’s vocals are even catchier. “When I saw skies were red/I was born, and I was dead” is completely stuck in my head. “Oh Yeah” also continues like a single composition. I’m even more awestruck by Liebezeit’s drums here. I just don’t understand how he can be as propulsive as “Hallogallo” with the bass drum all over the place like this. “Halleluwah” is a masterpiece. Those drums rain straight thunder. It’s genuinely hypnotic. This track feels like a real proof-of-concept for krautrock. You can really feel the funk in the middle of the song too. The swirling build from 15:00ish to 16:30ish is my favorite part of the track. “Aumgn” definitely sounds a lot like a Karlheinz Stockhausen composition, which makes it much more bearable when you’re familiar with that. The beginning section is the best part but this track loses my interest quickly and then goes on for almost 20 minutes. “Peking O”, despite being much shorter, is also much harder to listen to. You can only put up with Damo speaking in tongues over a jackhammer for so long. The primitive drum machine is cool though. “Bring Me Coffee or Tea” provides a nice reprieve after almost half an hour of some serious bullshit. The guitar work sounds so pretty. There are nice cymbals in the second half, some good melodies, it’s a good track.

How does one weigh grooviness and cool factor against the realities of an album listening experience? Tough to say. I think I like this less than Henry Cow, but not by a ton. I’ll put them right next to each other. I reserve the right to change this significantly though if I find myself coming back to “Halleluwah” a ton. I could see this eventually moving up to A Tier.

Favorite tracks: Paperhouse, Mushroom, Oh Yeah, Halleluwah, Bring Me Coffee or Tea

Least favorite track: Peking O
