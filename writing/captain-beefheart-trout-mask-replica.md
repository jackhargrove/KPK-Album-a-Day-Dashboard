# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Captain Beefheart (real name Don Van Vliet) had put out a couple of blues albums in the late 1960s with The Magic Band. They were weird, but overall not that crazy, more in the bounds of normal music. In 1969, Frank Zappa (a more popular musician who has two albums way later on the list) reached out to Captain Beefheart and offered to pay for an album with 0 creative restrictions. Captain Beefheart accepted, and he, Frank Zappa, and the members of the Magic Band (minus the older ones, who he kicked out) all moved in to a shitty house in LA together.

It was at this point that Captain Beefheart bought a piano. He did not know how to play the piano. He had never been formally trained in music theory. He couldn’t read or write music. Nevertheless, he sat down at the piano and played for days at a time, noodling around randomly until he found short riffs he liked. He composed thousands of riffs in this manner, and a member of the Magic Band wrote them in musical notation, assigned them to different instruments, and compiled them into songs.

For the next three months, the Magic Band rehearsed these songs nonstop. Everyone in the house was living in poverty, often eating only beans, and they all lived in cult-like devotion to Captain Beefheart. Finally, they recorded all of the instrumentals for the album in one 6 hour session. Captain Beefheart recorded his vocals separately, in an environment where he could only faintly hear the music he was singing over, so they are out of sync.

While the album was unsuccessful at the time, it has gone on to be the favorite album of many musicians and other artists. It’s similar to a phrase often used to describe the Velvet Underground: “They didn’t sell many records, but everyone who bought one went out and started a band.”

# Hargrove's review

While I feel like I have a really good understanding of why a certain type of person really loves this record, I am not that type of person. I have a rule with myself that I won’t speak confidently on an album without 3 listens, and this one really tested my resolve. I made it though: 4 fucking hours of this shit. There were some really interesting moments. Some songs have instruments with interesting riffs that get caught in my head. Some even managed to create a hypnotic groove despite the cacophony. I overall found Captain Beefheart’s vocals grating. I respect this album for what it is: maybe the only expression of true, unbridled creativity ever recorded. I am glad that I listened to it. I am glad that it exists. I am glad that I don’t ever have to listen to it again.

Favorite tracks: Frownland, Pachuco Cadaver, Sweet Sweet Bulbs, My Human Gets Me Blues, Sugar ’N Spikes, Wild Life

Least favorite tracks: The Dust Blows Forward ’n the Dust Blows Back, Well, Orange Claw Hammer (the three where Captain Beefheart sings a capella)

Rating: 1/10

(Note: for what it’s worth, in my scale I actually prefer a 1/10 album to a 5/10 album, because a 1/10 album tries and fails, whereas a 5/10 album doesn’t try at all)
