# Hargrove's review

I had remembered being vaguely positive toward a couple Car Seat Headrest songs around the time of the 2018 re-record of *Twin Fantasy* thanks to airplay on my beloved XMU radio station, but I never went beyond that. Over time, I’ve very much mentally grouped CSH in with the feckless, desiccated husk that was the mid-to-late 2010s indie rock scene. The Tumblr aesthetics didn’t help either. However, it pains me (really) to report that this record bangs. I have plenty of quibbles, but the headline here is that I enjoyed it. What most excited me about this album was how straightforward, loud, punchy, and even confident it was. It was not at all the limp, lifeless, weak noodle of sadboy vocals and softly strummed guitars that I expected. This record has guts, as seen in the powerhouse vocal performances, reverbed washes of guitars, forceful drumming, and major crescendos. I also appreciated the progressive song structures, whether in an obvious way like the 10+ minute behemoth “Beach Life-in-Death” or in more subtle ways, as in the onslaught of bridges and pre-choruses found in “Bodys”. For the purposes of this review, I will only be explicitly rating the original 2011 version (well, the 2012 version with enhanced bass as that’s what’s on streaming) because that appears to be the cover on our list. However, I gave both the 2011 and 2018 versions five listens each, and I will regularly be adding commentary on the 2018 counterparts for each track. Overall, and this may be my contrarian impulse, but I actually preferred the 2011 version. The 2018 version works best on striking bangers like “Sober to Death” and “Bodys” that I want to play loud, but I find the lo-fi haze helps keep the slower parts from becoming uninteresting. It also helps mask Toledo’s worst impulses, such as the over-emoting on “Stop Smoking”; you can actually hear it in both versions, but only if you’re specifically listening for it in the 2011 version. And, of course, the additional 16 minute runtime on the 2018 version is almost entirely fluff that makes the album more of a slog. Still, I like both versions quite a bit. It starts to lag a lot in the second half, and sometimes Toledo’s twee aesthetics could have used a counterweight. But I’m more impressed than I could have dreamed I would be.

“My Boy (Twin Fantasy)” starts things slow, but with a clear prelude of the great melodies to come. I like it quite a lot. Its 2018 counterpart is probably the one that suffers most from the hi-fi production as I really enjoy the blown-out vocals on the original. As a rule, the quieter I want to play a track, the better the lo-fi works, and I like this one quiet. “Beach Life-in-Death” has to be the most impressive thing I’ve ever heard someone make in his car. It’s an insane achievement for this kind of artist. The first section is definitely my favorite, particularly the driving pace and the up-and-down guitar line. I ADORE the “I spent a week in Ocean City” segment with the overlapping vocals. It’s the most invigorating indie rock I’ve heard in a while. I’m indifferent between the versions for the first third, but I find the slower midsection much more palatable in lo-fi on the 2011. The 2018 midsection loses my interest something awful. The song finishes strong with more sadboy OOMPH and MUSCLE. “Stop Smoking” is short but sweet, packing a great melody and powerful message into something quite simple. I prefer the 2011 version because Toledo goes a little bit too “Christina Aguilera singing ‘The Star-Spangled Banner’” on the 2018 one, but I do rather like this track. The album keeps up its winning streak with “Sober to Death”, maybe my favorite track on the album and the first where the 2018 version is clearly superior. It’s a complete banger and I want to hear it LOUD, but the lo-fi production makes that a bit too abrasive. Man, those drums are punchy as all hell, the chorus melody is great, and that extended outro has been caught in my head for weeks now. “Nervous Young Inhumans” is bogged down by its lengthy monologue outro, which becomes 10x more annoying on the 2018 version. However, even without that, I don’t love it. I don’t think the CSH style translates very well into a short power pop song. It’s not terrible (outside of the monologue), but it’s the worst so far musically. “Bodys” brings it right back with a banger though. I love the drum machine, the catchiness, and the anthemic shout along choruses. I love the stupid “Is it the chorus yet?” bit, especially because this song has so many recurring segments that are hard to classify as strictly verse, chorus, or bridge. And the 2018 section clears as a clean rocker. My least favorite track is “Cute Thing”, which isn’t awful, but the best parts are just fine and the worst parts are annoying. I really don’t care for the doo wop backing vocals. I also don’t like the substitution of Dan Bejar and John Entwistle for Frank Ocean and James Brown as the latter do not feel like real influences in the same way the former do. Still, I respect the guitar solo shreddage. “High to Death” is a bit downtempo for my taste, especially as I start getting fatigued of the album around this point. The reverb on the 2011 version at least makes the whole thing sound kind of psychedelic and shoegaze, while the 2018 version falls into straight up wimpiness. “Famous Prophets (Minds)” almost brings the length of “Beach Life-in-Death”, but it does not bring the same coherent progression. It’s not terrible, but it’s just not quite as satisfying. That is, until the “For three transgressions of Will/And for four, I will not revoke the punishment” section at the end of the 2011 version, which ROCKS so hard I have to make the entire track a favorite. Meanwhile, the 2018 version goes off into some kind of a lame extended outro that stretches over 15 minutes. It would be a candidate for my least favorite if I were reviewing the 2018 version. I like “Twin Fantasy (Those Boys)” as a closer, but as a track, it just doesn’t have the energy I want. Especially on the 2018 version, it’s kind of a slog to hear this an hour and ten minutes in.

See, I can like twee too-clever-for-its-own-good indie rock. It just has to have even the slightest bit of oomph. It will take some time to adjust to the idea that I like Car Seat Headrest though.

Favorite tracks: My Boy (Twin Fantasy), Beach Life-in-Death, Stop Smoking, Sober to Death, Bodys, Famous Prophets (Minds)

Least favorite track: Cute Thing
