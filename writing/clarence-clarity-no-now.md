# Write-up

Clarence Clarity (real name Adam Crisp) is a musician and producer from London born in 1985. He has used various names and played in multiple bands since the early 2000s but one thing that was consistent was that he valued his privacy and anonymity. In 2012, he began to use the name Clarence Clarity and showed his face for the first time on the cover of an EP in 2014. In 2015, he released his debut solo album *No Now*. The album received muted but good reviews and was compared to Prince by multiple outlets. His music was largely R&B, soul, and funk crated in the style of glitch pop. He released his second album *Think: Peace* in 2018 and his third album *Vanishing Act II: Ultimate Reality* in 2024. Both were thought of as decent but far too conventional compared to the weirdness of his debut.

In 2017, Clarence Clarity began producing music for other artists. This work would prove his most influential on the musical landscape of today. He produced Rina Sawayama’s debut mini-album *Rina* in 2017. Sawayama was an artist who was making banger pop music that was self-aware of its similarity to the music of Britney Spears. She also had a lyrical focus on social media, stardom, and the digital age. Though her music is not as formally inventive (though it is quite good), the early 2000s nostalgia and internet-focus made her adjacent to the budding hyperpop scene. She would even collaborate with Charli XCX before the two fell out in 2023 (a long story involving Taylor Swift). The glitchiness of Clarity’s solo work was also influential on hyperpop. Clarity produced Sawayama’s full-length debut *Sawayama* in 2020, which I reviewed for The D and is pretty good but nowhere near as good as *Rina*. Clarity would also be a go-to producer for Dorian Electra, one of the first hyperpop artists who got big and I was like “okay this is starting to get stupid”.

# Hargrove's review

This album is insane, man, There’s just so much going on. It took me quite a few listens to get a handle on things, about on par with *Henry Cow*. I did not expect this album to be similarly impenetrable to a polarizing avant-prog record. I’ve been familiar with Clarence Clarity for some time through my love of Rina Sawayama. The mini-album *RINA* is short (eight tracks but two are interludes) but I often cite it as an example of, like, a perfect album from front to back. One where I wouldn’t change a second of music. Clarity led production on that project, and I give him very high marks for it. He not only crafted some veritable bangers, but the cerebral, conceptual approach to late 90s Britney-style pop R&B became a vital component of hyperpop. Sawayama’s full-length debut *SAWAYAMA* was one of my most anticipated releases of 2020 and I reviewed it very favorably for The D. Clarity’s grip on production was a little loosened, and going back to it I don’t like it as much now as I did then, but “XS” is still one of my favorite pop songs of the decade. Despite this, I had never taken a real crack at Clarity’s solo work. It was…much weirder than I had expected. Sawayama was certainly adjacent to the bubblegum bass/hyperpop scene, but her music was never that out there. This album is pretty out there. It would be one thing if it were just the weird sounds, but it’s the sheer number of them that makes this album so challenging. It is busy as fuck. As maximalist as pop production can be. Between the glitchy instrumentals, the uncommon textures, and the inimitable vocals, I didn’t think I’d be able to get into this. I got through three listens with very little progress. But starting with “Meadow Hopping, Traffic Stopping, Death Splash”, I began to notice just how good the songs underneath were. There are a lot of tender, catchy, and just stellar R&B bops underneath the layers of glitch and noise. There are more beautiful choruses than many albums have songs. I’v grown to really like it. It’s Prince or D’Angelo had they been exposed to the horrors of the internet in their formative years. It’s a little bit too long, and some of the interludes needn’t exist, but man am I rocking with this album by now.

There are a lot of tracks, so I’ll focus on the standouts. “Will to Believe” begins the album in earnest with a banger. The way he says “liars” all stretched out gets me every time. The crooning is insane. “Alive in the Septic Tank” has another great vocal performance from Clarity, but I particularly love the almost-gospel female vocals in the chorus. It also has a particularly dark and grimy synth line that makes me feel like I need a shower. “Buck-Toothed Particle Smashers” continues the hits with perhaps my favorite chorus on the entire album. The synths are blaring like the music in a Vegas strip club. And the lyrics are fucking weird! The entire album has gross lyrics about sex, shame, religion, and the internet, but they first got to me here on this story of incest. “Those Who Can’t, Cheat” seems to be the consensus best track, which I don’t necessarily agree with but I do think it’s a banger. It is certainly an ear worm of a chorus. I also like the Middle Eastern flute and am unsettled by the lyrics. And I did I mention how goddamn smooth Clarity sounds on vocals? It’s a good track. After all of these nearly back-to-back bangers, there’s a bit of a lull until “Meadow Hopping, Traffic Stopping, Death Splash”. This was the first song to crack for me, and I think it’s the most accessible. “I was in Hollywood at the witch house/With the reptiles and the Catholics” is a hilarious line, and the ramp up into the chorus is orgasmic. It’s very catchy and very powerful. Again, there’s a bit of a lull until “1-800-Worship”, which has a chorus on par with the songs in the first half. “With No Fear” closes the album as one of the most bizarre, impenetrable tracks. I love the first half, which reminds me of Pink Floyd for some weird reason. It sounds like D’Angelo fronting Pink Floyd. It’s great. The second half is an intense glitchy sound collage that blows my mind.

Those are the crème de la crème of this album. There are other tracks that are good but just not quite on that level, like “Off My Grid”, “Let’s Shoot Up”, “Bloodbarf”, “porn Mountain”, and “The Gospel Truth”. The issue is that with an album this long and ambitious, it’s not clear how beneficial good but not great tracks are. I was also disappointed by “Cancer™ in the Water”, which is teased throughout by samples as the climax but just felt incomplete to me. “One Hand Washes the Other” is my least favorite of the full songs. I just don’t care for the twangy banjo. And then there are the little glitch interludes, which I could have done without.

This is a weird, sprawling album with few analogues. It is so full of ambition and creativity. At the same time, it’s a bit bloated and certainly self-indulgent. It only works because so many of the songs underneath would work as R&B tracks played straight. Strip away all the weirdness, and they’re still amazing. This allows the weirdness to add to it, and once you get comfortable with it, it’ll take you places. I am really feeling this album right now, which is surprising given my initial reactions to it.

Favorite tracks: Will to Believe, Alive in the Septic Tank, Buck-Toothed Particle Smashers, Those Who Can’t, Cheat, Meadow Hopping, Traffic Stopping, Death Splash, 1-800-Worship, With No Fear

Least favorite track: One Hand Washes the Other
