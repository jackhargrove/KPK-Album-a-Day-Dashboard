# Write-up

cLOUDDEAD was a hip hop trio formed in Cincinnati in 1998 by rappers Doneone (real name Adam Drucker) and Why? (later known as Yoni Wolf, real name Jonathan Wolf) with producer Odd Nosdam (real name David Madson). Apparently the name was coined in a knock-knock joke by Doseone’s 5-year-old sister, but I cannot find info as to what the joke was. While they are generally considered hip hop because that’s how they got their start, their actual music is a difficult to categorize fusion of hip hop, ambient, electronic, and psychedelia. In 2000 and 2001, they released six double-sided singles. Afterward, they compiled them all into one twelve track album released in 2001 as *cLOUDDEAD*. The songs contain many samples from both music and movies, including a scream from my favorite film *The Graduate* on “And All You Can Do Is Laugh (1)” and music from David Byrne’s soundtrack for the movie *The Last Emperor* on “Bike (1)”. It received good reviews from a few outlets at the time and is sometimes considered a distant precursor to the cloud ra p of the 2010s (etymologically unrelated though). They released a second album, *Ten*, in 2004 to similarly favorable reviews. cLOUDDEAD broke up sortly after its release.

Doseone began rapping as a teenager in the 90s. In 1997, he did a freestyle rap battle at a festival in Cincinnati with a pre-fame Eminem. Nowadays, he mostly works on indie game soundtracks. Yoni Wolf founded the indie rock band WHY? in 2004, which received some buzz from Pitchfork and others in the late 2010s. Odd Nosdam pursued a solo career as an ambient electronic artist. As mentioned yesterday, he has collaborated with Boards of Canada multiple times. All three were among the eight founding members of Anticon in 1998, an LA-based independent record label that signed artists described as “the hip-hop equivalent of post-rock”. Though none of Anticon’s artists are on our list, they did release an album from Zach Hill of Death Grips in 2008, and helped foster the 00s alternative hip hop scene that would eventually break through in the 2010s. Both *cLOUDDEAD* and *Geogaddi* are adjacent to the small illbient (“ill” + “ambient”) genre, a combination of hip hop and ambient that developed in the late 90s. It can be thought of as an even chiller form of trip hop. As far as I know, these two albums are the only ones on the list that warrant talking about the genre.

# Hargrove's review

After my disappointment at the Divine Styler album a couple weeks ago, I was cautiously excited to try out another deeply weird barely even hip hop album. But then I saw the length. Why is like every hip hop album from 1990-2005 over an hour long? Curse the CD era! I already complain about this enough even for more conventional hip hop albums that I really like, such as Outkast’s *Aquemini*, which is about the same length as this album. When it’s this out there and challenging, however? It’s brutal. One of the greatest virtues an experimentalist can have is brevity, so as to let the ideas breathe and digest. *cLOUDDEAD*’s length ended up delaying my ability to enjoy it significantly, and I still consider it a real negative, but by my final listen I was starting to get into it a bit. There are some really nice sections here, both from the more standard hip hop to pure instrumental ambient. The lo-fi production added a kind of dirty window hazy lens to the dreaminess which made for an intense hypnagogic feel. Odd Nosdam’s production creates cavernous, airy atmospheres. Doseone and Why? both choose to be kind of annoying on a lot of this record but, when they don’t, they’re both quite skilled rappers. The lyrics are abstract almost to the point of meaninglessness, but there were a surprising number of lines that stuck with me. I don’t know. It’s not like I’m crazy for this album, but it feels like what an experimental album should be. It has positive qualities that no other album does that shine through its many flaws. I don’t see myself returning to it often; I didn’t even add any songs to my liked song playlist because, like, that’s not the context I’d want to hear them. I have become kind of fond of it though. It’s a fun, weirdo record that I’m glad exists. If it were half the length, I could see myself having fallen in love with it.

As a whole, the “Apt. A” tracks are definitely the highlight of this album. The first rap section on “Apt. A (1)” is the best on the entire album. I also like the kind of Middle Eastern feel of the second rap section. “I fall asleep hoping tomorrow tastes like poems and honeysuckle” stands out as a great line. “Apt. A (2)” is almost just as good, particularly spaciousness of the first rap section and the additional power given by Doseone and Why? rapping in unison in the second. “C’est la vie, tchotchke” is a banger. “And All You Can Do Is Laugh. (1)” continues the quality. It’s more low-key and relaxing, conveying the strongest hypnagogic feel of any track. “Should the rules by which a desert cactus lives be adopted by the sycamore as well?” is clever, while “I can't count 40 fat women in spandex/Power walking circles around me/As I stare at a deserted baseball field/Writing a rap in red pen on the back/Of a printed e-mail folded twice” strongly evokes some suburban parks in Sacramento I’ve sat in before. And the scream at the end is sampled from *The Graduate*! “And All You Can Do Is Laugh. (2)” is the first real misstep, a collage of samples that quickly loses my attention. “I Promise Never to Get Paint on My Glasses Again. (1)” isn’t great either. That fast rap section does not fit the vibe at all. “It’s all the same shit but they call it cLOUDDEAD” ends it on a catchy note at least. “I Promise Never to Get Paint on My Glasses Again. (2)” continues the decline. It just feels aimless, and both the whispered and louder vocals are just grating. “JimmyBreeze (1)” snaps things back into shape, thankfully. The rumination on suicide is moving and the electronic texture interesting. However, by far the best part is the last two minutes. That bass line is CRAZY, and the fast drums are exactly what I’ve been looking for. If I could save that final two-and-a-half minutes as its own song, I would listen to it constantly. “JimmyBreeze (2)” is moving but a bit too choppy for my taste. I love “We seem to be sinking deeper into a vat of honey glaze/Shall we live/Or shall we laminate ourselves into a standard appetizing position” though. “(Cloud Dead Number Five) (1)” is a stellar ambient piece from Odd Nosdam. His IDM chops shine through here. It’s the track that really made me appreciate the benefits of the lo-fi production. The ending with the massive drums and the crazy synth rocks. “(Cloud Dead Number Five) (2)” is fine, but less striking than the first part. “Bike (1)” and “Bike (2)” add more or less nothing to this record. I don’t hate them, but ending after the “(Cloud Dead Number Five)” tracks would have significantly improved this record.

This is far from being one of my favorite records, but I’m really glad I listened to it. It’s admirable, if not consistently enjoyable.

Favorite tracks: Apt. A (1), Apt. A (2), And All You Can Do Is Laugh. (1), JimmyBreeze (1), (Cloud Dead Number Five) (1)

Least favorite track: I Promise Never to Get Paint on My Glasses Again. (2)
