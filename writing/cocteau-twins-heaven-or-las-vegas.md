# Write-up

As previously mentioned, Heaven or Las Vegas was Cocteau Twins' most commercial successful album. The album's themes largely revolve around birth and death. Guthrie and Fraser had just had their first child, whose first birthday coincided with the album's release. On the other side of the veil, Raymonde's father had during the recording of the album. Due to Guthrie's increasing cocaine usage, Raymonde and Fraser had increased influence on the record. Fraser's formerly incomprehensible lyrics occasionally lapsed into recognizable words on this record. The cover of the album is a long exposure take of Christmas lights being dragged across a backdrop. It has since become regarded as the band's best album and one of the greatest albums of all time.

# Hargrove's review

For the second day in a row, we have an album by a band that my parents have pushed on me since I was young but that I never got around to giving a real listen. This time, however, I already have experience reviewing the band because we listened to *Treasure* just a little over a year ago. I enjoyed that album, but never to the extent I wanted. I finished my review with the following: “This is a record that I do like, but I wish I liked a lot more. The idea of it is cooler than the execution in my opinion. I’m excited for when we get to *Heaven or Las Vegas*, because I think there are only a few changes that need to be made for me to really fall in love with Cocteau Twins. As it is, I think they’re only pretty good (relatively speaking)”. Well, we’ve gotten to *Heaven or Las Vegas*, and I have both good and bad news. The good news is that I very much did enjoy this better than *Treasure*. For one, I think the brighter tones of *Heaven or Las Vegas* complement this sound much better than the darker atmospheres of *Treasure*. There are certainly moments of gloom on this album, but for the most part it glimmers and shimmers and shines. Additionally, I just think it has better songs, straight up. “Cherry-Coloured Funk”, “Iceblink Luck”, “Heaven or Las Vegas”, and “Frou-Frou Foxes in Midsummer Fires” have stronger melodies and instrumental accompaniment than anything on *Treasure*. Really, though the closer “Frou-Frou Foxes in Midsummer Fires” is my overall favorite Cocteaus track to date, the first five tracks of *Heaven or Las Vegas* are essentially flawless. However, therein lies the problem that pervades the bad news: even after a few changes, I still have not fallen in love with the Cocteau Twins. Even when the execution approaches perfection and they improve on *Treasure* in every way, it just doesn’t excite me in the way i want it to. I don’t know exactly why. I always just want something more. “Cherry-Coloured Funk” is a great example. It’s so pretty. Fraser crushes the vocals. I like this song enough to put it on my liked songs playlist. But it doesn’t teleport me to a different world the way I think it should. It’s ethereal in style, but not in effect. And it always ends well before I want it to. So it goes. I’m content to like but not love this album.

I gave my take on “Cherry-Coloured Funk” in the opening paragraph and don’t have much more to add outside of how much I like the guitar tone. I like “Pitch the Baby” quite a bit. The shimmering, pulsating guitar reminds me a lot of Slowdive’s “Souvlaki Space Station”. I particularly like how busy the bass line is. Fraser gives a great vocal performance, particularly in the bridge. Plus, there’s a great breakbeat. Between the bass line and the drums, this track deserves the title “funk” more than the opener. “Iceblink Luck” is the closest this album comes to a pop song, and it does it much better than the pop song equivalent “Persephone” on *Treasure*. I like the anthemic chorus, the vocal melody in the verses, and the chiming tone at the top register of the instrumental that reminds me of jangle guitar. “Fifty-Fifty Clown” has a different atmosphere than the last few, reminding me of like a darker 80s urban environment thanks to the keyboard in the foreground. I like this atmosphere, which is enough for me to like this track. “Heaven or Las Vegas” begins with a gorgeous guitar line that continues to shimmer into the verses. The chorus itself is powerful, one of the strongest earworms on the album. A little over halfway through, there’s a killer guitar solo. It’s a damn good track, I just don’t understand how it ends up in conversations for the best song of all time.

After that immaculate first five, the second half of the album takes a steep tumble in strength of composition. “I Wear Your Ring” has a similar atmosphere to “Fifty-Fifty Clown”, but I don’t like it as much because of how badly it drags. It also reminds me of what I don’t love about *Treasure*, feeling less fleshed-out and self-contained than the first five tracks. I have similar complaints about “Fotzepolitic”, which has a weaker melody that makes it my least favorite track. “Wolf in the Breast” is an improvement over the last two with its soothing guitar line, but it’s still not one of my favorites. I don’t love the way that Fraser’s background vocals are layered together. “Road, River and Rail” has these hypnotic arpeggios coming at you in 6/8 time that I like, but it also has a darker atmosphere that doesn’t work for me like the brighter tracks do. Fraser also hams it up a bit too much in the chorus. After that wave of meh, “Frou-Frou Foxes in Midsummer Fires” comes in to blow every other track out of the water. I love this song. It’s easily the closest I’ve come to “getting” Cocteau Twins in the way that everyone else does. The enticingly downbeat intro draws the listener in, then the drum hits and launches the track into Fraser rapping over guitar wash. I adore it. Great, great track.

I would no longer say “I think [Cocteau Twins is] only pretty good (relatively speaking)”. I would promote Cocteau Twins to “good”, with no qualifier. But I have to put it below my alt wall at the top of B Tier. I just don’t feel any of its tracks as deeply as I do “1979” or “Blister in the Sun” or “When the Sun Hits” or “This Charming Man”. Despite that, it’s not all that far below these albums either, which is a testament to how impressed I was by the music regardless.

Favorite tracks: Cherry-Coloured Funk, Pitch the Baby, Iceblink Luck, Fifty-Fifty Clown, Heaven or Las Vegas, Frou-Frou Foxes in Midsummer Fires

Least favorite track: Fotzepolitic

# Aidan's review

I don't think I've ever listened to "Cherry-coloured Funk" sober before but I recognize it from every time I've spent time in the kitchen with Carms. Fraser ditches the incomprehensibility of Treasure for a real set of lyrics, but she's still lapsing in and out of recognizable English when I'm not reading directly off the lyrics sheet. The end result is quite nice, and produces the same feeling as a dream where you're not properly able to understand what everyone else is saying, despite feeling that you should. The strongest moments are the album is when one good line suddenly pierces through the muddle of dreamy effects. "I only want to love you" on "Pitch the Baby" is one such moment, and "burn this whole warehouse down" on "Iceblink Luck" is another. But those moments are too far and few between, as the lyrics are rarely as poignant and usually just lurk on the outskirts of understanding rather than rushing in and out. Unfortunately for a 10 song, 37 minute album, most of it is pretty forgettable and a lot of it sounds the same. I like how "Frou-frou Foxes..." spirals into a loop for the choruses, and the good tracks are good as singles. Dream Pop is notorious for "all the songs sound the same" and the lesser songs on here are very similar to the good songs, especially with the lack of recognizable lyrics. It's an effective album at cultivating a vibe, but I don't get a real impact out of it. Pleasant listen, and I'm sure I'll hear it again whenever I wind up in West Village.
