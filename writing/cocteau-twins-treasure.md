# Write-up

Pioneers of dream pop and one of the main inspirations for shoegaze, the only two genres Carms listens to, Scotland's Cocteau Twins were formed in 1979 as a duo between multi instrumentalis/DJ Robin Guthrie and bassist Will Heggie. The band existed for 2 years, not releasing an album or doing anything notable until the duo happened upon then 17 year old Elizabeth Fraser, who joined the group with her distinctive wordless vocals. Their early style was rooted in the gothic and post punk stylings of Siouxsie and the Banshees and Joy Division. They achieved their initial recognition thanks to a John Peel produced LP, and then released two well received and decently selling EPs. Heggie left the band during a European tour, forcing the duo and now couple to rethink their approach. The group met multi instrumentalist Simon Raymonde while working on a side project, and brought him in as Heggie's replacement. Raymonde's addition pivoted the band to the dreamier style with a series of LPs and EPs. Their profile steadily rose until they hit their commercial peak with Heaven or Las Vegas in 1990. Along with today's Treasure, it's considered to be the band's best album. The band began to struggle around the release of Heaven or Las Vegas, as Guthrie's substance usage began to strain his relationship with Fraser, despite their daughter being born a year prior. They broke up in 1993, but the band stayed together for another 4 years, before breaking up in the middle of recording their 9th album. The band attempted to have a reunion performance at Cochella in 2005, but Fraser cancelled because she found it too difficult to share a stage with Guthrie. One of the last songs the band wrote was recorded by Chinese pop star and actress Faye Wong, who you may know from Chungking Express. Guthrie and Raymonde went on to form their own label, Bella Union, and have been involved in several projects in various roles, while Fraser went on to work with Massive Attack on the album Mezzanine, which we have listened to previously.

Treasure was the band's first full album under their core lineup of Fraser, Guthrie and Raymonde. Brian Eno was almost brought in to produce, but Eno felt that the band didn't need him, which left Guthrie to manage the production. The band ditched much of their gothic influences in favor of a lighter sound that everyone decided to call "ethereal", spawning the sub-genre of ethereal wave. Fraser’s signature vocals are intentionally indecipherable, to the point that the band joined mbv in suing websites that attempted to transcribe them in 2022. The band was not particularly happy with the final result, but the critical reception was glowing both then and now. Robert Smith of the Cure found the album so romantic that he played it on his wedding day. The album was remastered in 2018, but this version does not appear to be on Spotify.

# Hargrove's review

My dad is a huge Cocteau Twins fan, and I love Elizabeth Fraser’s tracks on *Mezzanine*, but I’ve never actually listened to any of their records. I did like this one, but never as much as I wanted to. I love the idea of this band: dark angelic waif sings creepy gibberish over swirling strings and synths while an 80s drum machine ties everything together. They’re a fascinating and alluring oddity; even all of their pictures online are weird as fuck! While the atmospheres throughout *Treasure* were consistently pleasant, I did find myself wanting more out of a lot of the tracks, particularly on the second half. It’s hard to find much fault in Fraser’s vocals at any point (although parts of “Persephone” see her veering dangerously close to 90s R&B star territory), and the drum programming was simple but very effective glue, but the guitar and synth parts sometimes just didn’t match up in terms of quality. My favorite track on here is “Pandora (for Cindy)”. It nails a laid back, sunny atmosphere that I think is underexplored in the music I’ve heard from Fraser. The drum programming is light and crisp. It has my favorite synth line on the album, so straightforward yet so disorienting. And Fraser’s vocal interplay with herself on the “chorus”. Really great track. I’m a big fan of the opener “Ivo” as well, though I sometimes wonder if I’m overestimating how much I like it because it’s the point in the album where I’m paying the most attention. I *really* like the offbeat “eeh eeh ohhhh” vocal bit. It’s very Kate Bush in its construction. The guitar is simple, but the chords hit the right spot. For whatever reason, I don’t like the primary vocal melody on “Lorelei” as much as the one on “Ivo” (though it does get stuck in my head). However, I think the staccato secondary vocal rhythms are really nice. “Persephone” has my favorite guitar work on the album, the tremolo is a unique texture and it’s used very well. The drums are at their most complex as well. This is the one vocal performance where Fraser gets a little on my nerves, but it’s fine. “Beatrix” is my least favorite on the first half. I do like the exotic feel, and I dig the punchy vocals that kick in around 2:00, but there’s something about the rhythm that puts me off.

The second half of the album is much weaker in my opinion. The closer “Donimo” is the standout on this part of the record; the opening sounds almost like a Church choir singing a Latin hymn, then the drum programming kicks in to completely change the feel, and finally the wistful whining guitar in the last bit is so nice. Otherwise, the tracks are mostly forgettable. “Amelia” has an interesting and angular atmosphere, but very little specific stands out to me as I listen. “Aloysius” has a more choppy, consonant-y feel in Fraser’s gibberish that is cool, but that’s about it. “Cicely” has the best bass line and a reverbed vibraphone. “Otterley” is this album’s absolute bottom, just too static to reach the dreamy and ethereal qualities of the rest (though I give it points for Fraser’s faux-French).

This is a record that I do like, but I wish I liked a lot more. The idea of it is cooler than the execution in my opinion. I’m excited for when we get to *Heaven or Las Vegas*, because I think there are only a few changes that need to be made for me to really fall in love with Cocteau Twins. As it is, I think they’re only pretty good (relatively speaking).

Favorite tracks: Ivo, Lorelei, Persephone, Pandora (for Cindy), Donimo

Least favorite track: Otterley

# Aidan's review

Never really listened to the Cocteau Twins, but they've always been on the periphery of genres I've enjoyed, so I was looking forward to this album. When doing the write-up, I had it in my head that Cocteau Twins were one of those super unique 80s to 90s bands with a cult following. Yet while it’s not a bad album, it's just far more pedestrian than I expected. Fraser's vocals are great, but in the current day and age, wordless vocals are no longer as avant garde as they were. There's still a lot of good on this album, Lorelei is great, I really dig the staccato singing on Pandora, and Domino is a fitting closer. There's not a truly bad song on here, and it's a pleasant listen, I just was waiting for something more. Similarly to Visions, it performs some sort of magic trick where you don't realize how thing the instrumentals are until after a few listens, but it sometimes does rear its head on Cicely and Aloysius, which, while fine songs, don't manage to make the "ethereality" of the rest of the albums. If I didn't go in with such high expectations, I would probably have been much more laudatory, and as it is, I enjoyed the album, but I wasn't blown away the way I hoped.
