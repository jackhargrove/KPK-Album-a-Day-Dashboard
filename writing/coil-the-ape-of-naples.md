# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

I had never heard of Coil before this and I had no idea what to expect. This is a slow, simmering album, and my first cursory listen didn’t do much for me. But in my subsequent listens, where I paid closer attention, I was absolutely blown away. This is a really good album. Not only is every second of it positively haunting, but it has a specific atmosphere that I can’t think of any other album I’ve listened to having. It strangely has a rustic quality to it. Many of the songs make me feel like I live in a quirky rural village with a dark secret and an ominous traveling carnival has just rolled into town. It’s like the book *Something Wicked This Way Comes*. Or the video game series *Professor Layton*. It’s a looming sense of dread, but more than that it’s a puzzling sense of dread. Dread that I can’t understand. Dread that I will never understand. Life and death in a world of puzzles with no solution. This atmosphere is constructed so delicately with such an unusual arrangement of timbres and textures. Outside of “It’s In My Blood”, nothing sounds overtly industrial, but you can feel industrial fingerprints everywhere, from the bass line of “The Last Amethyst Deceiver” to the beep boops of “Heaven’s Blade”. This is an album as beautiful as it is heartbreaking. I really, really enjoyed it.

“Fire of the Mind” is an intriguing opener that pulls me into the unique atmosphere immediately. I love the droning accordion (?) which establishes the dark bucolic feel. This song evokes images of a hermit isolated in a cabin the woods. I imagine that’s how Cristopherson felt. “The Last Amethyst Deceiver” is fucking phenomenal. The bass slinks along, ominous and mysterious, while the piano creates a bit of cold mechanical motion. And then there’s the marimba. The marimba, my love. This marimba is my favorite part of the album. It’s everywhere and it adds so much while doing so little. Here, it creates pathos, an achingly beautiful representation of more delicate emotions. The lyrical imagery of scavengers and decomposers heightens everything to the next level. It’s a great track, but “Tattooed Man” is even better. This is the one that first made me think of the traveling carnival. Musically, it’s my favorite. Lyrically, “There’s a man lying down in a grave somewhere/With the same tattoos as me/And I love him, I love him, I love him, I love him, I love him” is devastating. “This is the dark age of love” indeed. Unbelievably good track. “Triple Sun” isn’t quite as good on its own as the previous three, but it keeps up the atmosphere nicely. The marimba is still doing its thing and the horns are a nice addition. “It’s in My Blood” is unfortunately a bit of a misstep. I appreciate the industrial sound, but it’s too abrasive and too loud for what the rest of the album does. It disturbs the smoldering and quiet rumination. “I Don’t Get It” is a strong recovery, however. It returns to the vibes of the early tracks, particularly “Tattooed Man”, and opens with such a confused, discordant, and ominous vibe. It has some of the best timbres on the album, from the stereo picking of the guitar to the lilting of the clarinet-like thing. There’s a moment around 4:30 where the icy piano kicks back in that sends shivers down my spine.

“Heaven’s Blade” moves into a more electronic direction and away from the dark countryside thing I’ve been digging. It’s a little mid, but it’s not bad. “Cold Cell” goes back to the atmosphere I want but I wish there were more happening in the song. The lyrics are interesting though. With “Teenage Lightning 2005”, the marimba is back and I’ve never felt better. It has a great interplay with the guitar. Then there’s the piano, which comes in high and cold. All of these work together to create a 12 bar blues and it sounds amazing. By the end, there’s such a pretty tapestry of sounds. “Amber Rain” has some of my favorite lines, like “It seems these days the weaker ones survive/What an awful way to find out you’re alive” and “I don’t expect I’ll ever understand/How life just trickled through my hand”. Just crushing. The rhythmic complexity of the flutes in the background makes it a bit of a tough listen though. “Going Up” closes with one of the coolest melodies I’ve heard in a while. It’s sad and depressing and resigned and wistful and exotic and melancholic. It carries the song, and it carries it well. The operatic singing sounds great, but I don’t quite understand why the final meditation on the loss of a life partner includes singing about a department store.

I was surprised at just how good this was. It really came out of nowhere. But it is stellar, superb, amazing, phenomenal, sublime. Not every song is a 10/10, but neither does any track dip below a 6/10. Very high highs, and very medium lows. I am glad I have listened to it.

Favorite tracks: Fire of the Mind, The Last Amethyst Deceiver, Tattooed Man, I Don’t Get It, Teenage Lightning 2005, Going Up

Least favorite track: It’s in My Blood
