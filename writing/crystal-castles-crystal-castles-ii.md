# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

As I mentioned in my review for the debut Crystal Castles album that we listened to at the beginning of this year, this is a band that I had heard of but never actually listened to before we started this list. I ended up enjoying that album a decent bit, slotting it in near the bottom of B Tier. I feel strong nostalgia for the post-financial crisis late 2000s, and hits like “Crimewave” and “Vanished” really scratch an itch for me. Additionally, the occasional track that let Alice Glass go wild appealed to me as someone who likes screamy edgy punk chicks. I wished that she had been on more tracks. However, the album was held back by a few forgettable or pointless songs that altogether made it a bloated, overlong project. Unfortunately, this album is a downgrade from the debut in just about every way. While I like “Celestica” a lot, there are few bangers that transport back to the late aughts like “Crimewave” and “Vanished” did. And though Crystal Castles answered my prayer to put Glass on more tracks, she really phoned it in here compared to the original. “Suffocation”, “Pap Smear”, and, again, “Celestica” stand out to me as tracks where Glass seriously enhanced the music with an interesting melody in the verses or chorus. Those are all tracks with relatively clean vocals; when she got louder or shriller, I found the effect much less enjoyable than I did on the debut. Worse, there are a few tracks where Glass contributes roughly the exact same style of vocals, obscured by distortion but clearly noticeable upon multiple listens. When she tries, she takes Kath’s production to the next level, but I found her lazy more often than not. This compounds into a major frustration when she’s on twice as many tracks as she was on the debut. Last but certainly not least, the issue of samey, forgettable tracks bloating the album is much worse here than on the debut. This wasn’t even a particularly long album at 52 minutes, but there were listens where it felt like it was stretching on longer than the two hours of *The Seer* last week. Not only do a solid third of the tracks feel completely non-essential, but even some of the best tracks go on for a minute or two longer than they need to. The only track I outright dislike is “Doe Deer (12” Version)”, but there are so many tracks, particularly on the album’s front half, that are fine in the moment but fall out of my head the second I finish listening to them. Ultimately, try as I might, this album ended up as mostly wallpaper for me. I could see myself getting the urge to listen to it again at some point, but I’m taking away few melodies to hum to myself, which is disappointing after the debut.

I respect how “Fainting Spells” starts out with serious earrape. No matter what I set the volume to, the opening of this album spikes my heart rate. You know me, I like the abrasive electronics and Glass’ distorted vocals that sound super creepy. It’s a good opener. “Celestica” is a pop banger and the prettiest Glass’ voice has ever sounded. I love the eighth note synth pattern in the background as well. It’s an ethereal and lovely song that I could see myself putting on my basement playlist if I had heard it back when. Though I like abrasion, the harsh noises of “Doe Deer (12” Version)” don’t feel like they’re in service of anything. It’s a prickly curiosity with a generic beat outside of the shrill sounds. I don’t hate “Baptism”, but it was the first track to cause me to question whether Glass has much range. I like the shifting beat, but she’s already starting to wear on me by track four. “Year of Silence” isn’t bad either, but that’s just so much of what this album is: indistinct songs that are fine to listen to but that leave no impression. The beat is fine, but doesn’t knock my socks off. I’m torn on “Empathy” because it at least has a memorable line that sticks with me in the intense sixteenth note synth melody. However, I’m undecided on whether I like it. It annoyed me a lot at first, but it’s grown on me as I’ve appreciated that it’s at least memorable. Glass also lays down a nice chorus. It’s just not enough to make it a favorite though. “Suffocation” is the best track since “Celestica”. I like the intro, the wall of atmospheric sound, the clean vocals from Glass, and the simple but bouncy beat. This is what Crystal Castles should sound like.

“Violent Dreams” is a great Kath instrumental. The four on the floor beat sounds subtler and more pulsating that the other tracks. Combined with the creepy gibberish of the vocal sample, it coalesces into a spooky but catchy chorus. “Vietnam” is a worse version of the prior track, a gibberish sample without the catchiness and a beat too generic and booming. Similar to “Empathy”, “Birds” was memorable straightaway but not for a good reason. It has that synth rhythm that sounds like water being squeezed out of a dropper, as best as I can describe. However, it’s grown on me. That sound has become enjoyable, and I appreciate the intensity of the chorus. “Pap Smear” is another good track. It’s got this amorphous synth wash that glimmers back and forth, the most effort Glass has put into a melody since the album’s beginning, and fun breakdowns. Both versions of “Not in Love” are good, but the radio version with Robert Smith is definitely better. Kath’s production rules, though I admit I’m a sucker for 123+123+12 patterns that turn 4/4 unbalanced. It just fits the shimmering synth perfectly. That being said, I don’t know what they were thinking with the quiet pitch-shifted vocals. Robert Smith’s version corrects this, inserting his anguished, dramatic voice that fits the atmosphere of the track.  “Intimate” has a somewhat interesting lead synth line, but I’m too fatigued with this album at this point to appreciate it. It’s all so indistinct and long. “I Am Made of Chalk” is a fun outro with cool watery sounds. I would be more into it if the album left more of an impression on me.

Perhaps my opinion has been colored by the truly elite run of banger albums over the last few weeks, but this was the first album in a while to leave almost no impression on me. I like enough songs (and don’t hate enough songs) to put it in D Tier or lower, but the bottom of C Tier feels right.

Favorite tracks: Fainting Spells, Celestica, Suffocation, Violent Dreams, Birds, Pap Smear, Not in Love (Radio Version)

Least favorite track: Doe Deer (12” Version)
