# Write-up

Crystal Castles - Crystal Castles

Crystal Castles was a Canadian electronic music duo consisting of Ethan Kath and Alice Glass for the majority of its history. Kath (real name Claudio Palemari) was a constant presence on the Toronto music scene, having achieved previous success with Guns 'N Roses knockoff band Kïll Cheerleadër, but had also been a member of a folk band and a hardcore band. He met Alice Glass (real name Margaret Osborn) when she was in 10th grade while he was 27, and a few years later asked her to record vocals over some songs he had been working on. The resulting songs were put onto an EP, which was posted online and the two split up. However, when the EP, titled Alice Practice, started to garner attention including from the then-trend-defining Pitchfork, the two reformed the group and called it Crystal Castles after a line from She-Ra. When the British teen drama Skins featured the duo performing the song in a club during one scene, they went from hip underground act to playing at Coachella, opening for Nine Inch Nails and supporting Blur. Their debut album, eponymously titled and usually referred to as "Crystal Castles (I)" to differentiate it from the two homonymous followups, was well received despite some criticism of the deliberately harsh tracks such as "Xxzcxcuzx Me".

Their followup saw  them featuring Robert Smith of the Cure on their lead single and recording in an exotic bunch of studios ranging from Detroit to an abandoned church in Iceland. It was slightly more commercially and critically successful, delving deeper into the combination of pop and noise rock from the first album. Their third album saw them forgoing computers, getting entirely new equipment and recording everything in one take, and was similarly received. The single "Kerosene" was a popular TikTok soundtrack for a while in 2022. In October 2014, Alice Glass left the band, saying that Crystal Castles was over and she was going to perform solo. Kath brought in a new singer, Edith Frances and started releasing new music accompanied with a passive aggressive note that several of the band's most famous songs, including Vanished and Crimewave, did not include Glass. With Frances, Crystal Castles released one album, Amnesty (apparently named after Amnesty International). While working on the followup in 2017, Glass went public with her allegations against Kath, accusing of him of abusing her, controlling her and threatening her. Despite Kath publicly denying everything, the accusations were corroborated by other women, who spoke of similar behavior dating back to before Crystal Castles had started. Kath has left the public eye while Glass is making music solo and looking into rerecording some Crystal Castles songs á la Taylor Swift.. Their music remains well-liked, with Lady Gaga being spotted wearing a Crystal Castles shirt last year.

Crystal Castles were noted for their usage of video game inspired beats, and many of their beats came from sampling a heavily modified Atari 5200. Kath also claims that Alice Practice came from a mic test by Glass, something she calls a lie meant to diminish her creative input. The band also sampled heavily. The vocals of "Vanished" are just a song by Australian band Van She with some minor edits. The same is true for "Crimewave", which samples LA band Health and was featured on Gossip Girl Season 1.

# Hargrove's review

Like the Mars Volta, Crystal Castles was a group I heard a lot about back on the indie forums in the 2010s but that I never listened to. Unlike the Mars Volta, Crystal Castles’ music is much more up my alley aesthetically. It so strongly evokes that late 00s Gossip Girl vibe, a period and atmosphere for which I feel intense nostalgia. Overall, I thought this album was alright. Good but not great. I appreciated the overall vibe, as well as the uniqueness of the electronic timbres and textures you get on this record. I also enjoyed the energy that Alice Glass brought to the songs she participated in. Her manic punk energy, alongside the harsher electroclash instrumentals Ethan Kath put on her tracks, add a certain edge to the whole album that balances out the vibe-iness of the longer instrumental tracks. However, I really question why this album is 52 minutes long. They had the material (and, frankly, the sound) for a super tight 30 minute album that could have been an all-time classic. There are some serious bangers on here, particularly in the first third. Why did they add so many half-baked tracks? This is just not a style that lends itself to long-windedness, even if the songwriting is consistently excellent. Oh well. I’ll enjoy the good tracks it gave me while not likely returning to this album as a whole.

The album opens with a stellar run kicked off by “Untrust Us”. I love the bouncy synth arpeggios, the super catchy vocal sample, and the feeling that it’s 2006 and we’ve reached the ultimate peak of human civilization. “Alice Practice” follows as far and away the best Alice track (though the others are still often quite good). She just sounds so fucking cool, honestly, and her screaming perfectly complements one of the most abrasive instrumentals on the album. It’s genuinely a punk track. “Crimewave” is another banger, and definitely one I’ve heard somewhere before. Kath chops up the vocal sample masterfully to create a truly distinctive sound. Unfortunately, “Magic Spells” blows, one of the worst on the whole album. It sounds kind of like a vaporwave track, but it’s 6 goddamn minutes long and just not that interesting. “xxzxcuzx me” is the most Alice of all the Alice tracks. She goes maybe a little too far on the vocals, but it all adds up to work for me. “Air War” is a bizarre oddity I’m glad exists but that I would never seek out. “Courtship Dating” is middling instrumentally, but Alice’s inflections on the chorus are enough to make it work for me. Stove burn (On my hands!)/Show them (To my friends!)/Make you participate” she says, sounding girlish to the point of being creepy. “Good Time” and “1991” are both time-padders with good vibes but not much to distinguish them. “Vanished” is decent but I don’t quite understand its popularity. I’ll take it for the catchiness of the vocal sample, though. “Knights” is forgettable. “Love and Caring” is another sick Alice punk banger. “Through the Hosiery” has the great line “How does it feel when you can’t feel nothing?”, but overall is relatively weak for an Alice track. The driving groove of “Reckless” makes it the best of the purely instrumental tracks. “Black Panther” is completely forgettable. “Tell Me What to Swallow” is an absolute bummer of a closer. I really do not care for it. What a lame, quiet, sad, and flaccid note to end on.

This is a super uneven album but it has a good vibe that appeals to me personally and a few real standout tracks. That’s enough for low B.

Favorite tracks: Untrust Us, Alice Practice, Crimewave, xxzxcuzx me, Courtship Dating, Vanished, Love and Caring, Reckless

Least favorite track: Tell Me What to Swallow

# Aidan's review

There's a lot of really well done disparate elements in this album. The video game influences are cool, Alice Glass's harsh noise pop stuff is cool, the synth pop stuff is cool. Yet it never really comes together. I got the feeling listening to this that Kath had an ear for catchy melodies without any sort of aesthetic judgement, while Alice was the complete opposite with her great vocals that sound like they're coming through busted speakers that never build into a solid melody. His songs often rework other people's songs, with vocals and all and just making them catchier, while her songs are uncompromising unique aesthetic vision. The two sides are united a few times later in the album on tracks like Knights and Love and Caring, but as a whole, most songs are either Alice or Ethan and it doesn't feel like a coherent album and neither individual shines that brightly. Vanished is a banger, but I'm not as impressed after listening to the original song that is also quite good. I've seen a lot of nostalgia for this album and I just don't get it. Maybe I had to be there.
