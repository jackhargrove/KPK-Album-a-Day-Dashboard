# Hargrove's review

Like the Mars Volta, Crystal Castles was a group I heard a lot about back on the indie forums in the 2010s but that I never listened to. Unlike the Mars Volta, Crystal Castles’ music is much more up my alley aesthetically. It so strongly evokes that late 00s Gossip Girl vibe, a period and atmosphere for which I feel intense nostalgia. Overall, I thought this album was alright. Good but not great. I appreciated the overall vibe, as well as the uniqueness of the electronic timbres and textures you get on this record. I also enjoyed the energy that Alice Glass brought to the songs she participated in. Her manic punk energy, alongside the harsher electroclash instrumentals Ethan Kath put on her tracks, add a certain edge to the whole album that balances out the vibe-iness of the longer instrumental tracks. However, I really question why this album is 52 minutes long. They had the material (and, frankly, the sound) for a super tight 30 minute album that could have been an all-time classic. There are some serious bangers on here, particularly in the first third. Why did they add so many half-baked tracks? This is just not a style that lends itself to long-windedness, even if the songwriting is consistently excellent. Oh well. I’ll enjoy the good tracks it gave me while not likely returning to this album as a whole.

The album opens with a stellar run kicked off by “Untrust Us”. I love the bouncy synth arpeggios, the super catchy vocal sample, and the feeling that it’s 2006 and we’ve reached the ultimate peak of human civilization. “Alice Practice” follows as far and away the best Alice track (though the others are still often quite good). She just sounds so fucking cool, honestly, and her screaming perfectly complements one of the most abrasive instrumentals on the album. It’s genuinely a punk track. “Crimewave” is another banger, and definitely one I’ve heard somewhere before. Kath chops up the vocal sample masterfully to create a truly distinctive sound. Unfortunately, “Magic Spells” blows, one of the worst on the whole album. It sounds kind of like a vaporwave track, but it’s 6 goddamn minutes long and just not that interesting. “xxzxcuzx me” is the most Alice of all the Alice tracks. She goes maybe a little too far on the vocals, but it all adds up to work for me. “Air War” is a bizarre oddity I’m glad exists but that I would never seek out. “Courtship Dating” is middling instrumentally, but Alice’s inflections on the chorus are enough to make it work for me. Stove burn (On my hands!)/Show them (To my friends!)/Make you participate” she says, sounding girlish to the point of being creepy. “Good Time” and “1991” are both time-padders with good vibes but not much to distinguish them. “Vanished” is decent but I don’t quite understand its popularity. I’ll take it for the catchiness of the vocal sample, though. “Knights” is forgettable. “Love and Caring” is another sick Alice punk banger. “Through the Hosiery” has the great line “How does it feel when you can’t feel nothing?”, but overall is relatively weak for an Alice track. The driving groove of “Reckless” makes it the best of the purely instrumental tracks. “Black Panther” is completely forgettable. “Tell Me What to Swallow” is an absolute bummer of a closer. I really do not care for it. What a lame, quiet, sad, and flaccid note to end on.

This is a super uneven album but it has a good vibe that appeals to me personally and a few real standout tracks. That’s enough for low B.

Favorite tracks: Untrust Us, Alice Practice, Crimewave, xxzxcuzx me, Courtship Dating, Vanished, Love and Caring, Reckless

Least favorite track: Tell Me What to Swallow
