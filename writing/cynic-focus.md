# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

On the eve of our Album a Day project’s conclusion, Cynic’s *Focus* closes out our final genre section: metal. While this was the genre I had the least familiarity with going in, I now consider myself an avid fan of the genre. Just last night, I took Dalton to see Mastodon and Deafheaven play in DC. Not only has the metal section of this list finally gotten me into acts I was familiar with like Black Sabbath, Metallica, Slayer, Kyuss, and System of a Down, but I’ve even fallen in love with artists I had never heard of before like Opeth, Bathory, Death, Sleep, Agalloch, Electric Wizard, Neurosis, and Liturgy. All in all, my conversion to metal is the most impactful shift in my musical taste as a result of this project. But even after a broad survey of nearly all major types of metal music, I still wasn’t ready for the bizarre mix of death metal, 80s King Crimson, Opeth, and jazz fusion on Cynic’s *Focus*. Across eight tracks, all with a similar sound, this record defies categorization or peer. From the skittering, swinging drums to the wistful vocoder to the deeply spiritual lyrics to the complex, overlapping guitar rhythms to the unmitigated ferocity of the loud sections, it’s a collection of sounds that don’t seem like they should go together. That last element is crucial; I was worried when reading about this album that it would cheap out on the crushing speed and volume of death metal, but thankfully it does not. There are quite a few points where you can easily tell that half of these guys previously played for death. And yet, there are quite a few points where you can’t. The quiet sections intrigued me as well, reminding me of my favorite parts of Opeth’s *Blackwater Park* (I have now read that Opeth cites Cynic as a major influence, which makes a ton of sense). The album’s songwriting is remarkably consistent, which is both a blessing and a curse. There’s not a song here that stands out as particularly bad. Outside of “Veil of Maya”, however, few songs stand out as particularly amazing either. Every track applies this idiosyncratic sound in more or less the same way, resulting in an album with neither troughs nor peaks. Still, it’s a good sound, and I’m happy to add this to my metal rotation.

“Veil of Maya” introduces Masvidal’s vocoded vocals, a sound that I’m kind of shocked we haven’t yet heard in a metal album. It works! The drums are as forceful as in any self-respecting death metal track and the guitar riff is razor sharp. The quiet section is soft but complex, like a mixture of Opeth and 80s King Crimson. I love the light jazz drumming in these sections as well. The title refrain is one of the few catchy vocal phrases on the album. It’s a great track. “Celestial Voyage” starts with a sharp riff as well that weaves its way across the length of the track. I wish there was a little more to it; the song itself feels underwritten and I’m left unsatisfied at the end. “The Eagle Nature” uses an uncommon and off-balance time signature in its chorus and you know I love those. The quiet sections on this track in particular remind me of Opeth. However, I find myself struggling to differentiate this track from others like “Sentiment” and “Uroboric Forms” after I’ve finished listening to the album. I wish it had more to distinguish itself. “Sentiment” alternates between tribal percussion and skittery jazz drums. It sounds like something a cult would play in the forest. “I’m But a Wave to…” is the only track in the albums midsection to rise above the rest thanks to how blisteringly fast and heavy the loud sections are. It’s the track that most reminds me of Death. The riff feels like classic heavy metal but keeps going in unexpected directions. “Uroboric Forms” starts loud, particularly with the apocalyptic drums. Halfway through, it hard shifts into a quiet section that I don’t like as much. It’s yet another decent but not remarkable track. I like “Textures” a lot. As an instrumental, it has a head start in standing out from the other tracks. The playing is remarkably complex, setting a bunch of repeating lines in different instruments against each other like a Steve Reich composition. It also weirdly reminds me of American Football. Eventually, it builds in intensity, finishing strong as one of my favorite tracks. “How Could I” opens with the super cool sounds of a Chapman stick. I also think it’s the track to most effectively use the vocoder since the opener. The lyrics grip me more than any other track, particularly “Freedom and reason shine through/Paddle upon the clouds on one's own canoe” and “Humility, take charge of me/Drown me in truth's modest waters/Loosen your grip identity/Free me from an ego's falter”. It’s a solid closer.

Ultimately, while I enjoy the sound a lot and do intend to revisit, the lack of standout tracks does force me to place it a tier below Opeth, and certainly below Death. But it was still a strong record, and I’m beyond glad that I get to experience completely unique sounds all the way up through the end of this project. It never got boring.

Favorite tracks: Veil of Maya, I’m But a Wave to…, Textures, How Could I

Least favorite track: Celestial Voyage
