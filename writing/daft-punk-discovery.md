# Write-up

Daft Punk was a French electronics duo from 1993 to 2021 consisting of Thomas Bangalter and Guy-Manuel de Homen-Christo. The band began as an high school indie rock trio named Darlin' after the Beach Boys song of the same name. Bangalter and Homen-Christo were joined by their classmate Laurent Brancowitz. The band lasted for six months, two gigs and four songs, and was mediocre by its members own estimations. Brancowitz later would join his younger brother in another indie rock band, Phoenix, which would become huge in its own right in 2009 with hit album Wolfgang Amadeus Phoenix. Bangalter and Homen-Christo decided to form another group, named after a negative review of Darlin' that described the music as "a daft punky thrash". Daft Punk was born.

Four years later, the duo released their debut album, Homework. By this point, Daft Punk was a buzzy name and had signed with Virgin Records after a bidding war, and the album lived up to the expectations, with the highly repetitive "Around The World" becoming a huge hit. With their fame established, they got future big name directors Spike Jonze and Michael Gondry to direct music videos for them, and founded their own label. For their follow-up, the duo sought to do something different, believing that Homework had spurred a group of imitators. Bangalter described their sound as "guitars that sound like synthesizers, and there are synthesizers that sound like guitars". Being electronic musicians, they also sampled heavily, both from a selection of underappreicated older artists and from their own playing in the studio. The biggest hit from the album, "One More Time", samples Liberian musician Eddie Johns, resulting in roughly a million dollars in royalties owned to Johns. Johns was unable to be found by his label for two decades after the album's release, until he turned up in a nursing home in Los Angeles. It is still unclear if he's been paid.

Discovery was a huge hit, both critically and commercially. However, several American rock publications were less enthusiastic than the consensus, including Rolling Stone, Pitchfork and the Village Voice. In the lead up to the release, the publicity-shy duo started wearing robot costumes as a way to put the focus on the music rather than their personalities. In place of traditional music videos, Daft Punk decided to create their own anime film, titled "Interstella 5555: The 5tory of the 5ecret 5tar 5ystem"

In 2005, the duo released their third album, Human After All. It was far less critically acclaimed than their previous two works. Starting in 2007, Daft Punk began to expand their cultural influence. Kanye sampled "Harder Faster Stronger" in his own mega hit "Stronger", they composed the soundtrack to Tron:Legacy, and performed in MSG with Phoenix. In 2013, they released their final album, Random Access Memories. The album featured a wide range of collaborations, including Pharrell Williams, Julian Casablancas and Panda Bear. "Get Lucky", "Lose Yourself to Dance" and "Instant Crush" were all huge hits and the duo won Album of the Year at the Grammys. They released a few more collaborations including "Starboy" with the Weeknd before calling it quits in 2021 via a video on their Youtube channel. The duo are still making music, and have recently been added to Fortnite.

# Hargrove's review

*Discovery* will always hold a special place in my heart as the first album I loved that my dad did not. Usually, my stories about when I got into a record involve the spring and summer of 2017 when I decided to explore a ton of music to broaden my horizons. *Discovery* is one of the select few that goes back way further than that. In my 8th grade English class, my teacher Mr. Downey liked assigning us essays on works beyond just novels and short stories. This included poetic analysis of early hip hop songs “The Message” by Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five and “Peter Piper” by Run-D.M.C., as well as an essay about the movie *School of Rock*. One day, we spent class watching *Interstella 5555: The 5tory of the 5ecret 5tar 5ystem*, the feature length anime film that accompanied *Discovery*. This must have been in late 2014, and all I knew of Daft Punk was “Get Lucky” from the year before. Of course, I HATED dance music and electronic music and pop music. I was all about that REAL rock music at the time. So I went into with my nose upturned, ready to sneer. Instead, I found myself completely enraptured by *Interstella 5555*, both its visuals and its music. When I told my dad about it he was nonplussed, which actually made me like it even more. It was my special thing, just for me and the tens of millions of other people who like obscure underground indie band Daft Punk. Anyway, this is all a long-winded and boring way of me saying that this album was formative for me as one of the first outside the rock idiom that I gave a real chance to. While the way I came to it may not have been especially common, having that general experience with *Discovery*, I think, is quite common. In my opinion, that happens because of the ideas from the rock idiom that Daft Punk worked in. I’ve always found EDM track structures unapproachable, the relentless repetition that abandons the verse/chorus/bridge structure. *Discovery* eases you in with pure pop songs like “Digital Love”, “Something About Us”, and “Face to Face” that help you relate to purer genre pieces like “Veridis Quo” or “Aerodynamic”. Additionally, I sincerely appreciate the adherence to and respect for the album format. These songs flow and fit together so beautifully. It’s such a basic pick for rock guy’s favorite dance album, but there are damn good reasons for that. Of course, it only works because the music itself is so damn good. It starts with one of the strongest four track runs of any album ever and then keeps peppering in consistent bangers through the penultimate track. This album’s greatest weakness is its closer, which stretches the blandest composition into 10 minutes to end on a joke. How are there two brilliant albums with an awful closer this week? But still, this record rules, and I feel an intense fondness for it.

*Discovery* begins with “One More Time”, an undisputed banger that’s a serious contender for greatest dance track of all time. God, that sample. It’s hard to even describe. It truly doesn’t sound much like anything else, at least not the way it’s used. And then you’ve got the autotuned vocals and the extended breakdown. It’s just a masterpiece. It’s something I don’t even really feel fully equipped to write about. I adore “Aerodynamic”, the most underrated of the opening four. The guitar work is genuinely aggressive. It actually approaches metal. Not like extreme metal or anything, but certainly Maiden or 80s power metal. It’s melodic but still shreds. On top of that, I love the interlocking rhythms. It’s incredible how much emotion “Digital Love” conveys despite how generic it reads on the surface. It works because of the Beatlesque melody. “There’s nothing wrong with just a little little fun” is an earworm that gets stuck in my head every time. “Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger” is another unimpeachable banger. In addition to the obvious, I think what makes it work is the recurring clanging on the cymbal for eight beats. I don’t know why, but it sounds amazing. The reordering of the lyrics in double time is also a highlight. This track takes the idea of industrial music literally and I love that. “Crescendolls”, “Superheroes”, and “High Life” are all similar songs that form the albums next phase. All three are based around one repeated vocal sample each. “High Life” is my favorite of the three; the sample is pure gibberish, and yet it conveys the feeling of the high life bizarrely well. It’s glitzy, glamorous, and uplifting. My second favorite is “Crescendolls” due to its bouncy, ascending main line. I find “Superheroes” fine but not great. There’s a hard edge to the start of the vocal sample that gives me a headache. Interspersed within this trio is “Nightvision”, a nice ambient atmospheric interlude.

While I find “Crescendolls” and “High Life” both super solid, “Something About Us” is the first total banger since the opening four. It’s so Goddamn funky! It struts in nice and slow and sleazy to set a fucking mood. I also love how French the vocals sound. Like “High Life”, “Voyager” does a great job of evoking its title through largely instrumental music. There’s something ambitious and adventurous and *Star Trek* about it. I love that bass synth melody, bouncy and light. “Veridis Quo” is my favorite deep cut because I fell in love with it as a kid. I just fucking love that Baroque melody. It sounds like it should be played on a harpsichord. It gets stuck in my head so bad! The dance instrumental in the background keeps continual motion as well. I respect Daft Punk for taking a big swing with “Short Circuit”; unfortunately, the swing missed. This track has its admirable elements, but it just ends up being aggravating more than anything else. It’s bouncy to the point of being annoying, especially with so many unpleasant sounds. The outro makes me think of a graveyard for electronics, though, so that’s a plus. *Discovery* finds one last banger in “Face to Face”. I was shocked to learn this week that the sample doesn’t actually say “face to face”. It sounds so much like it! Anyway, I love how it feels like an assemblage of so many little pieces, and I again appreciate the pop song structure. The album closes on its one serious flaw, “Too Long”. It’s such an uninteresting track, and it never fucking ends. Oh well.

I love this record and I will never stop listening to it. You should all be ashamed of yourselves for your bigotry against the French people.

Favorite tracks: One More Time, Aerodynamic, Digital Love, Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger, Crescendolls, High Life, Something About Us, Voyager, Veridis Quo, Face to Face

Least favorite track: Too Long

# Aidan's review

They may be French, but I'll be damned if they're not groovy as hell. "One More Time" is the only breathtakingly good song on this album, but there's not really a weak moment barring the opening minute of "Short Circuit" and an ending song that ironically than unironically overstays its welcome. Kanye has drilled the best part of "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger" into my head so much that the build up on the original feels a bit taunting, but it's just a victim of its own success. Thank god "Kid Charlemagne" gets to the good bit in less than 40 seconds. This is an album with a boatload of cool ideas packaged in great beats. I love the guitars on "Aerodynamic", the vocal sample on "High Life" and the bass line on "Voyager". "Digital Love" and "Something About Us" somehow avoid triggering my cynicism, and while they're not the strongest tracks, they do yeoman's work giving some vocals and variety to the album. Of the more sedate songs, "Veridis Quo" is by far the strongest though. One of those albums that's as good as everyone says it is. I was expecting far more stupid French stuff.
