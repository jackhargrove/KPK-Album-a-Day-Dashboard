# Write-up

Damaar was a war metal band formed in Beirut, Lebanon in 2004 consisting of vocalist and bassist Heathen, guitarist Nunrider, and drummer Napalm. The group broke up in 2006, releasing its only recorded work, *Triumph Through Spears of Sacrilege*, as a demo in 2007. It has developed a reputation in the underground war metal (raw black metal with death metal elements) scene for its extreme and brutal sound. “Desecration” is a cover of a song from the album *Fallen Angel of Doom....*by foundational war metal band Blasphemy, which we listened to earlier this year. The track “Preaching for Mass Suicide” features Filthy Fuck on vocals. In 2006, war broke out in Lebanon, and the members of Damaar left the country. There is very little information out there about this band. Guitarist Nunrider (Mhamad Safa) wrote about his experiences with the band here: https://monshakin.rehlamag.com/are-the-streets-still-for-dreaming/#thePostscript.

# Hargrove's review

Up to this point, my only experience with “war metal” was Blasphemy’s *Fallen Angel of Doom....*, a truly awful record that will in all likelihood finish this project as my lowest ranked metal album. I was originally excited when I saw that there was an obscure Lebanese band in the metal section with an Arabic name. It seemed like it would be something different, unusual, cool. Then, I saw that it was war metal. Then, I learned that one of the tracks is titled “Ode to Blasphemy”. Then, I realized that the track “Desecration” is not only a cover of a Blasphemy track, but a cover of my least favorite track on the *Fallen Angel of Doom....* album. These were not good signals. But when I finally listened to *Triumph Through Spears of Sacrilege* in this penultimate week of the project, I was pleasantly surprised to learn that it goes hard. Damaar makes war metal with the benefit of having heard what they did with black metal in Norway in the early 90s. Blasphemy had an interesting sonic concept, but coming up in late 80s Canada, it lacked the aural tools and techniques to make that concept listenable, let alone good. Damaar strips out the awful guitar solos, replaces the cartoonish growling of the vocals with shrieks, and leans into repetition instead of relying on extreme tempo. In other words, Damaar took the musical innovations of Mayhem and Burzum and the other Norwegian second-wave black metal artists and applied them to Blasphemy’s particular conception of unmitigated brutality. The result is convergent evolution with Portal, reaching the walls of dissonance through completely different means. Not only is the result pretty good, but this proper execution finally gives me better insight into whatever the fuck Blasphemy was trying to do with its debut album. Unfortunately, *Fallen Angel of Doom....* still sucks. It sucks bad. And, sadly, of the four full tracks on *Triumph Through Spears of Sacrilege*, one is a Blasphemy cover. Try as they might, it still sucks. Otherwise, there are three damn solid tracks on here. Things could be worse!

I wish I knew what they were saying on “فسق [Introduction]”, though not knowing adds to the post-apocalyptic feel. It captures the sound of a bombed-out wasteland perfectly (I imagine). “Preaching for Mass Suicide” sounds like Blasphemy off the bat, but a 100x more powerful version of Blasphemy mixed with Portal. It has so much Goddamn punch! It’s insane how maximal everything feels with zero breaks whatsoever. It doesn’t even need to keep an insane tempo for the wall of sound to crush you. The penultimate section has a normal, Ringo Starr-ass backbeat, whole the screaming of “DO YOU WISH TO DIE” melts my brain all the same. The climax incorporates a tasteful guitar solo. “The Goatphoenix” is even better. It starts out with the same level of calamity that I’ve come to expect from war metal, but it sounds so much more real. I don’t know what it is, but it’s as if there’s a jet engine beside the band, and yet they’re still holding their own against it. There’s another awesome slow backbeat section. I was quite surprised with how well Damaar uses dynamics across this album. These tracks all actually have multiple sections with buildups and breakdowns. The drum pounding in the final minute-and-a-half goes insane, plus I love the tinnitus drone. “Desecration” still sucks, sorry. I like Damaar so much better than Blasphemy, but even they can’t save this piece of shit. The chorus section sounds so stupid every time it comes around! The pounding drums are a nice addition but this track can’t be saved. When “Ode to Blasphemy (Onward to the Gates of Mekka)” starts, it’s remarkable how much more intensity it has than the Blasphemy piece of shit immediately before it. The guitars create a wall of dissonance that I enjoy. The three good tracks are difficult to distinguish from each other, but I care about that significantly less on a project this short.

At 19 minutes, this album is short, forcing me to give it the same short-album modifier I gave *Fugazi* last week. Additionally, a hit rate of 3/4 is only 75%, which doesn’t seem stellar on paper. However, the good parts hit so hard that I have to put this at least in low B.

Favorite tracks: Preaching for Mass Suicide, The Goatphoenix, Ode to Blasphemy (Onward to the Gates of Mekka)

Least favorite track: Desecration

# Aidan's review

I had a lot of trepidation going into this album but it solves most of the issues through volume and energy. The most stylistically similar album, Fallen Angel of Doom was one of my least favorite on the list and there's a cover of it on this album. They take "Desecration" by Blasphemy and just turn it up to eleven, turning the vocals from a dreary rant into a powerful howl. This album just sounds louder. The intro is also extremely cool, and being able to open with a cry of "Allahu Akbar" without feeling corny adds a certain something. This album has other virtues besides its energy, there's some nice bridge sections that come through the chaos on The Goatphoenix with a surprisingly mellow one-two beat, and the vocals are some of the best on a metal album I've heard, but the energy is the main selling point. A 20 minute blast that is hard to follow up.
