# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

I was a little apprehensive toward this album because it seemed like one of those incredibly lo-fi outsider music records that artists like purely for the creativity exhibited despite a lack of craftsmanship, and that’s not too far from the truth. However, I found myself liking it far more than I felt I ought to. Between the relentless sincerity and the simple but shockingly catchy little vocal lines, I succumbed a little bit to the charm of *Hi, How Are You*. The album’s two sides have entirely different concepts, both of which Johnston pulled off pretty successfully, all things considered. Side 1 mostly consists mostly of personal ballads, some accompanied by guitar or piano, some fully a cappella. The lyrics are largely personal and confessional, often making up for clumsy wording with the sheer openness of the sentiments expressed. Multiple tracks here really moved me, particularly “Poor You”, “Walking the Cow”, and “Despair Came Knocking”. The sparse instrumentation also gives these tracks an air of agoraphobia, as if they were recorded in a cavernous basement. Side 2, by contrast, often fills this empty space with instrumentals from 1920s jazz and blues. This could come across as hokey, but Johnston’s full commitment to writing proper vocal lines to complement them just makes it work. “Desperate Man Blues” and “Keep Punching Joe” legitimately could have come straight out of the Prohibition Era. While he chooses samples in a similar style to the Caretaker, he takes it in completely the opposite direction; instead of emphasizing the obsolescence of the sound, he faithfully adds to it to make it new. To be clear, I’m not declaring this one of my new favorite records or anything. I don’t foresee myself listening to this album or even many of the individual tracks too regularly. But I do see myself listening to it again every once in a while. To that end, I was quite impressed.

The album starts with “Poor You”, a bare and simple track where Johnston speaks more than he sings. And yet, the chorus melody is so damn pretty. I appreciate the sharing of something so personal in the lyrics. The topic of “Big Business Monkey” feels a little out of place, making it less emotionally resonant for me than some other tracks. Still, the instrumental is bouncy and fun. “Walking the Cow” picks up where it left off instrumentally, using that sound to craft the album’s best song. I like the chord progression better here and especially the off-kilter accordion-ish line around “Lucky stars in your eyes. The lyrics are phenomenal; I particularly like his heartbreaking delivery on “I really don't know how I came here/I really don't know why I'm staying here”. I’m a fan of the sharp chords on “I Picture Myself with a Guitar”, but the track is otherwise lacking. “Despair Came Knocking” brings in an ominous country sound with the guitar or banjo or whatever it is that makes it sound like I’m in *Deliverance*. The lyrics paint a vivid scene in Johnston’s mind. “I Am a Baby (In My Universe)” keeps the same ghostly, unsettling vibe as the last track. Of course, there’s an added element to this track now that Johnston has died, but I feel like it would have still put me off (in a good way) before that. There’s not terribly much to it, but it leaves a lasting impression. “Nervous Love” and “I’ll Never Marry” both feel incomplete, too short to meet their potential. The latter is my least favorite on side 1 because I don’t love the way Johnston’s voice sounds on it. “Get Yourself Together” is also very short, but I like it a good bit. The stomping beat and anthemic vocals make it reminiscent of an old work song or spiritual. I find the central metaphor of “Running Water” poignant, but musically I’m not so crazy about it.

“Desperate Man Blues” starts off side 2 with this awesome old ballroom sampling idea. This is a genuinely well-written 20s blues track. I enjoy listening to it quite a bit. And, of course, the lyrics are crushing. “Hey Joe” is a gorgeous piano ballad. I appreciate all the Beatles references, and the “I know you're thinking of your nervous love/I know exactly what you're thinking of” part shatters me. “She Called Pest Control” is my least favorite track on the album. It’s really just spoken word, and I somewhat fear what happened to make a woman “call pest control” on Johnston, as it were. Like “Desperate Man Blues”, “Keep Punching Joe” feels like a long lost track from the early 20th century. It contains two of the best lines on the album in “I guess I lean toward the excessive/But that's just the way it is when you're a manic depressive” and “How am I supposed to give love/If I never got love?”. It’s also interesting to hear Johnston start to get angry. “No More Pushing Joe Around” is fine, but it just feels like a worse version of the previous track, a cappella instead of with the cool instrumental. However, I really like the ending that brings back the instrumentals from all the preceding tracks.

This album still gets lumped in with novelty records in my brain, but damn if it isn’t a good and moving one. I feel changed enough by listening to it that I’m putting it in B Tier even though it’s not something I would generally put on for fun.

Favorite tracks: Poor You, Walking the Cow, Despair Came Knocking, I Am a Baby (In My Universe), Get Yourself Together, Desperate Man Blues, Hey Joe, Keep Punching Joe

Least favorite track: She Called Pest Control
