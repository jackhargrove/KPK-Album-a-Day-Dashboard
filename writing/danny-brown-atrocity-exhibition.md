# Write-up

Danny Brown (real name Daniel Sewell) is a rapper and producer born in 1981 and raised in Detroit. His half-Filipino father was a DJ of hip hop and house, while his mother introduced him to Dr. Seuss, inspiring Brown to start writing rhymes as a little kid. Brown’s parents were protective, keeping him away from the crime that surrounded them in Detroit. When Brown turned 18, his parents divorced and left him to fend for himself. He became a drug dealer to support himself as he pursued a rap career. He intended to leave the lifestyle at the first signs of legal trouble; however, he did not follow his own advice and ultimately served eight months in prison in 2007. In 2003, Brown became part of a group that had a modest hit. This earned him a deal with Jay-Z’s Roc-a-Fella Records, for which he moved to NYC. However, this didn’t pan out, and he was forced to return to Detroit. His punk rock-influenced fashion cost him an opportunity with 50 Cent’s label in 2010. A decade into his career and in his late 20s, Brown still had nothing to show for it.

Brown released his first album *The Hybrid* in 2010 on an indie label. It was the first recording where he used his signature high-pitched rapping voice. It received enough buzz that Brooklyn-based indie label Fool’s Gold signed him in 2011. He received support from rapper Q-Tip, who introduced him to A Tribe Called Quest producer Ali Shaheed Muhammad; Muhammad became Brown’s mentor. Brown’s first mixtape on Fool’s Gold, 2011’s *XXX*, proved to be his breakthrough. It received rave reviews, particularly for its point-of-view from a 30-year-old unsuccessful rapper.*XXX* is a fantastic record; “DNA” was on my basement playlist, and “30” is one of my favorite songs ever. Brown skyrocketed to the forefront of the hip hop scene, befriending A$AP Rocky, Childish Gambino, and Kenrick Lamar. *Old*, Brown’s second album, released in 2013. Its title is a reference to Ol’ Dirty Bastard of Wu-Tang Clan, a rapper to whom Brown is often compared. The album was another critical success and debuted at #17 on the Billboard 200.

In 2016, at the peak of his fame, Brown released his third album *Atrocity Exhibition*. It takes its name from a 1980 Joy Division song, itself named after a 1970 book of short stories. The Joy Division title signaled Danny Brown’s obsession with post-punk music and aesthetics that permeate the album. Lyrically, the album tells a harrowing and relentless story of Brown’s struggles with drugs, crime, and poverty. All but four tracks (“Rolling Stone”, “Really Doe”, “White Lines”, and “Pneumonia”) were produced by frequent Brown collaborator Paul White. “Downward Spiral” and “Tell Me What I Don’t Know” are built around samples from krautrock group Guru Guru, while “Golddust” and “Today” interpolate lyrics from Joy Division and Outkast, respectively. “Really Doe” is a posse cut that was put together at the behest of Kendrick Lamar. It also features Lamar’s Black Hippy bandmate Ab-Soul and former Odd Future member Earl Sweathsirt. “Ain’t It Funny” contains an atonal saxophone instrumental that was inspired by the no wave movement. It is often considered Brown’s masterpiece, along with its Jonah Hill-directed music video. The album earned Brown the most glowing critical plaudits of his career, but its unapologetically harsh sound hurt its commercial viability.

In the decade since, Brown has continued to evolve as an artist and release challenging, high-quality music. His most notable release since *Atrocity Exhibition* was his 2023 collaboration album with JPEGMAFIA, *Scaring the Hoes*. Many consider it one of the best rap albums of the 2020s. Later that year, he checked in to rehab for alcoholism and has been sober since. Brown’s most recent album, 2025’s *Stardust*, abandons hip hop production in favor of digital hardcore, digicore, and hyperpop. I saw him in Sacramento last November with Femtanyl and underscores opening. It was awesomeeeeeee.

# Hargrove's review

This such a good record. It’s experimental without sacrificing artistry, ambitious without sacrificing consistency, extravagant without sacrificing its unrelenting personal focus. Danny Brown is just such a great artist. He can have it all. I don’t remember exactly when or why I got into him, but it was either my junior or senior year of high school and this was my first album from him. I was listening to him before I even got serious about Death Grips, so I’m sure you can imagine how “Ain’t It Funny” blew my 17-year-old mind and reshaped the way I looked at hip hop forever. Who else was making instrumentals inspired by the *No New York* compilation? I didn’t even know what that was at the time, I just knew that the saxophones were scary and the guy rapping was living in some sort of permanent nightmare. Not long after, I got really into *XXX*, which is also a phenomenal record that has even higher highs in my opinion. But it cannot match the consistent, gut-wrenching, nausea-inducing intensity of *Atrocity Exhibition*. I feel like I need to take a shower every time I listen to this album. Danny describes sex, drugs, and rock ’n’ roll in a way that makes me feel viscerally sick. One line that really sticks with me comes from the opener “Downward Spiral”: “Had a threesome last night, ain’t matter what it cost/Couldn’t it get hard, tried to stuff it in soft/Had to fuck em both raw, keep my fingers crossed”. Has a three-way ever sounded less appealing? And yet you can’t look away. This…atrocity just pulls me into its orbit, keeping me focused on its display like some kind of…exhibition. In terms of quality, the album stays more or less completely consistent from front to back, falling off shockingly little for a 15-track album. Danny delivers some of the greatest verses of his career track after track after track, while the instrumentals refuse to grant you any reprieve either. I adore the high-pitched voice Danny puts on. I always have, but I think it complements the sound of *Atrocity Exhibition* better than any of his other albums. He sounds like a deranged, unwell, broken man. There’s just no ego in this thing whatsoever. At one point, he brags about having four credit cards. The short, fast, and hard lifestyle of rockstars/rappers has been explored in literally infinity works of every medium, but never has any one of them made it sound this genuinely unappealing. But, FUCK. I can’t help but want to rip lines with Danny in a bathrobe holding a sidepiece in a destroyed hotel room, at least just for a second. For one final point, I also just selfishly like that Danny clearly likes many of the same musicians that I do, and he leaves those fingerprints all over the album. I’m a sucker for the references to Joy Division, Nine Inch Nails, Miles Davis, John Coltrane, and countless more on other albums. And I mean, a well-executed hip hop album based on the aesthetics of post-punk was just always going to appeal to me. No way around it. Anyway, stream *Stardust*.

I know I say this a lot, but “Downward Spiral” really is a *perfect* opener for this album. In a record full of disgusting, harrowing experiences, this one might be the most disgusting and harrowing of all. Lyrical highlights for me include “I'm sweating like I’m in a rave/Been in this room for 3 days”, “Phone keep ringing but I cut that shit off/Only time I use it when I tell the dealer drop it off”, “And if I learned anything/Is don’t nod off with ya motherfucking cigarette burning”, “Everybody say, you got a lot to be proud of/Been high this whole time, don’t realize what I done”, “Smoking blunt after blunt, 'til my eyes start burning/Hennessy straight got my chest like a furnace”, and “You never know, one day you're here, the next you're gone/So I put it all up in these songs…”. It captures the same vibe as its Nine Inch Nails namesake. I love the guitar in the hook and the uneasy, uneven beat. More than anything else, I love the “I gotta figure it out” hook. It reminds me of “I must fight this sickness/Find a cure” from the end of the Cure’s *Pornography*. This album only works because Danny has some fucking fight left in him. I think it was a great choice for Danny to use his normal voice in “Tell Me What I Don’t Know” because it enhances the numb feeling of having seen it all. He lays down some fucking insane bars over spacey Pink Floyd synths and a prog rock guitar in the hook. “Rolling Stone” has another great guitar beat, this time with more of a muddy, spiraling post-punk flavor. The hook is a bit cheesy for my taste, but otherwise it’s another exemplary track. Lyrical highlights for me include “Some people say I think too much/I don't think they think enough” and “I’m walking on this long road/Will I come back?/Homie, I don't even really know”.

I really, truly mean it when I say “Really Doe” is the greatest posse cut of all time. It just blows my mind. Four great rappers all at the top of their form delivering perfect verses. My verse ranking is Earl > Danny > Ab-Soul > Kendrick, though Kenny gets bonus points for the heater hook. Earl just steals the show, though. He sounds so angry! I also love the creepy bells in the instrumental. Lyrical highlights include “She look like a centerfold/Mouth all on my genitals/Suckin' on it like she gettin' vitamins and minerals/I be on the chemicals, she be on my testicles/Poke her with my tentacle then put her out my schedule/Rollin' up them vegetables” (Danny), “Second grade, took my mama weddin' ring/Took that bitch to show-and-tell, now I'm married to the game to no avail” (Ab-Soul), “Sin is such a work of art” (Kendrick), “I was a liar as a kid so now I'm honest as fuck” AND “Look, I just broke up with my bitch 'cause we ain't argue enough” AND “I'm at your house like, ‘Why you got your couch on my Chucks?’/Motherfucker” (Earl). “Lost” has a cool chopped-up vocal sample beat that sounds like it could be on a Wu-Tang album, or maybe even come from Madlib. It also plays an important role as a short palate cleanser between the two best tracks on the album. However, I can’t put it in my favorites with the other heavy-hitters. “Ain’t It Funny” is a once-in-a-lifetime, decade-defining, phenomenon of a track. I said on my Oingo Boingo review that I love the demented funhouse vibe and, well, here you go. The lyrics are just so vivid and visceral. It sounds stupid, but I actually get this picture in my head of Danny looking at the physical embodiment of evil and feeling compelled to laugh. I can’t put it in any way that doesn’t sound contrite, but when I focus on that image at length, I give myself the willies.  There are simply too many lyrical highlights to list them all. Every line is a banger. If I had to pick one favorite section, it would be “Can’t quit the drug use or the alcohol abuse/Even if I wanted to, tell you what I'm gonna do/Imma wash away my problems with this bottle of Henny/Anxiety got the best of me so I'm popping them Xannies/Might need rehab but to me that shit pussy/Pray for me y’all ‘cause I don’t know what’s coming to me”.

I love how this album never lets up, and one of the ways it does so is by sequencing its second-most deranged instrumental in “Golddust” right after “Ain’t It Funny”. It’s another great track. The line “Bloody Marys at brunch/From last night still drunk/Popped a Adderall [REDACTED] that was my lunch” makes me uncomfortable because this is something I’ve actually done. ”Got to the point ain’t gotta buy drugs/[REDACTED] just give to em to me/Yea they think they showing love/And that’s what’s up/Kinda fucked up/Now I do way it more then I use to” also hits a little close to home. I love Danny’s cadence on “White Lines”, a rhythm that’s almost melodic in and of itself. I also love how claustrophobic it feels, as well as the lines  “How about you/And you and you and you/I’m tryna eat all y’all pussy” and “Lines and lines of coke/Nose bleeding got me with an itchy throat/Heart beating fast/Oh no/Oh no/Oh no/I hope it ain't 'bout my time to go”. “Pneumonia” combines a crazy catchy hook with insane industrial percussion. On top of that, I’ve had some lines from it just rattling around my head all day, like “Slurp that pussy up just like lo mein”. I want to ding “Dance in the Water” for being incessantly repetitive, but come on, it’s a fun dance track. I’ll get over myself and enjoy the fuck out of it. My least favorite track is “From the Ground”. I like Danny’s rapping, but I just don’t like Kelela’s overdone vocals at all. It’s a shame, the only real blemish on this album. Danny brings back the intensity with his unparalleled vocal performance on “When It Rain”. At this point, the instrumentals have shifted from the guitars and horns of the beginning to cold, hard electronic synths. Every ounce of humanity has been swueezed by now. “Today” brings in more cold synths and a great use of a classic André 3000 flow. I like the concept of “Get Hi” a lot, but I’m torn on the execution. I love the jazz references and its portrayal of how even weed can fuck up your life, but it’s just tough for an album this high-energy to get to this level of downbeat. “No homo, but your bitch go Tommy Dorsey on my trombone” is an incredible line though. I really like “Hell for It” as a closer to this album. It’s not too much of a bummer, but it’s not sappy feel-good optimistic dreck either. It’s just the reality that Danny has gotten himself out of worse situations before and he’ll give it his all to get out of this one. The music captures this as well, neither too downtempo nor too aggressive. Danny leaves us with resigned determination.

Phew, that was a long review. This album deserves it. I could talk about it for hours, days, weeks, months, years. There’s just so much substance to dig into. It makes me feel intense emotions, which, at the end of the day, is all I’m really looking for.

Favorite tracks: Downward Spiral, Tell Me What I Don’t Know, Rolling Stone, Really Doe, Ain’t It Funny, Golddust, White Lines, Pneumonia, Dance in the Water, When It Rain, Hell for It

Least favorite track: From the Ground
