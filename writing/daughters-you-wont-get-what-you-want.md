# Hargrove's review

Like many others, the first time I heard of Daughters was when Fantano gave this record a 10 in 2018. I never ended up really listening to it though because the cover art gave me bad vibes. Reading the insane number of Genius comments and annotations on this record, it appears to have been an entry point into noise and industrial music for a lot of people. Maybe if I had listened while I was a teenager, it would have played a similar role for me. However, listening to it now after years of experience with harsher music, as well as coming more than 230 records into this project that includes lots of experimental music, this album just falls completely flat for me. It apes the punishing rhythms of Swans without the full-blown, uncompromising commitment. It apes the pop structures of Nine Inch Nails without Reznor’s penchant for melody. It apes the downtrodden vocals of Sonic Youth and other more indie-adjacent noise rock bands without the complementary slow burn in the music. The result is an album with a mishmash of competing qualities that never quite gel, leaving it an unsatisfying listen. There are a few high points, particularly the driving songs at the beginning and the thicker atmospheres of the last two tracks. But for three-quarters of this album’s run time, I just want to be listening to literally anything else.

To start with a positive, I appreciate how “City Song” is clearly designed as an opening track for this album. There’s a long intro that takes a few minutes to fully snowball into the sound to come. I also enjoy the staticky production and the post-apocalyptic imagery in the lyrics. Unfortunately, it’s a bit too stop/start for my liking, and it never reaches a peak I’m happy with, but it’s certainly in the top half of songs on the album. “Long Road, No Turns” is another one that’s almost there but just not quite. It’s a decent proof-of-concept for merging Daughters’ brand of funhouse terror instrumental with a continuously driving pop track, but wow do the vocals bug me here. It might have made my favorites list if not for the following track, “Satan in the Wait”, which takes this exact style and brings it to its absolute height. Though the tired populist message in the lyrics makes my eyes roll, I enjoy the looming drone and the drums that crash like waves. My favorite part of the track is the cool little keyboard melody over the choruses. Now that they’ve finally established a sound I can get behind, it’s time for the next five tracks to completely suck. “The Flammable Man” is a noisier, less structured interlude, a sound that I don’t think Daughters has enough confidence to pull off. My absolute least favorite is “The Lords Song” thanks to its unbearably whiny vocals. I appreciate how “Less Sex” finally slows the album down, which was badly needed after how things had devolved into “The Lords Song”. Sadly, however, “Less Sex” just has a really weak hook and melody that drag the whole thing down into oblivion, though the horror synth in the last minute is nice. “Daughter” keeps things slow, but now with an annoying twangy country guitar and an unbearable vocal performance. “The Reason They Hate Me” picks the tempo up once again, leaving it simply forgettable instead of downright grating. Something bizarre happens with the final two tracks: both are good! I enjoy the narrative style in the lyrics of “Ocean Song”, with Marshall’s vocals reminding me of Slint or even the D Plan. It has a similar rhythmic groove as “Satan in the Wait”, the dissonant eighth note guitar barrage builds tension nicely, and the instrumental slowly picks up mass as the song goes on. It’s genuinely quite good. “Guest House” closes the album on a high note as perhaps the most intense track yet. It keeps the rhythm punishing underneath the first truly frightening vocal performance from Marshall. Finally, I feel the horror they’ve been trying to make me feel. Where was this earlier?

One metric I just records on is whether I’m excited, neutral, or reluctant to continue playing it after a few listens. For this album, I really had to force myself to hit play on my last two or three listens. I see merit in some individual tracks, but there’s just something about it I don’t vibe with at all.

Favorite tracks: Satan in the Wait, Ocean Song, Guest House

Least favorite track: The Lords Song
