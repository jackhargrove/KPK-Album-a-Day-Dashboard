# Write-up

See my above write-up on *Ziggy Stardust* for an accounting of Bowie’s early life and desperate struggle to become a rock star. See Aidan’s write-up on *Low*, the album immediately preceding *“Heroes”*, for background on the “Berlin trilogy”. To make a long story short, Bowie realized his cocaine addiction had become life-threatening around the recording of his funk rock opus*Station to Station* in 1976, so he recruited former Stooges frontman and heroin addict Iggy Pop to move to Berlin, kick their respective addictions, and make new music experimenting with brand new electronic music technology with producer Brian Eno.

Feeling inspired by his recent productivity, Bowie set out to make his second album of 1977 in Berlin. While he very much wanted to continue the musical direction of *Low*, he also hoped to include more of a rock and pop sound to expand on the austere minimalism. To that end, former (and future) King Crimson guitarist and bandleader Robert Fripp was brought in to play guitar on the album. Eno also returned as a creative collaborator, again using side 2 for ambient instrumentals. While recording *The Idiot*, Bowie had been fascinated by the way Iggy Pop had come up with his lyrics on the spot while recording on the microphone, and Bowie decided to use that improvisational approach himself on *“Heroes”* (the album’s cover is also a reference to the same painting as was *The Idiot*). The album was constructed piecemeal, with Bowie and producer Tony Visconti creating the basis of tracks sans melody, then Fripp improvising lead guitar parts over them, then finally Bowie adding his improvised vocals. Like *Low*, the music was inspired by krautrock; the album’s title comes from the NEU! track “Hero”, while “V-2 Schneider” is a reference to Kraftwerk’s Florian Schneider. As the second of the trilogy, as well as the only one recorded entirely in Berlin, *“Heroes”* is the record that most captures the sound of Bowie’s Berlin period. The studio was only 500 yards away from the Berlin wall, an oppressive presence that had a major impact on the record. On the album’s title track and lead single, “’Heroes’”, Bowie improvised a story of two lovers, one from East Berlin and one from West Berlin, dreaming of an easier life together. Though the song itself is hopeful, Bowie chose to include quotation marks in the title to undercut it with irony. Though the single was a commercial disappointment, it has slowly grown in stature over the years and is now one of Bowie’s most famous and beloved songs, often cited as among the greatest songs of all time. The album received similarly mixed reviews as *Low* at its time of release; since then, *Low* as an album has often overshadowed *“Heroes”*, but the track “’Heroes’” remains the most enduring song from the Berlin period.

Bowie wrapped up his Berlin experimentation with the world music-influenced *Lodger* in 1979. Itching to get back to reality, Bowie moved to NYC in 1980 and released *Scary Monsters (and Super Creeps)*, an album in line with the new wave of the day. Bowie became more famous than he ever had before with 1983’s *Let’s Dance*. Singles like “Let’s Dance” and “Modern Love” made one of the most iconic pop stars of the 80s, though his output for the remainder of the decade damaged his standing with critics. Though his popularity waned in the 90s, Bowie kept up with the alt rock and electronic music of the day, covering a Pixies song and collaborating with Nine Inch Nails. In 2004, Bowie had a heart attack live on stage, prompting him to retire from touring and music altogether. Many were satisfied, a conclusion to one of the longest and most accomplished careers in rock history. But in 2013, out of nowhere, Bowie surprise released the single for his first album of new music in a decade. The album was titled *The Next Day* and featured a familiar cover: it was *“Heroes”*, but with Bowie’s face blocked out by a wide box. Was Bowie back at age 66?

# Hargrove's review

To my memory, “’Heroes’” was the first Bowie song I ever fell in love with. I of course knew “Space Oddity” and “Starman” from when I was a little kid, and was introduced to “The Man Who Sold the World” during my intense 12-year-old Nirvana phase, but they were all just kind of songs I knew but didn’t think about. When I got Spotify for the first time, I think I was 15, and I added like 10 full Bowie albums to my liked songs playlist because I knew I was supposed to like Bowie. This resulted in my liked songs playlist being full of songs I didn’t recognize, effectively making it useless. But it was also how I was introduced to “’Heroes’”. It’s obviously super well-known, but it didn’t feel as ubiquitous as some of Bowie’s other hits. And, fuck, is it beautiful. The soaring guitar, the spacious synth, the hopeful-to-desperate story, and Bowie’s heartrending screams as the song crescendos. God is it a cool song. I’m glad this album is on the list just so that the track “‘Heroes’” is on it. I was less familiar with the rest of the album, but it hits a lot of the same notes as *Low*. Like, a lot of the same notes. It’s basically a more upbeat, funkier version of *Low* with one hit single and otherwise slightly worse quality. Well, I really liked *Low*. *“‘Heroes’”*, outside of “‘Heroes’”, is pretty damn solid despite not being *Low*. “Beauty and the Beast” is a super solid entry into the Bowie singles canon, “The Secret Life of Arabia” is one of my new favorite Bowie deep cuts, and “V-2 Schneider” brings back some of the cool space aesthetics of early Bowie. I thoroughly enjoyed listening to this album, even if my enjoyment always noticeably peaked with the third track. Look, it’s Bowie. It’s Bowie. It’s Bowie. He’s just so *cool*. It oozes out of every song.

The album starts strong with “Beauty and the Beast”, which has some fantastic Bowie vocal inflections. I love the way he says “gone wrong”. This track has a good mixture of manic energy with the characteristic cold delivery of the Berlin trilogy. It’s fun, it’s catchy, it’s danceable, and it signals this album will be a bit more fun than *Low*. I like the section of “Joe the Lion” that starts “It’s Monday…”. It’s the first one where Fripp’s guitar shines through. He’s such a nice addition to this album, always sounding out of sync and blurry but ultimately making everything so much dreamier. If God came down from heaven and declared “‘Heroes’” to be the best song ever written by humans, I don’t know that I would necessarily agree, but I would absolutely say “that’s fair”. It’s simply one of the greatest songs of all time. Every time I hear it, it’s like the first time all over again, a genuine marvel of a song. I can’t focus on anything else once Bowie starts yelling. No word describes this masterpiece better than “soaring”. It also balances hope with despair so masterfully. No doubt about it, this would be a desert island song pick for me. I love when Bowie plays sax, and, well, “Sons of the Silent Age” brings sax. It’s a cool deep cut, one you can tell was the only track fully written prior to recording. It’s kinda creepy, kinda catchy, and that’s what I want out of a Bowie track. “Blackout” is definitely my least favorite of the vocal tracks. It’s a bit of a mess. There’s too much going on instrumentally for the song to be this all over the place compositionally.

Moving to the instrumental side of the album, I find “V-2 Schneider” consistently pleasant. I love the off-beat sax which makes the whole track sound so trippy. The repetition of the title gets stuck in my head, and I like the space age, futuristic vibe. The descending piano line of “Sense of Doubt” sounds quite cool, but otherwise I’m a little borderline. It’s a great sound collage, but it doesn’t come together as well as the best instrumentals on *Low*. Fuck it, it sounds hard, I’m calling it a favorite. “Moss Garden” is too zen and peaceful for me. Leave it to Popol Vuh. “Neuköln” is a bit disjointed but I appreciate how aggressive it is compared to the other ambient tracks. I’m pretty neutral on it overall. When I listen ambiently, the sax at the end annoys me, but when I focus, I understand it. “The Secret Life of Arabia” is an actual hidden banger, a light at the end of the austere tunnel that was Bowie’s Berlin era. Who doesn’t want to sing along to the “secret, secret” vocals in this fun dance number? It has a funky ass bass line too. I would call it my second favorite track on the whole album after the title track. I’m really digging it.

“’Heroes’” is better than anything on *Low*, but overall this album has less aura when I listen to it in full. Still, it’s solidly above the average album we’ve listened to, even discounting the title track. I think a high A Tier placement fits.

Favorite tracks: Beauty and the Beast, Joe the Lion, Sons of the Silent Age, V-2 Schneider, Sense of Doubt, The Secret Life of Arabia

Least favorite track: Moss Garden
