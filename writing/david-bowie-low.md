# Hargrove's review

I’m not trying to be overly combative, and I’m less articulate than I typically am right now, but I just do not see how you can call this album “boring”. Putting aside the fact that “boring” is a lazy critique, there are quite a few bangers on this album. “Breaking Glass”, “What in the World”, “Sound and Vision”, and “Be My Wife” are all just upbeat pop songs, and damn good ones at that. “Speed of Life” and “A New Career in a New Town” are both carried by instrumental melodies that rival any vocal track (guitar and harmonica, respectively). And the back half presents ambient songs that are far more interesting and dynamic than ambient songs that came after. Not only a good album, but a diverse album, one that provides a deeply varied experience.

Side A and side B are very different, and I will discuss them separately. Overall, I really like the guitar melodies that structure most tracks on side A. “What in the World” is my favorite track on this side, and my favorite on the album; Bowie has this vocal line that is clearly sung, but with the cadence of spoken word, and it competes with the both the foreground guitar and the background synth in the most interesting way. It does all of this with a funky, disco feel. I’m a big fan. “Breaking Glass” opens with this catchy interplay between the guitar and the drums, and the intermittent descending synth is a personal favorite of mine. I also really like Bowie’s vocal performance here, there’s a certain hoarseness to it that makes him sound exasperated. “Sound and Vision” is just a banger, with this cascading synth like a waterfall, and there’s a nice call and response between Bowie and Bowie, high vocals to low vocals. “Be My Wife” has this super bluesy opening, and then the piano left hand is so high in the mix that it sounds mechanical, it’s really cool. the rhythm guitar in this track is also a highlight. “A New Career un a New Town” ends side A with an instrumental, but a different kind than will be coming up. I love the harmonica, which is eerily similar to the harmonica in the final track of his career, “I Can’t Give Everything Away” (NOTE: after writing this, I looked it up, and it turns out I am not the first to make this observation). If I had to choose a worst track, it would be “Always Crashing in the Same Car”, which is too lackadaisical and doesn’t develop an identity of its own. One thing I would like to note is how nearly every track is 3:00 or below; these are all fleeting pop ideas, and no track overstays its welcome.

Side B constructs an atmosphere altogether unlike side A; given my chronic fence-sitting, there’s something I appreciate about going half conventional, half experimental. Something about “Warszawa” feels futuristic to this day, with that eerie, reverberating synth never finding a moment of comfort. I also love the chord structure, and the Arabic-esque chanting. Just a composition that sounds like a cloudy, rainy day, with thunderstorms not quite active but imminent. “Art Decade” doesn’t do a ton for me, but it does sound nice. “Weeping Wall” reminds me of a personal favorite composition of mine, “Tubular Bells”, with the xylophone/vibraphone/whatever in the background. There’s a dissatisfied guitar making itself known, a synth playing a dark version of Simon & Garfunkel’s “Scarborough Fair/Canticle”, and a vibration that makes me think of my favorite Steve Reich compositions (NOTE: looked this up again and apparently neither the Simon & Garfunkel nor the Steve Reich comparisons are original; FUCK MY LIFE). “Subterraneans” starts slow, but the fucking uh grimy-ass Bill Clinton saxophone ROCKS.

This album is a banger, I’m sorry. The production is so crisp, the ideas so fleeting, the sound so varied, Bowie’s vocals so scattershot and unpredictable. While I actually really like side B, I would say that I prefer side A. I love Bowie’s absolute refusal to be categorized time and time again. I love how you can almost hear synth-pop. Maybe you just need to understand cocaine withdrawals to understand this.

Favorite tracks: Breaking Glass, What in the World, Sound and Vision, Be My Wife, A New Career in a New Town, Warszawa, Weeping Wall

Least favorite track: Always Crashing in the Same Car
