# Write-up

Deafheaven is a post-metal band formed in San Francisco in 2010 that, at the time of this album, consisted of lead vocalist George Clarke, guitarist and bassist Kerry McCoy, and drummer Daniel Tracy. Deafheaven is known for pioneering a style of post-metal called blackgaze, a fusion of black metal and shoegaze. Clarke and McCoy originally met in 9th grade, becoming friends after McCoy complimented Clarke’s Slayer t-shirt. They formed a duo in 2010 after leaving the grindcore group Rise of Caligula. The two adopted the name Deafheaven, two separate words combined into one, as an homage to seminal 90s shoegaze group Slowdive. The duo recorded a four-track demo in 2020 for just $500 and sent it to a few music blogs, generating enough buzz for them to hire guitarist Nick Bassett, bassist Derek Prine, and drummer Trevor Deschryver. By the end of the year, this quintet signed to indie label Deathwish, run by the vocalist of legendary mathcore band Converge. Deafheaven released its debut album *Roads to Judah* in 2011; it received decent critical praise, not from the metal press but instead from indie rock review sites like Pitchfork and The A.V. Club. After a year of touring, Deafheaven released a split EP with fellow San Francisco black metal group Bosse-de-Nage in 2012, covering two Mogwai songs.

By the end of 2012, Bassett, Prine, and Deschryver had all left Deafheaven on good terms, leaving only the founding members Clarke and McCoy. Along with studio drummer Daniel Stacy, the duo went to the studio to write and record Deafheaven’s second studio album, *Sunbather*. This record leaned even more into the lush shoegaze and indie rock-inflected sound Deafheaven was known for. The band focused on production much more than in previous works, using overdubbing, guitar effects pedals, and my bloody valentine’s glide guitar technique to create a bigger sound. *Sunbather* includes three interludes. While the first is purely instrumental, the second features Neige, guitarist of French black metal band Alcest, reading a passage from the novel *The Unbearable Lightness of Being*. The third samples a secret recording of opiate-addicted McCoy purchasing drugs from his dealer, as well as a shouting preacher in downtown SF. The album’s lyrics largely concern wealth inequality, with the title being inspired by the idyllic scene of a rich woman in a wealthy neighborhood sunbathing outside of her house. The color palette of the cover is inspired by the back of the eyelids when lying in the Sun. *Sunbather* released in 2013 to absurdly good reviews; aggregator Metacritic named it the most acclaimed album of the year. Once again, these reviews came from indie rock and even mainstream outlets. Many metal fans despised Deafheaven and other buzzy hipster black metal groups like Liturgy. Over time, however, many of these black metal purists have come around. The album was also a modest commercial success, reaching number 130 on the Billboard 200.

For the remainder of the 2010s decade, Deafheaven released two more albums in the style of *Sunbather* to continued critical success. In 2021, the band released its fifth album *Infinite Granite*, which abandoned metal almost entirely in favor of shoegaze and post-rock. Many fans were displeased by this development, and the group reached a creative nadir. With this feedback in mind, Deafheaven returned in 2025 with its sixth album *Lonely People with Power*. It included the hardest-hitting, most aggressive, and most black metal-oriented music the group had released at least since *Sunbather*, maybe ever. This album was fantastic and won back many fans who thought Deafheaven’s time in the Sun was over forever.

# Hargrove's review

When Aidan originally sent this list in the Cool PiKaps chat the day before we began the Album a Day thing, I said I had listened to 1/30 of the metal records. The record I had listened to was, of course, *Sunbather*. It occurred to me when I was 18 or so that I had gotten into all kinds of music but really had no idea about metal after Metallica. I looked up what metal Pitchfork liked, it recommended *Sunbather*, and that was that. I officially listened to metal. I could check that box. I listened to *Sunbather* a few times, enjoyed it, and moved on. Last year, after I enjoyed *Dopesmoker* and *The Mantle* so much early on, I saw that one of 2025’s buzziest new albums was a metal album by none other than Deafheaven. I remembered liking *Sunbather*, and I was starting to get a feel for metal, so I gave it a try and was blown away. It’s really, really, really good. I cannot recommend *Lonely People With Power* enough. It’s structured very similarly to this album, but it has an even more aggressive sound. Anyway, as of this writing I’ve officially listened to 22/30 of the metal albums on this list. That’s a 2,100% increase! Not only have I found more than a few records I like enough to listen to regularly, but I’ve also gotten a chance to really dig into the history of the genre. After all of that, I still really like *Sunbather* (and *Lonely People With Power*, which I honestly think I like more), even relative to these other fantastic albums. It’s just so damn good. For one, it’s masterfully structured. Four meaty tracks separated from each other by three genuinely inventive and thought-provoking interludes. The pacing and coherence is second-to-none. Then you have the actual sound of the record. Holy shit. It sounds as big as any shoegaze masterpiece ever has. The guitars coalesce into a warm blanket and combine with the exceptional, rapid-fire drumming to create an atmosphere so thick that only the ice-cold vocals can break through. Add to that a melancholic emo sensibility and it just works. All of it, all four main tracks, every second of this album sounds GOOD. And that’s just the baseline. The peaks kick into insane overdrive. My favorite moments on the album, the ends of “Vertigo” and “The Pecan Tree”, feel like a million orgasms at once. Amazing record.

“Dream House” begins tense, a tremolo guitar playing an unmistakably black metal chord progression. It keeps this feeling once things get loud, the blast beat drumming integrated to perfection. I also love the final section, especially when the guitar gets more indie and plays eighth notes on the same chord for two bars at a time. And that meandering guitar melody at the end! Plus, the repeated conversation “‘I’m dying.’/‘Is it blissful?’/‘It's like a dream.’/‘I want to dream.’” captures the vibe of the album perfectly. “Irresistible” is a gorgeous little interlude. I love that there are these little reprieves because they give each main track more weight. Unfortunately, however, I like all four of the main tracks, and this is the interlude that affects me least, so it’s going to least favorite track position. Thems the breaks. “Sunbather” gets more shoegazy than “Dream House” ever did, enveloping me in its warm rays. I love the bass line in the midsection, as well as how dynamic and active this track feels. My favorite mini-section is the part where the drums get harsh and metallic, the clanging reminding me of Swans’ *Filth*. “Please Remember” is one of the coolest interludes I can think of on any album. The reversed music under the book reading feels so sinister, though the French accent was already doing that on its own. I love the build into the jet engine, but not as much as I love when it gives way to delicate acoustic strumming.

“Vertigo” starts off more indie than metal for its first three minutes. I love hearing the drums go insane while everything else is so sullen and moody. Eventually it gets loud, and I’ll be honest, it drags a little bit in the midsection. However, it’s ALL worth it for the final three minutes, my single favorite part of the album. It just gets huge, with rattling guitar work droning all over. Have I mentioned how great the drums are? It’s just a crazy cathartic moment. And it of course ends with that beautiful arpeggio guitar. I love love love the final three minutes of this track. I also appreciate how it happens after the line “Focusing on light through the blinds/A slave to reality under a monarch in the sky”, really giving that recurring sun symbol a negative valence. The recording of the drug deal is so cool and is a genuinely daring thing to put on this record. For that alone, I love this interlude. It grounds the ethereal sound of this album so well. “The Pecan Tree” wastes no time getting loud; the drums set a relentlessly aggressive tone right off the bat and everything follows from there. I love the way they tease the guitar part from the final section about halfway through. And then when that final section does come in, it’s so amazing. It feels so good to scream “I am my father's son/I am no one/I cannot love/It's in my blood”.

I think this is a fantastic entry point to metal for anyone coming from an alt rock background and I can’t recommend it highly enough. Seriously, go listen.

Favorite tracks: Dream House, Sunbather, Please Remember, Vertigo, Windows, The Pecan Tree

Least favorite track: Irresistible
