# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Death Grips is an experimental hip-hop trio from sunny Sacramento, California made up of avant-garde drummer and creative leader Zach Hill, rapper MC Ride (Stefan Burnett), and producer Andy Morin.

Zach Hill made a name for himself in Northern California in the 90s and 2000s as an incredibly inventive and hardcore drummer, fronting the group Hella (and making a record in San Francisco with a very young Joanna Newsom, *Nervous Cop*). However, this didn’t translate to mainstream success, and by the late 2000s he had become homeless. In 2010 he was finally able to afford rent a suburban house in his hometown of Sacramento. One of his new neighbors, a waiter at a local restaurant named Stefan Burnett, introduced himself to Hill. Hill told Burnett that he played some pretty crazy drums. Burnett insisted that he could rap over anything, no matter how fast or unpredictable. They tested Burnett’s claim by jamming together, and found the formula that would define the band: Burnett’s aggressive rapping over Hill’s mind-melting drums. Hill recruited Andy Morin, a producer he had worked with previously, to make their beats, Burnett assumed the stage name MC Ride, and they called themselves Death Grips.

Death Grips released their first EP of 5 songs in March 2011 and started playing shows in Sacramento. A month later, the group released their debut mixtape, *Exmilitary*. Morin’s production on the album features significant sampling from famous songs and artists, including the Beastie Boys, Black Flag, Pink Floyd, and David Bowie. Because there was no way they could pay for all these samples, they were not allowed to profit off of the mixtape. However, *Exmilitary* quickly gained notoriety online, particularly its single “Guillotine”, which is to this day one of their most well-known songs. This internet stardom paved the way for a productive decade for the trio, with 6 more studio albums released from 2012-2018. This list contains 5 Death Grips projects, tied with Swans for the most of any artist. The group has laid largely dormant since 2019, with many speculating that they have broken up.

# Hargrove's review

Death Grips is one of my favorite bands of all time (Sactown 4 Lyfe), and *Exmilitary* is quite the explosive debut. They came out of the gate almost fully-formed, with the general vibe that would define their output for the rest of the decade. MC Ride’s paranoid lyricism and ultra-aggressive delivery, Zach Hill’s complex drum beats, and Andy Morin’s in-your-face production. However, while their later projects experimented with more electronic, hip-hop, and even pop sounds, *Exmilitary* keeps its sonic and aesthetic focus to punk rock. MC Ride yells over guitar riffs and bass lines that are very recognizable to those with high familiarity with 70s and 80s punk. It’s for this reason that *Exmilitary* has never been my favorite Death Grips album; I’ve always preferred the tighter pop structures of *Bottomless Pit* and *The Money Store*, or even the more refined rock work on *Jenny Death*. Despite this, list makers often give extra credit to *Exmilitary* for being their debut, the birth of their incredible decade of creativity. And it is quite good. Just not their finest.

The album’s opener begins with a speech from Charles Manson (yes, the cult leader and murderer) that sets the tone for MC Ride’s lyrics not only in this album but throughout Death Grips’ existence. Creating an almost *reductio ad absurdum* of individualism, Manson rambles about how he built his own way, discusses his extreme distrust of any authority, and generally sees no greater God than the self. MC Ride sums it up well in the chorus of the song, saying “I am the beast I worship”. None of Ride’s lyricism amounts to much on any song, by design; his protagonists are solipsistic, hedonistic, and trust no one but themselves, existing in a nightmarish world that follows no logic other than pleasure and extreme violence. Whether its drug use in “Spread Eagle Cross the Block”, sex in “I Want It I Need It (Death Heated)”, or violence in “Blood Creepin”, Ride is a slave to his primal desires. And whether its the cops in “Klink”, other rappers in “Lord of the Game”, or everyone in “Culture Shock”, Ride fucking hates everyone, and he wants them (and you) to know it. He’ll say it loudly and relentlessly until you get the message and go fuck yourself.

Morin’s production on this album is absolutely face-melting and a great sign of what to come. He’s constantly competing for your attention with Ride’s loud and manic rapping, and he holds his ground pretty damn well. My favorite beat by far is on “Culture Shock”, which could stand on its own as an industrial song were it slowed down. It’s glitchy and electronic, providing the perfect background for Ride’s ramblings on the modern world. Or there’s the insane bounce of “Guillotine” and “Takyon (Death Yon)” that turn these into the two most undeniable bangers of the entire record. He shows his mastery of guitar sampling on “Known for It” and “I Want It I Need It (Death Heated)”, making better rock music than most rock musicians. And his sampling of voices on “Lord of the Game ft. Mexican Girl”, “Klink”, and “Cut Throat (instrumental)” prove some of his most abrasive, as a human voice claims more of your attention than any instrument.

There is of course much room for improvement on this record. While Zach Hill played his role as creative director very well here, his drumming is much less exciting than it will be eventually. Some of the songs sound a little same-y, a problem that they very much fix on subsequent records. I don’t love the sequencing, as there’s somewhat of a front-loading of the bangers. And I just aesthetically prefer their later pop mode and electronic mode.

But damn if this isn’t an insane debut. “Guillotine”, “Takyon (Death Yon)”, and “Culture Shock” hold up to any song they’ve done since. If they released this today, I would feel very excited to see what they do next. Death Grips have released at least one 10/10, but unfortunately this isn’t one of them. It’s still great though.

Favorite tracks: Beware, Guillotine, Takyon (Death Yon), Klink, Culture Shock, I Want It I Need It (Death Heated), Blood Creepin

Least favorite track: Lord of the Game ft. Mexican Girl

8.5/10
