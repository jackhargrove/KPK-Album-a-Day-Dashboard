# Write-up

At long last, we have reached our fifth and final Death Grips album from the list. See above for my write-ups on the previous four DG records we listened to. Chronologically, *[REDACTED] on the Moon* falls between *No Love Deep Web* and *Steroids*. After releasing *No Love Deep Web* via self-leak in 2012, DG had been kicked off of its record label but improved its standing online. Another supporter at this time was Icelandic musician Björk, who sent the group a letter expressing admiration; in response, DG released two remixes of songs from her most recent album. DG created its own label Third Worlds in 2013 that had some loose connection with Universal Music Group. However, DG further alienated the music industry at Lollapalooza in 2013 when it didn’t show up to an after-party performance, instead putting on a playlist over a display of a fan’s suicide note and inciting a riot. As a result, Lollapalooza canceled the group’s performance the next day, as well as several other upcoming festivals. Near the end of 2013, DG released its third studio album, *Government Plates*. It continued *No Love Deep Web*’s austere, aggressive direction, focusing on hardcore EDM beats more than any other album until 2017’s *Steroids*. Actor Robert Pattinson plays guitar on the track “Birds”. The album received the group’s worst reviews to date, and is now commonly cited by fans as DG’s worst album.

Only half a year later, DG announced its next studio album *The Powers That B* in June 2014 and surprise-dropped the album’s first half, titled *[REDACTED] on the Moon*, the same day. It was released as a free download on the group’s official website. The album’s cover features MC Ride walking through Sacramento Historic City Cemetery, referenced in opener “Up My Sleeves” as “Broadway cemetery”. A sonic concept album of sorts, every track’s instrumental is composed entirely of Zach Hill’s drums and chopped up original vocal samples that Björk had recorded for the purpose of use in this album. Like *No Love Deep Web* and *Government Plates*, *[REDACTED] on the Moon* features cold, electronic hip hop production and nonstop aggression in the rapping as opposed to the rock instrumentation of *Exmilitary* or the pop hooks of *The Money Store*. The record received better reviews than *Government Plates* but some publications began to wonder whether the DG formula was getting stale.

In the announcement for *The Powers That B*, DG made it seem like the album’s second half, titled *Jenny Death*, would release imminently. However, weeks passed with no word and fans became impatient. One fan posted the question “Jenny Death when?” on 4chan’s /mu/ message board, a phrase which became a relentless meme among DG fans. This intensified the following month when DG announced that it was breaking up. “Jenny Death when?” became an article of faith predicting DG’s return. The group itself acknowledged this in early 2015 when it inevitably reunited and released the EP/mixtape *Fashion Week*, a bunch of EDM instrumentals with titles that spell out “JENNY DEATH WHEN” one by one. *Jenny Death* leaked online in March 2015 and DG responded by releasing the album officially later that day via YouTube. *Jenny Death* received rave reviews, easily the best since *The Money Store* three years earlier. Its sound notably shifts away from hardcore hip hop and back to the rap rock of *Exmilitary*, featuring guitars on most tracks, a cymbal-heavy drum kit, and some melodic singing from MC Ride. The suicide ballad “On GP” is commonly cited as the best Death Grips track, while *Jenny Death* features prominently in debates over the group’s best album. With both discs released, *The Powers That B* performed well commercially, hitting number 72 on the Billboard 200.

# Hargrove's review

After starting with *Exmilitary* almost exactly a year-and-a-half ago, we’ve finally come to our fifth and final Death Grips album on the list. I’m obviously a big Death Grips booster, both because I love aggro hip hop and also because I’ll rep Sactown until the day I die. That being said, I was a little annoyed at the time by the particular selection of Death Grips albums on the list. Obviously it had *The Money Store*, and you’ve gotta go with *Exmilitary* even though I think it’s a little bit overrated, but why *Steroids*? And *No Love Deep Web* and *[REDACTED] on the Moon* are fine, but why choose them over my favorites like *Bottomless Pit* and *Jenny Death*? I also think *Year of the Snitch* deserves more love and might have preferred to see it on the list compared to other selections. I bring all this up to point out that I think *[REDACTED] on the Moon* is a Death Grips album that I had overlooked even though I had listened to it countless times. You see, I’ve always listened to *The Powers That B* as a double album. I didn’t have separate playlists set up for *[REDACTED] on the Moon* and *Jenny Death* until just last week. I love *The Powers That B* all the way through, but my most loved tracks largely come from the *Jenny Death* half; combined with my failure to actually listen to the two albums individually, this led to me forming the half-baked opinion that *Jenny Death* was much better than *[REDACTED] on the Moon*. Listening to the latter album by itself over the last two weeks has made me realize just how well it works on its own. This album is really tight. At barely over half an hour, it doesn’t overstay its welcome for even a minute. Further, it weaves through its eight tracks so seamlessly that it resembles that megamix structure of *Steroids*. There’s not one lull, not one wasted moment on the entirety of *[REDACTED] on the Moon*. Further, the songs are damn good. The three that have always stood out to me are “Up My Sleeves”, “Billy Not Really”, and especially “Fuck Me Out”. I’ve considered that last one in particular among my favorite Death Grips songs forever. But after paying closer attention, I’ve come to appreciate “Black Quarterback”, “Say Hey Kid”, and “Big Dipper” quite a lot as well. Despite all of that, I still think *Jenny Death* is the better half of *The Powers That B* (do yourself a favor and listen to “On GP” right now). The competition is much closer now, though.

If there’s one thing I can rely on Death Grips for, it’s amazing album openers. “Up My Sleeves” does not disappoint. I love the way the beat shifts multiple times over the course of this track. In general, it feels like the beats change without care for when the tracks change on this album. The Björk clips also sound fantastic. My favorite Ride earworm from this track is “If I'm so necessary/Blank blank obituary/At Broadway cemetery”. “Billy Not Really” bangs, easily my second favorite track after “Fuck Me Out”. I adore Ride’s flow here. There are so many little vocal tics to adopt; the best is “ooh, shit, ooh, awhoo shit”. Instrumentally, I can’t get enough of the cascade of “ooh” at the end. This track has the perfect combination of beat and bars. “Black Quarterback” flows in perfectly from the previous track and starts with one of the album’s best verses. The beat sounds fucking amazing too. It has the single best Björk sample on the album. The hits keep coming with “Say Hey Kid”. “Don't it feel good to drive a bus?” is such vintage Ride. The track is not quite as good as the previous three but it’s still great. I love how it shifts gears again and again and again and again. The title phrase of “Have a Sad Cum BB” entered my lexicon as a teenager, but unfortunately the track itself does not stand up to the ones that came before it. It’s a decent instrumental, but nothing outstanding. By contrast, “Fuck Me Out” is an absolute Goddamn banger. I love this track so much. Ride sounds genuinely psychotic. It’s so challenging! The cacophony reminds me of the Dismemberment Plan’s “Girl O’Clock” in the way it aurally recreates frantic sexual obsession. The album’s only true miss comes with “Voila”. What makes *[REDACTED] on the Moon* work is the controlled chaos, how smoothly every section runs into the next despite the calamity of it all. “Voila” loses the thread and just descends into unpleasantness. I mostly blame Ride’s disjointed flows for this. “Big Dipper” might not be the most memorable Death Grips track on its own, but it’s a fantastic closer. It’s got a catchy hook and an unreal drum solo to close it out. “It's my pyre, agoraphobe if I want to, afford to be consumed/This pyre's my costume; get too close, it'll lick you/Like it likes you, 'cause it loathes you even more than I do” are also amazing closing lines. Plus, it works really well as a bridge into the start of *Jenny Death*.

*[REDACTED] on the Moon* is obviously better than *Steroids* and doesn’t really stand much of a chance next to *Exmilitary* or *The Money Store*. Its nearest competition among the other Death Grips albums on this list is *No Love Deep Web*. And after giving it some thought, they’re truly neck and neck. *No Love Deep Web* is bloated, especially with filler in the final third, but man are its highs higher. I think I have to give the slightest of edges to this album, but it’s damn close. My final Death Grips album ranking for this list is thus *The Money Store* (S Tier) > *Exmilitary* (A Tier) > *[REDACTED] on the Moon* (A Tier) > *No Love Deep Web* (A Tier) > *Steroids* (D Tier).

Favorite tracks: Up My Sleeves, Billy Not Really, Black Quarterback, Say Hey Kid, Fuck Me Out, Big Dipper

Least favorite track: Voila

# Aidan's review

This comes in around Steroids as my two favorite Death Grips album (projects?) so far if only for how much more I enjoyed MC Ride's vocals on the songs with less firm backing. His voice has always been powerful, but this is the first time I felt like it's been able to drive a strong beat. On The Money Store and No Love Deep Web, I felt that whenever the momentum dipped beneath "blistering", his yelling style lacked the percussiveness to drive a beat. The section on the back half of "Billy Not Really" when the beat fades to a singular snare and a one-two beat that sounds oddly like horses. Ride just stays in the pocket as he rattles off "No hand in sea of pearls/Buried in frozen Jheri curls" and "Shocker glares/Zulu somnambulic stare". The Bjork sampling is a cool fun fact but it's not really changing the establish DG formula. There's not any moments as infectiously energetic as "Get Got" or any lyrics I liked as much as "Artificial Death in the West". Still, it's a tight 30 minutes without the lowlights that marred the two other full length DG albums we've done and the best MC Ride album in my book.
