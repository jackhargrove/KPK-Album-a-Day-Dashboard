# Write-up

See above for a more comprehensive overview of Death Grips’ origins. Briefly, Death Grips is a hip hop group from Sacramento formed in 2010 composed of rapper MC Ride (real name Stefan Burnett), avant-garde drummer Zach Hill, and producer Andy Morin. They helped popularize the genre of industrial hip hop in the early 2010s, a defining style of hip hop in that decade. They released their debut mixtape *Exmilitary* in 2011, which we listened to back in February. It was a highly aggressive rap/rock fusion record that got huge online. It exemplifies what I refer to as DG’s “rock” mode, one of their three major styles in a system I made up. Because we’re going out of order, we have to skip over their debut full-length album *The Money Store* for now. What you need to know for chronology is that they signed to major label Epic Records in early 2012 and shortly thereafter released *The Money Store* to wide acclaim and #130 on the Billboard 200 album chart. It exemplifies what I refer to as DG’s “pop” mode, their second of three major styles. We’ll discuss it more when we listen to it.

The members of DG were having a tough time adapting to their success after *The Money Store*. They had never exactly been the type for “rules” or “expectations”. They announced international tour dates in April 2012, but at the end of the month decided to cancel the whole thing to immediately record another record. They holed themselves up at Hill’s house in Sacramento from May until August to create the album they called *No Love*. They formally announced in August (through *Pitchfork* for some reason) that it would be titled *No Love Deep Web*. Fitting with their popularity on the Internet, the release date was announced via an online alternate reality game: October 23, 2012. The record was ready, and fans were excited. However, Epic Records told the members of DG that they wouldn’t release the record until 2013. It was still too early, they could continue to make money off of *The Money Store*. DG was enraged by this. On September 30, they announced on social media that their label was delaying the completed album. The next day, DG uploaded the entire album for free on Twitter with an uncensored photo of Zach Hill’s erect penis with the album title written on it in sharpie as the cover (a censored version with a big black bar has also become iconic; my dad has both on vinyl). It would be downloaded millions of times before the end of the day. Needless to say, Epic Records was pissed that DG had leaked their own music, and they dropped them from the label by November.

Musically, *No Love Deep Web* establishes the third and final DG mode in my categorization: their “hip hop” mode. Yes, all of their albums are hip hop, but *No Love Deep Web* is a pure, hardcore representation of the genre. All subsequent albums fall into either their “rock”, “pop”, or” hip hop” mode. Coincidentally, the closer “Aritificial Death in the West” features a sample from “Being Sucked in Again” off of *Charis Missing* by Wire, which we listened to last week. The album received decent reviews, but decidedly not as positive as *The Money Store*. There are three more DG albums on our list: *The Money Store*, which came immediately before this record, and *[REDACTED] on the Moon* and *Steroids* which came after. DG’s story will continue in the next record we listen to by them.

# Hargrove's review

We have avoided a second DG album for an insanely long time given they have five on here, but it’s come all the same. I’m actually not crazy about the DG selection on here, despite how many there are. My favorite DG album *Bottomless Pit* didn’t make it, nor did my third favorite *Jenny Death*, while a mixtape I haven’t heard before in *Steroids* made it on, as did one of their most uneven albums in *[REDACTED] on the Moon*. And then there’s *No Love Deep Web*. This has been an album that’s vexed me for a long time. I would typically put it right about smack dab in the middle when ranking DG albums, but it’s hard to put into words exactly how I feel about it. “Come Up And Get Me”, “No Love”, and “Artificial Death In The West” are three of the best songs they ever did. There are some other decent tracks on here too, particularly near the beginning. But it was their second album in a year and made on such a short timeframe and I think it leaves something to be desired in the songwriting department. There are some forgettable tracks on here, which is disappointing coming after an album like *The Money Store* where every track has a hook that sticks in your head. Atmospherically, this album is stripped-back, almost minimalist. There’s very little fancy in any of these songs. I respect this on an artistic level, but I tend to prefer DG’s busier works. Like I said, I just don’t know. This is by far the hardest DG album for me to give a definitive opinion on.

“Come Up And Get Me” is one of my favorite DG songs. Ride’s delivery is, frankly, unbelievable. I don’t know anyone else who can paint a picture of mental illness and drug overdose so vividly. It’s hellish. The beat is so off that you never get used to it. Fantastic song. “Lil Boy” is my least favorite of the bangers on the first half, but it’s still a banger. It’s the first that shows the weakness in the songwriting department, but it makes up for it with interesting textures. “No Love” is also one of my favorite DG songs. Incredible lyrics, incredible vocal delivery, incredible bass, incredible drums. It is a harrowing song, both instrumentally and lyrically. It’s positively apocalyptic. It’s a shining example of the good DG can do with the stripped-back sound on this album. “Black Dice” is good in a *The Money Store* kind of way, with an instrumental hook catchier than most vocal hooks. “World of Dogs” is another banger thanks to the jazziness of the instrumental around the hook. Zach Hill is a saint. I also really like it lyrically (“It’s all suicide”). “Lock Your Doors” closes the string of bangers on a high note. “I got some shit to say just for the fuck of it” is a total bar. The bass synth is sweeping and cinematic, it feels like I’m in an empty post-apocalyptic hellscape ravaged by heavy winds.

“Whammy” is where the album really starts to suffer from lack of ideas. It’s still quite enjoyable, but things begin to feel a little more formulaic and a little less memorable. “Hunger Games” in particular leaves very little impression other than Hill’s drumming. “Deep Web” also, despite a Herculean vocal delivery from Ride. I have had a love/hate relationship with “Stockton” for some time because the chorus is so catchy and because Stockton is near Sacramento. Man that chorus is fucking annoying though. And they say it so many times. Not my favorite this time around. “Pop” is decent, a replacement level track. I particularly like the stuttering and fluttering electronics. “Bass Rattle Stars Out The Sky” has historically been a song I really liked, but I wasn’t feeling it this time out. It doesn’t bring enough new to the table so deep into the album. “Artificial Death In The West” though closes things on a high and weirdly cerebral note. The lyrics are so cryptic and enticing. It feels like Ride has some key to understanding the incomprehensible and he’s lording it over me. I also respect how they pull off this long, slow track perfectly.

This album has some of their best work, but they didn’t have enough ideas to sustain an entire album. It’s also stylistically not my preferred mode for DG. Their insane output would finally take its toll by the following year’s *Government Plates*, but you can start to see the cracks here. That being said, how can I rate an album with “No Love” poorly? You see my conundrum.

Favorite tracks: Come Up And Get Me, Lil Boy, No Love, Black Dice, World of Dogs, Lock Your Doors, Artificial Death in the West

Least favorite track: Stockton

# Aidan's review

I remember listening to Money Store, finding it cool and then reading about this album and thinking of how good of an idea it sounded like. MC Ride rapping about paranoia over even more futuristic beats? Sign me up. It lives up to the promise at times. Artificial Death In The West is one of my favorite songs although it's not super similar to the rest of the album. MC Ride might be the only person on the planet who could say "she shoot pussy through your chest you die" without sounding goofy at all. The first 5 songs all also manage to deliver and the beats on No Love and World of Dogs are particularly good, but the minimalism of the beats holds back the album as it goes on. When Ride's not relentlessly driven by a beat, I'm not as entranced by his howling, and songs like Hunger Games and Stockton feel meandering by comparison to the earlier songs on the album. Death Grips has always stood out to me for their energy, and while this album delivers at times and shows impressive range with its closer, it doesn't deliver on their strengths as much as other albums like The Money Store and its minimalistic style prevents it from having the style of Exmilitary.
