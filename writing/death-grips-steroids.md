# Write-up

**Death Grips - *Steroids (Crouching Tiger Hidden Gabber Megamix)***

Unfortunately we are doing these Death Grips albums in a weird order, but I'm going to follow it and do these write-ups in non-chronological order. This is the third DG album we've listened to out of five total on the list and the final one chronologically, coming out in 2017. It's the only one of their releases on the list to be an EP, the only one in the Electronic section, and the only one that I had not listened to. One of the two remaining DG albums on our list is *[REDACTED] on the Moon*, and that album's write-up will conclude with the release of DG's fourth album *The Powers That B* in 2015. Still riding out the peak of their success, DG released their fifth studio album *Bottomless Pit* in 2016, returning to DG's "pop" mode the first time since *The Money Store* in 2012. *Bottomless Pit* is my personal favorite DG album; it has some of their most abrasive and aggressive sounds ever, but the songwriting is so tight and catchy. I wish it were on this list. A month before the release of *Bottomless Pit*, DG surprise dropped their second EP (their first was their self-titled debut in 2011) *Interview 2016* on YouTube. It marked their first full exploration of EDM and IDM (although their third and worst album *Government Plates* in 2013 flirted with them as well).

They continued this style with their third EP, 2017's *Steroids (Crouching Tiger Hidden Gabber Megamix)*. A megamix is a type of remix that includes shortened versions of songs spliced together and playing one after another, though the 8 songs on *Steroids* had not been previously released. It was released on Facebook in the same post that announced their sixth studio album *Year of the Snitch*. The EP's title references gabber, a style of EDM that developed in the Netherlands in the early 90s. Gabber developed out of techno, rejecting the "excesses" of the genre by opting for a simple, stripped-back sound and very high tempos. Gabber was one of the early styles of what would eventually be called "hardcore" (not related to hardcore punk), an umbrella genre for fast and aggressive EDM of various styles. Unfortunately, gabber became popular with neo-Nazis in Germany in the late 90s; even though this was only a tiny part of the overall scene, similar to Nazism in black metal, it killed the style by the early 2000s. Thanks to MC Ride's signature style of scream-rapping, *Steroids* is considered a work of digital hardcore, a genre that combines hardcore punk vocals with hardcore EDM instrumentals. Hardcore squared. *Steroids* was re-released on vinyl in 2019 with two additional tracks on side 2, including one featuring legendary alternative metal bassist Les Claypool of Primus.

DG released their sixth album *Year of the Snitch* in 2018. Though a few tracks (particularly the great "Death Grips Is Online") still maintained the EDM sounds from their last two EPs, the album returned to the "rock" mode of *Exmilitary* and *Jenny Death*. In 2019, DG self-released a fourth EP *Gmail and the Restraining Orders*, which was just one experimental and improvised noise track for 28 minutes. DG had released at least one original piece of music in every year from 2011 to 2019; they would not release another until 2023. Even then, the only releases were two tracks found in the server files of the DG website. They did start touring again in 2023, though producer Andy "Flatlander" Morin unexpectedly went on hiatus in July. Earlier this year, there was some drama; a leaked DM from Morin in February said DG had broken up, but in April DG posted they were still together but with no Morin. This has led to much speculation as to what Morin did to get kicked out of the band. Just last week, drummer Zach Hill accidentally posted that the first new DG album in 7 years is coming in December and will be titled *Death Grips Is Online*. However, he quickly deleted it, and these guys are famously unreliable. What will their future hold? Only time will tell.

# Hargrove's review

Being the only Death Grips release on this list I hadn’t heard, I was looking forward to this one. I was also intrigued by its inclusion in the electronic section. It seems to me like they included it as a representative from EDM’s hardcore subgenre since it otherwise doesn’t have any classic albums. Overall, I thought it was fine. It’s pretty standard DG fare. Ride screams incomprehensible and paranoid lyrics, the beats aren’t all that much faster or more electronic than usual, and it’s a complete sonic assault. However, I wish it had more of the brilliant songwriting of *Exmilitary*, *The Money Store*, *Jenny Death*, or *Bottomless Pit*. This megamix was a cool way to get a few throwaways out the door, but it doesn’t change what they are at their core: throwaways. There are a few sections that I could see being fleshed out into full songs, namely “Bald-Head Girl” and the final two, but otherwise this EP suffers from the component parts not being all that gripping. It’s also very short, something I’ve docked releases on this list for in the past. I think there’s nothing wrong with short releases like EPs, in fact many of them are very very good, but it just feels wrong ranking them alongside full-length albums. I also struggle with the fact that this record takes up space on the list. There are multiple other DG records I would’ve preferred to have on here, particularly *Bottomless Pit* or *Jenny Death*. Beyond that, this is their fifth record, so it also might have been good to give this slot to a missing artist like Kraftwerk. Still, I shouldn’t be too harsh. It’s not bad for what it is, and it has some really great sections. It’s fine.

Section 1 (My Whole Life) starts things off kind of weakly. It reminds me of “No Love” but way worse. I like the “my whole life” refrain, but I am just not crazy about the instrumental. Section 2 (Bitch) is a definite improvement, though still not quite there. I like how abrasive the intro is, as well as the line “That bitch more ate up than my lunch”. Section 3 (Rites of Passage) is where things really get going. That ascending electronic line is fantastic. Section 4 (Bald-Head Girl) is the first true peak of the megamix. That electronic line wriggles like an eel. The refrain is great too, and I like lines like “Bathe in you like Bathory” and “Break you like a Franklin, spend you like a five”. Section 5 (Come and Go) is still good, but not quite as good as the last two. That two note synth line is just relentless. For whatever reason, I don’t like “You’re sleeping on a gold mine”, but otherwise the refrain is one of my favorites. Section 6 (Sick Like) is a bit of a trough in quality for this part of the record. It’s got classic DG abrasiveness, but doesn’t do anything special. Section 7 (Black Body), on the other hand, is my favorite section. I absolutely love the instrumental under the refrain. It sounds so cinematic and sad. It’s haunting. Part 8 (And I Move With It) ends things on a really high note. It’s a nice climax of the sounds we’ve heard so far that gets really loud and abrasive.

There were some cool sounds, there were some tired sounds. There were, comparatively speaking, not that many sounds. I could take or leave it, but it’s enjoyable to listen to.

Favorite sections: Section 3 (Rites of Passage), Part 4 (Bald-Head Girl), Part 5 (Come and Go), Section 7 (Black Body), Section 8 (And I Move With It)

Least favorite section: Section 1 (My Whole Life)
