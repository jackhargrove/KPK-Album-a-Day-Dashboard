# Write-up

See above for my three previous Death Grips write-ups. As we have already listened to the albums immediately preceding and following this one, this write-up will narrowly focus on *The Money Store*. And that might not be such a bad thing, as *The Money Store* was the record that really put DG on the map and is still often considered the group’s masterpiece. After generating much internet buzz with the 2011 debut mixtape *Exmilitary*, DG signed to major label Epic Records (a Sony subsidiary) in early 2012. Given DG’s uncommercial sound and independent punk flair. Many were surprised to see them sign to a major label so quickly. However, Epic agreed to give DG creative freedom over output, and in fact most of the material for *The Money Store* had already been recorded anyway (plus, as we now know, this deal didn’t last very long). Like other DG albums, the beats all combine Hill’s live drumming with Morin’s studio synth production. While this album does not recognizably sample major rock hits like*Exmilitary* did, strange found sounds are sampled, most notably tennis grunts from Serena and Venus Williams. Lyrically, MC Ride’s image is most defined by *The Money Store*, with the refrain “I stay noided” from “I’ve Seen Footage” becoming the group’s unofficial catchphrase (“noided” = “paranoid”). The album was a surprise commercial and critical success, hitting #130 on the Billboard Top 200 and receiving rave reviews from the indie blogosphere a la Pitchfork. A fledgling Anthony Fantano gave *The Money Store*his first perfect 10 on his YouTube channel and would later name “Hacker” the #1 song of the 2010 decade. “Get Got”, “I’ve Seen Footage”, and “Hacker” all remain among DG’s most famous tracks. Almost immediately after releasing *The Money Store*, the group moved on to the *No Love Deep Web* album cycle, which you can read about in my above write-up for that record.

# Hargrove's review

I STAY NOIDED. This was my first Death Grips album, as was true for a lot of people. Has any other band this challenging ever put out an album so tailor-made to recruit new fans? Maybe *Merriweather Post Pavilion*. While *The Money Store* hasn’t remained my favorite DG record (that would be *Bottomless Pit*, which takes the same formula of nonstop pop bangers and somehow makes it even better), it’s undeniably up there. Even if it’s not your thing, you have to take a step back and appreciate *The Money Store* for the accomplishment that it is. The three-minute hip hop bangers just. Do. Not. Stop. Coming. Every track has a synth line, a drum beat, flowy verses, and a catchy hook. That’s it. Plug and play. It’s such a simple pop rap playbook. And yet, no track sounds like any other pop rap song you’ve ever heard, nor do they even sound overly much like each other. It plays like a greatest hits compilation, with the sleekness of the setup counterbalanced by the insanity of the actual noises. Especially given the sheer volume of music DG released in the first half of the 2010s, you just have to marvel at the unrivaled burst of creativity. So many of the DG songs I listen to on a regular basis come from this album: “Get Got”, “The Fever (Aye Aye)”, “Hustle Bones”, “I’ve Seen Footage”, etc. It’s endlessly replayable. If you don’t “get” Death Grips, just listen to this album. It’s a showcase of what they can do when everything goes right.

The album begins with its absolute best track in “Get Got”, truly one of the greatest hip hop songs ever. I love Ride’s downcast inflection as he rattles off lyrics like a tongue twister. The synth sounds positively deranged, pulling you into the fucked up world of the lyrics so perfectly. And the hook is Goddamn catchy! It’s an amazing, amazing track. The music takes a more literally industrial turn with “The Fever (Aye Aye)”, where factory machinery crescendos into a cold, inhuman track. Morin’s synths steal the show for me here, so simple and repetitive yet so distinctive. “Lost Boys” turns a classic DG vocal sample flip and seriously syncopated drumming from Hill to create a great track. I appreciate how “Blackjack” is so much sparser than the tracks that come before and after it. It’s a small reduction in energy that plays well for the album’s pacing. I also love the incoherent effect on Ride’s vocals. “Hustle Bones” is the biggest BANGER since “Get Got”. The instrumental sounds like a nonstop accelerating sports car as Ride flows hypnotically for three whole minutes. Not only does it include a catchy hook, but one that’s incredibly fun to sing along to. Everything I just said about “Hustle Bones” applies to “I’ve Seen Footage”, perhaps even more intensely. If you’ve only ever heard one DG song, it’s probably this one. I love the lyrical symmetry it creates between the brutal hell of street life and the lawlessness of the internet. And how did DG ever sound this poppy? It’s crazy how good and tight the songwriting is. Another all-time track. “Double Helix” keeps things moving with a vocal sample flip and a crazy drum beat. It’s one of the most *Exmilitary* sounding tracks because the synth is less prominent. I had also never noticed the Beatles reference in “Blue jay way, don't belong” before. I adore the booming bass drum and sheer power of Ride’s delivery in “System Blower”. It’s another one that’s a joy to rap along too.

“The Cage” has one of the best synth lines on the album, so hectic and frantic that it actually puts me on edge. The line “Yeah, I got that man, fo' show/But tell 'em why you had to get 'em though” always gets stuck in my head, though I don’t really know what it means. The sped up Arabic sample at the beginning of “Punk Weight” is quite catchy. It also has a super fucked up bouncy synth that turns everything into chaos which, combined with the great flow and hook, makes it a great track. Unfortunately, “Fuck That” is the album’s only complete whiff for me. The tribal instrumental and Ride’s anti-rhythmic flow just never come together, making it sound like a mess. That’s a serious issue for an album otherwise so tight and clean! I’m not crazy about “Bitch Please” either, though it’s leagues better than “Fuck That”. Sure, the chorus is catchy, but it’s also stretched-out and incessant. I don’t know, I don’t hate it, but I don’t love it. “Hacker” closes the album perfectly, distilling the theme of internet wasteland and the sound of the album to great effect. It’s filled to the brim with memorable and quotable lines, including: Goin' back to Tangier with some Jordans and a spear”, “Gaga can't handle this shit, headed for the Sammy Davis wing” “When you come out, your shit is gone”, “I'm in your area”, “You'll catch a JPEG to the head, über reach/You're an intern, I'm Wikileaks”, “My existence is a momentary lapse of reason”, “Prodigal, fuck that, nautical/Teachin' bitches how to swim”, “The table's flipped, now we got all the coconuts, bitch”, “The cray-cray ultra-contrarian/Havin' conversations with your car alarm”, “Soon your crew will be serving sandwiches named after me/Vietnamese style, fool, please”, and “You speak of us in certain circles, you will be dethroned or detained”. It captures what the 2010s internet felt like so well with so many attention-grabbing things going on and the constant dynamic shifting.

This is definitely my favorite of the DG albums on this list. As I said above, *Bottomless Pit* is my favorite, and *Jenny Death* would be in competition for the number two spot. However, neither is on here, so as it stands: *The Money Store* > *Exmilitary* > *No Love Deep Web* > *Steroids*.

Favorite tracks: Get Got, The Fever (Aye Aye), Lost Boys, Blackjack, Hustle Bones, I’ve Seen Footage, Double Helix, System Blower, The Cage, Punk Weight, Hacker

Least favorite track: Fuck That

# Aidan's review

This albums opens and closes with so much energy. "Get Got" and "Hacker" are the best songs on this album (with "I've Seen Footage" and "The Fever(Aye Aye)" close behind), and they just explode with energy. These 4 songs just do everything right. They have wild beats (I find it difficult to intuitively distinguish between Morin's production and Hill's drumming) that somehow work, endlessly repeatable choruses and energy for days. The rest of the album often misses one or both of the former two. The incessant energy grates towards the middle but the album closes with its most creative song in "Hacker", which boasts the most interesting lyrics and beat on the entire album, even if the chorus is not as catch as some of its peers. Even on the back half, individual songs have their moments, I like the beat on "Cage" and the Latin music sample on "Punk Weight", but Ride's pounding delivery too often sounds the same because the beats are too exotic to feel integrated into the lyrical style. Good bits are exemplary and the bad bits are creative enough for me to move this up a half tier. Not always my favorite album, but there's few records that can claim to match its energy and that alone has drawn me back to it from time to time.
