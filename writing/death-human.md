# Write-up

Death was a death metal band formed in Altamonte Springs, Florida, outside Orlando in 1983 whose lineup at the time of this record consisted of vocalist, guitarist, and songwriter Chuck Schuldiner, guitarist Paul Masvidal, bassist Steve Di Giorgio, and drummer Sean Reinert. They helped establish death metal as a genre, as well as pioneering the subgenre technical death metal. Death was founded with the name Mantas by Schuldiner with vocalist Kam Lee and guitarist Rick Rozz as one of the first bands in what would become the prominent Florida death metal scene, counterparts to Possessed in San Francisco. The band assumed the name Death in 1984 after the death of Schuldiner’s brother. Death’s lineup was highly unstable, with Schuldiner the only consistent member. He briefly moved to San Francisco, ground zero for the burgeoning thrash metal scene, as well as Canada before settling back in his home state of Florida. Death signed to an indie label in 1986 and released its debut album *Scream Bloody Gore* in 1987. Recorded in LA by Possessed’s producer, it is often considered the first “true” death metal record, adding growled vocals, blast beats, and a slasher film brutality to the intensity of thrash. The band released two more albums with a revolving door lineup before a legal dispute in 1990 resulted in Schuldiner firing the rest of the band and only working with session musicians going forward.

The first record with this new setup was 1991’s *Human*, assembling an all-star cast of death metal pioneers from Florida and San Francisco. This higher level of skill and musicianship allowed Death to employ progressive song structures and complex arrangements, separating death metal further from its thrash roots. The album was a success, selling 94,000 copies and even getting MTV airplay with the video for “Lack of Comprehension”. It also received high critical acclaim, separating “technical death metal” from the mainline gore and brutality of artists like Cannibal Corpse.

Death continued until 2001, with only a brief hiatus from 1995-1997. Death released three additional albums after *Human*, each with a different lineup of musicians around Schuldiner; its sixth, *Symbolic*, is often considered its masterpiece. Sadly, Schuldiner was diagnosed with brain cancer in 1999 and, although his prognosis had some hope, he died of pneumonia contracted while weakened in 2001. He was 34 years old. Death died along with Schuldiner in 2001, though its many former members have taken part in some of the most acclaimed metal bands since.

Though the genre does not actually take its name from Death, the band was highly influential in crystallizing the sound of death metal. In the late 70s and early 80s, British bands like Motörhead, Iron Maiden, and Venom updated the original heavy metal sound with the speed and attitude of punk rock, with the latter two groups part of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal (NWOBHM) movement. This inspired thrash metal in the US, which combined NWOBHM with the speed and aggression of hardcore punk. Metallica, Megadeth, Slayer (all San Francisco area), and Anthrax (NYC) are among the most notable thrash groups. Further “extreme metal” styles largely take thrash metal as a template. In the case of death metal, SF-based Possessed and Orlando-based Death expanded on the over-the-top violent style and evil lyrics of Venom and Slayer, introducing growled vocals, faster kick drums, and sudden tempo changes. Death metal really took off in the early 90s and is perhaps the most recognizable extreme metal style today. Death helped establish Florida as a breeding ground for death metal, with Tampa becoming the unofficial capital of the genre thanks to death metal pioneers Morbid Angel. Death metal began its decline in the late 90s when the mainstream began to move on from the underground, and when Norwegian black metal became the style of choice for the underground.

# Hargrove's review

Holy FUCK this album kicks ass. What a shot of adrenaline straight to the dome. Slayer is my favorite of the big four thrash metal bands, and Death takes that vibe to the level of intensity I’ve always wanted. I liked this album so much that I listened to it 12 times this week, plus four listens for Death’s sixth album *Symbolic* which is also insane, plus one listen for Death’s second album *Leprosy*, and even two listens to Cryptopsy’s *None So Vile* just to compare Death with other acclaimed death metal to see if it holds up (it does). Super, super, super cool album. The first six (!) tracks are back-to-back bangers, then “Cosmic Sea” takes you to uncharted, progressive territory. Yeah, those first six are quite same-y and all follow more or less the same structure, but that structure Goddamn works, and this album is so short that it doesn’t wear out for me. The closer, “Vacant Planets”, returns to the style of the first six, but not quite as good, so it’s my least favorite. Until then, though, it’s just straight, relentless, nonstop heat. We’ve heard lots of black metal albums that make liberal use of blast beats, and I’ve been mostly neutral on them. They don’t do a ton for me, but I recognize that they’re a staple of the genre and don’t mind. Here, however, the blast beat drumming becomes a complete highlight for me. I don’t know what it is exactly, whether it’s a production technique, a superior drummer, or just a more complementary style of music, but the drumming on this album sounds amazing. My personal theory is that the blast beats are mixed lower here than in a lot of other extreme metal, creating a delightful thud instead of a choppy attack. Whatever it is, it creates the perfect basis for everything else. I really like the progressive song structures because they break up the more monotonous elements of metal, like repeated riffs or lackluster vocal melodies. Speaking of vocals, I like that Schuldiner’s are more clean than they are just plain death growl. However, his vocals are a little choppy sometimes, partly because of the way he sings and partly because his lyrical phrasing is just a bit clunky. The vocals aren’t bad per se, just the album’s weakest element. But overall, I had a lot of fun with this album. It’s definitely entering my workout album pantheon.

“Flattening of Emotions” starts with a sick, thudding drum intro before introducing this anxiety-inducing guitar riff. Then, all of a sudden, everything goes double speed, initiating you into the true pace of Death. I love the chorus sections with the sparser guitar accompaniment. It’s a perfect opener that sets the bar high. I appreciate the earnestness of the pro-euthanasia lyrics in “Suicide Machine”, a track that keeps the musical momentum going. The highlight for me is the Baroque-sounding riff that comes in at parts, as well as the mix of skank beat and blast beat sections. This track sounds like it’s shifting gears nonstop and I love it. “Together as One” sounds a lot like (and predates) my favorite guitar riff from Burzum’s *Filosofem* in the track “Jesus’ Tod”. It’s just an amazing fucking riff, sounding so anxious and unsettled. The chorus vocals on this track are also my favorites so far in the album. And God are the drums insane. The hits keep coming with “Secret Face” which, although the transitions are a bit choppy, the fantastic drums more than make up for it. There’s also an insane guitar instrumental. The album peaks with “Lack of Comprehension” thanks to its awesome slowed-down intro, perfect drumming, mini guitar solos, and the catchiest verses yet. The drums and guitar work together so perfectly on this track. “See Through Dreams” is my least favorite of the initial six-track run, though it’s still quite good. It just has the choppy transitions of “Secret Face” but without other elements that fully make up for it. I don’t love the weird off-beat clicking sounds at the start of “Cosmic Sea”, but after that this track becomes really, really cool. It’s an awesome montage of shockingly diverse sounds. Not only does it elevate the album as a whole, but it just sounds great on its own too. A super progressive track for an already quite progressive record. “Vacant Planets” returns to the formula of the first six, but for one it’s not quite as good as the others, and for two it feels weird to go back to that formula after the excursion that was “Cosmic Sea”. It still has good elements, particularly the guitar part in the midsection. As a closer, however, it stumbles instead of sticking the landing.

I’m going to put this in high A for now so that my opinion has time to settle, but I very much see myself listening to this in the coming weeks regularly and eventually moving it up to S. I just need to figure out whether I’m stoked on this album specifically, or just floored by a style that’s new to me.

Favorite tracks: Flattening of Emotions, Suicide Machine, Together as One, Secret Face, Lack of Comprehension, Cosmic Sea

Least favorite track: Vacant Planets
