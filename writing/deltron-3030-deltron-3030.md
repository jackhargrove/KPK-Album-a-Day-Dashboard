# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Deltron 3030 was a musical project consisting of rapper Del tha Funky Homosapien a.k.a. Deltron Zero (Teren Delvon Jones), producer Dan the Automator (Dan Nakamura), and DJ and turntablist Kid Koala (Eric Yick Keung San). Del the Funky Homosapien was born and raised in beautiful Oakland, California, and is rapper Ice Cube’s cousin. He began his career as the principal lyricist for Ice Cube’s backing group Da Lench Mob before breaking creative ties with Ice Cube in the mid 90s. After getting dropped by his major label, Del started his own label Hieroglyphics Imperium Recordings in 1998, featuring other alternative rappers. Dan the Automator is a native of San Francisco, California, whose parents were placed in Japanese internment camps during WWII. He began producing hip hop records as a teenager, and first gained prominence for producing beats for alternative rapper Kool Keith. Kid Koala was born and raised in Montreal, Canada, and became well-known for his avant-garde turntablism techniques, where he would scratch records featuring unorthodox sounds. In his solo capacity, he toured with and opened for artists like Radiohead and Björk. These three eclectic hip hop artists, already somewhat successful on their own, united to make one of the genre’s most bizarre records in 2000.

*Deltron 3030* is a hip hopera that tells the story of a former mech soldier in the year 3030 who becomes disillusioned with the interplanetary government and takes them down by rediscovering the ancient art of hip hop. Or something like that. Despite the record’s grand scope, it never takes itself too seriously to be concerned with having a “chronological” or “consistent” plot. The record features spoken word features from musical collaborators including Damon Albarn (previously of Blur, soon to be of Gorillaz), Sean Ono Lennon (son of John Lennon and Yoko Ono), Brad Roberts (lead singer of Crash Test Dummies), and others. Damon Albarn in particular is featured on three tracks, and he would repay the favor a couple years later when he started his new project Gorillaz; Del the Funky Homosapien is the featured rapper on “Clint Eastwood”, Gorillaz’ breakout single and a song I’m certain you’ve all heard.

Similar to emo, I’d like to give a quick history of alternative hip hop. During hip hop’s golden age in mid-1980s New York, there were a few hip hop artists in the outer boroughs that were making music with broader influences than the mainstream (jazz and funk, in particular) and were writing more complex lyrics than the mainstream. This includes the collective Native Tongues, groups like De La Soul (have an album on the list) and A Tribe Called Quest (genuine travesty that they don’t have an album on the list), and solo artists like Queen Latifah. In the 90s, you had alternative hip hop scenes in California and the South, with the Pharcyde and Del tha Funky Homosapien making waves in the former and Outkast getting big in the latter. However, alternative hip hop was never really able to gain a commercial foothold (unlike its counterpart, alternative rock) because of the dominance of gangsta rap and later hardcore rap. Alternative rap groups kept plugging away though, with Kool Keith’s *Dr. Octagonecologist* in 1996 and Deltron 3030’s self-titled album in 2000 earning cult followings, and Outkast breaking through to the mainstream with *Speakerboxxx/The Love Below* in 2004. Also in 2004, rap duo Madvillain (collaboration between MF Doom and Madlib) released their seminal album *Madvillainy*, setting a high water mark for alternative rap that many still think is its greatest achievement. By 2007, Kanye West had achieved great popularity, with his unorthodox production and decidedly *not* gangsta lyrics spurring many to call his rise to fame a victory of alternative rap over the mainstream.

# Hargrove's review

Another album that my dad loves so I had heard of it but I had never listened to it. I was already a huge fan of other alternative rap; A Tribe Called Quest, De La Soul, the Pharcyde, and Madvillain are among my favorite rap groups. Our second album from NorCal, we’re a deeply musically talented bunch. The concept of this album is something I could really see myself hating, but thankfully it’s presented with the right amount of levity and jokes to not come across as self-serious or overly ambitious. I didn’t love Del’s style on first listen, it was a little herky-jerky and seemed like he was having some trouble with the syllables, but after I got used to it I really came to appreciate it. Dan the Automator’s instrumentals are insane, very much the highlight of the album for me. The samples sound like they could be done in 1995 or 2035. Kid Koala’s turntablism adds flair here and there, but is overall a strong tertiary concern for me. Like Dan, I enjoy the nerdiness, I love gangsta stuff too but it’s always refreshing to see someone take rap somewhere it’s never been before. Album is a little longer than it needs to be, but otherwise I was a huge fan.

I really want to push back on the idea that Del has repetitive or samey flow. Just look at “Things You Can Do” vs. “Positive Contact”, for example. In the former, the flow is pretty even, stressed syllable followed by a few unstressed over and over. In the latter, it’s much less even, the intonation climbs and falls like an arpeggio, you have fragments of different length. You also soon after have “Virus”, where his “like so much wite-out” flow is also new. I think “Mastermind” contains both his most interesting flow and rhyme scheme, and I also love the rapid fire flow on “Time Keeps on Slipping”. If anything, I think Del varies his flow quite consistently, much moreso than someone like MF DOOM (whom I love, but who relied on the same flow (7 eighth notes on, final eight note rest) throughout a lot of *Madvillain*), and certainly moreso than your typical rapper. His hooks are also really strong throughout. “3030” and “Virus” are my two favorite hooks from Del, Damon Albarn’s hook on “Time Keeps on Slipping” is perfectly done, the “I’m caught in the grip of the city/Madness” hook in “Madness goes really hard, and unfortunately the hook in “Upgrade (A Baymar College College)” is my least favorite. I also appreciate how Del raps with the same bravado as any other rapper, but instead of bragging about money or women, he’s bragging about his mech suit and spaceship.

As much as I love Del, Dan the Automator’s beats are the heart and soul of this album. Every last one has this laid back, smooth 90s vibe, but the sampling is unlike anything I’ve ever heard. He pulls all these bits of instruments from modern classical pieces and he rearranges them so perfectly for hip hop. The drums are just so constantly crisp, and the non-classical samples (particularly in “Madness” are done very well too. I think “3030” is his personal masterpiece; I don’t get tired of it for all 7.5 minutes, the chords on the hook are so tense and haunting, the strings build at just the right moments, the outro is Just. So. Smooth. On the opposite side of the album, “Memory Loss” also has a stellar beat, with both the horns and the harpsichord-sounding-thingy utilized to perfection. I also loved the cymbals in “Battlesong”, the harmonica in “Time Keeps on Slipping”, and the apocalyptic strings in “Turbulence - Remix”. I enjoyed the turntablism on “Madness”, so shoutout Kid Koala there, but otherwise it wasn’t super prominent for me. In “Mastermind”, Del shouts out Dan the Automator as the mastermind of the album. So completely true.

This is such a sprawling album and I’ve barely scratched the surface, so I have some miscellaneous thoughts here. “Time Keeps on Slipping” sounds like such a proto-Gorillaz song, it’s cool to hear the wheels turning this early. I’m typically a skit hater, it really weighs down 80s and 90s rap albums, but I actually thought they were really done well here. “St. Catherine St.” is a cool idea and fleshes out the world of the year 3030, “New Coke” is a pretty funny caricature of an old man in the future, “National Movie Review” is hilarious especially if you’ve seen *Strange Brew*, and “Meet Cleophis Randolph the Patriarch” sounds like a Lonely Island track (NOTE: The Lonely Island are also from the East Bay and, sure enough, they actually featured on the second Deltron 3030 album in 2013. I have to imagine this was an influence on them). The skits actually serve a purpose beyond humor, they really make the year 3030 feel much more real than it would otherwise. I think “Upgrade (A Baymar College College)” is my least favorite beat, very lackluster, and I think “Love Story” is the least interesting track on Del’s end.

Really cool album and one where I’ve added multiple songs to my playlists. Really fires on all cylinders, and I really enjoy it. I do wish it was a little shorter though. The covee is also really pleasing to my eye, have I mentioned that yet?

Favorite tracks: 3030, Things You Can Do, Virus, Mastermind, Madness, Time Keeps on Slipping, Battlesong, Memory Loss

Least favorite track: Upgrade (A Baymar Cllege College)
