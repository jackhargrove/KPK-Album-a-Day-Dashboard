# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

Obviously Dinosaur Jr. is an important band in the history of alt rock, so I had a good idea of what the band was and what it was about from that, but before this week I had only ever actually listened to “Feel the Pain” from one of the band’s 90s records thanks to *Rock Band 2*. Well, the Spotify data suggests I listened to this album twice in October 2017, but I don’t remember that at all so just disregard. Anyway, I really, really liked it. More than Sonic Youth or the Pixies, it feels like the actual sonic qualities of alternative rock truly began here: the guitar tone, the wistful chords, the slurred vocals, the lo-fi production, the return of a 70s sensibility. It’s all here, like the amalgam of the genre I have in my head. That being said, one thing I really like about it that I feel like no one else in the genre successfully copied is the concreteness of its melancholy. That is to say, I enjoyed the lack of existential angst (at least on the Mascis songs, the Barlow numbers at the end are a different story). Mascis aims every dissatisfied and dejected lyric that leaves his mouth at specific people, relationships, or events. Don’t get me wrong, I love a good lament against the human condition as the next guy, but there’s something about Mascis’ frustration over lady troubles that feels so real. Mascis amplifies this feeling with both his vocal tone and his guitar work. There are seldom few albums this good at evoking one specific feeling constantly in every facet of the music. Every listen to this album conjures images of twilights in my teenage years spent outside at creeks and rivers, contemplating my friendships, love interests, and future. It’s powerful stuff. Barlow’s two tracks at the end lack that virtue, magnified by his artistic immaturity compared to Mascis’ at this time. Still, what a great record.

I love the squealing, squelching feedback that opens the album at the beginning of “Little Fury Things”, as well as Barlow’s screams. From a history perspective, I love those little connections to hardcore that make it easy for me to digest how we got from Black Flag to Pavement. The guitar and vocal melodies in the chorus are simply beautiful, no other way to put it. The verses are a little less so, but still quite good. Though the lyrics are cryptic, I find them powerfully wistful. I love love love this track. “Kracked” kicks up the tempo a notch but maintains that longing. While it’s not as good as the amazing tracks that sandwich it, I love the twang in Mascis’ vocals, the whammy guitar solo, and the chorus. “Sludgefeast” fucking rules. Murph hits the skins of his kit so Goddamn hard at the beginning of this track. I adore the syncopated rhythm in the verses, on which Mascis absolutely nails the vocal delivery. I don’t think I’ll get the line “Please wanna hang around” out of my head for some time. I like the blistering pace and beefy guitar playing of “The Lung”, but the songwriting is a little meh. “Raisans” forms a holy trinity with “Little Fury Things” and “Sludgefeast” as the best songs on the album. It has a great riff right off the bat and a perfect chorus where Mascis gets as close as one can to being whiny without going over the edge. The sample is a little fucked up, but it really does work. And HOLY SHIT that guitar solo. That is some vintage classic rock shredding. I enjoy the breakbeat drumming of “Tarpit”, but it’s just too lethargic for me. It’s my least favorite on the album. Mascis ends his run with “In a Jar”, which provides a much-needed pickup in pace after the last track. I really like the bass work here. I also can’t get enough of the parts of the chorus where the guitar drops out. This track is about on par with the first few. I’m a little torn on “Lose”. It’s got great energy, but it just kind of passes me by. It feels like a regression to simpler punk when compared to the Mascis part of the album. I do like the line “I can’t believe I was chosen to exist” though. I’m glad “Poledo” exists for the weirdness factor, but I don’t get a ton out of it. I prefer the collage section to the ukulele section, certainly. I also think Barlow might have had some self-esteem issues. He makes Mascis seem headstrong by comparison.

This was a cool experience as one of the few foundational classics in my musical architecture that I hadn’t yet listened to. It lived up to the hype more than most. I’m so psyched I get to listen to some of these songs forever now.

Favorite tracks: Little Fury Things, Kracked, Sludgefeast, Raisans, In a Jar

Least favorite track: Tarpit
