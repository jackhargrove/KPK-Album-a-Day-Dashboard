# Write-up

DJ Screw (real name Robert Earl Davis, Jr.) was a hip hop DJ born in 1971 and based in Houston. He was born in the small town of Smithville, Texas, and was raised by his single mother between Smithville, Houston, and Los Angeles. Screw was introduced to hip hop at age 13 in 1984 after watching the movie *Breakin’*, a musical about break dancers. He also enjoyed classical music and became a gifted piano player after seven years of lessons. Screw began to DJ with a rapper friend of his, making his own DJ rig. Allegedly, Screw would destroy any record he disliked by scratching it with a screw, earning him his stage name. With a few years of DJ experience under his belt, Screw began developing his signature slowed-down style on DJ mixes in 1990. While he originally only made personalized mixes for friends, customers began asking for copies of existing mixes once he became known locally. The MCs of the budding Houston hip hop scene in the early 90s enjoyed rhyming over Screw’s mixes, so they formed a loose collective based around Screw as DJ. This eventually grew into the Screwed Up Click. George Floyd (yes, that George Floyd) was a member of the Screwed Up Click in the 90s before his better-known activities in Minnesota.

Screw signed to BigTyme Recordz in 1994, a major Houston hip hop record label with a roster that also included famous rappers like Bun B and Pimp C of UGK. Screw’s popularity grew across Texas, so he opened a shop in Houston called Screwed Up Records and Tapes to more efficiently sell mixes to his fans. 1995’s *All Screwed Up, Vol. II* was the third of five DJ mixes that Screw produced for BigTyme Recordz. In retrospect, it’s usually considered his masterpiece. This mix exemplifies the hip hop subgenre and production technique known as “chopped and screwed” that DJ Screw pioneered. A chopped and screwed song is a pre-existing hip hop song that has been heavily slowed down to about 60 beats per minute (screwed) and then rearranged into a new order (chopped). Many speculate that the goal of chopped and screwed music is to emulate the effects of lean, an opioid mixture of codeine cough syrup and Sprite.

DJ Screw was discovered dead in his Houston studio in 2000. He had died of a codeine overdose caused by sipping lean. He was 29 years old. When he died, Screw was a big deal in his local scene, but virtually no one outside of Texas had heard of him. But in 2004, the single “Still Tippin” by Mike Jones became a major hit, exploding Houston hip hop onto the national stage. As the founder of the Screwed Up Click to which many Houston rappers belonged, DJ Screw became known and revered figure in hip hop history. Perhaps sadly, the thriving hip hop scene is famous for its heavy consumption of lean, the very drug that killed DJ Screw. Though he’s been dead for 25 years, DJ Screw’s influence lives on multiple orders of magnitude greater than when he was alive. While the original location no longer exists, three Screwed Up Records and Tapes still operate in Texas. Houston native Travis Scott included the track “R.I.P. Screw” on his 2018 smash hit album *Astroworld*, demonstrating Screw’s continued influence on his hometown scene. And perhaps most importantly, Screw’s chopped and screwed technique was the single most important influence on the cutting-edge electronic music scene of the 2010s. Daniel Lopatin used Screw’s techniques to make *Chuck Person’s Eccojams Vol. I*, spawning the entire vaporwave musical and aesthetic movement. Further down the line, this led to the wildly popular “slowed and reverbed” music videos online, all of which owe their existence to DJ Screw.

# Hargrove's review

Much like with metal music, I’ve discovered that when you turn the tempo of hip hop way way down, I quite like it. First it was Lil Ugly Mane’s *Mista Thug Isolation*, whose dirty but witty lyrics and thick atmosphere won me over despite him being a white guy from Virginia who called himself Lil Ugly Mane. Then, it was Three-6 Mafia’s *Mystic Stylez* which, while not perfect, was good enough to earn a B Tier despite being a 75-minute long scattered album. Not long after, we listened to Travis Scott’s *Rodeo*, where my favorite parts were the slow, atmospheric sections. And now, fuck, I love this DJ Screw record. It’s my favorite of any record I’ve listed so far. Of course, these four records all have something else in common: they’re examples of Southern hip hop. And I do feel a real connection with the sex humor and drawling of the Southern sound. But I brought up metal because *All Screwed Up, Vol. II* triggers the same pleasure centers of my brain as sludge metal or doom metal or stoner metal. When we listened to Electric Wizard’s *Dopethrone*, I mentioned that it made me feel like I was swimming through molasses, desperate for a gasp of air. DJ Screw’s glacial, low-pitch, sleazy soundscapes give me that exact same feeling. It makes the air around me feel like it weighs a million pounds. It feels like I’m lost in a corn maze flooded from top to bottom with marijuana smoke. I fucking love it. It’s simply the best. I can’t put into words exactly why that feeling appeals to me, but it’s worked across not only multiple artists, but multiple genres. As I mentioned previously, I also love the filthy lyrics on this thing. No exaggeration, my jaw dropped when I read along to “Pregnant Pussy” for the first time and my mouth did not close until the song ended. Talk about too far! It’s really fucking funny though. Pimp C again left me with a hilarious story about someone’s sister eating his ass on “Tell Me Something Good”. Speaking of, I’ve found out that I really like UGK and I plan to explore their work during the break. In addition to the production and lyrics, I found that the DJ mix format helped create a less segmented overall record. I loved hearing a beat start halfway through one track and continue until halfway through the next. This slurring between one track and the next added to the surreal atmosphere. My biggest complaint is that the quality drops off significantly after “Inside Looking Out”, and thus is much longer than it needs to be. That being said, I feel like I wouldn’t mind that at all in a real party setting because even if the quality was lower, the vibes were all still there. So this bothered me less than it did on other records. Overall, this is easily my favorite hip hop record on the list from an artist I wasn’t previously familiar with.

“Intro” is pretty funny I actually don’t love it as an intro. It’s good to hear Screw’s voice, but he just sounds kind of meek. Thankfully, “Short Texas” is amazing right off the bat. The ascending choir voices begin such an amazing tension build at the beginning of each verse, which kicks into overdrive halfway through with the synth. Something about the chords allows the beat to build and build and build without really adding too many layers. Bun B and Pimp C both lay down banger verses, and that synth just sounds massive and sludgy. The ascending choir voices continue into “Wreckless”, where I’m super impressed by the storytelling on Point Blank’s verses. To me, this track validates Screw’s thesis that slowing down rap makes it easier to follow. Halfway through, the choir voice beat switches out to a more G-funk sounding one. “Pregnant Pussy” has some of the most absurd lyrics I’ve ever heard, making it one of the few hip hop tracks in recent memory to genuinely shock me. I’d be lying if I said it wasn’t hilarious though. Halfway through, the G-funk beat shifts into this sleazy jazz club saxophone beat that I love. Point Blank impresses me once again with his thoughtful verses on “After I Die”. “I wonder what my family gonna say/Maybe they was looking for me to die anyway” and “And all I got to say/I was tired of this bitch ass Earth anyway” are lines about death that have stuck with me. And I still love that sleazy sax beat. “Backstreets” is the weakest track so far, but it’s still quite good. The verses are fine, and Screw goes a little overboard with the stuttering vocal edits, but I really love the hook.

Screw delivers another banger UGK remix with “Tell Me Something Good”. Pimp C gets me laughing every time with “I didn't fuck ya girl, but your sister pussy tight/Fucked her in my homeboy's Caddy last night/She sucked my dick, I bust a nut in her throat/And she didn't take it out, and she didn't even choke/I fucked her doggystyle, and the bitch just threw me/Turned me on my stomach, started lickin' out my booty/Now everybody in the hood/Know that your sister lick a booty real good, hehhahaha!”. I love the new beat, where this woodwind thing is paired with a bright vibraphone or something that sounds like an oasis in a haze of smoke. 20-2-Life didn’t wow me on his first track, but he spits some fascinating bars on “Inside Looking Out”. I feel like I’ve never heard this aspect of prison life articulated in rap before. The sad guitar strumming of the beat brings the album to lull vibes-wise, but that seems intentional, and I’ll allow it. I was very surprised to learn that “My Mind Went Blank” is Screw’s most popular remix because I found it only okay. Despite the twang, the new beat is sparse, and Point Blank’s bars aren’t his finest. My least favorite track is “13 Ways”, where the subpar PSK-13 goes for some kind of reggae hook. “Straighten It Out” is fine; Point Blank’s has some decent bars, but the beat has gotten far too low-key for me. PSK-13 returns on “Headin fo My Trunk”, where his voice sounds too gruff and aggressive for the music. However, the ascending synth from the beginning reappears near the end of this track, which is a huge plus. The album ends on a high note with “Da Music”. For some reason, Point Blank’s “Playa haters hate me and I respect 'em for that” has become a tic for me. This track has the same vibes and quality as the amazing run at the beginning of the record.

There were a lot of similarities between this album and Dr. Dre’s *The Chronic* last week. I liked this one a lot, but I couldn’t justify ranking it above *The Chronic*. But that’s fine, because, I mean, it’s Dr. Dre. I’m still putting this very high in A Tier.

Favorite tracks: Short Texas, Wreckless, Pregnant Pussy, After I Die, Backstreets, Tell Me Something Good, Inside Looking Out, Da Music

Least favorite track: 13 Ways
