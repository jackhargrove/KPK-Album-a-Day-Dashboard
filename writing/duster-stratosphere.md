# Hargrove's review

My gold standard for slowcore is Red House Painters and this didn’t quite live up to them, but I respect how they took a different approach to what is overall a not very diverse genre. To start with some things I didn’t like, I think they took the lo-fi approach to an unpleasant extreme. There were a few songs where the tape was so damaged that it created a warbling effect that I did not care for one bit. I also didn’t like how low in the mix the vocals were on some songs. I can see how both of these things play into the aesthetic of the record, but they took away from the listening experience for me. I also thought the lyrics were generally too cryptic for how few lines there were; I think opacity works better when there’s a higher volume of lyrics to comb through and appreciate the poetry, while shorter lyrics work better with starkness and frankness. And finally, even though many were short, there’s just too many tracks. They end up blending together to the detriment of the songwriting, and I think it would have been better if there were fewer but more fleshed-out songs.

All of that being said, this record has a lot going for it. That Pink Floyd-esque spaciness works weirdly well with the slowcore sound, and it made it a pleasant listen from beginning to end. They really sound like a combination of Modest Mouse and Slint, two of my favorite bands, and it created a lot of good vibes. And though I criticized the volume of the lyrics earlier, some songs were very well-written. I liked the drug lyrics on “Reed to Hillsborough” a lot, and, as someone who numbs his anxiety with booze and sometimes goes too far with it at parties, I really related to both “Heading for the Door” and “Inside Out”. I in particular loved the recurring, cryptic references to New Year’s Eve, giving me the feeling that so much of the emotion here spills out of a shitty experience at a NYE party. It reminds me of “The Ice of Boston” by The Dismemberment Plan. When the vocals were actually audible, they were really nice and complemented the atmosphere well. In addition to the three songs I’ve already mentioned, the standouts for me were: “Gold Dust”, which is a really pretty instrumental with a guitar line that is both bright and wistful. “Echo, Bravo”, which, despite the jarring intro, had some of the most powerful, radiating guitar work I’ve heard in this genre. “The Queen of Hearts”, a great instrumental that reminded me a lot of Slint. “Earth Moon Transit”, which has killer chord changes and is louder than most other songs. And “The Twins / Romantica”, which is shocking to me that there’s apparently no human drummer given how strong the drums are. This is a solid B Tier album for me, one that I am excited to revisit and explore further.

Favorite tracks: Heading for the Door, Gold Dust, Echo, Bravo, The Queen of Hearts, Inside Out, Reed to Hillsborough, Earth Moon Transit, The Twins / Romantica

Least favorite track: Topical Solution
