# Write-up

Electric Wizard is a doom and stoner metal band formed in Dorset, England in 1988 who at the time of this album consisted of guitarist and vocalist Jus Oborn, bassist Tim Bagshaw, and drummer Mark Greening. Oborn originally formed the band as Lord of Putrefaction, releasing three demos and a collab album under that name between 1989 and 1991. After releasing a fourth demo, now under the name Thy Grief Eternal (later just “Eternal”), the band finally assumed the name Electric Wizard in 1993, combining the Black Sabbath songs “Electric Funeral” and “The Wizard”. That same year, it coalesced around the Oborn, Bagshaw, and Greening lineup. The band released its debut album in 1995, playing slow and heavy like Black Sabbath at a time when aggressive genres like death metal, black metal, and industrial metal were in vogue. The band’s sophomore LP, 1997’s *Come My Fanatics…*, earned critical acclaim and has retrospectively been deemed a classic of the doom metal genre. It also was Electric Wizard’s first to incorporate the doom-adjacent sounds of sludge and stoner metal.

*Dopethrone*, Electric Wizard’s third album, released in 2000. After the release of its second album in 1997, the band members spent three years in disarray, as the three members faced major injuries, drug addiction, and legal troubles (Bagshaw broke into a liquor store, Oborn set a car on fire). However, the owner of the band’s independent record label forced them into the studio in 2000 to record a new album. The trio entered the studio with only three songs written, creating the rest of the album on the spot through jam sessions. Oborn and Bagshaw wrote the music collaboratively, which they had also done on previous albums. As you can imagine, the recording process was marked by excessive drug use. *Dopethrone* is often called stoner metal thanks to its slow, fuzzy sound and weed-influenced lyrics; however, this coinage was mostly applied by American journalists drawing parallels between Electric Wizard and Palm Desert scene in California that had been established for nearly a decade. British journalists instead called it simply doom metal, though they noted additional influences from stoner, sludge, and 70s heavy metal, as well as punk rock. Notably, the lyrics are far more violent and hateful than is typical for stoner metal. Oborn cited Lovecraft as inspiration for the album’s lyrics, though the title track “Dopethrone” was based on a loose story Oborn had heard about someone who literally built a sofa out of cannabis. The album was a commercial and critical success, though some critics found it a little over-the-top.

Electric Wizard toured the US for the first time after the release of *Dopethrone*, nearly getting arrested for drug possession in Virginia. The band released a fourth album in 2002. The next year, Bagshaw and Greening both left, leaving Oborn to rebuild the band around himself. Electric Wizard has since released five more albums. The lineup has constantly fluctuated, with Oborn and new writing partner (and wife) Liz Buckingham the only consistent members. No studio album has been released since 2017, though the band is still active and released a live album in 2024.

# Hargrove's review

Man oh man do I love this slow metal shit. Sabbath, Kyuss, Sleep, Neurosis, even Agalloch may be blackened folk metal but it still ticks a lot of those boxes for me. I guess I shouldn’t be surprised that I like the weed metal stuff given I’ve been a huge Queens of the Stone Age fan for years, but it’s seriously been one of the most notable things I’ve learned about myself over the course of this project. I love it when it feels like that next cymbal crash will never come, when the riff fills every nook and cranny of your cranium simultaneously. On *Dopethrone*, Electric Wizard gives me more of that top-shelf slow metal that I love. It really is quite good for all those reasons I just described about the style writ large. It’s like Aidan with indie rock for me now: as long as it’s decently well-made, I’ll like anything with that general sonic vibe. Oborn found such a stellar guitar tone on this album, the perfect mixture of fuzz and edge. I adore the drumming, which commands multiple tempo shifts executed to perfection. I like the way Oborn’s vocals are mixed too, a kind of lo-fi, buried sheen that I might even say predicted the Strokes by a year. My biggest gripe, and what keeps this below Sleep and Kyuss, is that I don’t think the hatred, anger, and existential despair thing works with this sound quite as much as the silly stoner stuff does. Whether in the lyrics directly, or just through the biting vocal delivery, Oborn is MAD on this album. And there are plenty of albums I love that do anger really well, like Nine Inch Nail’s *The Downward Spiral*. But when I hear this fuzzy, slow, booming sound, I want to hear about bong rips in the desert. Still, this album rules quite a bit. I constantly feel like I’m swimming through molasses while listening to it, desperate to breach the surface for air. It’s like a sinister warm hug. I love it.

Though the album later holds multiple 10+ minute epics, the short opener “Vinum Sabbathi” packs in the same punch as any of those tracks in just three minutes. The sample at the beginning opens the album in such an iconic way, while the music on this one most reminds me of a Kyuss track with that heavy fuzz and the buried vocals. The lyrics are straight up Mayhem black metal ideas, which really surprised me upon my lyrics listen. “Funeralopolis” starts slow and quiet for over a minute and a half, creeping along like classic rock until the distortion really hits. From there, this track just builds and builds and builds like a snowball rolling down a hill until it becomes an unwieldy, ear-splitting behemoth. While generally I don’t love the spiteful lyrics on this record, they really work for me on this track. The apocalyptic imagery becomes quite overwhelming and powerful. I interpret it as someone who would rather see the world end instantly in flames than slowly in ice. The 15-minute epic “Weird Tales - I. Electric Frost - II. Golgatha - III. Altar of Melektaus” starts at the high intensity where the previous track left off before inverting its progression and slowly dissipating into nothingness. Those first five minutes are just slow, thick, and heavy, yet just after 6:00 the drums kick it down a notch further than U even thought was possible. It’s sooooooooooooo sludgy and viscous. I LOVE IT. I really enjoy Oborn’s vocal delivery on “Barbarian”, particularly the “no” at the end of each verse. It also has an imposing riff making it a powerful track. “I, Witchfinder” competes with “Funeralopolis” for the position of my favorite track. The whole thing is so slow. I love it when the drums feel labored, then every time the vocals start anew it’s like a tidal wave forms. And there’s a long-ass outro that I wish would never end. “The Hills Have Eyes” feels like an afterburn to said outro and not much more. The album’s biggest miss is “We Hate You”, where the anger becomes directionless and over-the-top. Musically, it doesn’t stand out much either, nor does it have the space to grow into anything. Thankfully, “Dopethrone” ends the album on a high note. It’s right back to what worked so well about the first six tracks. This album does kind of blend together and it’s hard to pick favorites, but only because it’s so consistently good.

What can I say, I expected to like this album, and I did like this album. I cannot believe I never listened to slow metal like this before we started this project. It’s insane to me that I went through my life without it. I spent most of the break listening to Kyuss, Sleep, and this.

Favorite tracks: Vinum Sabbathi, Funeralopolis, Weird Tales - I. Electric Frost - II. Golgatha - III. Altar of Melektaus, Barbarian, I, the Witchfinder, Dopethrone

Least favorite track: We Hate You

# Aidan's review

I went into this expecting to hate yet another doom metal album, but this album rules. It never takes itself too seriously, and the samples, especially the one that opens the album from a news report on Satanism, are so much fun. Right after that, the album starts rocking. The incredibly lo-fi vocals are a great match for the overpowering guitars, and the basses are as groovy as any metal album we've done so far. The best parts are the most blues-inspired, and the last few tracks drag a bit, but for a 75 minute album, it keeps remarkable momentum. Great album.
