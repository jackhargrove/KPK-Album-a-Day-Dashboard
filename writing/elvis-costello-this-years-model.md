# Write-up

Elvis Costello, real name Declan MacManus, is an English singer and songwriter. Born to a trumpet player and a record store clerk in London, he was brought up around music constantly. His first favorite band was The Beatles, and he started playing in front of clubs for free by age 14 after his parents moved to Liverpool, hometown of the Beatles. He was unsure about pursuing a full career in music until a friend was killed in a car accident, which caused him to resolve to pursue his dreams. He was not very successful initially. At 18, he started working as a computer operator and continued that line of work for the next 7 years, marrying at 21 and living with his parents. His first band was a folk rock duo with Allan Mayes called Rusty that played in a few Liverpool clubs. He then moved back to London and started going by Declan Costello. Costello was a family name his father had once used. His next act was Flip City, a pub rock band which also found little success. He changed his stage name again, this time to D.P. Costello (Patrick was his middle name) and continued to try to make it playing. Finally, he got signed to a small independent label in 1976, Stiff Records. The label polished Costello's image and convinced him to go by "Elvis”. His first few singles did not sell well, but the full album, My Aim Is True, sold decently and was a critical favorite, allowing Costello to quit his job.

Costello had used Clover, a country rock group (which would turn into Huey Lewis and the News) as his backing band for My Aim Is True, but he wanted a sparser sound for the tour. To that end, he auditioned for a new backing band. He wound up with Pete Thomas on drums, Bruce Thomas (no relation) on bass and Steve Nason on keyboard. Nason would go by "Neive", a play on "Naive" after he asked a bandmate what a groupie was. The backing band was dubbed "The Attractions" and they started touring. During their tour, they played on SNL. In a famous episode, they were asked not to play "Radio, Radio", a song critical of mass media, but while performing the replacement song, the anti-Mosley "Less Than Zero", he suddenly stoped and played "Radio, Radio" instead. The story goes that Lorne Michaels gave the band the bird for the entire duration of the song. All parties involve insist that was not a big deal and Costello has return to SNL twice more, including a reenactment of that episode.

Today's album, This Year's Model, was Costello's first album with The Attractions (although they didn't get credited until the following one). It was roughly as successful as Costello's previous album, but contains many of his best-regarded songs such as "Pump It Up", "No Action", "Radio Radio" and "(I Don't Want To Go To) Chelsea", although the last two were excluded from the UK and US versions respectively. Their next album, Oliver's Army, was their most successful, and featured a cover of his long-time producer Nick Lowe's "(What's So Funny 'Bout) Peace Love and Understanding" (Pub rockers apparently love parenthetical).

His next few albums would see Costello cycle through genres, as his commercial fortunes diminished and he began to feud with his backing band. His seventh album, Imperial Bedroom, was still extremely critically popular, winning the Pazz and Jop Poll, but he followed it up with 1984's Goodbye Cruel World, an album that he would write "Congratulations! You've just purchased our worst album" in the liner notes of the rerelease. He would ditch the Attractions after that, and they would go on to make their own albums without him.

Costello would proceed to collaborate heavily, a habit which he continues to this day. While he got back with the Attractions 2 years later, he would work with a host of famous names, including Paul McCartney, Fall Out Boy, an Argentinian electro-tango band and the Pogues. 5 years ago, he released a version of This Year's Model in Spanish with covers by Spanish-speaking artists.

# Hargrove's review

Elvis Costello has always been one of my mom’s favorite artists, but I’ve never checked him out much except for “Radio, Radio” and “Less than Zero” (and, of course, the SNL performance that unites the two). Oh, and “Pump It Up”, a track I discovered from an episode of *Malcolm in the Middle*. Before this, I could have named three Elvis Costello songs, I liked all three quite a bit, and yet still I didn’t think to listen to any of his albums. That turned out to be an incredibly stupid decision because *This Year’s Model* is quite good! It’s a tight collection of bangers that sounds like if the Beatles or the Stones came out of the UK punk milieu. I could listen to tracks like “No Action”, “The Beat”, “Pump It Up”, “(I Don’t Want to Go to) Chelsea”, and “Radio, Radio” all day. The guitar and organ lines drive every song, the rhythm section keeps things movings, and I quite like Costello’s charming snarl. That being said, I had two major issues with this album that hold it back from sneaking into A Tier or higher. For one, I never connected with the lyrics in the way that I hoped to. I had heard a lot about Costello being a witty, clever snarker who elevated the bite of UK punk into a poetic art form. Outside of a spare line or two, however, I got precious little from this album’s lyrics. At the very least, not as much as I had hoped to get out of them. Every song is competently written, sure, and the flow is there, but not much truly wowed me. Secondly, despite this album’s respectable brevity, a it still contains a few bad tracks. In particular, I dislike both “Little Triggers” and “Living in Paradise”, two tracks I found genuinely annoying upon repeated listens. I also found “You Belong to Me” and “Night Rally” to be forgettable, but “Little Triggers” and “Living in Paradise” are memorable for the wrong reasons. I made playlists for both the UK and US releases to compare the two; “Radio, Radio” on the US version is so much of a stronger closer than “Night Rally” on the UK version that the US version wins off of that alone, but “(I Don’t Want to Go to) Chelsea” is sorely missed. Anyway, damn solid album that’s fun to listen to and has serious highlights.

I love the brief vocals-only intro to “No Action”. It’s a great way to start an album so focused on Costello’s personality. I love the rest of the track too, a great sarcastic pop song with a catchy chorus. “This Year’s Girl” begins with a Beatlesesque drum line, later exhibiting shades of “You Won’t See Me” on the bridge. It maybe goes on a little too long, but the strong vocal delivery from Costello makes the track work. “The Beat” is easily my favorite non-single. It’s been stuck in my head all day more than any other song. I love the semi-slurred vocals and the tumbling synth line. Compositionally, this might be my favorite on the album, from the lovely pre-chorus to the unresolved tension of the instrumental after the chorus. I also love the various keyboard textures, from tropical synth to sharp-as-nails organ. Finally, “I don't want to be a lover/I just want to be your victim” was one of the few lyrics to stand out to me on the album. “Pump It Up” includes one of the all-time great guitar riffs, made even better by the organ shadowing it. The vocals have relentless momentum, building into a triumphant chorus. It’s just a super fun track, a great addition to pretty much any playlist. I’m not a fan of “Little Triggers” at all. It feels lightweight even for a lilting waltz. There’s just not much substance to it at all. It annoys me on each listen. “You Belong to Me” is a fine, if forgettable, track. It has a good guitar and organ line but the vocals are a bit insipid. It’s greatest crime is contributing to a lull at the end of side 1.

“Hand in Hand” brings back quality to kick off side 2. It has a fantastic build from verse to pre-chorus to chorus, just expert songwriting and compositional skills. “(I Don’t Want to Go to) Chelsea” is one of the finest examples of the proper integration of reggae into new wave. I love this style of music, but it walks a fine line and can get annoying fast. This one walks the tightrope perfectly. I love Costello’s pouting vocals, clever lyrics like They call her Natasha when she looks like Elsie”, and the catchiness of the melody. “Lip Service” has a fun sing-along chorus. It sounds a little bit generic, but it’s decent enough for a deep cut. I do not like “Living in Paradise” at all. The verses drag on and on, with the music stopping for each line before going into an extended, stupid little lick. The chorus is hokey too. I enjoy the breakneck pace and energy of “Lipstick Vogue”, but it comes a little too late in the track listening. It could have also been cool with a tighter composition. The UK version of the album ends on a downer with “Night Rally”. It’s a confusing choice. By contrast, the US version ends on the highest of high notes with “Radio, Radio”. It’s one of the highest energy tracks ever released, a raucous, fun time with gems like “I wanna bite the hand that feeds me/I wanna bite that hand so badly”. I will always love this track.

Overall, a damn solid album from Elvis Costello, good enough for the top half of B Tier. The energy of late 70s UK music is just so infectious.

Favorite tracks: No Action, This Year’s Girl, The Beat, Pump It Up, Hand in Hand, (I Don’t Want to Go to) Chelsea, Lip Service, Radio, Radio

Least favorite track: Little Triggers

# Aidan's review

Costello doesn't have the range of topics of Dylan, but when he's writing about sexual frustration and mass culture, he's as good as they come. He's not getting some, but the fault lies in consumerist culture rather than the girls who won't go with a nerd like him. It's a little tired as a theme, but his songwriting is clever and catchy enough to pull it off. There's the extended double entendres in "The Beat" and "Pump It Up". There's the ties between mass culture and dating life in "This Year's Girl" and "Hand in Hand" (calling a relationship "syndicated" is brilliant). He even manages to be clever when decrying the quality of hookers, saying "They call her Natasha but she looks like Elsie". And the melodies are tight, punchy, with high energy bass lines. "Night Rally" is no "Less Than Zero" and slower songs like it and  "Little Triggers", aren't the best on the album, but they're limited and provide a bit of a breather on an album that toes the line on how long a high energy pop record should be. There's a hissing sound on the chorus of "Radio, Radio" that annoys the shit out of me, but it's the best song on there even with it. Costello's lyrics and melodies are enough to keep it going though, and it's a blast to listen to.
