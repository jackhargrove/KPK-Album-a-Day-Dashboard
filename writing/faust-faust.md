# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

Well, this is definitely a weird one, but it’s honestly pretty enjoyable throughout. It’s clearly a deconstruction in the same sense that Beefheart’s *Trout Mask Replica* and Royal Trux’s *Twin Infinitives* and Sun City Girls’ *Torch of the Mystics* and the Pop Group’s *Y* are deconstructions, but it improves on that formula so much just by being listenable. I guess it’s most direct comparison is disc 2 of CAN’s *Tago Mago*, and it definitely blows that out of the water. I don’t for sure know exactly what it is Faust has to say, but it combines the sound collage sections with the actual musical sections so much better than any other comparable example that I’m okay with that. I also really appreciate just how clearly playful and comedic a lot of it is. I guess this is kind of true of all deconstructive albums, but I don’t know, these guys are actually kind of funny. Overall, my biggest criticism is that this is a kind of artsy album that doesn’t actually leave me with a lot to say afterward. But it’s easily the most enjoyable album of its kind for me to listen to in the moment.

I listed “Why Don’t You Eat Carrots” as my least favorite track, but that’s not really intended as a slight. It’s just because it doesn’t have a real rocking section like the other two tracks, which are my favorite parts of the album. Still, there’s a lot in this opener to like. The Stones and Beatles samples at the beginning are quite a cool way to open an album, and then the distorted carnival music really was not what I was expecting from this album. It feels like living inside of a funhouse mirror, or riding an abandoned, hellish carousel. It does pick up on the middle a bit when the fuzzy electric guitar joins the horn, but it’s fleeting, and never reaches the heights of the other tracks. I do like the foreign film sounding convo at the end. “Meadow Meal” starts kind of weak with the construction. I like it a lot once the vocals get started though. The lyrics are nonsense, but I love the rhythm of the vocals in the same way that I like rapping, plus there is some really nice guitar underneath. Then, the album breaks into a scorching, blues-y rocker for the first time, which is quite invigorating, The organ outro is fine, but that rocker is so satisfying. The closer, “Miss Fortune”, is clearly the album’s highlight. It starts with soft wah wah over a slow drum beat as the drums slowly transition into motorik at last. However, the motorik is really low in the mix, which is a weird effect. It crescendos over the first 5 minutes into a Hendrix-y solo before crashing into nothingness the first time. There’s a noodly section of operatic singing over ringing cymbals, but it then builds into an even better crescendo. It just has more heft than the last one. There is unfortunately some bullshit afterward, but the ending is beautiful. I love the alternating monologue and the soft acoustic fingerpicking. It’s all pretty cool.

Overall, this just feels like what I want my ambient or background music to sound like. Ever-changing, sonically diverse, funny, and nice on the ears. I had a good time with this album. Admittedly, I did listen to *Faust IV* a couple times too and it’s really, really nice. I think listening to a less experimental album from a band can make their experimental work make more sense.

Favorite tracks: Meadow Meal, Miss Fortune

Least favorite track: Why Don’t You Eat Carrots
