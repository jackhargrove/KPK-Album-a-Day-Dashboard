# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Fleetwood Mac is like the messiest band of all time and has had a truly insane number of roster changes over the years, so I’m not going to cover too many specifics here. The band formed in London with Peter Green on guitar and vocals, Jeremy Spencer on slide guitar and piano, drummer Mick Fleetwood, and bassist John McVie. The band was named for the latter two and, coincidentally, they were the only two to stick with Fleetwood Mac for the entirety of its existence. Between 1967 and 1974, Fleetwood Mac’s lineup constantly changed, and they released 9 studio albums. In 1974, Americans Stevie Nicks and Lindsey Bunckingham joined on vocals and guitar, respectively. They became the two most prominent members of the band and their first album, 1975’s *Fleetwood Mac*, proved to be the group’s most popular up to that point. As of 1977, the band’s lineup was Lindsey Buckingham on guitar and some lead vocals, Stevie Nicks on some lead vocals, Mick Fleetwood on drums, John McVie on bass, and Christine McVie on keyboard.

Fleetwood Mac is known as a group with an extremely messy dynamic, and the reason for that is simple: all of them were constantly fucking each other. Stevie Nicks and Lindsey Buckingham had participated in an on-again, off-again relationship since before they joined the band in 1974, and John McVie and Christine McVie had been married for 8 years. In 1976, these relationships fell apart after the success of *Fleetwood Mac* and the stress of the following world tour; Nicks and Buckingham broke it off for good, the McVies divorced, and Mick Fleetwood found out that his wife (not in the band) was having an affair. Despite these personal troubles, and despite the fact that half the band now hated the other half, the show had to go on. Fleetwood Mac as a band was too popular and too valuable to stop at the peak of their success. So they began recording their 11th album, *Rumours*, coping with drugs, partying, sex, and other lifestyle choices that could be used as a parody of the 70s.

The result, while sometimes hilarious, has also been noted for its emotional power. Lindsey Buckingham plays guitar and sings vocals on the chorus of Stevie Nicks’ “Dreams”, where she says to him “In the stillness of rememberin’/What you had and what you lost/“. Nicks sings backing vocals on Buckingham’s “Go Your Own Way”, where he calls her a slut (“Packin’ up, shackin’ up’s all you wanna do”). These people played these songs together live on stage for years!

# Hargrove's review

I was in the same boat with I imagine a lot of other people with this album: I had heard “Dreams”, “Go Your Own Way” (this one has always been my favorite), and “The Chain”, but I had never listened to the full album. Of course, when I listened, I realized I definitely had heard a few other songs before without realizing it, but a good amount of it was new to me. Overall, I don’t know how much I loved some of the songs outside of the huge singles. I did think the album cuts on the back half were much stronger than the ones on the first half. I’ll keep listening to “Go Your Own Way” (I think it was one of my top songs on my 2023 Spotify wrapped), but probably not adding a ton of new songs to my playlist. And although I did already know the story of the making of this album, it is one of my favorite music stories, and I won’t lie, it does give me a slightly greater appreciation.

“Go Your Own Way” is just a masterpiece of male obstinance, nothing else makes me feel like a man more than this song. Lindsey Buckingham is so clearly wrong in his lyrics, so oblivious, so self-interested, it’s honestly fantastic. Beyond that though, the instrumental rocks too. Mick Fleetwood puts the downbeat of the drums on 2 instead of 1, which makes this track feel so off-kilter. One of my favorite songs of all time. “Dreams” is also quite good. It answers the heartfelt pleading of “Go Your Own Way” with seething, feminine resentment. Nicks’ words are just dripping with venom and spite, yet remain surprisingly subtle. The bass line and the drums are both fantastic, and the chorus is crazy catchy. “The Chain” is probably my least favorite of the hits, but it’s still quite good. It opens side 2 with an ominous, blues-y feeling, John McVie’s bass is STELLAR and the heart of the track, and I really like the guitar that closes the song.

This album unfortunately has more than a couple songs I really dislike, mostly concentrated on side 1. I find the opener “Second Hand News” very annoying. I don’t enjoy the vocal melodies, the singing is annoying (how long do we ned the “bow bow bow bow”s?????) and the guitars are not for me. I really don’t like “Never Going Back” either, another song where I don’t care for the guitar. On top of that, the atmosphere is pastoral in a way I find grating, the vocals are schmaltzy, there’s limited bass, and no drums. “Songbird” is a terrible closer for side 1 and kills the momentum of “Go Your Own Way”. It’s whimpering, saccharine, corny, and I don’t like the vocals, though the piano is a bright spot.

Once we get to side 2, the deep cuts get much better. “You Make Loving Fun” is super funky, it really reminds you that this was released in the heart of the disco era. I also really like the chord change after the chorus. “I Don’t Want to Know” is quaint, the instrumental is nothing to write home about, but I love the duet between Nicks and Buckingham. I’m genuinely torn on “Oh Daddy”; it does a great job of building up this dark atmosphere, the drums are perfect for the song, and the vocal melody is great. But man, do I dislike Christine McVie’s vocals here. “Gold Dust Woman” is a perfect closer for the album. A great contribution from Stevie Nicks, honestly rivaling “Dreams”. The guitar is folksy in the best way, the chorus is so nice, and the vibe is moody. I also love how the guitar gets noisy at the end.

Great hits, mediocre album. My view of Fleetwood Mac is pretty much unchanged from before I listened to this album all the way through. Kind of disappointed, I really wanted to like it a lot more.

Favorite tracks: Dreams, Go Your Own Way, The Chain, You Make Loving Fun, Gold Dust Woman

Least favorite track: Never Going Back
