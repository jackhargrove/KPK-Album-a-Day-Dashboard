# Write-up

Frank Ocean was born in Long Beach, California in 1987 as Christopher Breaux. He grew up in New Orleans with his mother and grandfather after his father divorced his mother and left them. He studied English at the University of New Orleans for a year before Katrina hit in 2005, forcing him to transfer and soon after drop out. After dropping out, he moved to Los Angeles to pursue a career in music. He quickly became an established songwriter, working with big names including Justin Bieber, John Legend and Beyoncé. He then shifted to trying to make his own music. He adopted the stage name Frank Ocean, drawn from Oceans 11 (the original one starring Frank Sinatra, not the 2001 Clooney remake). He also joined the hip hop collective Odd Future.

Odd Future's full name is Odd Future Wolf Gang Kill Them All. It was founded in 2007 while most of the members were still in high school, and released two mixtapes and an album as a collective, all between 2008 and 2012. Its notable alumni besides Ocean include rappers Tyler the Creator (the group's unoffical leader) and Earl Sweatshirt, Jackass member Jasper Dolphin, and musician Syd, who fronts soul band The Internet. The group also had an Adult Swim show called Loiter Squad which ran for three seasons. Odd Future broke into the mainstream in the early 2010s, with the aforementioned members (besides Dolphin) all releasing critically and commercially successful albums, starting with Tyler's Goblin (and specifically the song "Yonkers") in 2011.

Soon after joining Odd Future, Ocean got signed to Def Jam, the beginning of a troubled 7-year tenure at the label. He released his debut mixtape, Nostalgia, Ultra, in 2011 to critical acclaim and praise from many prominent artists including Kanye (then at his arguable peak), Nas and Jay-Z. The album was done with minimal input from Def Jam and the samples used on it are still not cleared, resulting it being unavailable on streaming to this day. Ocean was quickly minted as an up-and-coming star, appearing on Kanye and Jay-Z's collaborative album Watch The Throne, and featuring heavily on Odd Future releases. His debut album, Channel Orange, was released the next year to massive critical and commercial success, including a Grammy nomination for Best Album (and a win for Best Urban Contemporary Album, a category which was quietly renamed during Woke 1). Ocean also came out publicly as bisexual before the album's release, which drew comparisons to Bowie.

Ocean's followup album, tentatively titled Boys Don't Cry, was teased starting in 2014, but took until 2016 to be released. Before the album was released, he released a visual album, Endless, on August 19th, 2016 (10 years ago to the day) which fulfilled his contractual obligation to Def Jam, allowing him to release his next album independently. The next day, he released Blonde, his second studio album, on his own label, Boys Don't Cry. The album topped the charts and managed to outshine the already fever-pitch praise for Channel Orange, ranking among the best albums of this year and all time in many lists.

Since the release of Blonde, Ocean has not released a new album and has instead dabbled in various mediums. He's published a photo essay in trendy magazine i-D, he's founded a fashion brand, and is rumored to be filming a movie in Mexico City. He has released a few new songs, several of which premiered on his radio show, Blonded Radio, and later went on to be included in his own radio station in GTA V. Another song, "Little Demon", was posted online in 2020, but has yet to receive an official release to this date. He has only performed live once since 2017, at Coachella in 2023 (after cancelling in 2020 for obvious reasons), which was not recorded and received poorly. His younger brother, who features on “Futura Free” off of Blonde, died in 2020 in a car crash.

# Hargrove's review

Man, what a high school classic this was. Few things make me feel like a teenager again in the way that listening to *Blonde* does. I was relatively late to the Frank Ocean party. I spent two weeks in the summer of 2016 working at a family camp near Tahoe and remember some of my coworkers getting all worked up over social media posts from Frank Ocean announcing a new album. I wouldn’t start seriously getting into music until the spring of 2017 and was particularly disconnected from contemporary popular music, so I paid it little mind. I finally began listening to Frank Ocean in earnest sometime in late 2017 or early 2018 thanks to a summer playlist compiled by r/indieheads that included the track “Sweet Life” from *Channel Orange*. It remains one of my favorite Frank Ocean tracks and was one of three on my basement playlist (alongside “Pink + White” and “Nights”). “Sweet Life” got me into *Channel Orange* as a whole, and, after a while of that, I finally got into *Blonde*. I still love both albums. My favorite between the two of them still shifts back and forth; *Blonde* is better overall, but, man, the highlights on *Channel Orange* are AMAZING. In particular, dozens and dozens of listens to “Bad Religion” got me through a brutal unrequited crush on a girl in early 2019. As for *Blonde*, my opinion after listening for the last two weeks remains the exact same as when I was in high school: it’s 50 minutes of banger after banger after banger surrounded by 10 minutes of the most frustrating bookends I’ve ever heard. I don’t know that there’s ever been such a good album in history that starts with anything as annoying as the high-pitched voice in the first three minutes of “Nikes”. What the FUCK was he thinking. When his voice returns to normal, the track finishes strong and *Blonde* becomes a fucking monster. “Ivy”, “Pink + White”, “Solo”, “Self Control”, “Nights”, “White Ferrari”, “Seigfried”, what an absolute murderer’s row of perfect tracks. Plus, even most of the experiments and interludes like “Skyline To”, “Good Guy”, “Solo (Reprise)”, “Pretty Sweet”, and “Close to You” rule. “Futura Free” starts off alright before trailing off into five minutes of uninteresting interviews to close the album. It’s so bizarre! How can an album stumble so hard out of the gate AND completely fail to stick the landing, and yet everything in between is elite? The bookends prevent this from being a perfect album, but damn if it isn’t still great. Frank nimbly weaves his voice in and out of these airy, amorphous, atmospheric instrumentals. Few songs consistently get me teary-eyed like “Self Control” or “White Ferrari”. Frank knows how to tug at my heartstrings, and he tugs hard. I hope he’s doing well and that he returns someday.

WHY could Frank not have just dropped the stupid voice after like four bars on “Nikes”??? Just a baffling choice. It’s so frustrating because the final third of the song with a normal voice goes quite hard. This could have been a banger! Even in the parts with annoying vocals, you have memorable lines like “But if you need dick, I got you” and “Why you think I’m in this bitch wearing a fucking yarmulke”. Simply one of the most frustrating pieces of music ever put to wax. Frank starts making good decisions on “Ivy” and never looks back (until “Futura Free”). This track shows off the beauty of his winding vocals like no other. he weaves a tapestry out of nothing over pure dreamy vibes from the guitar. My heart breaks every time I hear “I thought that I was dreamin' when you said you love me/The start of nothin’” and “I ain't a kid no more/We'll never be those kids again”. The album gets even better with “Pink + White”, its first local peak. The gorgeous atmosphere and dense layering make it a fierce competitor for my favorite Frank Ocean track. It evokes meadows and flowers and sunshine and summertime despite lines like “It's all downhill from here”. “Be Yourself” is funny but kind of dumb. It’s not as good as “Good Guy” but better than “Facebook Story”. I don’t like “Solo” quite as much as “Ivy” or “Pink + White”, but it’s still damn good, particularly the first verse. It’s remarkable how full it feels even when it’s just Frank’s voice and an organ. The verses are elite, but the chorus holds the track back a bit. “Skyline To” feels the slightest bit insubstantial, but it’s pretty enough that I don’t care. It may be wallpaper, but it’s gorgeous wallpaper. Despite the recognition it has received, I still feel like “Self Control” is underrated. What a tearjerker! It’s hard to stop myself from belting out “You cut your hair, but you used to live a blonded life” every time I listen. I love love love love love it. “Good Guy” is my favorite interlude, Frank tells the story so well, powerfully conveying the despondency and disappointment of the experience. It  packs the punch of a full track in under a minute.

Frank’s immortal masterpiece comes in the form of *Blonde*’s centerpiece, “Nights”. No word fits the behemoth opening verse other than “masterful”. Frank finds an absolute flow state before shifting into an instantly memorable chorus over swelling synths. And, of course, what follows is one of the all-time great beat switches. Line like “Can't keep up a conversation/Can't nobody reach you”,  “Did you call me from a séance?/You are from my past life/Hope you're doin' well, bruh”, “Ooh, 'nani-'nani/This feel like a Quaalude/No sleep in my body/Ain't no bitch in my body”, “Wanna see nirvana, but don't wanna die yet/Wanna feel that 'na-'na, though, could you come by?”, and “Shut the fuck up, I don't want your conversation/Rollin' marijuana, that's a cheap vacation” haven’t left my head for the better half of a decade. I love the contrast between the exasperation of the first chorus delivery and the despondency of the second. It presages the sheer brokenness of this album’s second half. I find it genuinely mind-boggling that 3 Stacks came out of nowhere to lay down a verse as flawless as he did on “Solo (Reprise)” uncredited. Like, holy shit. Every bar is just pure gold. He sells the delivery on “After twenty years in, I'm so naive I was under the im—/‘Pression that everyone wrote they own verses/It's comin' back different, and yeah, that shit hurts me/I’m hummin' and whistlin' to those not deserving/I’ve stumbled and lived every word, was I working/Just way too hard?” perfectly. I love the sudden noise bomb that starts “Pretty Sweet”. It eventually settles into something frantic but smooth. The children’s choir singing “We know you're sugar/We know you're sweet like a sucker/Pretty sweet” gets stuck in my head every time. “Facebook Story” is admittedly pretty stupid. I believe he’s telling the truth but, like, it’s hard to believe he’s telling the truth. The French accent is infectious though. Sure, “Close to You” isn’t Frank’s melody, but he fits it in so perfectly. I have a soft spot for the Carpenters and I just love this tack. It’s the right amount of tender and hopeless. The back half of *Blonde* hits all the right notes. “White Ferrari” is fucking beautiful, just the masterpiece of the back hald. What a chorus! It shift through melodies and instrumentals like a chameleon, an absolute downer masterpiece. What am I supposed to do when hearing “I care for you still, and I will forever/That was my part of the deal, honest/We got so familiar” other than break down and cry? Like “Self Control”, I think “Seigfried” is completely underrated. It’s the low point of the album (complimentary). I can’t really think of anything that sounds more dejected. I love the guitar work, but when the strings kick in and the vocals break down, just inject it into my veins. Unfortunately, the album begins to slip with “Godspeed”. This track on its own is fine but not my favorite. Neither the melody nor the atmosphere much resonate with me. The first half of “Futura Free” is decent; the first verse is a little shaky, but verse three in particular has bars like “Tyler slept on my sofa, yeah/[REDACTED] go back that far”. But then the interviews start and I tune out. What a let down of an ending. Oh well.

This would be a strong contender for SS Tier if it had a different opener and closer. Alas, it’s stuck with just one S. What can you do?

Favorite tracks: Ivy, Pink + White, Solo, Skyline To, Self Control, Good Guy, Nights, Solo (Reprise), Pretty Sweet, Close to You, White Ferrari, Seigfried

Least favorite track: Nikes

# Aidan's review

This is an album that I really struggled to consolidate my thoughts on. On one hand, Ocean is talented. The obvious comparison is Bowie, not just for the parallels in boundary breaking as bisexual stars (Bowie might be just “bisexual” but it was the 70s), but in his talent for compelling vocal melodies that come seemingly out of nowhere. The big singles on here are incredible. "Ivy" is my personal favorite, the bridge just over half way through starting with "broke your heart last summer" is a tremendous earworm. "Solo" and its reprise are nearly as good in their wide ranging imagrey/ I love Andre 3000's line "So low that no more high horses, so hard to wear Polo/ When I do I cut the pony off". I'm also enough of a Pitchfork reader to be a sucker for the loose Wagner reference on "Seigfried". At the same time, there's some massive creative risks on this album that just totally whiff. The voice on "Nikes" is annoying and the lyrics "she need a ring like Carmelo" just irks this shit out of me. On the back end, Futura Free's lengthy silence into a not-great spoken word piece just doesn't land either. In the middle, "Be Yourself" is about average as a skit, meaning that it's pretty funny the first time around and rapidly ages, while Facebook Story is dreadful. In a week with Daft Punk, this was the most obnoxiously French shit I had to listen to, and feels dated in all the wrong ways. The fact it kills the momentum of one of the coolest transitions in this album in "Pretty Sweet" annoys me even more.  "White Ferrari" brings it mostly back though. "Close to You" and "Good Guy" are both venial sins in my book. It's nice that the catchy singles tend to be the long songs and the annoying stuff tends to be shorter, barring the opener and closer, but it feels like great songwriting talent marred by a lot of bad ideas relating to the album format. I'm putting it in A along with MM...FOOD and Life of Pablo, albums that I had similar feelings on. Damn cool album, and I can see if I listened to this at 16 I would have more forgiveness for the bad parts (the museum bit on Twin Fantasy doesn't exist in my mind), but alas I'm 8 years past that.
