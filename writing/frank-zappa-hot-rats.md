# Hargrove's review

Frank Zappa is one of the largest artists who I have never once listened to and knew next-to-nothing about. Going in to this, I knew that he was kind of a comedy rock guy, he was relevant across multiple decades, and he produced *Trout Mask Replica*. Given all of that, *Hot Rats* was very much not the album I expected. It’s more or less just 40 straight minutes of instrumental jazz jamming. There’s no fluff, no distraction, no ego, just some good old-fashioned guitar wanking. I didn’t find it transcendent or anything, but I did find it quite enjoyable to listen to. I would get pretty zenned out each time and let it wash over me. Everything flows together nicely and each track feels connected to the last. Multiple tracks have a 70s TV show theme song feel to them, which I can’t imagine is a coincidence. The solos are consistently quite good, with the guitar a recurring highlight, but lots of good fiddle and winds as well. The melodies are a bit of a weak point in my opinion; they’re all nice enough while I listen, but this record really could have used an all-time memorable melody to kick it up into A Tier. As it is, *Hot Rats* is just kind of a fun, enjoyable, vibey jazz fusion record that I had a good time listening to and that I’m currently finding difficult to say things about.

“Peaches En Regalia” is definitely a good track, but I don’t entirely understand its status. It’s my third favorite on the album, as well as the one that most sounds like TV music. I like every timbre used and each solo, with the clarinet part being my favorite. I like “Willie the Pimp” even better. I’m a big fan of the Beefheart vocals because it’s just the perfect situation for them, appropriately bluesy and greasy. The central melody, whether in Beefheart’s vocals or the fiddle, has serious swagger. The extended instrumental section is full of super funky guitar solos and immaculate drumming. I particularly love the recapitulation of the fiddle at the end. The album peaks with “Son of Mr. Green Genes”. The opening section of this track is so grandiose that it wins me over right off the bat. There are a lot of great moments, and I feel satisfied when it ends. “Little Umbrellas” is a cooldown for the best part of the album. It’s the first true slow track, with a spy jazz feel. I especially like the flutes. “The Gumbo Variations” is the most pure jazz of all the tracks, reminding me of *Headhunters* in particular. The sax, fiddle, and guitar solos are all good in isolation, but they go on for way too long, and it makes this track feel incredibly bloated. I don’t love it. The album ends nice and chill with “It Must Be a Camel”. It’s probably the weirdest track, going all over the place and never falling into any one groove, but it’s consistently enjoyable to listen to.

This album is a solid addition to B Tier. Nothing revelatory, but another mood I have at my disposal when I want to.

Favorite tracks: Peaches En Regalia, Willie the Pimp, Son of Mr. Green Genes, Little Umbrellas

Least favorite track: The Gumbo Variations
