# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

I had never listened to Zappa until a couple weeks ago, and *Hot Rats* was nothing like I expected. It was sleek, smooth, complex, and an enjoyable listen. Unfortunately, *Uncle Meat* sounds a lot more like what I was worried Zappa would sound like. It is an utter clusterfuck of a record. Most of the individual tracks are listenable, but in succession they become so disjointed that it tries my patience before the end of side 1. And given that this is a double album, there are four sides to contend with. Why on Earth did this need to be a double album? It is exhausting to listen to this record, which is even more of a shame because there are some real gems on here. “Dog Breath, in the Year of the Plague” and its successor “The Dog Breath Variations” play with a very strong melody. “Sleeping in a Jar” also touches greatness, though I’m hopelessly biased by its appearance in *Madvillainy*. I was utterly charmed by Ian Underwood in “Ian Underwood Whips It Out (Live on Stage in Copenhagen)”. I enjoy “Mr. Green Genes” as much as I did when it was on *Hot Rats*. And the entire King Kong suite of tracks on side 4 really is an accomplishment. It’s just a shame that I can’t even begin to understand what the rest of the record was going for. Most charitably, I can chalk it up to being the same thing that Faust would go for on its self-titled debut just a few years later, a quasi-ambient recontextualization of creepy carnival music. I did come around to that one. But *Uncle Meat* is both too long and too choppy for that to work here. Clearly, the Mothers had a ton of material to cram out the door before Zappa fully went out on his own. I wish they had saved half of it for archival outtake albums.

“Uncle Meat: Main Title Theme” starts the album with one of its nicer instrumental pieces. It definitely signals weirdness but is still quite the pleasant listen. It’s almost baroque, a feeling that comes back again and again as the album progresses. I am really not crazy about the spoken word skits like “The Voice of Cheese”. I’m somewhat interested in the mixture of blues and Spanish guitar on “Nine Types of Industrial Pollution”, but it’s just too aimless and random and noodly in the worst way for me to care. “Zolar Czakl” is a short but pretty cool Faust-like (Faustian?) instrumental. We finally have the first real song with “Dog Breath, in the Year of the Plague”. I find the vocal melody and 50s vibes both very nice, and so I like this song a good bit. “The Legend of the Golden Arches” feels like a more complete version of “Zolar Czakl”. I particularly like the harpsichord stuff at the end. “Louie Louie (at the Royal Albert Hall in London)” is one of the funnier interludes, and I’m glad this historically significant song found its way onto our list one way or another. “The Dog Breath Variation” brings back a melody I already really liked, so I’ll take it. I have no idea if I would still “Sleeping in a Jar” without Madlib having sampled it, but that’s just not the world we live in. Shit slaps. I’m not thrilled to hear from Suzy Creemcheese again on “Our Bizarre Relationship”. “The Uncle Meat Variations” is more baroque Faustian jamming. I like some of it, but it’s long and I dislike more of it than I like. And boy am I already tired by this point in the listen. “Electric Aunt Gemima” is catchy as shit but the annoying vibe and voices ruin it. I admire this album’s schizophrenic structure, but “Prelude to King Kong” is too free jazz for my liking. “God Bless America (Live at Whiskey A Go Go)” is a very funny song to hear on this album, though it’s really just a novelty. “A Pound for a Brown (On the Bus)” brings more circus music. How on Earth am I still on disc 1 of a double album? Why is this a double album? I love the story told on “Ian Underwood Whips It Out (Live on Stage in Copenhagen)”. Underwood seems like a good dude, and he sure does play pretty damn mean.

I liked “Son of Mr. Green Genes” on *Hot Rats* and I like it in its original form here on *Uncle Meat*. I enjoy the new, plodding tempo, the nonsensical lyrics, and the vibrato marimba thing. “We Can Shoot You”, on the other hand, sucks very badly. It’s the most annoying track on an album just lousy with them. “‘If We’d All Been Living in California…’” is honestly a pretty bold inclusion on this record. It’s a genuinely interesting insight into Zappa’s personality and the dynamic within the Mothers. I enjoy “The Air”, another fun 50s ditty that makes for a pleasant listen. It also adds a cool rhythmic trick where the verses go on and on. It reminds me of a song on Beefheart’s debut. “Project X” starts out pleasant and then veers off into total bullshit. There are cool elements on “Cruising for Burgers” but it’s a little dense and I don’t have the energy left at this point for it. This album is just relentless. I do, however, rather like the entirety of the King Kong suite. It’s the only point on the album where the album feels like one coherent thing for an extended period of time. “King Kong (As Played by the Mothers in a Studio)” immediately generates momentum. It introduces both a decent melody and a pulsating, off-rhythm bass that drives everything. The keyboard soloing and swinging drums on “King Kong (In Its Magnificence as Interpreted by Dom DeWild” are fantastic. “King Kong (As Motorhead Explains It)” has a sax solo so lively that you can hear the player gasping for air. My least favorite section is “King Kong (The Gardner Varieties)” because the timbre of whatever that instrument is gives me a headache. However, it’s not that bad. There’s a little bullshit on “King Kong (As Played by 3 Deranged Good Humor Trucks)” but at least it’s short. “King Kong (Live on a Flat Bed Diesel in the Middle of a Race Track at a Miami Festival…The Underwood Ramifications)” ends the jam on a high note.

I don’t hate everything about this album, but I’m having a very hard time justifying not rating it near the bottom. Even if I intellectually respect some of the choices, it’s just an absolute chore to listen to until the final side. I’ve never felt this drained by an album before.

Favorite tracks: Uncle Meat: Main Title Theme, Dog Breath, in the Year of the Plague, The Legend of the Golden Arches, The Dog Breath Variations, Sleeping in a Jar, Ian Underwood Whips It Out (Live on Stage in Copenhagen), Mr. Green Genes, The Air, King Kong (As Played by the Mothers in a Studio), King Kong (In Its Magnificence as Interpreted by Dom DeWild), King Kong (As Motorhead Explains It)

Least favorite track: We Can Shoot You
