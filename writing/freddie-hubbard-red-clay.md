# Hargrove's review

Thanks to this list, I’ve discovered that I rather enjoy jazz fusion. Miles Davis’ *Bitches Brew* and Herbie Hancock’s *Headhunters* are two of my favorite jazz records so far, and, from the more fusion less jazz end of things, I really like Steely Dan’s *Aja*. On Freddie Hubbard’s *Red Clay*, I’ve found another record in the style that I quite like. Well, at the very least, I adore the first two tracks. Side two of this record is unfortunately pedestrian in comparison. Still, adding “Red Clay” and “Delphia” to my personal jazz canon made this well worth the listen. Talent and chemistry ooze out of every note played by this quintet. Not only is there no weakest link among the five players, this consistent level at which they play hovers well above average. To me, the two stars of this record are Herbie Hancock on piano and Lenny White on drums. While I love all the soloing too, this album really leaves its mark in the rhythm section, and the gliding piano and shuffling drums imbue so much charisma. I don’t know how Herbie’s fingers can move like that; they’ve been touched by God, I suppose. This strength is most apparent during solo sections, where the piano and drums keep a constant, dynamic crescendo without stepping on any of the soloists. It’s truly impressive. Ron Carter’s bass is fine as well, but it stands out to me less often. Joe Henderson lays out some banger tenor sax solos, but I want to give Freddie Hubbard particular credit for his soloing, as well as his playing during head melodies. Similar to Davis on *Bitches Brew*, he plays the trumpet in such a way that you can tell he’s leading everything you hear. Every note he plays cuts through, straight down the middle, commanding attention even over the fun rhythm section. Sadly, the album loses its groove a bit on side two, when things get choppy. But the first two tracks work so well that I have to put this in either high B Tier or low A Tier.

The start of “Red Clay” reminds me of *A Love Supreme*, a nice intro of (good) bullshit where the players collect themselves before starting in earnest. Everything goes quiet around the one-minute mark, and then the drums and bass turn on the funk and never look back. This track does a great job of keeping enough jazz in the delicate jazz-funk balance; I prefer *Headhunters* as an overall record, but *Red Clay* does a better job of asserting the jazz elements. Herbie’s electric piano sounds like pure sex, as do White’s shuffling drums. Hubbard has a stellar trumpet solo, followed by a nice on from Henderson’s tenor sax as well. Right after, Herbie delivers the best solo on the entire album. Everything builds even throughout the solo, resulting in a cacophony controlled by Hubbard’s trumpet. This is a great track, on par with “Chameleon” or “Bitches Brew”. The second track, “Delphia”, provides a fantastic downtempo counterpart to the title track. It keeps the funky charm despite slowing things way down. It sounds like funky Charlie Brown to me in a way, with the relaxed grooves and similar chord progressions. I give a lot of credit to Hubbard’s composition, which flits around wonderfully from instrument to instrument. Of course, Herbie’s piano is important to moving things along as well. The rhythm and smoothness do not carry over into the next track, “Suite Sioux”. There are a lot of good sounds here, but it’s disjoint and chaotic. The drums are jittery and the solos sound kind of aggro. “The Intrepid Fox” gets things back on track a bit, but the drums are still too choppy for my taste and it ends up only alright.

If this album were only the first two tracks, I think I could justify putting this in A Tier. As it is, I’m putting it near the top of B Tier. I can definitely see myself moving it up in a future readjustment.

Favorite tracks: Red Clay, Delphia

Least favorite track: Suite Sioux
