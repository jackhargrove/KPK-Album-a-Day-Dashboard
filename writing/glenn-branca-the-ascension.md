# Hargrove's review

You know it’s gonna be fun when one song has to specifically point out that it’s consonant. But actually unfortunately it wasn’t as fun as I had hoped. I fucked up and let myself build expectations around this one, as I was so intrigued by the genre marker “totalism” when I first heard about it a few weeks ago. I was so curious what it would sound like. One guy described it as a “symphony of chainsaws”. This is why I try not to read too much about the sound of something before I listen. I felt pretty underwhelmed overall. I was hoping for something completely alien, but it just ended up being more dissonant post-rock. It was still good though, and I’d love to talk about the parts I liked. Some albums cause me to see a color very vividly, and this album made me see brown the entire time. Everything about this album is a dark, muddled brown. The sounds are dull and loose, and they all blend into each other like a pile of rubber bands (or exhaust pipes). The opener “Lesson No. 2” was one of my favorites, as especially the first half really leans into that puddle of mud atmosphere. I also respected the repeated chord in the second half, as it was one of the few parts of the album that genuinely hurt my ears. I also really enjoyed the closer, “The Ascension”. It brings back that overwhelming muddiness from the opening, and it features an awesome drum breakdown early on. The use of silence is brilliant (eat your heart out, John Cage), really making you feel viscerally just how much noise is going on at once. I also really like the way that it build, feeling more and more immense like a wave that never crashes. Parts of it feel like if the orchestra in “A Day in the Life” by the Beatles were the entire song.

The entire middle of the album didn’t do too much for me though. I like the first melody in “The Spectacular Commodity”, but once it gets into a groove it just feels a little too…upbeat? Feels weird to say, but there was a brightness I didn’t love. I did like how it sounded like melodies were being passed back and forth. “Structure” didn’t really stand out to me in any way despite repeated listens, it felt very insignificant. “Light Field (In Consonance)” had a cool surf-y descending main guitar line, and I liked the staticky guitars in the foreground, but again the crescendo/accelerando does little for me. I do really like the use of stereo with the cymbals though at the end.

Overall, I think I need more time to digest and explore this. I will definitely listen more in the future, but as of right now, I’m underwhelmed.

Favorite tracks: Lesson No. 2, The Ascension

Least favorite track: Structure
