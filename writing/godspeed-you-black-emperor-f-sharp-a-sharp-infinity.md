# Hargrove's review

I listened to the two canonical GYBE records back around freshman year of college, and I’ve only ever really gone back to *Lift Yr. Skinny Fists Like Antennas to Heaven* since. I couldn’t really have told you why, as *F♯ A♯ ∞* did leave an impression on me, or so I thought. I remembered the apocalyptic atmosphere, I remembered a decent number of the spoken word samples, and I remembered enjoying it. So why did I never really go back? I now know it’s because “The Dead Flag Blues” fucking sucks. Well, that’s an overstatement. But man is that an all style no substance composition and it’s really frustrating to listen to. It does a great job of building up the atmosphere that the rest of the record will get deeper into, but there’s very little by way of actually enjoyable music. It takes *so long* for anything real to form, not until after the 10:00 mark do we get the quasi-Western section. The outro is a nice bit of music, but it feels completely unearned after there was no real build or musical intensity prior. I was having trouble working up the will for re-listens on this, and it was super surprising to me. I came to the realization earlier today when I drifted off to sleep during a listen. I woke up at some point during a faster section on “Providence” and I was absolutely floored, it was amazing. I really think the opener holds this album back, requiring too much buy-in up front before you’ve seen what they can really do.

“East Hastings” shows more good sense right off the bat than “The Dead Flag Blues”, with a tighter sample and some nice bagpipes. “The Sad Mafioso…” still drags in its intro, but at least the unresolved guitar line and drums make for a genuinely ominous atmosphere. Eventually it builds into this heavy, lethargic groove that I really enjoyed, almost reminiscent of some doom metal. The violin, viola, drums trio is really pretty, and the accelerando is extremely well done, very impressive work for what is probably the first accelerando they ever put on wax. The droning strings in that section also add a lot of atmosphere. Unfortunately, “Drugs in Tokyo”/“Black Helicopter” goes back to extended noodly noise. I can put up with it more because it comes after Godspeed have finally demonstrated what they’re capable of, but man does this album have a much worse killer-to-filler ratio than *Skinny Fists*.

“Providence” is by far the finest composition on this album, and really impressive how it holds my attention for half an hour. The guy in the sample sounds like a mixture of Trump and Bernie, and the ambient instrumental sounds like *Kid A* before *Kid A*. “Dead Metheny…” is perhaps my favorite movement, the violin in the beginning is really stellar and I love the part with the smoldering back-and-forth between the bass and the guitar. The bells remind me of *Tubular Bells*, and I love that record so it’s a great compliment. The tribal drumming at the end of the build is very well-done too. “Kicking Horse on Brokenhill” is a breakdown done well, where steam starts to build again immediately. The directionality here is nice given the aimlessness of some other parts of the record. The overarching melody in this movement is my favorite in the record, and I’m intrigued by the surf-y tremolo in the guitar. The silence is an unfortunate remnant of the CD era that is frustrating in streaming (looking at you “Irene” by Beach House) but I’ll look past it. The outro is one final explosion with an interesting effect on the panning of the drums that causes them to be out of sync. Great composition.

I really don’t care for “The Dead Flag Blues”, but some of “East Hastings” and all of “Providence” makes up for it. Definitely not as good as *Skinny Fists*, but I think they work great in tandem, with the apocalyptic atmosphere in this one contrasting with the hope that is to come.

Favorite tracks: East Hastings, Providence

Least favorite track: The Dead Flag Blues
