# Write-up

Godspeed You! Black Emperor is a post-rock band from Montreal that formed in 1994. The original 3 members of the band are Efrim Menuck on guitar and keyboard, Mike Moya on guitar, and Mauro Pezzente on bass. Over the next few years, the band would grow in size immensely, with many artists joining and leaving in a revolving door fashion. Eventually, the group stabilized before their debut album with Menuck, Moya, and David Bryant on guitars, Pezzente and Thierry Amar on bass, Aidan Girt and Bruce Cawdron on drums, Sophie Trudeau on violin, and Norsola Johnson on cello. Their name comes from a 1976 Japanese documentary on a gang of bikers known as the Black Emperors. As Carms has pointed out, GY!BE are noted for their extreme left-wing politics and anti-government beliefs; while on tour in Oklahoma in 2003, they were detained by the FBI on suspicions of terrorism (although they were completely cleared). The group released their first album, *F♯A♯∞*, in 1997 to limited success but developed a cult following from word-of-mouth. It is also on this list. In 2000, GY!BE released their second album, *Lift Your Skinny Fists like Antennas to Heaven*, to critical acclaim. It has since found itself on many “Best Albums of the 2000s” lists and is considered their signature record, although *F♯A♯∞* is also well-loved by their fanbase. GY!BE released their third album, *Yanqui U.X.O.*, in 2002 before starting a 10 year hiatus. Since they got back together in 2012, GY!BE have released 5 more albums.

*Lift Your Skinny Fists like Antennas to Heaven* was GY!BE’s critical breakthrough, as well as a breakthrough for the brand of post-rock played by GY!BE (which I refer to as “mainline post-rock”, but that is not a real term). The album contains 4 20 minute compositions that are all divided into movements that were named in a diagram that was included with the record. The musical sections are all instrumental pieces with a particular focus on crescendos (a crescendo is the classical music term for when music gradually gets louder; some have dubbed GY!BE’s style as “crescendocore”). The instrumental sections are spliced together with field recordings, or non-musical recordings that were made outside of a studio. Efrim Menuck (who is the creative leader of GY!BE) is a graduate of film school, and he got the idea for sound collage from his days editing movies. GY!BE’s debut album *F♯A♯∞* used field recordings to create a crushing, post-apocalyptic soundscape; *Skinny Fists* is much brighter in tone.

This is the third post-rock album we’ve listened to, after Slint’s *Spiderland* and Swans’ *Soundtracks for the Blind*. However, this album is more the type of sound that the average music fan thinks of when they hear “post-rock” than either of those albums are, which is why I refer to this style as “mainline post-rock”. Like the other post genres, post-rock is a reaction to rock music that uses the same general instruments and ideas of rock music but repackages them in a new and artsy way. For post-rock specifically, the vast majority of bands reacted to rock music by using guitars, bass guitars, and drums to make sprawling, largely instrumental ambient pieces that are composed in a way not dissimilar to classical. Post-rock bands are often heavily influenced by minimalist contemporary classical composers, particularly Steve Reich. Although *Spiderland* more or less invented the genre, Slint’s hardcore roots separate them from later developments. *Soundtracks for the Blind* on the other hand is closer to mainline post-rock, but the use of all those old Swans recordings make that record altogether unlike anything else; Swans’ later records like *The Seer* and *To Be Kind* are much more similar to today’s album. GY!BE were leaders of what is called the “second-wave” of post-rock that gained popularity in indie circles in the early 2000s, alongside bands like Explosions in the Sky, Mogwai, and Sigur Rós.

# Hargrove's review

Don’t know why but having some procrastination issues today. This is the third album in a row that I’ve already heard and have strong opinions on. I’m excited that I’ve only already heard one of the 5 albums next week. This album is great and is pretty much a perfection of the entire post-rock genre. It’s an hour and a half of masterful tension build and relief. I sincerely respect the desire to pursue this level of beauty and elegance. This was probably my first full listen in 2 years. Interestingly, I liked it less today than I usually do. Might be a mood thing because I have a bit of a cold, but I actually think part of it is that I didn’t listen to *F♯A♯∞* first. I like this album better than *F♯A♯∞*, but the crushing apocalyptic hellscape of *F♯A♯∞* makes the uplifting joy of some of the brightest parts of *Skinny Fists* feel all the sweeter. There were some parts of “Storm” for me today that rung hollow when usually it’s one of my favorite tracks of all time. My ranking reflects a middle ground between my historical love of this record and my more muted response today.

“Storm” is a great opener, and the first movement in particular is an incredible mission statement for the album. There’s great build and crescendoing, I’m a big fan of the non-waltz 3/4, I really love the the swelling during the transition from bar 8 to bar 9 of the repeated 9 bar phrase. Just so aspirational and ambitious. My least favorite part today was the horn section. The second movement really takes its sweet time to build but I like the wailing guitar that starts around 10:40, the great dissonance around 12:20, land the bounciness that begins around 14:00. I really like Aidan’s analysis that the AM/PM field recording grounds everything in reality. The radio chatter in the final movement is super eerie, but overall I wish it hadn’t ended on such a whimper.

“Static” is my least favorite track, the primary movement “World Police and Friendly Fire” is phenomenal but the other 10 minutes are just not dynamic enough for me. Things really don’t start cooking at all until the third movement, 4 and a half minutes into the piece. Once the strings start though, they are hauntingly beautiful. In the fourth movement, the guitar ostinato feels aimless in a nice way, and the drone is cool too. The violin melody is awesome and feels very cyclical, and the accelerando at the end is extremely well done. The final movement is far too long for how little is going on.

The field recording at the beginning of “Sleep” is one of the better ones, plays with the idea of nostalgia in an interesting way. I don’t know shit about Coney Island, but the way the guy was describing it just sounded very untrustworthy to me. I think he’s full of shit. I really like the guitar in the second movement (“Monheim”). It’s absolutely aching; I love the way you can hear the individual strings being strummed. There’s also something refreshing about how low-key this movement is in comparison to the grandiosity of the rest of the record. The third movement (“Broken Windows”) is also great, and around 18:30 shit gets super emo. This movement really clarifies for me what I like about post-rock that I hate about progressive rock, it’s the influence of punk and especially hardcore in both style and method that just didn’t exist when prog was in its heyday. And the drums are funky as hell. Was my favorite composition listening today.

My favorite part of the montage at the beginning of “Like Antennas to Heaven…” was “Glockenspiel Duet”. Just sounded cool. The 5th movement has a great surf vibe at the beginning before transitioning into something altogether quite similar to conventional indie rock. The back section of this movement in particular is one of my favorite moments on the album. The pulsing in the final movement is a great way to end the album.

I will probably go the Aidan route and try to listen to this at least once a year going forward. It had been too long since my last listen. I really do think *F♯A♯∞* is important in a way for building up some tension that this album releases for me. And on a good day, this really does speak to me. At the right moment, this album feels like a complete triumph of the human spirit.

Favorite tracks: Storm, Sleep, Like Antennas to Heaven…

Least favorite track: Static
