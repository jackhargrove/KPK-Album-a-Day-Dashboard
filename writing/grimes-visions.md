# Write-up

"Basically I'm really impressionable and have no sense of consistency in anything I do." - Grimes

Grimes is the stage name for one Clair Boucher, age 37, from Vancouver Canada. Grimes began making music in her freshman year at McGill, taking her stage name from the options for music genre on MySpace (not knowing at all what "grime" music was). That same year, she attempted to ride from New Orleans to Minneapolis on the Mississippi in a homemade houseboat. Her first two albums, Geidi Primes (inspired by Dune) and Halfaxa, were released in 2010 and were moderately successful, enabling her to make a living as a musician after she was kicked out of McGill later that year for skipping classes. She began work on visions while burned out from the stresses of touring and quickly ran into writers block. Her manager gave her a strict 3 week deadline to have the album done, and facing down a time crunch, Grimes turned to the friend of artists from the Beatles to Warhol: amphetamines. With the windows blacked out, she worked for 9 days without food or sleep, finishing most of the album on the last day. The album was recorded on GarageBand. To release the album, she signed to the label 4AD, home to Pixies, Cocteau Twins, Bauhaus, St. Vincent and Big Thief among others.

Visions was a mega hit and launched into her current stardom that has allowed her to make a litany of tabloid worthy choices, including dating Elon Musk. She released 2 more well received albums, Art Angels and Miss Anthropocene before trading her hard drugs for Twitter. She has gone on to give "the worst performance in the history of Coachella", delayed her 6th album, "Book 1", for 3 years and counting, and released her own, different AI thing called "Grok".

# Hargrove's review

Obviously a record I was already familiar with, and a great record. “Oblivion” and “Genesis” are obviously both synthpop masterpieces, but this album is genuinely really deep. For a record with two songs that have over 10x the streams of any other, there is really not much filler here. I don’t care for “Eight” much at all, and “Skin” drags on a bit too long at the end of the record, but for the most part this is a tight collection of consistently good tracks. I’ve always respected how “Infinite Love without Fulfilment” opens mid-drum loop, throwing you into the weird world of Grimes with no warning. “Genesis” is bouncy synth perfection, but the weirdly intense drum line I think is what really makes the track. “Oblivion” does with melody what “Genesis” achieves through layering and percussion, creating an earworm that hasn’t left my head since the first time I heard it. “Eight” is kind of annoying, and I wish it were not on this album. “Circumambient” picks things right back up again with that alien feel, but what I especially like about this track is the descending synth line that feels ever-so-slightly off-beat. “Vowels = space and time” is a personal favorite of mine and one that I find myself going back to often, driven by a simple but angular downbeat synth and some beautiful vocal work. “Visiting Statue” is nice and short, with a descending vocal line that I rather like. “Be a Body” is yet another standout, bringing back the same idea of the synth I like from “Vowels = space and time” and one of the best builds on the entire album. “Colour of Midnight (Antiochus)” is a strong album cut, nice and bouncy. “Symphonia IX (My Wait Is U)” is another song that could’ve been a single, sounding absolutely angelic from front to back. “Nightmusic” is certainly a deep cut, but it introduces some sounds I like that we haven’t heard elsewhere. “Skin” has a pretty melody, but it just goes on too long for this deep into the record. “Know the Way” finishes the record perfectly, with the peaceful water flowing into elegant guitar work and a gorgeous conclusion. Really great record, so beautiful, so unique.

Favorite tracks: Genesis, Oblivion, Vowels = space and time, Be a Body, Symphonia IX (My Wait is U), Know the Way

Least favorite track: Eight

# Aidan's review

Grimes has some of the worst artistic instincts of someone who has made a living making relatively critically acclaimed art. If you watch the (self directed) music video for Genesis, Grimes drives a desert around in a Cadillac Escalade and cradles an albino python, interspersed with clips of her swinging around a sword in the ocean wearing a hat that says “PUSSY”. And yet, in Visions, despite a seeming general inability to recognize bad taste and the effects of a shipload of amphetamines, Grimes doesn’t fuck with the formula, and the result is amazing. It’s 40 minutes of dream pop R&B beats with Grimes’s already exotic voice layered in effects. After listening to it a few times, it’s remarkable how little the record does considering how exotic it sounds. There’s not a ton of instrumentals, it’s just the synth playing simple melodies with a drum machine backing and Grime’s vocals aren’t really that treated. Her voice is perfectly suited to this style, Oblivion is my favorite song and her lisp adds to the childlike vocals about vulnerability. There’s not really a weak song that you can point to. Nightmusic’s opening chant reminds me of Lana’s A&W over a decade later, the constant repeating of the chorus of Skin is catchy enough to work and the songs have just enough variety to be memorable while maintaining a consistent style. The only thing I can point to bad instincts bleeding through is the fucking around with stereo that happens a bit too much for my taste. But that’s a nitpick against what it generally just a great album
