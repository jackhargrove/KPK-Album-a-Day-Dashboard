# Write-up

Have a Nice Life is a rock duo from Middletown, Connecticut consisting of musicians Dan Barrett and Tim Macuga. Instead of playing different instruments like in most bands, Barrett writes the core songs, Macuga fleshes them out and adds instrumental layers, and Barrett produces. Have a Nice Life formed when Barrett and Macuga met in 2000, both around age 20, and chose to leave the bands they were in to play with each other. They were both fans of post-punk and goth rock, particularly the 80s group Sisters of Mercy, and tried to emulate that sound in their music. They also take influence from shoegaze bands like my bloody valentine, post-punk bands like Joy Division, black metal bands like Sunn O))), industrial rock bands like Nine Inch Nails, and Swans bands like Swans.

Have a Nice Life began the process of recording their debut album, *Deathconsciousness*, in 2003. The duo lived far apart from each other and worked real jobs, so the process of recording was very long and slow. Their recording budget was under $1,000 and they used comically lo-fi equipment. At one point, their hard drive crashed and they lost all the masters. At some point, Barrett’s dad died, and this convinced him to lock in and actually get the album done. While traveling in Europe, he was struck by inspiration and drafted a 70 page novella telling the story of a fake Medieval Italian man named Antiochus and a cult that successfully kills God. Barrett is mentally ill and his art deals heavily with his depression and his obsessive fear of death. They slightly altered a late 18th century painting of a dying French revolutionary to use as the cover. *Deathconsciousness* was finally completed and released in 2008, 5 years after production began. It was released on Enemies List Home Recordings, Barrett’s own independent record label.

After release, *Deathconsciousness* received virtually no attention. Have a Nice Life were an unknown Connecticut local band not signed to a major label and with very limited distribution. A couple smaller publications picked it up and gave good reviews, but the album was almost entirely ignored and forgotten. Except…this was 2008. The publications no longer had a monopoly on music discussion. The internet, namely /mu/ and RateYourMusic.com somehow caught wind of the album in the years after its release, and they became obsessive fans. To this day, it’s one of the most notable albums to have been “discovered” by the internet. As this was happening, though, Barrett and Macuga had no idea. Barrett started a solo project called Giles Corey where he could make even more depressing music. He has another solo project, Black Wing, for his electronic music. He and Macuga also have two other bands together: Gate, a post-hardcore group, and Nahvalr, a black metal band. They also released two more albums as Have a Nice Life, one in 2014 and one in 2019. Both to this day work normal jobs. Barrett owns a real estate marketing agency, and Macuga is a high school English teacher. But the reputation of *Deathconsciousess* kept growing. Its notability increased to such an extent in the 2010s that by the end of the decade they were able to sell out shows (in indie venues). And in the 2020s, the renewed interest in shoegaze has increased its popularity even further. Now, obviously Have a Nice Life is still no Taylor Swift, but it is shockingly well-known for a passion project self-released by an indie band 20 years ago. It is often considered one of the most depressing albums of all time.

# Hargrove's review

I am in the weird position where I think this is a really good album but at the same time I’m a little bit baffled at just how highly regarded it is. It’s at #60 all time on the RYM charts! I don’t really know what tone to use for this review as a result, so I’ll just be very explicit with where I stand. I really like the sound of post-punk, and I think some of the tracks on here do that sound justice as much as any band from the early 80s. “Bloodhail” in particular is a modern classic of post-punk. I think it’s remarkably impressive that so many good songs were made by a couple of nobodies and that it’s had the impact that it’s had. I think “Earthmover” is a good song but I think it’s overrated, I would probably rank it fourth on this album. I intended to read the booklet that comes along with the album this week but I didn’t have the time, so I will refrain from making a judgment as to the quality of the overall story in the album. Unfortunately, this also applies to lyrical analysis because the lyrics make so many references to the content of the booklet. I think this album, which is genuinely lo-fi, does a good job of balancing the line between so lo-fi as to be grating, and trying to imitate higher-fi production techniques. It’s right in that sweet spot. However, I do think there’s a bit of an over-reliance on that lo-fi wall of noise effect. I do think this album justifies its existence as a double album more than most, but it’s still too long. We don’t need all of those forgettable tracks on the second half of disc 1. It could definitely have been pared down to 70 minutes.

“A Quick One…” is a fantastic tone setter for this album. The repeating guitar part is foreboding enough on its own, but the background electronic flourishes are straight up ominous. The whole thing is also very spacey. I also appreciate the irony of making a song referred to as “A Quick One” a 7 minute slow ambient piece. “Bloodhail” is far and away the best song on the album. The guitar work makes the song, it’s so powerful and it transitions beteen simmering and climactic so seamlessly. I’m also a huge fan of the consolidating vocal lines at the end. A true post-punk banger. I don’t hate “The Big Gloom” as much as Carms does, but it certainly doesn’t need to be as long as it is. It’s probably the most shoegaze track on the album and I enjoy the washed out effects. It’s alright. “Hunter” is a bit on the slower side but the drum programming is quite good. The best part is two-thirds of the way through when it gets loud and picks up. Enough to make it one of my favorites. The rest of side 1 is unfortunately underwhelming. “Telephony” is, to me, the most unjustified track on this album. It’s short and adds basically nothing other than a decent drum line. “Who Would Leave…” I’m a little bit torn on. The atmosphere is cool and creepy and I like the vocal melody, but it feels out of place and doesn’t propel the album forward at all. “There Is No Food” is a good atmospheric closer to the first half, but once again it’s just another okay song.

“Waiting for Black Metal Records…” injects some much-needed life back into the record right away. I love the drum programming, I love the volume, I love the driving guitars, I love the velocity, and I love the build. A banger. The intro to “Holy Fucking Shit: 40,000” shakes up the sound a bit in a good way. The electronic beep boop elements are really interesting. They’re bright and upbeat, but so, so cold. The song feels like a barren, snowy Caribbean island. The vocal melody is among the catchiest on the entire album. When the second half gets loud and overbearing, almost oppressive, it’s just phenomenal. Probably my second favorite track after “Bloodhail”. “The Future” is a bit of a short interlude, but it’s good and additive. “Deep, Deep” was consistently one of my favorite songs, and it was not a name that I had remembered in my mind coming in like “Earthmover” or “Holy Fucking Shit”. The pulsating synth in the chorus is positively transcendent. The effect on the vocals is so cool. The guitar is relentless. In my opinion, this is the most underrated song on the album. “I Don’t Love”, on the other hand, might be the most overrated. It’s not terrible by any means, but it’s kind of just…meh. I think my issue with it is that I find the melody kind of annoying. The wall of sound and the drum programming are good though. “Earthmover” is certainly a banger, and a worthy closer to the album. That little synth motif ties the entire track together so well. Once the wall of sound hits it doesn’t look back. An intense and emotional song.

Overall, I would say this album is “overrated”, but only in the sense that people in my circles have decided it’s among the greatest albums of all-time. I don’t agree with that, but I do think it’s very good. I’m in a pretty good place life-wise right now though, I would love to revisit next time I’m depressed.

Favorite tracks: A Quick One Before the Eternal Worm Devours Connecticut, Bloodhail, Hunter, Waiting for Black Metal Records to Come in the Mail, Holy Fucking Shit: 40,000, Deep, Deep, Earthmover

Least favorite track: Telephony
