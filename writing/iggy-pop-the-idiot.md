# Write-up

To most people remotely familiar with Iggy Pop, he's known for his former copious drug use, continued crazed concert antics and his continued good health at 78 despite the former two. That depiction is a stark contrast to his roots, as he grew up as James Osterberg Jr., a well behaved, well liked and well dressed kid just outside of Ann Arbor. He played the drums starting in 5th grade and was a member of a few high school bands, including the Iguanas, which got him the nickname "Iggy". He attended the local University of Michigan for a while before dropping out and moving to Chicago to pursue a career playing blues rock. He played in a few bands before forming the Psychedelic Stooges with brothers Scott and Ron Asheton on drums and guitar respectively and Dave Alexander on bass. There, he also got the last name Pop from a local cartoon character said to resemble Iggy. Pop has cited two inspirations for the Stooges, the Doors, specifically the on stage antics of Jim Morrison, and the Untouchable, a completely unknown girl's band from Princeton who gave them a place to crash and a performance that apparently wowed the Psychedelic Stooges. The band signed with Elektra and dropped the "Psychedelic" after getting the OK from Moe.

The Stooges were better known for Pop's on stage performances than any of their music. He would cut himself with glass shards, smear food on his chest, flash the audience with his penis, and stage dive, a move which he is often credited with popularizing. They released a self titled album, and a followup, Fun House, neither of which was critically or commercially successful at the time. By this point, the entire band was addicted to heroin and their unheralded playing had gotten even worse, and two months later, Pop found himself in Max's Kansas City, a nightclub in Union Square, and met David Bowie. The two soon hit it off and Bowie persuaded him to sign a new recording contract and get the band back together. Pop reformed the Stooges for a third album, Raw Power, which Bowie produced. It did not sell well, and the production was criticized for its mixing by Pop and Bowie. Despite poor sales, it managed to be a key influence on the Sex Pistols, Smiths and Kurt Cobain. The Stooges broke up again, and would not reunited until 2003.

Trying to stop chasing the dragon, Pop checked into a psychiatric hospital, where Bowie was one of his few visitors. When he got out, Pop went with Bowie to West Berlin, both aiming to finally kick their respective drug habits. Bowie's stint in Berlin would become famous for his Berlin trilogy (of which Low was the first), but first he began work on The Idiot with Pop. Bowie set to work assembling a backing band for Pop, including the studio owner and onetime Magma bassist Laurent Thibault, Michel Santangeli on drums and Bowie on everything else. Santangeli was told his tracks were only demos, and was fired after two days, but his drumming is most of what you hear on the album, with the notable exception of Nightclubbing, which has a drum machine. Pop's role in the album was relegated to lyrics, which he mostly improvised, a technique that Bowie would borrow for Heroes. Bowie would also cover China Girl on his album Let's Dance. The title was taken from the Dostoevsky novel (and, Pop believed, meant to be a jest at him from Bowie).

Musically, the album foreshadows the stylistic innovation Bowie would display during his Berlin trilogy on tracks like Nightclubbing (which was sampled on The Downward Spiral), Funtime, and especially Mass Production. The album was vastly different from Pop's earlier work and is retrospectively as seen more as a Bowie album than a Iggy Pop one. The follow-up, Lust for Life (Lana copied him) was closer to the proto-punk style that the Stooges were famous for and featured his two best known solo songs, The Passenger and Lust for Life. Iggy is still playing live (and shirtless) today, and only stopped stage diving in 2024.

# Hargrove's review

This is a pretty cool album. I had been looking forward to it because I didn’t exactly know what to expect. It was intriguing that there was an album recorded in 1976 from an artist who was never a part of the actual punk scene that gets the label “post-punk”. While it is very Bowie dominated, providing a clear blueprint for the better execution on *Low*, Iggy’s creative contributions should not be discounted. While the lyrical quality varies widely from track-to-track, I found myself genuinely moved by the bittersweet nostalgia of “Dum Dum Girls”, the self-loathing of “China Girl”, and the sarcastic send-up of nightlife in “Nightclubbing”. He also delivers his vocals very differently to how Bowie would, giving new meaning and feeling to this style of music. In particular, I find the sudden crescendo of “It's in the white of my eyes” on “China Girl” one of the most satisfying parts of this album. Though I understand the tendency to credit everything to Bowie, Iggy does more than enough to make this album not just a worse version of *Low* or *“Heroes”*. Musically, I found most of the songs to be decent. No real duds, but nothing too mind-blowingly good. “Dum Dum Boys” is far and away the best track, while (hot take alert) “Nightclubbing” has to be my least favorite. I don’t come to that decision lightly, I think it’s a good track and has a lot of things going for it, but it just had negative elements that stood out while the other tracks really didn’t. Anyway, I found this a tight, solid album with a fascinating story and place in popular music history. It’s certainly above average. I have very few direct complaints. It doesn’t resonate with me in the way that my favorite albums do, but it’s an extremely worthwhile listen.

“Sister Midnight” is a super cool track that singlehandedly bridges the gap between the funk of *Station to Station* and the art rock of *Low*. It’s a genuinely great funk song! They do everything right: they jam hard on the 1, there’s a great bass line, and Iggy channels the charisma of James Brown. It’s a fun track. “Nightclubbing” has a lot going for it. It’s sleazy, slinky, has an awesome freakout guitar solo, and effective satire of nightlife. It’s just too slow and almost academic to really resonate with me. I prefer it when Iggy isn’t this detached. Great use of a drum machine though. “Funtime” uses the driving motorik beat of krautrock effectively. There’s not much more to it than that, but it’s enough to elevate a short, fast pop song to greatness. The best part of “Baby” is the crunchy, all-over-the-place bass line. The lyrics do little for me, but I do like the way he says “baby” every time. “China Girl” is a damn good song. I have never loved Bowie’s version, but Iggy’s crazed deliver, combined with the haunting atmosphere created by the bells and synths, makes it rock. “Dum Dum Boys” got me pretty good, I’m not gonna lie. I’m moved by the spoken word intro, hypnotized by the central riff, and in awe of how much Iggy clearly loves those dumbasses. You can only say “They just stood in front of the old drug store/I was most impressed/No one else was impressed, not at all” about your best friends in the world, and I really feel for him at “Well now, I'm looking for the Dum Dum Boys/Hey, where are you now when I need your noise?/Now, I'm looking for the Dum Dum Boys/The walls close in and I need some noise”. Bowie absolutely kills it on the sax solos of “Tiny Girls”, and I appreciate the guitar skank beats and relaxed, lilting rhythm as well. I do wish it was a bit more substantial though. “Mass Production” goes on a little long and slowly, which holds it back, but it creates a really enrapturing atmosphere. There’s quite a lot going on both texturally and melodically, all backed by the super hypnotizing rhythm section. It’s admittedly quite cool, but I don’t know how much I’ll revisit it.

Good album. I’ve given *Lust for Life* a few listens to and I think I prefer this one, but I can’t say for sure just yet. Also, since I haven’t mentioned it yet: Iggy looks insane on the cover.

Favorite tracks: Sister Midnight, Funtime, China Girl, Dum Dum Boys

Least favorite track: Nightclubbing

# Aidan's review

I've enjoyed some of Iggy's other albums, but this is a Bowie album without Bowie lyrics, which are one of his greatest strengths. Iggy makes about as good of a go at it as I can imagine, with China Girl in particular being a great song. I find Iggy better than Bowie at authentically certain brand of snarling hedonism (although both definitely did their share of hedonism) on songs like Funtime, but he lacks Bowie's ear for catchy vocal melodies that make similar songs to Sister Midnight and Nightclubbing catchy on Bowie's albums around the time. It's not a bad album, and Bowie's production is excellent, but I'd rather have the Classic Coke of Station to Station or Low. Lust for Life is an album that I feel plays much stronger to Iggy's strengths
