# Write-up

While they are known as a core part of the early 2000s post punk revival scene, the core of Interpol is actually half from across the pond. Daniel Kessler was the impetus behind the band's founding, after working at Domino Records and getting a degree at NYU, he got it in his head to start a band with him as lead guitarist. He met up with fellow student Carlos Dengler on bass and Paul Banks on vocals and rhythm (American but born in Britain). After an EP with drummer Greg Drudy, the band replaced him recent Florida transplant Samuel Fogarino. The band played a significant amount of gigs, especially in the Luna Lounge on the Lower East Side, where peers such as the Strokes, Yeah Yeah Yeahs, Nada Surf and The National would also play. It is now the site of the nightclub The Knitting Factory. On the back of their first two EPs, their plentiful gigs and Kessler's industry knowledge, the band landed a contract with indie label Matador Records (which features pretty much all of my favorite bands as well as Queens of the Stone Age on their roster). For production, they brought on Gareth Jones and Peter Katis, the later of whom has produced every record by both Interpol and The National, probably contributing to their similar sounds.

There's no real mythos to the band, Matador never did the type of promotion, word of mouth or otherwise, that leads to mythical stories about the creation of the album. Regardless of the lack of intrigue, the band generated plenty of buzz by simply being that good. Pitchfork famously gave the album a 9.4 (later downgraded to a 7.0 because woke). The band drew comparisons to peers like the Strokes and forerunners like Echo and the Bunnymen but the most common comparison was to Joy Division, and specifically Banks's similar vocals to Ian Curtis. The music video for Obstacle 1 certainly didn't lean away from the comparison either. The album was one of the truly great albums to come out of the 00s New York post punk scene and while the band never managed to make anything nearly as good, they still sell records today off the hope that they might.

# Hargrove's review

“PDA” is one of the greatest songs of all-time. I will not hear any dissent on this objective fact. Incredible, incredible, incredible song. “Untitled” and “Obstacle 1” join it to make three of the best songs in the 00s indie canon. In addition to their similarities in sound and scene, however, Interpol has one other major shared quality with The Strokes: both of their seminal debut albums, while consistently good, are extremely uneven, featuring a handful of generational bangers amid a bunch of above average but forgettable songs. This has always been my attitude toward both records at least; listening today, I was able to develop a newfound appreciation for some of the deep cuts on *Turn On the Bright Lights*. That being said, my high-level assessment of the album as a whole hasn’t changed. One thing certainly not affected by perhaps slightly lackluster songwriting: just how fucking cool Interpol was. Everything about this album oozes aura. The cover is one of my favorites of all time, the red on black and the shining lights evoking the exact same urban nighttime setting as the music and the lyrics. Paul Banks may be doing an unabashed impression of Ian Curtis, but he is a complete natural at doing so. The guitars are so skillfully dissonant, creating a feel that only post-punk records can. And the rhythm section. Oh, the rhythm section. The bass and drums in this album blow pretty much any other indie band out of the water. It’s crazy how consistently the bass steals the show in these songs. Carlos Dengler does his hero Peter Hook proud. What this album lacks in deep cuts it makes up for in swagger. Somewhat. Because it’s still frustrating how forgettable some of the back half is.

“Untitled” is a crazy good opening song on a debut album. It’s also fantastic specifically as an opener, beyond being just a good song. The bass line is beautiful and the entire vibe is burning, yearning. A really cool track. “Obstacle 1” starts this album 2 for 2, building on and surpassing the opener. It’s the first one to use this weird effect on Banks’ voice that I really like that makes him sound like he’s talking to you from a radio. The guitar is stellar, the bass is better, the vocal line is super catchy, and the drums are great too. “NYC” often gets lumped in with the rest of the opening four as one of the great songs from the beginning of this album, but I actually disagree. This song is certainly catchy and recognizable, so it doesn’t have the same problem as some of the later songs. However, it kills the momentum of the surrounding songs for me which is a no-go. It’s too slow and not quite good enough to make up for it. It’s not a bad song by any means, but I just don’t love it. “Subway is a porno” is admittedly a great line though. “PDA”, as I said earlier, is an era-defining piece of music that blows me away every time I listen to it. One of the greatest songs ever written. “Yours is the only version of my desertion that I could ever subscribe to/That is all that I can do” is an indescribably cool opening line for a song. The eighth notes on the guitar are so dissonant and angular and catch my attention so hard. What I think I really love is how frantic this song feels, not only within the vocal and the instrumental but also with how quickly it flits from section to section. The outro section is very pretty as well. “Say Hello to the Angels” is not one of my favorite songs, though like “NYC” it’s quite recognizable. It’s too bouncy for my taste. As opposed to “PDA”, I think the rushed vocals in the chorus don’t work well, and the bass line always makes me think of “Are You Gonna Be My Girl” by Jet (derogatory).

“Hands Away” is a nice if innocuous little interlude that signals that the album is about to get much less iconic, though I do like the back-and-forth between the thudding kick drum and the soft hi-hat in the opening. “Obstacle 2” is a forgettable song that I’ve never really paid much attention to before but that I actually really liked this time around. I really like the lyrics, and it has one of the best vocal deliveries on the album, especially in the chorus. “Stella was a diver and she was always down” is the highlight of the second half of the album. It’s super dark, just very very grim. Something about it musically sticks out even among the other songs as particularly bleak but without sacrificing pace or energy. The guitars over the “She was alright…” parts are just haunting. The line “She was my catatonic sex toy” when it comes near the end is so gross and jarring. Really cool track. “Roland” is pretty forgettable. It’s my least favorite, and I don’t have much more to say than that. The first half of “The New” is similar, but it builds into such an amazing and cathartic breakdown that I have to put it in my favorite tracks. “Leif Erikson” is one of the better forgettable songs, but it doesn’t feel like much of a closer. It’s fine. I like the lyrics.

This album is really hard for me to rank because I don’t think it’s bad at any point, and a few of the songs are simply transcendent, but most of the album hovers just above average in the songwriting department. How far can vibes alone carry an album? Pretty damn far, honestly. I’m putting it in A Tier right around where I have Nick Drake.

Favorite tracks: Untitled, Obstacle 1, PDA, Obstacle 2, Stella was a diver and she was always down, The New

Least favorite track: Roland

# Aidan's review

Interpol does "cool" as well as anyone since Miles Davis, and this album just radiates with style to the point where its flaws go unnoticed in its shadow. The production is a major component of it, but Interpol has kept the same producer throughout their career but never reached close to the same dizzying heights. The album is crystal clear in its focus, from the fantastic opener, that establishes the motif of lone guitar starting off each song. It feels incredibly well suited to their live performances, there's just enough of an opening riff to let everyone know to shut the fuck up before launching into the song with gusto. Kessler's lead guitar is so simple but it so good, and they love to randomly cut out all the instrumentals and let  Indie sleaze may have been a retroactive label, but nobody embodied it better than these guys, they had the suits and misogynistic undertones to make it in finance but damn did those guitars fuck. All of this creates a kind of alchemy in which you don't care how similar the songs are or the generally meh lyrics. They're at their best when they're fast, Obstacle 1, PDA, Stella and Roland are my picks for the best songs on the album. Untitled is also great, although it's more of a mood setter than an individually great song. Even the great songs aren't anything more than good singles, but it's just such a focused album, it works. I first heard this album freshman year of college,  and I come back to it pretty often. I'll be reminded of how damn good these guys were, play it through a few times and then the flaws become apparent after a few listens. Give it a few months and it's another day or two to listening to it on loop. I'll rag on Pitchfork for bitching out and lowering the review from a 9.5 to a 7.0 but that's honestly how I feel every time I listen to this, it'll blow you away at first and then the magic wears away a bit and it's merely a good album. I don't know where that really corresponds to on a tier list, but it's an album I highly recommend if you have any fondness for anything like it.
