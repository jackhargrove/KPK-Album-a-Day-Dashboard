# Write-up

John Maus is an American outsider musician based in Los Angeles. He is heavily associated with his close friend and occasional collaborator, Ariel Pink. Maus was born in 1980 and raised in Minnesota. In the early 90s, he became obsessed with Nirvana (a similarity with Pink, who considers Nirvana the last great rock band) and his personal icon Syd Barrett, Pink Floyd’s first lead singer who fried his brain on drugs. However, he also took a strong interest in Western classical music, from Baroque through to modern classical composers like John Cage and Morton Feldman. He moved to Santa Clarita at age 18 in 1998 to study at the California Institute of the Arts, where he met and became close friends with Ariel Pink. Pink showed Maus the many tapes of bizarre, unrecognizable music he had made, bringing him into hypnagogic pop on the ground floor. He was an early member of Ariel Pink’s Haunted Graffiti. After both left college, Pink got discovered by Animal Collective, and Maus came along for the ride, playing keyboard for Panda Bear’s European tour. He very slowly put together his first two albums, *Songs* and *Love Is Real*, which released in 2006 and 2007, respectively. Much like Pink’s early work, Maus’ music received terrible reviews, driving him to go back to school and pursue academia. He would earn his Ph.D. in political philosophy from the University of Hawaii in 2014.

In the meantime, Maus continued to produce and perform his own weirdo style of music. He became known for his unique concerts in which he would scream and flail his body over pre-recorded music. His music heavily resembles Ariel Pink’s hypnagogic pop, but with more of a focus on the synthesizer-heavy and post-punk styles of the 80s than the pop styles of the 60s. He released his most well-known album, *We Must Become the Pitiless Censors of Ourselves*, in 2011. The title is a quote from French philosopher Alain Badiou, with whom Maus studied in grad school. Maus’ goal with the album was to write the “perfect pop song”. The track “Hey Moon” is a cover of a song by Swedish musician Molly Nilsson, while “Cop Killer” based on a track by Finnish electronic musician Peter Hajba. Otherwise, all the songs are originals. The album was Maus’ first to be critically acclaimed. Notably, it came a year after Ariel Pink’s breakthrough with *Before Today*. Due to his lack of musical training, music industry support, and recognizable tradition, as well as his naïve sincerity, Maus is often considered an outsider musician. We have another outsider musician later this week, Divine Styler, and have previously listened to outsider musicians Ariel Pink and Captain Beefheart. Later in the list, we will also listen to Daniel Johnston and Moondog.

Maus has released more albums since 2011, but none that have been particularly notable. As Ariel Pink’s influence on the music scene became more and more clear by the late 2010s, his stock rose as well. However, on January 6th, 2021, tragedy struck; a group of supporters of outgoing President Donald Trump invaded the Capitol in an attempt to overturn election results, and Ariel Pink and John Maus were both spotted near the crowd. While the extent of their participation is not clear, this, combined with sexual harassment allegations against Ariel Pink, made both quickly lose relevance in the music scene. While Pink has continued to spiral into more and more controversy, Maus has largely laid low, and neither one has spoken to the other since late 2021. Maus released his 6th album earlier this year to minor anticipation.

# Hargrove's review

This album was unfortunately pretty mid. I’m a huge Ariel Pink fan and had heard of John Maus through that, but never checked out his music. There are some decent songs here, a few good melodies and a unique atmosphere, but it doesn’t do anything remotely as fun as Pink’s best works. It doesn’t make me question whether I’m dreaming. I don’t know why exactly it doesn’t work for me in the same way. Maybe it’s the generally unfinished feeling. Theoretically, this style of music shouldn’t need fully formed song ideas to work; bits and pieces actually complement the ideas of false memory and hazy dreams, as exemplified by Pink’s early tapes. In practice, though, the different sections and builds within songs like “Not Enough Violence” or “Round & Round” just feel so good. It can simulate flipping through a radio in your brain. Here, you mostly get one idea per track that plays out with a verse, a chorus, a verse, and a chorus. That’s enough for some songs, but it doesn’t amount to much over the course of an album. There’s a lot to like, but there’s also a lot that’s unsatisfying.

I like how “Streetlight” starts in the middle of the synth line. It makes it feel like I’m entering a world that already existed before I pressed play. It’s also a good synth line. “Quantum Leap” is one of the best tracks in no small part because it feels fully formed. It also straight up sounds like a Joy Division song. That bass riff is just great. I also like the delivery on “the dead zone is a sign of the times, hey”. “…And the Rain” is pretty forgettable. “Hey Moon” seems to be the song everyone loves, but it doesn’t do a ton for me. It’s undeniably pretty, but it’s just slower and more relaxed than I want from this atmosphere. “Keep Pushing On” has decent verses but a really grating chorus. “The Crucifix” is a forgettable interlude. “Head for the Country” returns to some quality though. I love that funky synth line, I love the synth melody on the chorus, and I love the vocal melody as well. “Cop Killer” is another good song. It’s so goddamn catchy and the lyrics are truly striking. “Matter of Fact” is super annoying. It’s kind of funny, but also super basic with not much to it. “We Can Breakthrough” sounds pretty cool. I like the swirling Gregorian chants and the uneasiness of the time signature. “Believer” ends things as perhaps the most fully-formed track on the album. It feels like a real song, not just a short exploration of an idea. It’s also the one that most reminds me of Ariel Pink in a good way.

It’s a fine album, but not one I see myself wanting to listen to particularly often. There are just better options that evoke these feelings but stronger. Still, there are two or three nice tracks I’ve added to my liked songs playlist, so not a complete waste of time.

Favorite tracks: Streetlight, Quantum Leap, Head for the Country, Cop Killer, We Can Breakthrough, Believer

Least favorite track: Matter of Fact
