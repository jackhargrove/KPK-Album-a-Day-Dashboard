# Hargrove's review

I’ve always felt this album is a little overrated, and my listening over the past few days hasn’t really shaken that view. While I think “New Dawn Fades” is a stunning song and an all-time great, and there are a few other tracks on here that I enjoy but not quite to that same level, most of the tracks don’t get more than a “this is decent” out of me. I think Ian Curtis was a phenomenal vocalist, and he’s consistently a highlight through nearly every song. He’s so distinctive with that deep monotone, and the production makes him sound so far away. I also respect its commitment to a monolithically dark atmosphere, although now that I’ve heard *Pornography* by The Cure I think that they did it better. Peter Hook’s bass lines are also typically interesting, a sign of his dance music future. Beyond that, though,  most of the songs just leave me wanting. A song almost never ends leaving me satisfied, I usually want something more dynamic, or with more moving parts. None of this is to say I hate this album by any means, in fact I respect it and think it’s good; it just never really stands out to me as a record I want to listen to again and again.

“New Dawn Fades” is a bonkers track, and if every song was like this it would be an all-time great record. That guitar part is fantastic, and it rumbles in so forcefully. Both the bass and the drums are simple but seething, bubbling under the surface of the booming guitar and the tension building in the vocals. The vocals completely explode in the second verse, giving the song a healthy climax in the middle. And then the song Fades into the distance, like a New Dawn (I guess). Really great track with great instrumental parts and great build. I also rather like the opener, “Disorder”. It’s one of the danciest tracks between the driving bass line, the angular guitar line, and that electric drum sound. It also has a stellar vocal melody. I could definitely hear this one as a New Order song. “Day of the Lords” didn’t stand out to me at first but it ended up growing on me. I really enjoy the ominous droning around the chorus, as well as the rapid release in the vocal melody. The last song that really stands out to me above the rest is “Shadowplay” if only because of the amazing guitar part between the vocals.

Other than the songs in the paragraph above, the rest kind of blend together for me. “Candidate” is dreary and forgettable outside of Curtis’ vocals. “Insight” doesn’t even have the notable vocal performance, although I did enjoy the breakdown at the end. “She’s Lost Control” is a tough one for me because people loooooooove this song, but I just really don’t get it. The lyrics are genuinely quite affecting, but I don’t think this song is well developed. The vocals are catchy I guess, but I don’t get the dance vibe that so many seem to. “Wilderness” has a kickass bass line but not much else. “Interzone” is just way too upbeat for this album, and the guitar lick sounds like they’re trying to sell out an arena. I respect “I Remember Nothing” for its bleakness, but it’s a pretty static track.

All in all, I want to like this record much more than I do. I got 4 tracks out of it that I think are really great, and I suppose that’s enough, but I never think it stands up as a whole. I admit there is some element of this album’s legendary stature working against it for me, but it just never sounds like I hope it will. Ian Curtis was truly gifted though, RIP.

Favorite tracks: Disorder, Day of the Lords, New Dawn Fades, Shadowplay

Least favorite track: Interzone
