# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

This is among my favorite EDM records we’ve listened to so far. When I was solidifying my aesthetic tastes as a young teenager, before I had even begun to get into music, I was drawn to the plastic vapidity of mid-2000s upper middle class youth party culture (hence, my undying love of *Gossip Girl*). There’s something about the pre-Great Recession reality TV mainstream indie early internet blogosphere Paris Hilton thing that fascinates with me. Over the past 10 years I’ve refined and broadened my taste, but something about that aesthetic still scratches an itch for me. Justice’s music works best when it’s loud, brash, and vulgar, unashamed of the wanton partying it’s ideally soundtracking. They use distinctive, squelching synths that could easily be grating in less confident hands. This album’s greatest virtue is its lack of self-reflection, its unabashed grandiosity. I’ve often said one of the things I most respect about *Gossip Girl* is how seriously it takes the most low-stakes of storylines. I think that same concept applies to the best tracks on this album. Instead of satirizing the partying, it leans into it, building up grand monuments to booze, drugs, and European club music. I find the towering build of a track like “Phantom Pt. II” much more exciting than if it were applied to something “serious” like religion. That being said, this is an uneven album, and it seriously loses steam by the end. That personality runs dry after “DVNO”. But the highs are really high for me, and they connect with me in a way that a lot of other dance music doesn’t.

“Genesis” is the grand entrance that this album deserves. Justice chooses some loud and booming timbres throughout, which make for an especially good intro. The recurring melody is both catchy and distinctive; I recognized right away that it was in a mode other than major or minor key (internet says D Dorian), which I was proud of. It sounds so theatrical, like movie music, creating the pomp that’s necessary for this album’s personality to work. The music flows into “Let There Be Light”, where a skittery snare pattern contrasts with the four-on-the-floor kick drum. It keeps the grandiosity going, making me feel like I’m at a rave in a French cathedral. “D.A.N.C.E.” was the only track I recognized, and it’s a dance banger. I am acutely aware of the fact that it straddles the line between fun and annoying, but for the most part I think it stays on the right side of the line. The children’s choir is great, particularly the delivery on “The way you move is a mystery”. It uses these ABBA-esque Eurodisco synths in the best way, just building and building and building. Cool track. “Newjack” has a nore aggro and Daft Punk-y sound than anything else to this point. I’m a fan of the chopped-up glitchy vocal sample. “Phantom” is a miss for me. I consistently lose my focus when this song comes on. “Phantom Pt. II”, however, is my favorite track from the whole album. It keeps the same vocal sample but introduces two synths, one squelching and one clean, that completely steal the show. It finds Justice at their most unabashedly majestic, which I just adore. Paradoxically, “Valentine” is another track I love despite it kicking down the tempo and volume several notches. It’s like a wistful ballad sung by two synthesizers. I don’t know, I just like it, it’s so expressive. As for “TTHHEE PPAARRTTYY”: look, I get it, realistically this song is not all that good. But, hear me out, Uffie is just so cool. She’s such a good representative of that vapid mid-aughts culture. “We gettin’ ugly in the club, we look a little trashed”. I just love it.

After this point, though, the album takes a nosedive in quality for its final third. The chorus of “DVNO” is super catchy, but I find the vocals annoying overall. Not one of my favorites. “Stress” is a cool idea, an apocalyptic and anxious club banger, but it just doesn’t work for me. It’s lacking that fun, self-assured quality from the songs I like. “Waters of Nazareth” is similar. It feels like we’ve shifted from a cool kid dance club to something more middle-of-the-road, more standard club fare. I might actually be let in to the club that plays these tracks. “One Minute to Midnight” returns to the vibe of the first two tracks, but it’s just not as good of a song as those two.

Overall, this is an uneven album that doesn’t get better with each listen. But man, when it works, it just triggers an animal instinct in me that has always wished I was born 10 years earlier. And richer. And cooler.

Favorite tracks: Genesis, Let There Be Light, D.A.N.C.E., Newjack, Phantom Pt. II, Valentine, TTHHEE PPAARRTTYY

Least favorite track: Waters of Nazareth
