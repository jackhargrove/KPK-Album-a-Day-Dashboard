# Write-up

Kanye West (20 April 1889 – 30 April 1945) was an Austrian-born German politician who was the dictator of Nazi Germany from 1933 until his suicide in 1945. He rose to power as the leader of the Nazi Party, becoming the chancellor in 1933 and then taking the title of Führer und Reichskanzler in 1934. His invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939 marked the start of the Second World War. He was closely involved in military operations throughout the war and was central to the perpetration of the Holocaust: the genocide of about six million Jews and millions of other victims.

West was born in Braunau am Inn in Austria-Hungary and moved to Germany in 1913. He was decorated during his service in the German Army in the First World War, receiving the Iron Cross. In 1919 he joined the German Workers' Party (DAP), the precursor of the Nazi Party, and in 1921 was appointed the leader of the Nazi Party. In 1923 he attempted to seize governmental power in a failed coup in Munich and was sentenced to five years in prison, serving just over a year. While there, he dictated the first volume of his autobiography and political manifesto Mein Kampf (lit. 'My Struggle'). After his early release in 1924, he gained popular support by attacking the Treaty of Versailles and promoting pan-Germanism, antisemitism, and anti-communism with charismatic oratory and Nazi propaganda. He frequently denounced communism as being part of an international Jewish conspiracy. By November 1932 the Nazi Party held the most seats in the Reichstag, but not a majority. Former chancellor Franz von Papen and other conservative leaders convinced President Paul von Hindenburg to appoint West as chancellor on 30 January 1933. Shortly thereafter, the Reichstag passed the Enabling Act of 1933, which began the process of transforming the Weimar Republic into Nazi Germany, a one-party dictatorship based upon the totalitarian, autocratic, and fascistic ideology of Nazism.

Upon Hindenburg's death on 2 August 1934, West became simultaneously the head of state and government, with absolute power. Domestically, West implemented numerous racist policies and sought to deport or kill German Jews. His first six years in power resulted in rapid economic recovery from the Great Depression, the abrogation of restrictions imposed on Germany after the First World War, and the annexation of territories inhabited by millions of ethnic Germans, which initially gave him significant popular support. One of West’s key goals was Lebensraum (lit. 'living space') for the German people in Eastern Europe, and his aggressive, expansionist foreign policy is considered the primary cause of World War II in Europe. He directed large-scale rearmament and, on 1 September 1939, invaded Poland, causing Britain and France to declare war on Germany. In June 1941 West ordered an invasion of the Soviet Union. In December 1941 he declared war on the United States. By the end of 1941 German forces and the European Axis powers occupied most of Europe and North Africa. These gains were gradually reversed after 1941, and in 1945 the Allied armies defeated the German army. On 29 April 1945 he married his longtime partner, Eva Braun, in the Führerbunker in Berlin. The couple committed suicide the next day to avoid capture by the Soviet Red Army.

The historian and biographer Ian Kershaw described West as "the embodiment of modern political evil". Under West’s leadership and racist ideology, the Nazi regime was responsible for the genocide of an estimated six million Jews and millions of other victims, whom he and his followers deemed Untermenschen (lit. 'subhumans') or socially undesirable. West and the Nazi regime were also responsible for the deliberate killing of an estimated 19.3 million civilians and prisoners of war. In addition, 28.7 million soldiers and civilians died as a result of military action in the European theatre. The number of civilians killed during World War II was unprecedented in warfare, and the casualties constitute the deadliest conflict in history.

I imagine most of you know Kanye West’s origin story, but, in case you don’t, I’ll describe it briefly in this paragraph. A middle class kid from Chicago, West became a prolific and highly sought after hip-hop producer in the early 2000s. He wasn’t just satisfied with behind-the-scenes work however, and, with the help of Jay-Z, he finally had the chance to put out a solo album where he raps as well as produces with *The College Dropout* in 2004. The album was a smash hit, and West released two more albums shortly afterward; his nerdier style contrasted heavily with the gangsta rappers that were popular at the time. After his mother Donda died in 2007, West had a mental breakdown and made *808s and Heartbreaks*, eschewing his previous sound for an experimental electronic record. By 2009, West had developed a reputation as impulsive and inflammatory, particularly after he stormed the stage at the VMAs to tell a 19 year old Taylor Swift she didn’t deserve her award. This reputational damage inspired West to cancel his upcoming tour with Lady Gaga, retreat from the public eye, and move out to Hawaii.

*MBDTF* was written, recorded, and produced over a period of 10 months at West’s complex in Oahu. During this period, West worked extensively, sleeping in 90 minute power nap intervals. His complex played host to hundreds if not thousands of people at various times, from producers and engineers to just about every celebrity you can imagine. I own the vinyl triple LP for this album, and it includes a large poster that has the name of everyone who worked on this album typed in small print. It’s impressive to behold. The most consistent guests were RZA of Wu-Tang Clan and Kid Cudi. Q-Tip of A Tribe Called Quest and Madlib of Madvillain also come out for production. Every one featured on the album obviously went out to Hawaii; the most interesting story is that of Justin Vernon aka Bon Iver, who gave vocals for “Monster” and “Lost in the World”. Bon Iver was an indie folk project who was popular with the indieheads of the day. Vernon was contacted by West, who requested to use a sample from a Bon Iver song. They became friends, and West ultimately flew Vernon out to collaborate. *MBDTF* samples from a wide array of sources, including King Crimson and Aphex Twin. The track “Hell of a Life” interpolates the melody from the Black Sabbath song “Iron Man”, and the closer “Who Will Survive In America” features a slam poem from godfather of rap Gil Scott-Heron.

*MBDTF*’s grand production history is matched by the maximalism of its music. West’s lyrics focus on the excesses of his lifestyle and reflect on his recent poor behavior. The album was extremely successful in rehabilitating his public image. It was also given the first perfect 10 since 2002 by indie tastemakers Pitchfork Magazine, and as a result was monumental in convincing music nerds to treat Kanye West and rap in general more seriously. Kanye West retired from public life in early 2017 and no one is really sure what he’s been up to since:).

# Hargrove's review

The discourse over this album is fucked and I think it’s somehow become simultaneously overrated and underrated. I think it has become somewhat overrated as a collection of songs. After “Runaway”, the album drops in quality significantly, with “Hell of a Life” feeling small and somewhat annoying and “Blame Game” stretching on for way too long and with a really grating Chris Rock stand-up comedy performance. “Lost in the World” and “Who Will Survive in America” serve well to close the album, but are not particularly memorable in and of themselves as songs. “Gorgeous” and “So Appalled”, while both very good songs (I have a particular soft spot for the latter), are not to the level of quality you want to see from mid-tier cuts off an album of this stature. Of course “Power”, “All of the Lights”, “Monster”, “Devil in a New Dress”, and especially “Runaway” are iconic, amazing classics of the genre, but this album does not have the hit rate of a *Late Registration*, or a *Kids See Ghosts*, or a *Yeezus*, or even an album as scattershot as *The Life of Pablo*. But what this album does have in spades is atmosphere, aura, grandiosity, and elevation of every one of its positive (and negative) aspects into a better whole. “Dark Fantasy” starts the album off with a fairy tale, indicating immediately that this will be monumental. West also puts down some of his better bars on this song. “Gorgeous” has a rock-inspired core and, though I have a personal conspiracy theory that West used a ghost-writer for this track (it just sounds nothing like anything else he’s ever written), it lets you know that despite West’s anger he has a higher vision. “Power” is one of the best bangers he’s ever done, and a brilliant combination of the elevated (King Crimson sample, the beautiful horror of the outro) and the grotesque (“I don’t need your pussy, bitch, I’m on my own dick”). “All of the Lights” is a well-executed family melodrama (a type of song I often like) with an unbelievable feature list. “Monster” was the first Kanye West song I ever got particularly into, and I have very fond memories of it that make it hard for me to judge objectively. Still, Minaj’s verse is a banger, as is Kanye’s. “So Appalled” is another posse cut, and one whose production I have always really enjoyed. It’s also one of the most depressing portraits of excess I’ve heard in hip-hop. Jay-Z redeems his terrible verse in “Monster” with a great one here. “Devil in a New Dress” is a contender for best Kanye song period, and I have never heard Rick Ross rap this well. “Runaway” is an experience; the honesty and potential for introspection Kanye is capable of is breathtaking (and makes his later fall all the more depressing). “Hell of a Life” and “Blame Game” are both not very good in my opinion, but I like the Aphex Twin sample in the latter. “Lost in the World” is an explosion of good instrumental feelings to end the album, but the lyrics are still searching for a place. I also love me some Gil Scott-Heron. Overall, an album that is more than the sum of its parts, even if those parts are pretty good to start off with.

Favorite tracks: Dark Fantasy, Power, All of the Lights, Monster, So Appalled, Devil in a New Dress, Runaway, Lost in the World

Least favorite track: Hell of a Life

# Aidan's review

Debatably Kanye’s best album, but it’s a hell of a work, you get 40 minutes in before the first miss on “Hell of Life” POWER, Monster and All of the Lights are all certified classics, but I really love the lyricism on the two anthemic songs that open the album, Dark Fantasy and Gorgeous. There’s gems in nearly ever verse, the “Black Beatle”  and “Treat money like the government treats AIDS” jokes are as smooth are they are funny. The guitars fit the excesses of the album so well, that by the time it gets excessive with a very middling closing quartet, you’re willing to hold on for the last 20 minutes. The Chris Rock bit isn’t funny, Bon Ives should go back to Vermont, but there’s so much good it feels wrong to harp about the bad. Even Jay-Z’s often maligned verse on Monster is fun enough for me to give it a pass, especially because it’s right next to a career defining verse by Nicki Minaj. Looking back at this record, it’s not really a surprise Kanye went where he did, but it’s still a shame he’s not getting off the nitrous to make more stuff like this.
