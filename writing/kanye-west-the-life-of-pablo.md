# Write-up

Kanye West is a hip hop producer and rapper born in 1977 and raised in Chicago. See my above write-ups for *My Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy* and *Yeezus* for more information on his earlier career. After *Yeezus*, West went on a highly acclaimed tour with then-rookie rapper Kendrick Lamar in 2013. He also released three collab singles with Paul McCartney in 2014 and 2015. During this period, West truly earned his reputation for announcing albums that will never exist. First, he promised *Yeezus II* in 2014, then *So Help Me God* in 2015. Then in early 2016, he announced *SWISH*, followed by *Waves*. While the former two albums never materialized, the latter two did see a release in February 2016 with a last-minute name change to *The Life of Pablo*. This title refers to Pablo Escobar, Pablo Picasso, and Paul the Apostle. After multiple delays to tweak tracks, West released *The Life of Pablo* exclusively on Tidal, Jay-Z’s new lossless streaming service. It would not be released on other platforms for nearly two months. In an unprecedented move, West announced after release that he was still working on the album and would continue to update it as time went on. In March, he swapped out a lyric on “Famous” and changed the mix, and then a few days later he substituted a remixed version of “Wolves” that also separated Frank Ocean’s outro into its own track. At the end of the month, he released a third update that altered 12 of the tracks. One more update to “30 Hours” followed in April, followed by the final update in June that not only changed a few existing tracks but added an entirely new one to the end of the album, “Saint Pablo”.

West described the album as “the gospel according to Ye”, prominently fusing classic gospel sounds with his hip hop in tracks like “Ultralight Beam”, “Father Stretch My Hands, Pt. 1” and “Low Lights”. However, the album features a schizophrenic variety of sounds, from trap to autotune pop to art pop to industrial hip hop. The lyrics largely focus on West’s real life struggles, most famously his cousin stealing his laptop in “Real Friends” and his immense debt in “Saint Pablo”. Lines regarding Taylor Swift in the single “Famous” also set off a chain of events that reignited the feud between the two. Of the many featured artists, Kid Cudi, Frank Ocean, André 3000, and Kendrick Lamar all have albums on our list. Additionally, Travis Scott received a songwriting credit, while Madlib received a production credit. “Mighty Healthy” from Ghostface Killah’s *Supreme Clientele* is sampled on “No More Parties in LA”. The album received moderately positive reviews but was highly successful commercially. It was the first time an album went #1 on the Billboard 200 with a majority of its units coming from streaming, as well as the first streaming-only album to go platinum. In late 2016, West went on the Saint Pablo tour, which was cut short after he first announced his support for President-elect Donald Trump and stormed off the stage during a November performance in Sacramento. The Kanye West story will continue when we get to his final album on the list, his 2018 collab album with Kid Cudi *Kids See Ghosts*.

# Hargrove's review

As a relatively late convert to Kanye, *The Life of Pablo* was his first album release cycle I personally remember. I still hadn’t become a Kanye fan at that point; that would come just a few months later, in the summer of 2016, when I first gave *My Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy* a listen on Pitchfork’s recommendation. But, man, do I remember how outrageous the *Pablo* release was. There were constant delays and revisions, the clusterfuck of the Tidal exclusive release, the outrageous Taylor Swift line in “Famous”, the hilarious tweet about being in $53,000,000 worth of debt, the audacity to release an album unfinished and continuously update it in public. I viewed all of these things negatively at first because of my anti-Kanye posture, but, fuck, if I wasn’t still following along. When I did become a Kanye fan later that year, MBDTF was the gateway, wjile *Yeezus* was the one I really fell in love with. I tried both *808s and Heartbreak* and *The Life of Pablo* to stick with the later Kanye stuff, but they didn’t stick. A month later, I finally listened to his debut trilogy and finally became a full-fledged fan, even getting into *808s* eventually. *The Life of Pablo* remained the most elusive, capturing Kanye at his most grating, his most insane, his most arrogant, his most Kanye. But over time, that grew to be the version of Kanye West I liked most. The first song to break through was ironically the last added to the album, “Saint Pablo”. That beat is simply unimpeachable, while the first verse was his best flow since the second verse of “New Slaves”, forming a holy trinity with the final verse of “Gone’. What kept me coming back to “Saint Pablo”, though, was the honest, vulnerable, logical outlining of his positive qualities and how they morphed into his worst behavior. After that, the dominoes started falling one by one. The realizations that “Ultralight Beam” didn’t sound like any other rap song, then that “FML” into “Real Friends” into “Wolves” is one of the greatest three-track runs in his discography, then that “Famous” is a pop rap banger, then that “30 Hours” is one of Kanye’s most beautiful tracks ever, then that oh yeah Kendrick Lamar is on “No More Parties in LA” and kills it like always. Even as I wore out Kanye’s other records and they started to get a little old to me, the sheer manic diversity and slow-burn delayed gratification tracks kept me coming back. The sequencing of *The Life of Pablo* is so important to preventing it from collapsing under its own weight; if three of the best tracks don’t come in the final quarter, I call the album an overlong mess. But it just works. It really just works. The White Album is my favorite Beatles album because it’s the clearest window into the group’s mindset as it was rapidly disintegrating. Well, *The Life of Pablo* is Kanye’s White Album.

I don’t like “Ultralight Beam” quite as much as I once did, but I have to admit it’s a pretty great and inventive song. I really can’t say that anyone else has combined hip hop and gospel so seamlessly. It’s also a great mood-setter. Chance the Rapper saying “I met Kanye West, I'm never going to fail” will always be an all-time funny line though in retrospect. “Father Stretch My Hands, Pt. 1” provides the perfect contrast to the weightiness of the opener with its debauchery. I mean, “Now, if I fuck this model/And she just bleached her asshole/And I get bleach on my T-shirt/I’ma feel like an asshole” is just an insane thing to say. It’s also got a vibey beat and doesn’t overstay its welcome. “Pt. 2” is a fine trap song. Nothing too special, but I love the sonic diversity it adds. “Famous” is one of the great pop songs in Kanye’s stronger, up there with “Gold Digger” and “Stronger” and “Heartless” and “Power”. Rihanna sounds so lovely yet so threatening on the chorus, while Kanye spits some bars that are audacious even for him. “I feel like me and Taylor might still have sex/Why? I made that bitch famous (*Goddamn*)/I made that bitch famous”. Definitely gross, but fuck, I believe he believes that. “Feedback” goes in another direction again with an industrial beat reminiscent of *Yeezus*. “If Hov J then every Jordan need a Rodman” is a great bar from Kanye, and maybe the most inadvertently humble I’ve ever heard him sound. “Low Lights” is a low light, but it’s overhated in my opinion. It’s not the greatest song in the world, but I love that it continues the gospel flavor. “Highlights”, by contrast, offends me more in that it’s only okay. “Low Lights” through “Waves” is easily the weakest stretch of the album in my opinion. “Freestyle 4” has some nice horrorcore vibes but doesn’t really stick with me. “I Love Kanye” is a bright spot, a genuinely delightful and original interlude. “Waves” has always felt like more style than substance to me. It never really feels like the song actually starts, it’s all just prelude. It’s in one ear, out the other for me.

After a few tracks in the wilderness, “FML” rejuvenates things as we enter the album’s superior second half. I love this song. The Weeknd is perfect for this chorus, the perfect mix of grimy and defiant as he belts “They wish I would go ahead and fuck my life up/Can't let them get to me/And even though I always fuck my life up/Only I can mention me”. Kanye’s first verse is a bit slow, but his second overflows with passion. You ain't never seen nothing crazier than/This [REDACTED] when he off his Lexapro” and “But I'ma have the last laugh in the end/‘Cause I'm from a tribe called "Check-a-ho” are two lines that always stick with me. Somehow, the next track is even better. “Real Friends” is one of the great confessional hip hop tracks. I just love how self-aware Kanye is on this record, something that unfortunately does not continue into his future work. The song initially feels like it will be primarily accusatory, but he gets to “I'm always blamin' you, but what's sad, you not the problem/Damn, I forgot to call her, shit, I thought it was Thursday/Why you wait a week to call my phone in the first place?/When was the last time I remembered a birthday?/When was the last time I wasn't in a hurry?” right away. “I guess I get what I deserve, don't I?/Word on the streets is they ain't heard from him” indeed. And I love the inclusion of the cousin laptop story. It’s easily one of Kanye’s best tracks ever. “Wolves” isn’t quite that level, but probably around as good as “FML”. The line “Then I said, ‘What if Mary was in the club/‘Fore she met Joseph, around hella thugs?’/Cover Nori in lambs' wool/We surrounded by the fuckin' wolves” is my favorite kind of Kanye bar: a Biblical reference to the birth of Jesus as metaphor for his own petty issues. It’s like when he chopped up “Strange Fruit”, a song about lynching, to back up verses about a break up. On top of that, the singing in the track is gorgeous. “Frank’s Track” is a decent continuation but it really doesn’t need to be standalone. “Siiiiiiiiilver Surffffeeeeer Intermission” just adds a little more personality to the record.

“30 Hours” is another contender for my favorite Kanye song. It’s just so full of great quotable bars: “My ex says she gave me the best years of her life/I saw a recent picture of her, I guess she was right”, “I wake up, all veggies no eggs/I hit the gym, all chest no legs”, “You was the best of all time at the time, though”, “I remember being nervous to do Victoria's Secret/’Til I pictured everybody with they clothes off”, “Skrrt, skrrt, skrrt, like a private school for women”, “I need a happy beginnin', middle and endin’”, “Got a hotel room, three stars for you/You call down for an omelet, girl, it's 5 in the morning/You realize we at the DoubleTree, not the Aria/Only thing open is Waffle House, girl, don't start with me”, “I used the Western Union for you like it's no prob’/‘Cause you was in college complainin' 'bout it's no jobs/But you were suckin' a [REDACTED] dick the whole time/Well, I guess a blowjob's better than no job”, “I just be like, it was my idea to have an open relationship/Now a [REDACTED] mad”. Damn near the whole song, man. I again love the use of a clearly personal story, the immaculate sample, and the extended outro. “No More Parties in LA” is a tad bit too long, but it features a banger verse from Dot and a banger verse from Ye. That’s kind of all you need. The beat is also pure MBDTF maximalism, another nice new sound for the album. “The problem ain't I be drivin', the problem is I be textin’”, “I be worried 'bout my daughter, I be worried 'bout Kim/But Saint is baby Ye, I ain't worried 'bout him”, and “And as far as real friends, tell all my cousins I love 'em/Even the one that stole the laptop, you dirty motherfucker” are my favorite bars. “Facts (Charlie Heat Version)” is a basic trap song on an album that already had a better basic trap song. It wins my least favorite track spot for the redundancy. “Fade” is quite good though. I love the house beat and the fact that the Post Malone is less annoying than it should be on paper. “Saint Pablo” competes with “30 Hours” and “Real Friends” for the spot of my favorite track on TLOP. I adore that metallic flute sound in the instrumental. Kanye lays down one of his finest verses ever, with lines like “My wife said I can't say no to nobody/And at this rate, we gon' both die broke/Got friends that ask me for money knowin' I'm in debt/And like my wife said, I still didn't say no” giving insight into his psyche, “This generation's closest thing to Einstein/So don't worry about me, I'm fine” and “Skate on the paradigm and shift it when I feel like” converting braggadocio into poetry, and “The ultimate Gemini has survived/I wasn't supposed to make it past twenty-five” calling back to the opening track on his debut record. Man, at the time this felt like such a fitting closer to his career. Oh well.

I’m still feeling this record as much as I was a decade ago. It’s kind of got something for everyone, bits of every era of his sound up to 2016. It’s a great record.

Favorite tracks: Ultralight Beam, Father Stretch My Hands, Pt. 1, Famous, Feedback, I Love Kanye, FML, Real Friends, Wolves, 30 Hours, No More Parties in LA, Fade, Saint Pablo

Least favorite track: Facts (Charlie Heat Version)
