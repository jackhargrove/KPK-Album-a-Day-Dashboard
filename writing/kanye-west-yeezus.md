# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: I imagine most of you know Kanye West’s origin story, but, in case you don’t, I’ll describe it briefly in this paragraph. A middle class kid from Chicago, West became a prolific and highly sought after hip-hop producer in the early 2000s. He wasn’t just satisfied with behind-the-scenes work however, and, with the help of Jay-Z, he finally had the chance to put out a solo album where he raps as well as produces with *The College Dropout* in 2004. The album was a smash hit, and West released two more albums shortly afterward; his nerdier style contrasted heavily with the gangsta rappers that were popular at the time. After his mother Donda died in 2007, West had a mental breakdown and made *808s and Heartbreaks*, eschewing his previous sound for an experimental electronic record. By 2009, West had developed a reputation as impulsive and inflammatory, particularly after he stormed the stage at the VMAs to tell a 19 year old Taylor Swift she didn’t deserve her award. He retreated from the public eye for nearly a year, and made his most acclaimed work *My Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy* in Hawaii with an endless list of collaborators and co-producers. He followed that up with a collaboration album with Jay-Z in 2011, and a compilation album of artists on his personal label in 2012.

West began recording his 6th solo album in early 2013, abandoning his previously elaborate style of production for cheap and no-frills studio environs. The year prior, Death Grips took the underground rap world by storm with their industrial hip-hop masterpiece *The Money Store*, and this proved to be a key influence on West’s new album. As with most Kanye West albums, his production collaborators are too many to list here, but the most notable were Thomas Bangalter of Daft Punk, Arca, and Travis Scott. With mere days left before release, West sent a version of the album estimated to be only 30% complete to legendary hip-hop producer Rick Rubin. Rubin, notable for his minimalist style, stripped the album’s production back heavily, giving the songs a spare feel that contrasted with the maximalism of *My Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy*. Named *Yeezus* (a combination of Kanye West’s nickname “Ye” with “Jesus”, just in case that wasn’t obvious), the album released in June 2018 to critical acclaim.

# Hargrove's review

The middle record in my favorite 3 album run from Kanye, *Yeezus* has some fucking bangers. This record is so aggressive, so in your face, so nonstop. In the hip-hop ecosystem at this time, Kanye played an extremely important role; he would convert the underground zeitgeist into the mainstream. It’s the same role that Charli XCX plays with hyperpop. In the case of *Yeezus*, Kanye put his elite pop sensibilities onto a genre of unbelievably harsh hip-hop music that is ever so near and dear to my heart. I remember hearing the synth from “On Sight” that opens the album and just being so shocked. The album remains consistently shocking throughout its runtime, both in terms of its sonic onslaught and its depraved lyrics. It does lack a certain freshness and unbounded creativity that albums by artists like Death Grips have, and the back half is noticeably weaker than the front half, but it has so many songs that are the perfect packaging around a sound and vibe that I cannot get enough of.

Kanye has a particular knack for opening songs, but “On Sight” might be his masterpiece. Putting the harshest instrumental on a harsh album at the very beginning took some balls, and it’s a strong mission statement for what’s to come. “Yeezy season approaching, fuck whatever y’all been hearing” indeed. The children’s choir break in the middle is a nice touch, and Kanye breaking out into laughter when he says “I put my dick in her mouuuuuuth” is his personality at its most charming. “Black Skinhead” is the single, and is an undeniable banger. The breathy beat is unique, the bass synth and the drums are phenomenal, and Kanye’s rapping on the hook is exhausting (in a good way). “I Am a God” is one of my favorites; the lyrics and premise are just peak Kanye absurdity, the drum machine is so heavy and imposing, and the synth is chilling. Genuinely one of the most atmospheric hip-hop songs ever made. “New Slaves” again is just driving and driving and driving and driving, you still can’t take a breath. Very Rick Rubin drum beat. And I have long said that the second verse is tied with the last verse on *Late Registration*’s “Gone” for the best flow (different than lyrics, Carms) Kanye has ever delivered. “Hold My Liquor” is the weakest track from the first half, but I still find it very enjoyable. It’s similar to “I Am a God” in that it’s atmospheric, it’s just that the atmosphere is a little less interesting.

“I’m In It” starts the record’s second half, and, while I do really enjoy this song, I consider it kind of a guilty pleasure. This song is just so vile, but it has some of the Kanye lines that stick with me the most. “Eating Asian pussy, all I need was sweet and sour sauce” “Put my fist in her like a civil rights sign” “I be speaking Swaghili”. I also repsect the dog barking sample. “Blood on the Leaves” might be the ultimate masterpiece of this album. Chopping up a Nina Simone song about lynching in the Jim Crow South, only to rap a melodrama about divorce. Classic fucking Kanye. The beat drop is also insane, he does such a good job of building tension for 2 whole minutes before the booming synths punches you in the face. Unfortunately, I don’t care for the next two tracks. I’ve heard “Guilt Trip” a hundred times now listening to this album, and it has never left an impression on me. The synth is kinda pretty, but it’s just a nothing song. “Send It Up” is even worse. It has the second harshest instrumental after “On Sight”, but it’s not abrasive in an interesting way. It’s pretty simple, and I just don’t like it. And Kanye’s flow is unbelievably boring. “Bound 2” is a nice reprieve to end the album, a ray of hope after 9 tracks of depravity and darkness. The sample is perfectly done, Kanye is lovable and charming, not my favorite track but consistently enjoyable.

One of Kanye’s finest. The sound works so well with his personality. Kind of a perfect counterpart to MBDTF, the minimalism following the maximalism.

Favorite tracks: On Sight, Black Skinhead, I Am a God, New Slaves, Blood on the Leaves

Least favorite track: Send It Up
