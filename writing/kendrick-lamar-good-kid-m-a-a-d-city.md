# Write-up

Kendrick Lamar Duckworth is a rapper from Compton, California, in Los Angeles County. According to published music reviewer Jack Hargrove, he is the best hip hop artist of all time. Both of his parents were born and raised on the South Side of Chicago, but they moved to Compton in 1984 after his father faced issues from his gang affiliation. Lamar was born in 1987 and grew up in Section 8 housing in Compton, the same year and city in which the founding group of West Coast hip hop, N.W.A., formed. Lamar grew up in one of the most notorious hoods in the country (a mad city) during its explosion to national prominence in the hip hop scene. Though he grew up in Piru (a subset of the Bloods) territory, he never belonged to a gang himself (a good kid). At age 5, he witnessed both his first murder, a drive-by shooting, and the violence of the LA riots. While he always had an interest in hip hop and rapping, Lamar’s 7th grade teacher introduced him to poetry and taught him how to analyze it academically. Despite being a nerdy kid, Lamar was finally dragged into the criminal world of Compton in high school. However, he pulled himself out of it before graduation. One formative episode from this period involves Lamar taking his first ever puff of a joint and discovering after that it was laced with PCP, inducing an unexpected and unpleasant high. Lamar has refrained from drugs and alcohol ever since. Lamar began rapping in public as a teenager using the name K.Dot. He became friends with Dave Free, another aspiring artist, who helped him put together a mixtape and get it to record executive Anthony “Top Dawg” Tiffith of independent label Top Dawg Entertainment (TDE). Coincidentally, and unknown to him at the time, Tiffith had robbed the chicken restaurant Lamar’s father worked at before he was born and spared his life (see the song “DUCKWORTH.” off *DAMN.* for the full story). TDE signed Lamar to a contract that included equity, and not long after he formed a supergroup called Black Hippy with labelmates Ab-Soul, Schoolboy Q, and Jay Rock. Lamar also signed a development deal with Jay-Z’s label Def Jam, but was dropped after a meeting with Jay-Z went wrong.

Lamar released a string of mixtapes at the end of the 2000s as K.Dot for TDE. They are, for the most part, not good. He released his first good project, the mixtape *Overly Dedicated*, in 2010. It was the first project credited to “Kendrick Lamar”. With the exception of one mixtape that used Lil Wayne production, production for these mixtapes was handled by TDE in-house production staff. *Overly Dedicated* caught the attention of Dr. Dre and Snoop Dogg, who began to groom Lamar as the future of West Coast hip hop. In 2011, Lamar was named to the XXL Freshman Class and released his debut full-length album *Section.80*. It was a modest commercial success that charted and garnered Lamar name recognition. It was also a massive critical success. In 2012, Lamar signed to Dr. Dre’s label Aftermath and began recording his major label debut, *good kid, m.A.A.d. city. GKMC* was a concept record billed metaphorically as “a short film by Kendrick Lamar” that tells a semi-fictionalized story of Lamar’s teenage years in Compton. *GKMC* was a massive critical and commercial success and helped launch Kendrick to the top of the rap game. It has not left the Billboard 200 Albums chart since its release 13 years ago, making it the longest charting hip hop album of all time. He released *To Pimp a Butterfly* in 2015, which is in my opinion (and, of course, the opinion of many others) his best album, but it’s annoyingly not on this list. Anyone with ears should listen to it, it is truly one of the greatest albums ever made. It’s also a concept record, though not story-based like *GKMC*. It instead explores the entire history of black music from hip hop to funk to jazz to disco to soul and how it relates to the black experience in America. While *TPAB* was Kendrick’s critical peak, he reached his first commercial peak in 2017 with *DAMN* and his first ever solo number one single “Humble”. He went on a five year hiatus from releasing study albums after the runaway success of *DAMN.*, only writing music for the soundtrack to the movie *Black Panther*. He also became the first non-classical and non-jazz musician to win the Pulitzer Prize for Music in 2018. In 2022, he returned with his fourth album *Mr. Morale and the Big Steppers*, an unexpectedly challenging double album that was a tough sell commercially but loved by critics. In 2024, Kendrick finally topped his first commercial peak from *DAMN.* through a widely publicized beef with Drake that resulted in the number 1 record “Not Like Us”. This was followed by a surprise fifth album at the end of 2024, the successful *GNX*. At the beginning of this year, he played the Super Bowl halftime show. As of right now, he’s the most popular he’s ever been and is inarguably one of the most famous rappers to ever live.

Though the voicemails are scripted skits and characters like Dave don’t correspond directly to real people, *GKMC* largely tracks with major events in Kendrick’s life. The cover is a real picture of a baby Kendrick being held by two uncles and his grandfather. The album’s story is non-chronological, telling a (mostly) coherent story through a series of vignettes. It opens in the middle of the story, with Kendrick taking his mother’s van in an attempt to hook up with a girl named Sherane, though it ends up being a ruse and two men rob him when he arrives at her house. “Bitch Don’t Kill My Vibe” flashes forward to the modern day, where Lamar reflects on the rap game and ends with a flash back to the first time he ever rapped. “Backseat Freestyle” recreates that first rap, with Lamar in a car with his gangbanging friends. They then go to burgle a house in “The Art of Peer Pressure” and narrowly avoid the police. On “Money Trees”, they revel in their loot and dream of getting rich. Lamar then gets horny on “Poetic Justice” (horny teenagers? No wonder this is the song with Drake), the last of the songs focusing on the good times (rapping, friends, money, fucking). At this point, the events of the opener happen and Lamar gets robbed as a result of his horniness. “good kid” opens up the bad times section, relating the red and blue of the Bloods and Crips to the red and blue of police sirens and explaining how both threaten to end his life. These bad experiences trigger the flashbacks of “m.A.A.d. city”, where Lamar reminisces on all of the horrible violence he’s seen in his life. It also reveals one of the meanings of “m.A.A.d.”, “My Angel’s on Angel Dust” (the other is “My Angry Adolescence Divided). These memories drive him to drink, and in “Swimming Pools (Drank)” Lamar goes to a party with his friends where he loses himself in alcohol. In the skit at the end, however, a shootout results in Lamar’s friend Dave getting killed. The first half of “Sing About Me, I’m Dying of Thirst” tells three stories. The first verse follows Dave’s brother as he tries to avenge Dave’s death, ending with him getting shot himself. The second follows the sister of the title character from “Keisha’s Song” on *Section.80* in which Keisha is a prostitute who is murdered. Keisha’s sister goes into the business as well and eventually “fades away”, likely dying of AIDS. The third verse is told from Kendrick’s point-of-view as he wrestles with whether to continue living his current lifestyle when he knows it will kill him. The second half of the track continues this reckoning, ending with Kendrick finding religion. “Real” concludes the storyline of Kendrick’s parents, who have been leaving him worried voicemails throughout the album. His dad tells him that being a gangbanger isn’t “real”, being a responsible family man is real. His mom tells him she loves him, that she hopes he gets out of his environment, and helps others avoid it in the future. The album’s closer “Compton” takes place outside the chronology of the story as Kendrick reflects on his conflicted love for his hometown. The album’s production was done by TDE’s in-house team and is a cross between the West Coast G-Funk style of Dr. Dre and the 90s Southern style of OutKast.

# Hargrove's review

One of the best hip hop albums ever made. Absolutely. I definitely prefer *To Pimp a Butterfly*, but GKMC really is almost as good. And that’s insane for such an album by such an inexperienced artist. I complain a lot about concept albums because so few of them consistently act on their concept. GKMC is a crazy outlier in that it just tells a story all the way through. It’s one of the most cinematic albums I’ve ever heard. It feels like I’m watching a movie at some points, not just in the skits but even in the verses of songs like “The Art of Peer Pressure”. My dad still believes the parent voicemails used in the albums are real (I’ll never have the heart to tell him otherwise), a credit to their authenticity. The flow of the story is just insane, non-chronology at its finest. Speaking of flow, Kendrick’s flow and lyricism are damn near a perfection of the rap form. A lot of people get kind of squeamish when you anoint Kendrick the best rapper of all time (looking at you, McGary), but I think it’s just self-evident. There is no one else who has ever sounded so natural and so varied while delivering perfectly-written lyrics. There’s not a single slip-up. Even “Backseat Freestyle”, which is purposely clumsy, just rips. And finally, what the fuck were they cooking up in the TDE in-house production team? Obviously Kendrick is a great rapper, but it’s somewhat underrated how consistently great his beat selection has been his entire career. Man, is it great here. “m.A.A.d. city” is just transcendent in its production. “Money Trees” and “Sherane a.k.a. Master Splinter’s Daughter” also come to mind (and “Wesley’s Theory” from TPAB. How is it even at all fair that the best rapper of all time just gets handed some of the best beats as well? It’s fucked up, man). Anyway, great album. Nothing new.

“Sherane a.k.a. Master Splinter’s Daughter” is such a great example of what GKMC does perfectly. It’s a tone-setter intended to introduce the story and set up the first skit. It should just be a throwaway. Most other artists would make it a throwaway. But Kendrick uses one of the single best beats he has and lays down a fantastic verse. I have always been in awe of how good this song specifically is. That bass is fucking nuts man. “Bitch, Don’t Kill My Vibe” remains one of Kendrick’s all-time best bangers. That hook is magical both melodically and lyrically. I would’ve loved for Lady gaga to be on this record, but honestly Kendrick’s delivery is kind of necessary. “Backseat Freestyle” is hilarious. Another example of a song that a lesser album would’ve been happy to use throwaway musical elements for because it’s such a story song. “Martin had a dream/Kendrick have a dream” and “I pray my dick get big as the Eiffel Tower/So I could fuck the world for 72 hours” are just masterful “first ever freestyle” lines. “The Art of Peer Pressure” just keeps it going man. One of my favorite rap songs ever, lyrically speaking. The imagery is so vivid, and the storytelling so enrapturing, it just makes me see what’s happening on the back of my eyelids. Then “Money Trees” follows up one of Kendrick’s best written songs with one of his best produced. What a ridiculously good song. And that hook too. “Poetic Justice” is the first lull in the album, but it’s still pretty good honestly. I get annoyed by Drake’s voice now after the last half-decade, but he was once quite good and he does fine here. My big complaint is that the hook is annoying, but it’s really not that big of a deal.

“good kid” is a track that I’ve sometimes felt was a bit insignificant in the past, but I really don’t give it enough credit. The “red and blue” turnaround is done so well and, though the production is a bit low-key, it’s so grimy West Coast you gotta love it. “m.A.A.d. city” is another song that could well be the best Kendrick has ever made. Perhaps the most iconic beat switch in hip hop history. Kendrick’s delivery in the first half is so animated, I think his willingness to stretch his voice is so important to the cinematic qualities of the album. It’s also a track where the lyrics are so good and flow so well that I have them all memorized purely through osmosis. “Swimming Pools (Drank)” is a pop banger that the album needed, the second half’s answer to “Bitch, Don’t Kill My Vibe”. This is another track where I have every last word memorized. Kendrick isn’t really known for hooks, but you wouldn’t know it from hearing the absolute banger they have on “Swimming Pools (Drank)”. “Sing About Me, I’m Dying of Thirst” (“SAMIDOT”) is one of the best songs ever of any genre. What a daring pick for a commercial debut. A 12 minute rumination on life and death. The three verses of the SAM section are three of the best verses ever laid to wax. It still hits me like the first time when I hear Dave’s brother get shot, or Keisha’s sister fade away. I don’t even know what to write because it’s so hard to put it into words. IDOT is the lesser section, but it’s still haunting, especially the backing vocals. This album’s biggest weakness is that it doesn’t know how to end. “Real” gets a lot of emotion out of me from the parent voicemails, but man that hook is not very good. It’s a little incessant. “Compton” is a fine song, but it feels so anticlimactic for a closer. One of the major things that makes TPAB better than GKMC is that TPAB ends perfectly.

“Money Trees”, “m.A.A.d. city”, and “Sing About Me, I’m Dying of Thirst” would all be the best song ever written by 99.9% of rappers. The concept is immaculate in both conception and execution. I legitimately got choked up when I was writing the summary of the story in the write-up. This is a great album, no two ways about it.

Favorite tracks: Sherane a.k.a. Master Splinter’s Daughter, Bitch, Don’t Kill My Vibe, Backseat Freestyle, The Art of Peer Pressure, Money Trees, good kid, m.A.A.d. city, Swimming Pools (Drank), Sing About Me, I’m Dying of Thirst

Least favorite track: Real
