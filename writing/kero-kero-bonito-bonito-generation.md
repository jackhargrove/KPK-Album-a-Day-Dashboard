# Write-up

Kero Kero Bonito is a band formed in London in 2011 consisting of singer Sarah Midori Perry (sometimes called Sarah Bonito), producer Gus Lobban, and producer Jamie Bulled. Lobban and Bulled were childhood friends who were fascinated by the Japanese hip hop scene, and in 2011 they put out advertisements online on message boards for Japanese immigrants living in London to form a band with them. Sarah Midori Perry responded. Perry was half-British, half-Japanese and lived in Japan until she was 13 and her family relocated to London. She has native fluency in both English and Japanese, and was pursuing a BA in Fine Arts at Kingston University London. She checked all the boxes, so the trio formed Kero Kero Bonito. The meaning of the name is ambiguous, but the “Kero Kero” part likely comes from the Japanese onomatopoeia for frog croaking. They released their debut mixtape *Intro Bonito* in 2014. Though it established their playful and upbeat aesthetic, the music is far more hip hop in form than any of their later releases. Sarah raps more than she sings and the instrumentals are sparser. In 2015, they released the single “Flamingo”, which would later go viral and become kind of a signature song.

KKB decided they wanted to make a full album with more fleshed-out songs, so they set to work. After over a year of work, they released *Bonito Generation* in 2016. It received moderate acclaim from music publications and reached number 9 on the Billboard World Albums Chart (chart for non-English language albums). They became well-liked on the internet because of how unique, funny, and weird they were, as well as their digital aesthetic. “Trampoline” is commonly regarded as among KKB’s best songs. In early 2018, KKB released the single “Only Acting” and shortly after the *TOTEP* EP. This EP shifted their sound immensely; instead of electropop, it was noisy indie pop/rock and shoegaze/dream pop. Sarah also was no longer singing in Japanese. This was a huge change in style, and some people reacted very negatively. In late 2018, they released their second album, *Time ’n’ Place*, one of my favorite albums of all time. It continued in the direction of the *TOTEP* EP, even including the song “Only Acting”. This was probably their peak of notoriety in the non-Internet music world (I remember “Make Believe” getting play on Sirius XMU). Although people missed the in-your-face cutesiness and Japanese, it’s pretty kick-ass music. They released the EP *Civilisation I* in 2019, leaving behind the guitars from their last album and moving to synthpop. Though it was only three tracks, it generated a good amount of buzz. COVID derailed plans, and they didn’t release the follow-up *Civilisation II* until 2021. It was again only three songs, but it was even better than the first and made a lot of people really excited. Since then, KKB have more or less vanished. They did a theme song for the Pokémon World Championships in 2023 but very little else. As far as we know, though, they are still an active group.

KKB are heavily associated with the British indie label PC Music led by A. G. Cook. Though they were never signed to it, Lobban’s solo project Kane West was, and they have collaborated with artists on the label like Danny L. Harle and Hannah Diamond. PC Music began in 2013 and, along with affiliate SOPHIE, developed a danceable, sugary sweet, and heavily ironic style of music that would become known as “bubblegum bass”. Though none of KKB’s music is bubblegum bass, their hyper-positive digital aesthetic made them natural fellow travelers, and KKB would be very influential on the genre that bubblegum bass grew into: hyperpop. After Charli XCX and 100 Gecs (KKB featured on the remix album for *1000 Gecs*) brought the PC Music sound to a broader audience in the late 2010s, hyperpop as a genre exploded. While KKB was decidedly not hyperpop, their sound continues to be influential on the genre and subsequent artists in the mid-2020s.

# Hargrove's review

I’ve spent all day saying how much I love KKB in the chat, and I don’t want to belabor the point too much. I really like them. I have enjoyed every era, every musical mode, they’ve had as it happened. I love their early “Flamingo” and *Bonito Generation* weirdo hyper-positive computer music. I love their middle period shoegaze indie pop guitar music. I love their most recent glitchy synthpop stuff, even if there was very little of it and it’s not so recent anymore. They’re just good artists. All three of them commit fully to every bit, every concept, every style. Don’t get me wrong, I love a good irony-poisoned work (lord knows I’m irony-poisoned myself), and I do absolutely think some of this album is tongue-in-cheek, but I admire how seriously they take everything. I credit her because she’s at the forefront, but Sarah makes everything work. I have always had a soft-spot for audible accents in a woman’s singing voice (I’ve overlooked some of Courtney Barnett’s pedestrian (at best) qualities thanks to her Aussie accent), but more than that Sarah kills every last line delivery. “I don’t know you, but that sounds fresh”. “Just to let you know that I don’t want to do anything today”. “Waking up is the hardest part, but, then, it’s essential”. Every last line from “Trampoline”. These have all stuck with me for years not because they’re profound or clever, but because Sarah says them so whimsically. Even though I know the radical positivity thing is on some level a bit, it never sounds that way. And the Japanese verses are such a cool signature that you don’t find anywhere else. Adds a ton of personality. The production complements all of this extremely well. Gus and Jamie do something very difficult: they cooked up some music that’s both thematically fitting and bangers. The video game synths, the playfulness of the hip hop elements, the gliding Japanese city pop stuff, it all fits what Sarah is doing so well. More importantly, every song is crazy catchy and danceable. They’re just well-constructed. That adds to the theme too, I think. None of this would work if it wasn’t perfect pop. It’s a necessary quality.

I don’t feel the need to go through each track individually. My least favorite is “Try Me”. Though I think the lyrics are clever, I find the chorus a bit grating. Every other track is on my favorites list. Special shout outs to “Waking Up”, “Big City”, “Break”, and “Trampoline”.

I’ve heaped a lot of praise, and I do mean every word, but the novelty factor does hold this album back a bit. It’s not an album that I’m always in the mood for. I don’t want to say “gimmick” because the connotation is too negative, but certainly this is such a unique style that it needs to be in the proper setting. I would rate *Time ’n’ Place* and both *Civilisation* EPs higher because they channel all this creativity and talent into music that’s more broadly listenable. Still, there’s nothing quite like *Bonito Generation*. It’s a singular record. The way that I enjoy is quite similar to the way that I enjoy Ween, so it’s fitting that I’m ranking this just two slots above *The Mollusk*. *Time ’n’ Place* would likely be in my top 5 of the list so far if we listened to it.

Favorite tracks: Waking Up, Heard a Song, Graduation, Fish Bowl, Big City, Break, Lipslap, Paintbrush, Trampoline, Picture This, Hey Parents

Least favorite track: Try Me
