# Write-up

Kids See Ghosts was a short-lived hip hop duo formed in Wyoming in 2018 that consisted of rapper and producer Kanye West and rapper and singer Kid Cudi (real name Scott Mescudi). Both were already established artists at the time. Kid Cudi was raised in Cleveland before moving to NYC to pursue a music career. Kanye West discovered him in 2008, signing him to his GOOD Music label and releasing Cudi’s mega-successful debut album *Man on the Moon* in 2009. Throughout the 2010s, West and Cudi remained frequent collaborators, though both drifted into more experimental work. Cudi fell on hard times by 2016, checking into rehab and revealing his struggles with depression and suicidal ideation. In my last Kanye West write-up, we left off with yet another controversy when he became a vocal proponent of Donald Trump’s in 2017. He dropped off the face of the earth for nearly a year, like his retreat to Hawaii during the recording of *My Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy*. He holed up at a ranch in Jackson, Wyoming, a famous ski destination. He resurfaced in April 2018 to announce he had been diagnosed with bipolar disorder, something no one was surprised by. He also announced his plan to release five albums with his production by five artists in five consecutive weeks during the Summer of 2018. Though people were skeptical given West’s public mental health crisis, Pusha T’s *Daytona*, the first record of the series, shut them up on its May 25threlease. It was well received, rejuvenating the career of the 41-year-old Pusha T. It also contained subliminal disses against Drake, with whom Kanye was beefing at the time. Within days it kicked off one of the greatest battles in rap history, capped off with Push’s “The Story of Adidon”, but that’s a story for a different time. On June 1, West released his 8th solo studio album *Ye*, which garnered critical acclaim.

The third record of the series, *Kids See Ghosts*, was released on June 8. It featured hip hop heavily influenced by psychedelic and alt rock, reflecting Cudi’s growing affinity for rock music. The opener “Feel the Love” begins with a verse recorded by Pusha T less than eight hours before the album’s release. André 3000, formerly of Outkast, contributed production to “Fire”. “Freeee (Ghost Town, Pt. 2)” is a sequel to the track “Ghost Town” from *Ye*, interpolating its most famous lyrics. “Kids See Ghosts” features Mos Def on the hook and bridge using his legal name Yasiin Bey. “Reborn” and “Cudi Montage” are the only two tracks on which West was not credited as a producer. “Cudi Montage” is built around a guitar sample taken from a 90s home recording of Nirvana’s Kurt Cobain that had been made public in 2015. The album’s artwork was designed by Japanese artist Takashi Murakami, who also designed the cover for *Graduation*. The album was a commercial success and received much critical acclaim.

West finished out the Summer of 2018 by producing Nas’ *Nasir* and Teyana Taylor’s *K.T.S.E*, released one week and two weeks after *Kids See Ghosts*, respectively. Neither was terribly good. West and Cudi performed their only live performance as Kids See Ghosts at Tyler, the Creator’s LA music festival Camp Flog Gnaw in 2018. Cudi continuously teased future releases from Kids See Ghosts in the years following 2018, but West had clearly lost interest in the idea. The duo officially broke up in 2022 when Cudi announced that he and West had a falling out and would no longer work together. West’s unmedicated bipolar disorder, which he characterized as his “superpower” during the 2018 Wyoming sessions, has increasingly destroyed his life in highly public ways in the last eight years, though I’m sure I don’t need to get into specifics. As of 2026, he remains at a low point in both his artistic and personal lives. For now, that shuts the door on my four-part saga of Kanye West.

# Hargrove's review

As I was saying last night, I associate this album with a particularly enjoyable time in my life, and therefore I am a little biased toward it. But, look, it’s got serious bangers. In just seven tracks, it gives you four basement-worthy bangers, one moving slow song, and two solidly above average tracks. There’s no filler, which is good because filler would be absolutely damning for an album of this length. I really love the way that Kanye and Cudi use psychedelia on this album. I always loved the color scheme of the album cover, and so much of the album gives off that exact vibe of vivid blues and oranges and pinks. Everything sounds spacious and grand, from the airy production to the gospel choirs. There are numerous great guitar samples, particularly the sweet Cobain find on the closer. Additionally, from the perspective of rapping, I think this album is Kanye’s best. I don’t think it’s a hot take to say that Kanye’s rapping has always lagged behind his production, and for every performance at the level of “Gone” or “New Slaves” you also have to deal with a “Highlights”. Here, however, Kanye contributes one verse to each track other than the opener, and they range from good to absolute top shelf. I went into this album a Kid Cudi skeptic and, while it didn’t assuage all of my concerns, he did impress me here. Most of his verses are tasteful and I think his aesthetic sensibilities mostly complement what Kanye was going for here. That being said, (incoming hot take) I think “Reborn” is overrated and easily this album’s weakest link. I’m sorry. But man, this is a great record. It’s such a joy to listen to. It’s sad that Kanye was hit by a bus immediately following its release.

“Feel the Love” is one of my favorite album openers of all time. What a way to open an album! Cudi finds the perfect hook to start things off before we’re treated to a vintage Push verse. I’ve always like the line “We not worried 'bout no other [REDACTED], we them other [REDACTED]”. And near the end, immediately after "Love to fuck to trap music”, Kanye comes in with, in my opinion, the single greatest moment of all the Wyoming sessions. I LOVE his gun scatting on this thing. It could easily have been trash, but he commits so hard it becomes infectious. I also love the way it overlaps with Push’s verse, implying that it’s Kanye’s over-the-top impression of trap. And then the pure AURA when he barks “Where the chorus?”. It just puts me in a good mood every time. I like “Fire” on its own merits, but I worry that it squanders some of the energy from the opener. Kanye’s verse is great, and I do like the marching tempo of the drums, but it’s a little too slow and restrained. I would maybe have preferred to hear it later on the album. It does have one of Cudi’s better verse though. It has so much going for it, but it’s just missing something. By contrast, “4th Dimension” is a BANGER. This was on my basement playlist, and for good reason. Kanye flips the Louis Prima sample to perfection while laying down his best verse on the whole album. There are sooooooooo many quotables on this thing, from “It feels so good, it should cost/Bought a alligator, I ain't talkin' Lacoste” to Ugh, uh”/Like a mix of Master P and Rick Ross, uh-uh” to “She said I'm in the wrong hole, I said I'm lost, uh-uh” to, of course, “Might need an intervention for this new dimension/That's too new to mention, or fit in a sentence/If I get locked up, I won't finish the sent—“. I love the weird cackling, I love Cudi’s verse, I love how the track is barely over two-and-a-half minutes, and I love the sample at the end. What a PERFECT song.

Next up is another BANGER in “Freeee (Ghost Town, Pt. 2)”. I’m always impressed by how talented Kanye is at translating exactly what he feels into music. This track captures a manic episode so perfectly that it puts the listener into the same state. It’s also the track where I feel Kanye and Cudi are the most integrated, where they sound the most like an actual duo instead of two solo artists. And the echo on the hook gets me going but good. With “Reborn”, I unfortunately just don’t quite get what everyone sees, at least as compared to the rest of the album. The hook is certainly catchy, but it’s also so simplistic and redundant. At over five minutes, it takes up so much real estate on such a short album. I’m also just not that invested in Cudi’s personal struggles. Kanye’s verse is his least good on the album, and, to cap it all off, the synths remind me of Radiohead’s “Kid A” (the song, not the album) which I dislike. I’m hating right now, it’s not bad per se, but I just think it has issues that get overlooked. “Kids See Ghosts” takes us back to our regularly scheduled BANGERS. I didn’t realize until this week that the Yasiin Bey on this track was Mos Def, but I like him a lot here. He puts down an amazing hook. The rest of the track drives wonderfully, and I especially love the wood block thing in the beat. Kanye’s verse rules, containing great lines like “I like breakfast in bed, but I love breakfast and head” and “Got a Bible by my bed, oh yes, I'm very Christian/Constantly repentin', 'cause, yes, I never listen” and “No not Miami, Switzerland”. The Cobain guitar sample on “Cudi Montage” works so perfectly. It’s just a haunting little loop, and I also think Cobain’s life story fits nicely into the story of this album. I’m still a Cudi skeptic, but this song does a lot to win me over. It’s well-made and moving. Kanye’s verse feels a little unrelated to the rest of the track, but when it has lines like “Everybody want world peace/’Til your niece get shot in the dome-piece/Then you go and buy your own piece/Hopin' it'll help you find your own peace”, I’m happy to look the other way.

I docked points from mbv’s *you made me realise* for being only 17 minutes as I think that inherently impairs its ability to compete with some of the full-length albums on that list, and I will apply that standard fairly here as well. It does fucking rule though.

Favorite tracks: Feel the Love, 4th Dimension, Freeee (Ghost Town, Pt. 2), Kids See Ghosts, Cudi Montage

Least favorite track: Reborn
