# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

I have been dying to hear how this record sounded ever since we listened to *In the Court of the Crimson King* in February of 2025 and I read that this 60s prog band made a new wave record just over a decade later that people actually liked. I mean, that’s crazy, right? Genesis completely destroyed its legacy for doing the same thing while Rush and Yes suffered serious aura losses. What could a record possibly sound like to actually make this shift work? Well, as it turns out, all you need to do is make a math rock version of *Remain in Light*. I absolutely adore *Remain in Light* (I have it at number 20 out of 291 at the moment) but the one real complaint I had at the time we listened was that all of the reviews cited polyrhythms and I was disappointed to find that to largely untrue. I find it genuinely pretty funny that King Crimson made a version of the record with extremely complex and winding rhythms in an attempt to satisfy me personally. And, overall, I was pretty satisfied. “Frame by Frame” is a strong contender for the best track we’ve heard in any of the three King Crimson albums on the list; the only real competition is “Red” from *Red* and “The Court of the Crimson King” from *In the Court of the Crimson King*. I also found myself getting the guitar rhythms of “Elephant Talk” and “Discipline” stuck in my head, while Bruford’s drumming on “Indiscipline” pleased me greatly. This album certainly has many strengths. Of course, it’s simply nowhere near as good as *Remain in Light*. By my measure, Belew does as good a job as he possibly could have in the frontman role, but I just don’t think he’s cut out for that kind of role. He’s no David Byrne. In fairness, who can be? But still, I found him subpar at times, though not for lack of trying. Additionally, while the rhythms were exactly what I wanted, the songs just aren’t as pristine and perfect as every track on *Remain in Light*. This is true even of the good tracks but especially glaring for the bad tracks. I cannot stand “Matte Kudasai” or “The Sheltering Sky”. But overall, this was a very solid album that I enjoyed listening to.

The album starts off strong with “Elephant Talk”. It’s hilarious to me that it’s canonically the album track to immediately follow “Starless”. Belew is good-not-great on the vocals, but lines like “These are words with a D this time” make me smile, as do the elephant noises. The guitar lines are simultaneously funky and sharp as a razor blade. Its rhythm gets stuck in my head almost as much as the banger that follow, “Frame by Frame”. The interlocking rhythms of sound so fucking COOL. I’m a sucker for rhythms that sound like they’re missing a beat, such as “Gyroscope” from *Emergency & I* and “Imposter” from *Only a Lad*. Well, go ahead and add “Frame by Frame” to that list. It also has my favorite performance from Belew, just the right amount of dramatic as he delivers perfectly paranoid lyrics. Sadly, I don’t like “Matte Kudasai” at all. “Moonchild” and “Providence” are an easy one and two for the worst King Crimson tracks I’ve heard, but this competes with “I Talk to the Wind” from *In the Court of the Crimson King* and “The Sheltering Sky” from later in this album for spot three. I hate this style of cheesy ballad. Belew makes it worse by chewing the scenery something awful. On top of that, the instrumental is one of the least interesting on the album. The album gets its heaviest on “Indiscipline”, and I like it when King Crimson gets heavy. Bruford especially shines when things get heavy, presaging the metallic clanging percussion of *Filth* here as he did on *Red*. I also find Belew’s storytelling alluring, which is also true of “Thela Hun Ginjeet”. I don’t love how thick Belew lays it on with his delivery of “Heat in the juuuuungle streeeeeeeeeeeeeet”; that being said, I do like the chanting of “Thela hun ginjeet”. It’s also cool to hear the contrast between Belew telling a fake story in the first “verse” and a real story in the second. However, my issue with the track is that the music takes too much of a backseat to the story recording. That prevents me from naming it a favorite track. It’s much better than “The Sheltering Sky”, though. As much as I don’t care for “Matte Kudasai”, this track goes on for more than EIGHT MINUTES with a completely forgettable rhythm in the background and uninteresting solos. Thankfully, “Discipline” ends the album on a high note with a nice interweaving guitar jam. It’s a great way to end on the album’s strength.

I gave two re-listens each to *In the Court of the Crimson King* and *Red* this week to help me place this album on my ranking. I appreciated *In the Court of the Crimson King* a little more this time around, but I still find it far more annoying than good. *Red* held up just as well for me as it did the first time; “Starless” is still overrated, but “Red” and “One More Red Nightmare” are still bangers. Ultimately, *Red* > *Discipline* > *In the Court of the Crimson King*, though *Discipline* is significantly closer to the former than the latter. Its high points are similarly high, but there’s too much fluff to cut. It’s a fun, solid album that fits perfectly in low-to-mid B Tier.

Favorite tracks: Elephant Talk, Frame by Frame, Indiscipline, Discipline

Least favorite track: The Sheltering Sky
