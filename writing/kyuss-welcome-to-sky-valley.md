# Write-up

Kyuss (pronounced KAI-uss) was a stoner metal/stoner rock band that formed in Palm Desert, California in 1987 whose most well-known lineup consisted of guitarist Josh Homme, vocalist John Garcia, bassist Scott Reeder, and drummer Brant Bjork. Kyuss was the most important band of the Palm Desert scene of the early 90s, and its members went on to form other major desert rock bands, notably Josh Homme of Queens of the Stone Age and The Eagles of Death Metal. The band originally formed in 1987 when they were all teenagers (Homme was 14!) and originally included Nick Oliveri on bass, who would leave the group in 1992 but who would be a founding member of QotSA. They originally called themselves Katzenjammer (German for “hangover”), but ended up renaming themselves Sons of Kyuss after a monster from a Dungeons & Dragons set they had. They released their debut EP in 1990 and shortened their name to Kyuss. Throughout the early 90s, Kyuss made a name for themselves locally in Palm Desert, playing at large outdoor parties. Josh Homme’s guitar playing in particular became very recognizable, as he would not only tune his guitar down, but he would play through a bass amp, which gave his guitar a booming, low-end-heavy sound. They released their debut album in 1991 and their second in 1992, earning them enough fame to tour Australia with Metallica. In 1994, Kyuss released its third album, *Welcome to Sky Valley*, its largest commercial success to date. However, drummer Brant Bjork left the group shortly after recording was finished. Kyuss released its fourth and final album in 1995, *…And the Circus Leaves Town*; it was a relative commercial failure, and the band broke up immediately afterward. Because of Kyuss’ legendary status, there have been many attempts at a reunion, but Josh Homme consistently refuses to partake.

*Welcome to Sky Valley* is Kyuss’ most celebrated album, and the most representative album to come out of the Palm Desert scene. The original CD release grouped all 10 songs into just 3 tracks, making it impossible to easily listen to individual songs and forcing the listener to listen to the whole album. *Welcome to Sky Valley* is prototypical stoner rock, with down-tuned, fuzzy guitars and extended jams. Some have compared Kyuss to the Velvet Underground given their influence in modern hard rock circles. The Palm Desert scene is best known for producing Queens of the Stone Age, a direct spin-off of Kyuss that took the overall spaced-out stoner vibe but added cleaner riffs picked up from grunge during Homme’s interlude in Seattle in the late 90s.

# Hargrove's review

Given how much I hate weed and stoner culture, it’s funny to me how consistently I love stoner music, and this is no different. Queens of the Stone Age are one of my favorite bands, but despite general familiarity and my dad loving them, I had never listened to Kyuss before. Honestly, this album just rips. It’s the atmosphere of Sleep with the songwriting of QotSA. It also often sounds like a live album, with the handoffs from instrument to instrument during the jams feeling so real. It would’ve been awesome to go to one of the desert parties they played at. The only miss for me is “Conan Troutman”, and that’s the shortest track. Otherwise, this is just super fun from front to back, a supremely enjoyable listen. “Gardenia” starts everything off with the fuzzy atmospheric guitar right away, introducing the unique flavor that will define the record. I’m not usually a big jam guy, but I find the ones on this record enjoyable because of how good the drumming is, which keeps everything grounded. On this particular track, the jam is so psychedelic baby, and the cymbals are used to great effect. I love the textures in the guitar, everything works. The central melody of “Asteroid” is phenomenal, but I have to keep it off of my favorite tracks list just because I don’t love the free improvisation breakdown halfway through. It reminded me of “Moonchild” from *In the Court of the Crimson King*, and we can’t be having that. Otherwise though, it’s a pretty good track, and the accelerando at the end really reminded me of something off of *Daydream Nation*. I was a bit skeptical at the beginning of “Supa Scoopa and Mighty Scoop” but the chorus kicks so much ass that it won me over. I think the main guitar riff is potentially the best on the album, and the drumming in the second half is amazing. “100°” is a fun, concise track that contrasts with the much longer tracks found elsewhere. The main vocal melody is good but the drums are the star of the show. “Space Cadet” fucking rules and is the best song on the album. That acoustic bass line is so good for repetition, and I particularly love the tiny bit of anticipation every time the accented beat approaches. I generally love it when metal goes acoustic, and it works wonders here. I was ready to leave “Demon Cleaner” off of my favorite tracks list because the “yeah yeah yeah yeah” refrain is annoying, but the ending of the song is so phenomenal that it makes up for it. Every time I listen to this I don’t expect it to build as magically as it does but WOW do the drums just drive things to a fucking climax. “Odyssey” has some of the most overbearing guitar work on the album (in a good way). The songwriting is unfortunately a tad insubstantial, but the guitar is plenty to win me over. “Conan Troutman” unfortunatley goes too much in a thrash direction and I don’t think they pull it off. It’s still fun, but its vibe is just too different from everything else for me. “N.O.” on the other hand has the most stoner metal vibes to me, and I think that’s great. The drums are the highlight of the track, but I also enjoy the stop-start guitar licks. “Whitewater” is a complete and total banger. There’s something so ominous about the guitar riff, and the drums are great too. Th song just rips. “Lick Doo” is fine, doesn’t move me one way or the other. I need to spend more time with this (even though I got 6 listens in this week), but as it stands now I still definitely prefer *Songs for the Deaf*. This is pretty damn close though.

Favorite tracks: Gardenia, Supa Scoopa and Mighty Scoop, 100°, Space Cadet, Demon Cleaner, Odyssey, Whitewater

Least favorite track: Conan Troutman

# Aidan's review

Wasn’t a fan of Josh Homme’s other album on this list, Songs for the Deaf and I enjoyed this a lot more. Still some songs that are pretty similar to Songs for the Deaf, with Homme’s signature “I can’t believe it’s not a bass guitar” lead guitar. Gardenia and Spa Scooper defiantly tread the same ground, but they’re a touch slower and the instruments are far muddier, which I enjoyed more. However, the real treat are the more instrumental trio of Space Cadet, Demon Cleaner and Whitewater (minus the jokey Lick Doo section). Homme’s guitar really shines as he works into a groove and I think the vocals fit really well with the laid back style. The lyrics aren’t anything to write home about, but the guitars are enough to make it worth a listen. I didn’t find the album outstanding, but a it’s a solid album with a lot of influence.
