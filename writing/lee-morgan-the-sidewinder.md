# Hargrove's review

This is a nice but ultimately completely forgettable jazz album. I have now listened to it 14 (!) times over the last two weeks, and I can still barely keep anything from any of the tracks in my head. Fundamentally, the issue is that the lead instruments just aren’t all that great or memorable. Trumpet plus tenor sax is about as generic a jazz solo instrument pairing as you can get, and neither Morgan nor Henderson do anything special with the instruments to make anything stick. It’s a shame too because the rhythm section on this record is phenomenal. Higgins shifts seamlessly between half time and double time on the drums frequently, Cranshaw has some genuinely inventive bass lines, and Harris steals the show in solo sections on his piano. But the lead instruments just can’t pull their weight in the solos, nor can Morgan’s compositions stand out. It’s really just an in one ear, out the other album for me. And I gave it so many listens!

I’m so surprised that “The Sidewinder” was a massive crossover hit. It barely leaves any impression on me at all. I like the bounciness of the rhythm section, but it lacks the kind of melody it needs to be truly great. Then, once you get into the solos, nothing outshines the piano. The bass solo is also decent, but I feel like it just highlights the nothingness of everything else. Look, it’s a nice jazz track. I wouldn’t hate hearing it playing in the background. But it’s really nothing special. I like “Totem Pole” way more. It’s the only track with a head melody that stays with you once you enter the solo section. I really enjoy the alternation between slow sections and fast sections, something that extends past the head and into the solos. I also love the piano vamp in the solo section, something I never thought I would specifically point out about a jazz track. The solos are also better on this one than the opener. “Gary’s Notebook” is pretty decent too and also better than the title track. This one has a cool quarter note groove that sounds super interesting in swung music like this (without eighth notes, the quarter notes are just played straight, which gives it the jarring rhythmic feel). “Boy, What a Night” and “Hocus-Pocus” are both forgettable snoozers. I like the latter better than the former because of a standout piano solo.

This is the shortest review I’ve written in a while, but I really don’t have a ton to say. It’s not terrible, but it’s not special either. It’s something I’d want to hear playing at a club, not something I would put on of my own volition, and definitely not something I would put on an essentials list.

Favorite tracks: Totem Pole, Gary’s Notebook

Least favorite track: Boy, What a Night
