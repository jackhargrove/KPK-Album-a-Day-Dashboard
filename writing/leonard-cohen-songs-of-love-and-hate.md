# Hargrove's review

This is our first true folk singer-songwriter album, a genre that I think has really high highs (love me some Bob Dylan and Joni Mitchell) and really low lows (hate me some Donovan and Ed Sheeran). The only Leonard Cohen songs I had heard before this were “Some Like It Darker” off of his final album before he died in 2016 and, of course, “Hallelujah”. Otherwise, he’s been a pretty big blind spot for me and someone I’ve been meaning to check out for a while. I found this to be much more on the Dylan side of the spectrum, but overall I thought there was something…lacking. There were a few songs that really resonated with me (namely, “Avalanche”, “Dress Rehearsal Rag”, and “Famous Blue Raincoat”), but I thought most of the other tracks were good but not great. The lyrics did remind me of Dylan in their opacity and philosophy, but there was never a line that completely blew me away in construction like Dylan’s “The ghosts of electricity howl in the bones of her face” for example. I don’t want this to sound overly negative, I would definitely say that I liked this album and would like to listen to more Leonard Cohen albums, but I must admit that I found it difficult to keep this album playing over and over all day in a way that I have not with most other albums.

“Avalanche” is completely brilliant from beginning to end. The guitar sounds so cool; I believe that Cohen called it “chop guitar”, but I know it as tremolo guitar. There’s something about it that is very evocative of Americana to me and conjures a kind of lonely, hopeless cowboy vibe. The swelling strings are also really well done, and I have to imagine they were a major influence on Van Dykes Parks’ string arrangements on Joanna Newsom’s *Ys*. The lyrics are absurdly opaque but also haunting and fascinating. I spent hours today just reading over them again and again and trying to glean what I could. All the stuff about “You who wish to conquer pain” is intriguing to me, as are the narrator’s mood swings from verse to verse. Ending with “It is your flesh that I wear” is striking as well. My favorite lyrics on the album, though, come from “Dress Rehearsal Rag”. Cohen doesn’t say the word “suicide” even once, but it was immediately apparent to me what it was about. The first verse of self-loathing is extremely well-done, as is the reminiscence of the third verse. I’m also quite impressed by naming this routine of self-hatred as a “dress rehearsal” for suicide. The melody on the chorus is superb, as are the strings and the children’s choir. “Famous Blue Raincoat” has my favorite vocal performance; Cohen has a great poker face here, his emotions are really tough to crack. That works very well with the lyrics, as I still can’t tell whether this dude is angry, happy, or neutral over his wife fucking this other guy. Great melodrama, made greater by the waltzing guitar, and I think it’s underrated vis a vis ambiguity. The chorus has this great lilting quality to it as well.

The above three tracks are transcendent and they stand up to anything Dylan or Joni have ever released. The other 5 tracks are merely good. “Last Year’s Man” is the only track that compares to “Avalanche” in terms of lyrical opacity, but I don’t find the imagery quite as evocative in this track. I like the strings and choir at the end, but the rest of the track is a bit too sparse. Still has some highlights though, particularly verse 2 (describing my dream woman, I want to be one of those soldiers). “Diamonds in the Mine” is probably my least favorite track, it feels like the most generic 60s activist folk of any song on the album. The whole Lorax thing is pretty overdone and banal. I do like the random pornography of verse 2 though, and Cohen’s screaming as the song goes on gets pretty fun. I like the structure, concept, and the guitar work of “Love Calls You By Your Name”, but the melody isn’t my favorite, and I think the “Between…” refrain doesn’t work terribly well. I really respect the ending of “Sing Another Song, Boys”: “But let’s leave these lovers wondering/Why they cannot have each other/And let’s sing another song, boys/This one has grown old and bitter”. I love ambiguous endings, and I just appreciate that Cohen leaves their story hanging once it’s not interesting anymore. The music is a bit upbeat for my tastes, but I do love the crescendoes at the end of each verse. I really love the concept of “Joan of Arc”, but I wound up finding the structure to be a little conventional and unimaginative. The dual tracked vocals are cool though. And huge respect to Cohen for how he’s obviously in love with Joan of Arc in multiple songs. I’m right there with you, Lenny.

This album has some real greatness on it, but it’s just a little too scattershot. That being said, I did appreciate how much of it came from a place of raw emotion, raw sadness. While sometimes the hate seemed to win out, there was always just enough love, just enough sweetness to keep it going. Will definitely listen to more Cohen in the future.

Favorite tracks: Avalanche, Dress Rehearsal Rag, Famous Blue Raincoat

Least favorite track: Diamonds in the Mine
