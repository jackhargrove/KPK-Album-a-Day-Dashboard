# Write-up

Les Rallizes Désnudés were a Japanese noise band whose sole permanent member was lead singer and guitarist Takashi Mizutani. The band's name is typically said to be a mistranslation of the nonsense phrase "naked suitcases" into French, although various members have said it instead comes from Japanese slang, the novel Naked Lunch or a Kyoto based theater group. The band started at Doshisha University in 1967, where Mizutani gathered band members among fellow left wing students. While the band itself was not political, its members generally had very strong left wing ties, specifically to the Japanese Red Army terrorist group. The original bassist of the band was a member of a plane hijacking with the Red Army after leaving the band and currently lives in exile in North Korea. The band was completely unknown to the West and most of Japan during its existence. This is possibly due to Mizutani keeping a low profile post-hijacking. They also did not release any albums while active. After a few of their live performances were released to some acclaim in the 1990s, the band reformed for a few years and recorded a few more releases. Around 1999, the band became to be known to the American underground, being mentioned in print and there are some anecdotal reports of bootlegs circulating and listening parties taking place. By 2007, there was a book written on the Japanese underground music, and more articles were being written about the band. Interest in the band really took off around 2020, as music nerding became a popular pastime among people stuck at home with nothing else to do but listen to obscure Japanese bands. Unfortunately, Mizutani died in 2019, a fact that was only discovered in 2021. In recent years, the band has created a website and began releasing official recordings. '77 Live is their most well liked one.

# Hargrove's review

This album is not my thing and I struggle to see why this band has developed such a cult following. Their history is not as mysterious or ominous as people online make it out to be, though I do concede the plane hijacking thing is interesting. Their heavy feedback/fuzz sound wasn’t even really ahead of its time; sure, they use fuzz to a much greater extent than any 60s garage rock band, but like the Velvet Underground was making full fuzz drone tracks 10 years earlier. I think the survival via later tape trading communities is kind of cool, but as a born and bred vinyl boy I never get that wowed by anything on cassette. None of this would matter though if the music were particularly interesting or good. Unfortunately, it’s just…meh. There are a couple of tracks where the bass line becomes significantly heavy and the repetition becomes fun to get lost in. But overall, this album is just kind of bland. I don’t know. I enjoyed it a little more on my final listen, but that’s pretty standard. The second track, “夜、暗殺者の夜 (The Night, Assassin’s Night)”, is the only thing that saves this album from the approaching the very bottom of my ranking. It’s a real song with a good bass riff and a super catchy vocal melody. Otherwise, this album feels like what I call an “experience” album, where the music is more focused on creating an interesting sonic experience than leaving you with well-crafted songs. *Bitches Brew* is an example of an experience album that pulls this off perfectly, making you feel like you’re undergoing an experience every time you listen. And it makes sense to apply this framework here, as it was performed live in concert after all. Maybe the experience was better then. But listening to it on Spotify, I don’t get caught up in the experience at all, and instead am left with nothing when it ends.

“Enter the Mirror” begins things slow and vibe-y, with the feedback distortion not yet at crazy levels. The slow tracks on this album are the absolute worst. They don’t even provide the propulsion and motion that some tracks do, instead just a vibe that does not move me. This one is painfully slow, with the vocals feeling like they’ll never end. The distorted guitar solo at the end is good though. “夜、暗殺者の夜 (The Night, Assassin’s Night)” is by far the best track. It has a super catchy bass line that reminds me of some really great 60s stuff, even like the Beach Boys or Beatles but with a ton of fuzz. The vocal melody slaps as well, and it maintains its quality throughout its length. “氷の炎 (Flame of Ice)” is not quite as good but is still decent. It has a crushing bass sound with a 6/4 rhythm that I really appreciate. It’s decent. “記憶は遠い (Memory Is Far Away)” eschews the pattern of putting white noise feedback over real songs and instead just focuses on the white noise feedback. The groove slows down a lot as well. The result is not great. “夜より深く (Deeper than the Night)” starts disc 2 with more long, slow, static, motionless dreck. But this time it sounds like cowboy music, making it worse. It’s my least favorite track. “夜の収穫者たち (Reapers of the Night)” is a borderline favorite because it keeps things fast and short. It’s propulsive, with an eighth note bass line that goes nonstop. It’s decent, but I can’t think of why I’d listen to this over something else. “The Last One” is similar in style to some of the earliest songs, relying on one big, hulking bass line to repeat across the song. It’s just not as good as the other tracks like that. And too long as well. This whole album is too long.

Even when it works, I don’t want to be listening to it. I can’t even hate it because it’s just nothing. It does nothing for me..

Favorite tracks: 夜、暗殺者の夜 (The Night, Assassin’s Night), 氷の炎 (Flame of Ice)

Least favorite track: 夜より深く (Deeper than the Night)

# Aidan's review

I love a "Thing Japan" as much as the next guy, but this is just an egregious case of it. There's a great baseline lurking in most of the songs, and the blown out speakers feel far better than they have any right to, but there's just not a lot there. The story of the band is so cool, from their terrorist bassist to the way that their music was preserved through bootleg tapes until they gained acclaim nearly a half century later, that I really want to find some glimmer of genius to top off the narrative. I can't. It's not horrendous; I enjoyed my listens and Assassin's Night is a good song, but I can't in good faith say that it's good music. I'll still give it a few points for being from Japan though.
