# Write-up

Lightning Bolt are a noise rock duo originally from Providence, Rhode Island. The band is composed of drummer/vocalist Brian Chippendale and bass guitarist Brian Gibson. The two met in 1994 during their freshman year at the Rhode Island School of Design and quickly formed a band with a friend, Hisham Bharoocha. Bharoocha soon left for another band, which would become local peer Black Dice. Soon after forming the band, Chippendale and his roommate established "Fort Thunder", an abandoned warehouse that would serve as the base of operations for underground music and art until it was demolished in 2002 to make way for a supermarket. The band started as an improv band and only started writing songs after 3 years of playing. It took another 2 years to put out their first album and since then they have released 7 total albums, which mixed studio and live recordings. The band draws from Japanese noise groups like Boredoms and avant garden composers like Sun Ra and Phillip Glass. Chippendale uses a microphone from a home telephone for all of his vocals, which, along with the band's penchant for loud instrumentals, makes his vocals difficult to understand. The band is known for their "guerrilla gigs" in which they would play in unexpected locations and times, including on the floor of concerts, immediately after the opener, in kitchens, on parking lots and, during one festival, at noon on the doorway of famed DJ (and after his death, known pedophile) John Peel's tent. The band are currently touring with Boredoms drummer Yoshimi P-We's group OOIOO.

# Hargrove's review

This album really clicked with me. Given my proclivity for rhythm sections, it’s perhaps not terribly surprising that a bass guitar and drum rock duo won me over, though it’s certainly a unique sound. It takes some adjustment, but once you let the pounding rhythms and weirdly Baroque melodies wash over you, it becomes mesmerizing. Gibson’s bass lays the foundation for the entire album, acting as the timekeeper, groove-setter, and even contributing melodies on around half the songs. This allows Chippendale’s drums to take the place of a lead guitar, showing off all over the place. As much as I like the drums, I do think the bass is my favorite part of the album. Chippendale also provides some of the most minimal vocals I’ve ever heard, inaudibly buried in the mix and covered with feedback. These vocals act more as a textural piece, giving an eerie feel to the whole production. It’s all so simple, and yet it just works. I don’t really know how to describe it. It just works. It all comes together into a complete whole. The album’s front half clears its back half by a good deal, but the back half is still enjoyable, if a little less inspired. On the whole, I just love the combination of unusual rhythms with repetitive pounding. It’s a little like a math rock Swans. Repetition and shifting rhythms might sound antithetical, but Lightning Bolt puts them together with ease.

“Hello Morning” starts the album with an atmosphere of Gibson and Chippendale are tuning their instruments. It feels like a collecting of thoughts. “Assassins” begins the album in earnest and kicks off a chain of bangers. The bass starts pounding your eardrums right away. It’s just relentless. I like this track a lot, particularly the staccato structure near the end. “Dracula Mountain” is one of two tracks with a recognizable vocal melody and, like many of the bass melodies, it sounds super Baroque. I like the mathy, stuttery, uneven rhythm a lot, as well as the way the bass eventually follows the vocal melody. It took quite a few listens before “2 Towers” clicked with me, but now I really like it. It’s interesting how disconnected the bass and drums sound on this track. They start off playing antagonistic lead lines and then develop into separate rhythmic patters once the repetition starts. It’s deeply off-putting at first, but eventually it becomes hypnotic in the same way that Radiohead’s “Weird Fishes/Arpeggi” is. Then the bass melody picks up in the final two minutes, sounding almost like an actual writhing bolt of lightning. It’s hard to describe, but very very cool. “On Fire” contributes another Baroque melody, though it also kind of sounds like metal with the scales in tremolo. The drums go a bit off the rails at the end, but I still really like this track. “Crown of Storms” is my favorite cut on the album. I just do not understand how the bass gets a tone that Goddamn muddy. It boggles the mind. It’s so dissonant and rusty and loose, and yet simultaneously it plays these Baroque melodies of flailing electricity between the ceaseless pounding. Complete brutality, in the best way. After this point, the album drops off in quality, thoush it’s still quite enjoyable. “Longstockings” is the most accessible, poppy track until the chaos of the final minute. I find myself wanting a little more out of the understated main section and a little less out of the noisy coda. “Wonderful Rainbow” is a nice, quiet little interlude with a fascinating chord progression. “30,000 Monkies” is absolute chaos, constantly on the verge of forming into something but never quite getting there. It’s the most disappointing track to me. “Duel in the Deep” ends the album on a high-intensity Swans-esque grinding bass and drum groove. I like the way it closes the album, but it’s definitely not one of my favorite tracks.

This album is a cool little novelty that impressively sounds completely distinct even after 240+ albums. I got into a really nice groove with it and enjoyed each listen. I’m sad to have to put it down.

Favorite tracks: Assassins, Dracula Mountain, 2 Towers, On Fire, Crown of Storms

Least favorite track: 30,000 Monkies

# Aidan's review

I was a bit harsh on these guys when I first listened to them, comparing them to Merzbow to Hargrove, but this is an album that lives and dies by how much energy can be packed a drum beat over wild guitar noise. The opener and first few songs do a good job of setting it up, with a drone-y first track leading int to a raucous "Assassins", and then easing up with a recognizable baseline in "Dracula Mountain" and even mostly cutting the fuzz in "2 Towers". Unfortunately, at that point, the album mostly runs out of tricks to keep the energy going and while all of the other songs are more balanced between raw noise and quieter bits, the albums wears out its welcome far before it wears through its 40 minute runtime. I honestly like most of these songs individually, the drone into noise schtick is a soft spot of mine when Sonic Youth or Boredoms does it, but the transitions here are just so harsh, and there's so little else to dig into here, the energy escapes me and it's left feeling too much like a roller coaster of chaos and calm without any reason to push through. I'm very sympathetic to the ideas behind this record, but this record is so singular with the ideas of its sound that there's little else to latch onto.
