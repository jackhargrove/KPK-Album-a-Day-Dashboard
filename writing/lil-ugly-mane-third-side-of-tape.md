# Write-up

Lil Ugly Mane (real name Travis Miller) is a rapper from Richmond, Virginia, born in 1984. See my above write-up on his debut album *Mista Thug Isolation* from over a year ago for biographical and career details. *THIRD SIDE OF TAPE* was the third and final installment of the *Three Sided Tape* project Lil Ugly Mane worked on following the release of *Mista Thug Isolation*. The *Three Sided Tape* all feature archival tracks that Lil Ugly Mane had made between 1999 and 2012. *Three Sided Tape Volume One* and *Three Sided Tape Volume Two* both released in 2013 and were about one hour long each. By contrast, *THIRD SIDE OF TAPE* is over two hours long, featuring six approximately 20 minute tracks made up of around 50 individual musical ideas stitched together. The project is noted for its mind-boggling genre scope, spanning hip hop, punk rock, ambient, electronic, industrial, and black metal. “SIDE ONE-B” samples João Gilberto, while “SIDE THREE-B” samples GZA (from the track “Living in the World Today” off of *Liquid Swords*), Björk, and Slick Rick. The album was re-released in 2021 with a new cover and the track listing broken up into constituent parts instead of as massive collages. The 2021 version is the one found on streaming platforms. The album was received positively by the internet, drawing conceptual comparisons with Swans’ *Soundtracks for the Blind*.

# Hargrove's review

Given that we’re now so close to the end of the list, almost every remaining review represents the last of something. Lil Ugly Mane’s *THIRD SIDE OF TAPE* is our last super long album over 1:30:00 that requires two days to listen to. To be honest, I haven’t had as much of a problem with this category of record as you might think. Sure, I complain about albums being too long often, but I actually find albums in the 50-75 minute range to be the worst offenders. An album over 75 minutes often has legitimate reasons for it in a way that an album just trying to round out CD space does not. Unfortunately, I do have an issue with the two-plus hour length of *THIRD SIDE OF TAPE*. Holy FUCKING shit, what an unbelievable slog of an album this is. Every listen feels like an eternity. I’ve been excited for this album because it was clear to me with *Mista Thug Isolation* that Lil Ugly Mane is crazy talented and I wanted to see what he could do with a bunch of different genres. What makes genre-hopping albums cool is that they present a ton of different sounds under one unifying artistic vision. But because *THIRD SIDE OF TAPE* is collected from two decades worth of unrelated archival material, it does not feel like one consistent artistic vision despite it all being made by the same guy. It’s more akin to a poorly constructed playlist than a diverse, eclectic album. Unsurprisingly, most of the best segments are hip hop. That’s where Ugly Mane’s talents lie. That being said, so many of the hip hop tracks feel incomplete, consisting of little more than a hook and a beat. More surprisingly, most of the post-punk/darkwave/alt rock segments work too. Some of the EDM segments are decent, but there are a lot of misses. The industrial and noise segments suck so bad. And there are so many of them! And his forays into metal, reggae, and country exist more to check a box than they do to make interesting art with any of those genres. I’m not mad at Ugly Mane at all for making this; the dude appears to have serious interests in a ton of genres, and it’s totally fair to put together music you have on hand when you have fans that want to listen. I am, however, furious at /mu/, RYM, Fantano et al. for leading me to believe that there’s any reason to listen to this if you’re not some kind of Lil Ugly Mane historical preservationist. What a waste of my time.

I listened to this using the original release instead of the streaming version, meaning that the album is sorted into six super long tracks made up of various segments. However, I went through and matched the segment titles from streaming to this version so that I could more easily discuss specifics. Despite containing my absolute least favorite segment on the album, “SIDE ONE-A” is in the top half of tracks on this album. “twin tip” starts it off strong with decent instrumental hip hop. Only two minutes in, it then pivots to a horrendous four minutes of industrial noise with the Throbbing Gristle rip-off “use”. Why even place this here? It’s like Ugly Mane didn’t want this record to have a chance. “wurdemkittykatzat” is solid hip hop, especially in contrast to the previous segment. I wish there were more to it though. “rather be broke” is the best segment on this track. It would make a banger hook on a *Mista Thug Isolation* track. The first alt rock segment, “grott”, is a bizarrely decent instrumental emo jam. I could genuinely see myself enjoying this on a Midwest emo album. “zlim” ends the first track with some subpar hip hop. “SIDE ONE-B” goes for genre whiplash harder than any other track by a wide margin. It SUCKS. Bizarrely, “needledrinker” starts things off on a high note. What a banger beat! I wish it lasted more than two minutes. Then “country crott commercial” comes in with a generic fiddle and acoustic guitar just so that there’s a country song on here. “luck and fate” sets post-punk guitars against a robotic 808 loop. It’s not great. “plauz” exists just so that there’s a generic reggae track on here. Given that it’s the first EDM track, my instinct was to write off “bitrate” as well. However, it’s not bad techno. I would listen to an album of this. “who’s getting rich?” returns to hip hop with a lackluster, overlong beat. While I generally like Ugly Mane’s darkwave stuff on here, “crawl” is easily the least interesting. “predominance” has an unorthodox sound that I could see myself liking as a palate cleanser on a better album; here, though, it’s just more bullshit to deal with. Weirdly, this shitty track ends well, as “can i?” contains maybe the best hip hop beat on the album. That horn sample rules! I wish the rest of this track weren’t so awful.

“SIDE TWO-A” is almost entirely forgettable from front to back, making it a waste of time and my least favorite as a result. “dredd” starts things off with yet another hook and beat hip hop track. The beat is only a little above average while the hook blows, making it a bad segment. “wellma” is an interlude too short to leave an imprssion. I enjoy the spacey synths of “luxurious conduct”, but the techno/trance/boom bap fusion does little for me otherwise. The only segment on this track worth a damn is the fuzzy, lo-fi punk of “punish explode”. I like the jarring transition from the previous segment, the searing guitar, and the catchy chorus. “leave” merges a house vocal sample with techno synths. It’s fine, a little better than the other EDM segment on this track. “murt” closes things with a stupid home recording of some guy singing a calypso. How the fuck are we only halfway through this album? “SIDE TWO-B” jumps from genre to genre almost as much as “SIDE ONE-B”, but it does so more fluidly. It also contains multiple of the precious few long-form hip hop segments on this album. “fluid” is a fine, hazy instrumental hip hop track, but I’ve lost any patience for fine but insubstantial music at this point. “dist” is a bit more soulful. I could see this beat on *Mista Thug Isolation*. Still, it doesn’t blow me away. “jack scruby” is a bland experimental piece with sixteenth note hi-hats over a sample of someone choking or something. I find it immensely unenjoyable. “Sittin’ on Chrome” is a simple, unimpressive house segment. The album finally has its first long-form hip hop segment with “dert sceptre”. There are verses and everything! And wouldn’t you know it, it bangs. Ugly Mane spits a hard fucking verse over a confrontational beat. There’s the ticket. “casting a spell” returns to instrumental hip hop. It’s one of the better beats on this track but it feels like it’s missing something after we got verses on the last segment. “filth and satan” is genuinely black metal, not just “black metal influenced”, I’ll give it that. I must ask, though: is it a good black metal track? It’s okay. It feels closer to genre pastiche than a real effort to make black metal. I don’t think I would particularly enjoy it if it came up on a black metal album we listened to for the list. “runner” transitions out of black metal and straight into rave EDm. It’s kind of relaxing for the first minute because it’s in half time and the synth line is pretty. That synth line is enough for me to name this segment a favorite as it’s one of the only melodies I can remember after a given segment ends. “rennur” is a short jazzy interlude that transitions into a slightly longer instrumental hip hop interlude. Whatever. We get another full-length hip hop segment with “SUNDAY DRIVE”. The beat’s not anything special, but damn are there some bars here. “clune” is another annoying interlude, this time using some kind of carnivalesque video game noises. And “sog” ends the fourth track with the millionth chill instrumental hip hop segment. He must have just had files upon files of these lying around.

I can’t believe I’ve come to this decision given how strongly I dislike the opening segment, but “SIDE THREE-A” has to be my favorite track. Every second of the final two-thirds at least has redeeming qualities. You have to first get through “lamb”, though, and that’s not easy. It’s another long, drawn-out Throbbing Gristle knock off. Ugly Mane is just not good at these. He doesn’t make them intriguing in any way. “so much” makes me giddy based purely off the fact that it’s a full-length hip hop segment. It’s not the greatest song I’ve ever heard, but it’s a real oasis in the desert. “the serving” is another damn solid post-punk/alt rock segment. Ugly Mane has a knack for melancholy hooks and fuzzy guitars, who knew? “infinite rave” is happy hardcore (EDM), making it aesthetically offensive to me. Damn, though, does it have a nice chorus. I also have to give it credit for being one of the few segments I’ve remembered between album listens. And I have to admit, “evil world” makes me laugh. “I sent a bomb to a computer expert” is a hilarious phrase. “SIDE THREE-B” takes a considerable step back in quality from the previous track, but, hey, at least it’s almost over. “transport helicopter” contains some of the blandest instrumental trap I’ve ever heard. “shakey little legs” brings more hardcore EDM, giving the end of this album an odd rave bent. Whatever that string instrument sample is, I never want to hear it again. “bush gardens” creates a weird collage of TV commercials and rapid-fire electronic earrape. It sucks! “separate and stay” has only the intro of an upbeat guitar pop track before cutting off, making it an unsatisfying tease. “un in” hard pivots to metal, but this time the beefy riffs and plodding tempo make it sound more doom than black. I like it much better than the black metal segment. “caught in the web” returns to the classic hip hop beat plus a hook formula, reminding me once again that this album is completely pointless. “colm” consists of industrial noise for twenty seconds. That’s okay, though, because it’s just a warm up for three more minutes of industrial noise with “cellar”. Someone needs to tell Ugly Mane that he brings nothing new to this genre! “keep me going” and “toid” are both in that post-punk darkwave style that Ugly Mane actually does bring something valuable to. I like both segments a good bit. “equipped for the job” combines noisy kick drums with clashing vocal samples. It’s incessant and aggravating. I hate it. “cardinal” ends the album in the only way it knows how: a decent but entirely insubstantial instrumental hip hop beat.

To put it bluntly: I’m pretty glad that I never have to listen to that album again. It’s a full hour shorter than *69 Love Songs* but feels just as long. And I fucking hate *69 Love Songs*. I really didn’t want things to go this way, but I think I have to put it in F Tier. Only Fishmans’ *98.12.28 Otokotachi no Wakare (98.12.28 男達の別れ)* and Les Rallizes Dénudés’ *’77 LIVE* match the feeling of wasted time that I’ll never get back. I’m putting this above both because the highs are at least higher, though I’ll be re-listening to the former next week, so it’s subject to change.

Favorite tracks: SIDE THREE-A

Favorite segments: twin tip, rather be broke, grott, needledrinker, bitrate, can i?, punish explode, dert sceptre, runner, SUNDAY DRIVE, so much, the serving, un in, keep me going, toid

Least favorite track: SIDE TWO-A

Least favorite segment: use
