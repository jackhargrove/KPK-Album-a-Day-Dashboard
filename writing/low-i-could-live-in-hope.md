# Hargrove's review

I was nonplussed on first listen because it seemed like it was doing a lot the same things Red House Painters did but with a vocalist/lyricist I connected to less, but it grew on me with successive listens. The vocals are positively dreamy, both when they’re singing solo and in duet. This is also a really good album for a lazy Sunday so that might have played into it as well. Despite my issues with the sequencing (“Sea” right after “Lullaby” is an awful decision), this works really well as an album, keeping a strong atmosphere from track to track. Outside of some major standouts though, I wasn’t crazy impressed with the songwriting. “Words”, “Lazy”, “Lullaby”, and “Rope” are great standalone tracks, but some of the other ones don’t do much to distinguish themselves from the broader mood and atmosphere. Still, for this kind of music, that’s not entirely a bad thing. When I was riding around on an e-bike earlier, this was an amazing soundtrack to an overcast and chilly Sunday. Sad without being depressing, slow without being funereal, dreamy without being formless. “Words” is one of the best song and pulls you in right off the bat as an opener. The bass intro is stellar, and the bass continues to be a highlight through the rest of the album. The chorus is nice both vocally and melodically, and the atmosphere is instant. Great track. “Cut” is one I enjoyed as well, though not as much as some of the other standouts. The drums add a lot in my opinion, the guitar during the instrumental bits is uncertain and ominous, and the dark folk elements mesh well with the overall sound. “Lazy” is by far my favorite track. I love the brittle textures in the verses, the melodies in both the verses and the chorus (particularly the latter), the tension throughout, and the twang. “Lullaby” works well both in the airy vocal opening part, and the extended guitar instrumental. The build in the back half is executed perfectly. “Down” has little unexpected moments of intense power that you don’t get in the rest of the album, specifically the loud guitar parts and the vocals on “down, down, down”. I love the darkness of “Rope”, especially how they hint at the title without actually saying it. Still, there are some tracks that I didn’t love. People online really like “Slide” but it left me kind of unsatisfied; I just don’t think the core song itself is very strong. “Fear” is good but largely forgettable. “Sea” is the only song I actually dislike, not just because it comes after “Lullaby” but because I do not like the chorus. “Sunshine” does a good job at bringing out the darkness inherent in the original song, but something about it rubs me the wrong way and I’m having trouble putting my finger on it. Nevertheless, as I said previously, even when I don’t love some of these songs, they still do a great job at continuing and strengthening the mood (except “Sea”). I’m putting this in B Tier because while I think it’s very good, the overall style isn’t what I’m typically looking for in the way that the higher albums are. But I did really enjoy this listen, and I do intend to keep returning to it.

Favorite tracks: Words, Cut, Lazy, Lullaby, Down, Rope

Least favorite track: Sea
