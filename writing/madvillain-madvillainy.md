# Write-up

Madvillain was a hip hop supergroup duo consisting of rapper MF DOOM and producer Madlib formed in 2002. We have already listened to solo albums from both as part of this list: MF DOOM’s *MM..FOOD* and Quasimoto’s (Madlib’s alter ego, who also appears and is credited on *Madvillain*) *The Unseen*. I did a write up on both individually for those albums which can be found above. The NYC and Atlanta-based DOOM released his debut solo album *Operation: Doomsday* in 1999. Though it remained largely underground, the LA-based Madlib heard it and decided he wanted to work with DOOM. In 2001, Madlib’s label Stones Throw reached out to DOOM’s management in Atlanta proposing he come to LA to work with Madlib (though Stones Throw is now the largest and and most legendary alternative hip hop label, at the time it was small and broke). Though he had never heard of Madlib, DOOM agreed and flew out to LA; after hitting it off upon their first meeting, DOOM signed a contract with Stones Throw on a paper plate.

DOOM and Madlib composed most of *Madvillainy* in 2002. The two became inseparable, both at the studio and in their personal lives. Madlib took a trip to Brazil that same year, where he went crate digging and produced many of the beats on a sampler in his hotel room. Unfortunately, the demo of the album was stolen in Brazil and leaked online. This was one of the first major internet leaks, and it caused both DOOM and Madlib to give up on the project. DOOM returned to solo work, while Madlib made a collaboration album with J Dilla in 2003. They couldn’t stop thinking about the Madvillain project, however, and decided to finish and release it in 2004. While the beats stayed largely the same, DOOM re-recorded all of his verses, replacing his older hyperactive style with a cool, calm, laidback style. The album’s tracks are almost uniformly under 3 minutes long, giving short bursts of hip hop without hooks. The production uses superhero cartoon samples to create a loose narrative of a supervillain. DOOM’s verses focus on wordplay and style over meaning. The cover art depicts DOOM wearing his signature mask; though it was inspired by the cover of *In the Court of the Crimson King*, it has also been compared to *Madonna*.

*Madvillainy* was a smash commercial and critical success upon release. It sold over 150,000 copies, becoming the first big seller for Stones Throw Records. Beyond just the hip hop world, it was acclaimed by rock-oriented publications like *Pitchfork*, and even non-musical publications like the *New York Times*. In the years since, its stature has only grown, with “Accordion” and “All Caps” considered two of the greatest hip hop tracks of all time, and the album itself often ranking on all-time albums lists. After its success, DOOM and Madlib began working on a sequel called *Madvillainy 2*. While Madlib did his part, the notoriously reclusive and hard-to-reach DOOM kept slacking. Madlib released a remix of the original album called *Madvillainy 2: The Madlib Remix* in 2008 to try to inspire DOOM, but to no avail. Working with DOOM became even harder after he was denied entry into the US in 2010 and relocated to the UK. DOOM tragically died in 2020. Madlib claims to have 10 demos for *Madvillainy 2* that DOOM sent to him and he intends to release them at some point with the blessing of DOOM’s family.

# Hargrove's review

This is one of my very favorite albums of all time. I love it so much. Like many other albums, I first listened to it in the summer of 2017. While I enjoyed it quite a bit at the time, my M.O. then was to give albums a few listens, pick out a few tracks, and move on. The tracks I picked out were “Accordion”, “America’s Most Blunted”, “Rainbows”, and “Figaro”. I listened to these relatively frequently over the next 7 years, with a full album listen maybe once a year. Then, in 2024, I gave it one of those occasional full album listens and fell in love with it all over again. While I don’t completely remember, I think what happened was that I noticed how good “All Caps” was, listened to it a bunch of times in a row, and then went back to the full album. I couldn’t stop listening over and over and over again for months. It was the most I’ve ever gotten into an album in recent memory. It just works. It’s very close to a perfect album, which, when you think about it, doesn’t really make sense. It’s an album full of maximalist sample collages, free-form lyricism, weird druggy effects (like the Lord Quas voice), and short vignettes. With that description, you would assume that it’s one of those albums that’s kind of a mess but has really high highs so you push through the bullshit to get to the moments that work. Not so here. This is a shockingly consistent album, perhaps even moreso than *In Rainbows*. “The Illest Villains” sets the bar high and it stays there for nearly 50 minutes. There aren’t ups and downs, peaks and troughs, quality dips and returns. It’s sleek, polished, focused, concise, organized. These are not the adjectives you usually hear alongside this kind of music. And all of this only scratches the surface as to what makes this album so good. It’s just so much *fun*. DOOM and Madlib sound like they’re enjoying every moment. They inject just the right amount of comedy and levity into the exact right places. I could listen to it for hours, days, months, years, a lifetime. There are (rare) albums that I prefer, but this might be my number one desert island disc. I get something new out of it every time I listen. Like *Abbey Road*, my mood improves every time I listen. I can’t recommend it more. Highest honors.

“The Illest Villains” is a super fucking cool intro that sets the scene perfectly. The line “the villainous pair of really nice boys who just happened to be on the wrong side of the law” sticks with me most. “Accordion” begins with maybe the greatest introductory line in rap history “Living off borrowed time, the clock ticks faster”. I have actually thought this to myself in stressful situations and it really gets me going. Genuine chills. It’s impressive how DOOM can construct such whack, madcap atmospheres with more menace and edge than almost any other music. The rest of his singular verse here also brims with bangers like “Don’t touch the mic like it’s AIDS on it” and “Get more cheese than Doritos, Cheetos or Fritos/Slip like Freudian, your first and last step to playing yourself like accordion”. And none of this even mentions how masterfully Madlib chops up such a bizarro sample. It doesn’t get any better than that. “Meat Grinder” starts with a fun, catchy interlude, then moves into a crazy funky bass line and a twangy guitar sample. The way DOOM says “You can find the villain in satin, congas” sticks with me. “Bistro” is just a silly interlude, but the execution is unbelievable. Everything feels grand and luxurious, with smoothest of smooth soul samples and our host DOOM exuding pure sex. I adore DOOM’s delivery of “Shh, he about to start the speech, come on, SILENCE” on “Raid”. Madlin flips a BANGER jazzy piano sample that’s bouncy and driving. MED’s verse is pretty good but honestly DOOM is on such a roll on this album that any bars other than his feel like a missed opportunity. “America’s Most Blunted” was my first love on this album, and that sticks with me. It also helped introduce me to Steve Reich. It’s basically a sound collage in terms of the sheer number and spontaneity of the samples. The bass line is pure sex, “America’s most blunted” is fun to say, and, while Lord Quas has bugged me elsewhere, his weirdo voice is PERFECT for this track. “Drop X so you can have good sex/I smoke dank so I can grow me a shank*”* goes so hard. It has a super funny outro too.

“Sickfit” is an absolutely baller, super vibe-y instrumental. I love the sound ampled on the and of 3 every bar. “Rainbows” is weird as fuck but they pull it off really well. The way that DOOM sings, “That what he drink Drano” has been stuck in my head for years. I love the way Madlib integrates the comic book vibe into the very beat of the song, with the olden-sounding trumpets and the woman screaming. I also think this is genre-defining for the idea of “psychedelic hip hop”. “Curls” sports a relatively low-key beat, but I really like DOOM’s storytelling here. “Never lied to ma when we said we found the moolah/Five-hundred somethin' dollars layin' right there in the street/Huh, now let's try and get somethin' to eat” in particular appeals to me. “Do Not Fire!” is a decent instrumental. Nothing remarkable about it. From front to back, banger lines ooze out of “Money Folder”. “Let he who is without sin cast the first stone/After you, who's last? It's DOOM, he's the worst known”, “So he can bring it to you live in stere-ere-o”, and “Don't mind me, I wrote this rhyme lightly/Off of two or three Heines, and boy was they fine, G/One black, one Spanish, one Chinee’” come to mind for me. It also has one of the funkiest breakbeats. I have a soft spot for “Shadows of Tomorrow”. Sure, it’s a bit of a one trick pony, but the atmosphere just kills. I get so many of the verse lines stuck in my head, particularly the inflection Lord Quas has on “Today is the preview of tomorrow, but for me”. It just has a cool atmosphere. “Operation Lifesaver aka Mint Test” is short and kind of silly but also has a very powerful instrumental. I’ll take it. “Figaro” is one that I got into in my first listen or two. The basic boom bap beat gives it a slow but menacing swagger. I also adore the way DOOM says “Where my [REDACTED] go? Figaro, figaro”.

“Hardcore Hustle” is the only track that doesn’t really work for me. Not only is there no DOOM, but the laughing sounds get under my skin. In fairness, though, there are much worse worst tracks you could have. “Strange Ways” is great, particularly the way the meter shifts between the beginning and ends of the verses. It’s always kind of aura-losing whenever DOOM raps about breakups like he does on “Fancy Clown”, but Madlib chops up the sample so perfect that I just have to give it to him. “Eye” is a tad bit on the weaker side, but it’s an iconic sound to me and I can’t imagine the album without it. “Supervillain Theme” is the best instrumental by far, and that’s really saying something. How does Madlib create this much tension in the way he chops up the sample? It’s pure eargasm sex. “All Caps” is just GOATed. It’s one of the best tracks in any genre ever. It’s unbelievably, unbelievably, unbelievably good. From the ridiculous piano sample, to the comic book flute, to the perfect bars, has there ever been a track so amazing to listen to? Beyond that, it contains some of my favorite lines, like “Don’t talk about my moms, yo”, “Villain: the worst guy”, and, of course, “Just remember ALL CAPS when you spell the man name”. And every time I listen, no matter what, I have to do the \*pop\* along with him. “Great Day” has some of DOOM’s smoothest wordplay. “DOOM, are you pondering what I'm pondering?/Yes, but why would the darn thing be wandering?/She's like a foundling, barely worth fondling” is quite fun and whimsical to say. The fake out of “Spit so many verses sometimes my jaw twitches/One thing this party could use is more—ahem…/Booze” also rates as one of my favorite moments on the album. I’ve always found “Rhinestone Cowboy” an ever-so-slightly disappointing ending, but at the same time I don’t know how you could properly end this album. I appreciate the audience noises that make it sound like I’m in a club with the show ending. It’s not one of Madlib’s strongest beats but it’s got great rapping from DOOM.

This was a long review, but this album earns it. It’s such a beautiful lightning in a bottle one-of-a-kind joy of a record. RIP DOOM.

Favorite tracks: The Illest Villains, Accordion, Meat Grinder, Bistro, Raid, America’s Most Blunted, Sickfit, Rainbows, Curls, Money Folder, Shadows of Tomorrow, Operation Lifesaver aka Mint Test, Figaro, Strange Ways, Fancy Clown, Supervillain Theme, All Caps, Great Day, Rhinestone Cowboy

Least favorite track: Hardcore Hustle

# Aidan's review

I've grown a lot fonder of MM..FOOD since we reviewed it and this is probably an even better lyrical outing from MF DOOM. Pretty much every line is brilliant ("Slip like Freudian/Your first and last step to playing yourself like an accordian", "Borderline schizo, sort nice tits thought", "My n\*\*\*\*\*s take no like Kobe"), and the skits are as good as they get, from the opener to "Bistro" to the closing applause on "Rhinestone Cowboy". While the highs are incredible, there's a lot of short, forgettable tracks, which combined with the constant shifting between skits and rapping makes this album feel like walking through an old city, there are beautiful sections next to mundane ones on a winding path. Tracks like "Eye" and "All Caps" are amazing, but on an album that averages 2 minute a track, slower parts like the beginning of "Great Day" and all of "Rainbows" aren't as funny as either the skits or DOOM's rapping. I could see myself getting to like this more with time like MM..FOOD, but for now the chaos drags down the brilliance just a tad.
