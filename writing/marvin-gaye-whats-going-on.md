# Write-up

Marvin Gaye (originally Marvin Gay Jr.) was a soul singer born in 1939 in Washington, DC. He was raised in DC housing projects. His father, Marvin Gay Sr., was a strict, controlling man who would often viciously beat Marvin Jr. (foreshadowing). Gaye’s mother introduced him to gospel singing. In 1956, at age 17, Gaye dropped out of high school and enlisted in the Air Force, but was quickly discharged. He returned to DC in 1957 and formed a vocal quartet named the Marquees. While the Marquees found little success on their own, they found success in Chicago as session musicians, singing background vocals on hits by artists like Chuck Berry.

Gaye was recruited by fledgling label Motown in Detroit in 1960. He released his debut single as a solo artist in 1961, a jazz standard that emulated his idol Frank Sinatra. It was unsuccessful, so Motown put him to work as a session drummer. Gaye also developed his songwriting skills at this time. His third solo single, 1962’s “Stubborn Kind of Fellow”, became a hit. His 1962 hit “Hitch Hike” was used by the Velvet Underground as the basis for “There She Goes Again” on their debut album. He released a series of even bigger hit singles in 1963 and 1964, many written by Motown songwriters Holland-Dozier-Holland. As the 60s wore on, he became one of the most popular artists in the new soul genre, an evolution of R&B which features pop-oriented compositions, gospel vocals, and lush instrumental arrangements. Gaye’s best-known works from this period include 1964’s “How Sweet It Is (to Be Loved by You)” and 1967’s “Ain’t No Mountain High Enough”. Gaye’s first #1 single on the Billboard Hot 100 was 1968’s “I Heard It Through the Grapevine”.

Gaye released his eleventh studio album *What’s Going On* in 1971. The album takes its name from its lead single, a track written in response to police brutality. The risk-averse Motown refused to release it because of its heavy themes, but Gaye leveraged his stature and went on strike until they released it. This act of rebellion earned Gaye creative control over the album, an arrangement unheard of at Motown. Soul’s first concept album, *What’s Going On* tackles numerous social injustices across its nine songs. Gaye is credited as a writer on every song, though all but two have additional writers. When mixing the title track, two different vocal takes were accidentally layered together; Gaye liked this effect and used it for the rest of the album. The album was his most successful to date, selling over a million copies and producing three top 10 singles. It was also highly important in establishing the progressive soul genre, where artists like Stevie Wonder would push soul music forward with longer songs, concept albums, and deeper lyrics. The Strokes released a cover of “Mercy Mercy Me (The Ecology)” featuring Josh Homme of Queens of the Stone Age on drums in 2006.

Gaye continued to release hits, becoming the highest-paid black performer in music by the mid-70s. However, he began to face personal struggles, including drugs, divorce, and IRS scrutiny. The latter prompted him to move to London in 1980 as a tax exile. A feud with Motown led to Gaye leaving the label in 1981. He spent the rest of that year planning a comeback. This effort succeeded with the release of his most successful single, “Sexual Healing”, in 1982. Tragically, Gaye’s comeback was cut short at the peak of his fame. He relapsed on cocaine which spurred paranoid delusions, causing him to move in with his parents in 1983. Gaye and his father argued constantly. On April 1, 1984, Gaye broke up a fight between his parents where he attacked his father. In response, Marvin Gay Sr. shot Marvin Gaye Jr., killing him. He was 44 years old.

# Hargrove's review

I had listened to this record once before, back in 2021 when I saw it recommended on quite a few top albums of all-time lists. Nothing really stuck with me except “Inner City Blues (Make Me Wanna Holler)”, which I added to my liked songs playlist. I did not listen to the album again. I generally like the soul music I hear, but for whatever reason the critically acclaimed album stuff rolls off my back because I did the same thing around the same time with Stevie Wonder’s *Songs In the Key of Life*. And it happened again this week; the first two listens just did not stick with me. Thankfully, the nature of this project forced me to keep pushing, and rapidly the appeal started to smack me in the face. I really don’t know why it took so long. It’s not a particularly challenging or unorthodox record. Maybe it’s just too smooth. And damn, if it isn’t *smooth*. I am now really digging it, with four or five of the songs simultaneously stuck in my head. Obviously Gaye is a fantastic singer, but the gliding strings and the strut of the bass line make this album lush, warm, and oh so smooth. The strings in particular remind me a lot of Sinatra’s *In the Wee Small Hours*, the way they climb and flit around like a movie score. However, I think the effect works better with this kind of instrumentation, adding grandeur to the music instead of standing alone opposite powerhouse vocals. I really enjoy the album’s continuity, particularly the suite-like structure on side 1. It’s all just quite good. I don’t have any specific complaints other than that the lyrics are a bit simplistic. That this album doesn’t resonate with me beyond being a collection of some sleek pop songs certainly lowers its ceiling. But they are some damn fine pop songs.

I think part of what held me back with this album was the title track, “What’s Going On”. Clearly it’s held in high regard by many, but I just could not get into it. It wasn’t until my 7th or 8th listen that it finally clicked, and it clicked quite good. That was where I was truly sold. That chorus sounds so damn smooth with the finger snaps and the gliding strings and the chord change after the vocals. I love the scat singing too. Banger track. “What’s Happening Brother” begins the side 1 suite with the interesting lyrical concept of a veteran coming home and not recognizing his country. This song’s instrumental is one of the more cinematic, while Gaye sings in a quieter, more resigned voice that I really like. “Flyin’ High (In the Friendly Sky)” has a fun bass line, but the song itself is just a little too lowkey. It’s my least favorite on the album. I wasn’t crazy about “Save the Children” at first because of the weird spoken then sung vocal dynamic, but I’ve really come around on it. It’s got an almost psychedelic hypnotism to it thanks to the swirling triplet bass line and the swinging cymbal. I also truly appreciate the plea for caring in the lyrics;“Who really cares?/Who's willing to try?/To save the world/That's destined to die” gets me, man.  “God Is Love” works as an interlude in the suite essentially. It doesn’t stand out much, but it works. “Mercy Mercy Me (The Ecology)” is another banger single. I cannot get the verse melody out of my head. “Right On” starts side 2 with a crazy complex structure. It’s easily the most progressive track on the album. It’s basically Latin funk; I feel like I’m listening to War with that guiro. I also love the super jazzy flute and the banging piano, as well as the sentiment of “Some of us are aware that it's good for us to care”. It’s an awesome track, and the least pop on the album. “Wholy Holy” is another interlude-style track. It’s one of the album’s weakest, but it’s nice and I don’t mind it. The album closes on “Inner City Blues (Make Me Wanna Holler)”, my favorite song. Holy banger. It just sounds great.

I’ve heaped a lot of praise, but I do think it’s notable that I don’t really feel much different now that I’ve “gotten” it. It’s great at what it does, but it doesn’t feel like a revelation. It does, however, feel pretty damn good.

Favorite tracks: What’s Going On, What’s Happening Brother, Save the Children, Mercy Mercy Me (The Ecology), Right On, Inner City Blues (Make Me Wanna Holler)

Least favorite track: Flyin’ High (In the Friendly Sky)
