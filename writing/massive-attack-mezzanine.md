# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Massive Attack is a group from Bristol, England that was instrumental in developing the trip hop genre. It’s original lineup consisted of rapper and producer 3D (Robert Del Naja), rapper and producer Tricky (Adrian Thaws), DJ and producer Daddy G (Grant Marshall), and DJ and producer Mushroom (Andrew Vowles). The four met in the early 80s when they were all members of the Bristol party and musical collective The Wild Bunch. The group began in earnest in 1988 and released their debut studio album, *Blue Lines*, in 1991 to immediate critical and commercial success. In retrospect, *Blue Lines* is often considered the first true trip hop album, although the term would not be coined until 1994. Massive Attack released their second album, *Protection*, in 1994, further developing their sensual and ambient style of hip hop. Also in 1994, fellow Bristol band Portishead released their debut album *Dummy*, solidifying trip hop as both its own genre and as a critical darling. Tricky left Massive Attack in 1995, and the group had a few rocky and uncertain years. With Tricky gone, 3D became the undisputed leader of the group, and he was the main producer of their third album, 1998’s *Mezzanine*. It was a smash hit, selling over 4 million copies and the single “Teardrop” was a top 10 hit in the UK. However, Mushroom became very dissatisfied with the group, and he left in 1999. In the 25 years since, Massive Attack have remained active as a duo of 3D and Daddy G, but they’ve only released two more studio albums. They are friendly with many other British musicians, in particular Thom Yorke of Radiohead, Damon Albarn of Blur and Gorillaz, and Brian Eno. They are also very politically active, almost always on the far left end of the spectrum.

*Mezzanine* features significant contributions from guest vocalists. In particular, Elisabeth Fraser of Cocteau Twins sings lead vocals on 3 tracks. *Mezzanine* has a darker vibe than the first two Massive Attack records, and it also incorporates significant influence from post-punk and industrial music. These new influences were a result of the singular artistic vision of 3D, but they pissed off Mushroom to the point that he didn’t want to be in the band anymore. If you agree with Mushroom and like the overall sound but wish it was lighter, definitely check out their first two records. *Mezzanine* also features quite a few samples, notably a sample of The Velvet Underground’s “I Found a Reason” on “Risingson” and a sample of The Cure’s “10:15 Saturday Night” on “Man Next Door”.

Trip hop initially developed as the musical component of the Bristol underground scene. The scene initially came together in the early 1980s as a response to the growing influence of hip hop but the lack of clubs in Bristol dedicated to hip hop culture. A major component of this Bristol art scene was graffiti, and extremely famous graffiti artist Banksy came up as a part of the scene. There have been rumors for years that 3D of Massive Attack himself is secretly Banksy, but this is unproven. Musically, the Bristol scene developed a quiet, downtempo, ambient, and moody form of hip hop that stood in stark contrast to the developments in hip hop in New York. Post-punk was a major influence on trip hop, in particular gothic rock bands like Siouxsie and the Banshees and The Cure. Massive Attack and Portishead proved to be the biggest artists to come out of the original trip hop scene. Trip hop proved very influential outside Bristol, with Sacramento producer DJ Shadow pioneering his own brand of instrumental hip hop based off of the style. Icelandic pop singer Björk incorporated trip hop production into her highly acclaimed first three albums, and Britpop artist Damon Albarn created his own trip hop outfit Gorillaz that became very successful in the 2000s. More recently, Lana Del Rey combined trip hop with her more conventional pop styles to pioneer a sound very distinct from her contemporaries on the 2010s.

# Hargrove's review

Drunk and tired, I’ll keep this one short. I like this album. I like Portishead a little better. I like trip hop a lot. It was a great innovation in hip hop, and it sounds damn good. I think this album does some cool things with the bass. I think the atmosphere is also great. This album sounds like sex. I would love to experience that someday. Elisabeth Fraser is divine. This album does great things with atmosphere, but it never blows me away. For that reason, it is high B tier.
