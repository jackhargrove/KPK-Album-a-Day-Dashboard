# Write-up

Megadeth is a thrash metal band formed in 1983 in Los Angeles whose most well-known lineup consisted of guitarist and vocalist Dave Mustaine, bassist David Ellefson, guitarist Marty Friedman, and drummer Nick Menza. Although Megadeth has undergone numerous lineup changes in their 40+ year history, with Mustaine as the only constant member, this lineup was their longest lasting, and the one in place during the recording of *Rust in Peace*. Dave Mustaine was the original lead guitarist of seminal thrash metal band Metallica, joining in 1981. Mustaine had a serious drug and alcohol problem, as well as a serious personality problem (he was and is very unpleasant to be around), which strained his relationship with his Metallica bandmates. In 1983, Metallica went to NYC to record their first album. While there, they finally became fed up with Mustaine, so they kicked him out of the band and put him on a bus back to Los Angeles. When he returned to LA, he vowed to form a new band that was faster and louder than Metallica. This band became Megadeth, which had extremely unstable membership throughout the mid-1980s. Megadeth released their independent debut, *Killing Is My Business…and Business Is Good!*, in 1985 to enough success to sign to a major label. Their second album, 1986’s *Peace Sells…but Who’s Buying?* was very successful in the underground metal scene. Their third album, 1988’s *So Far, So Good…So What?*, was their first to go platinum. In 1990, Megadeth released their fourth album, *Rust in Peace*, which is still today considered a masterpiece of thrash metal. In the 1990s, Megadeth changed up their sound to be more melodic and commercial. They have continued to release records through 2022 (with another planned for this year), and have sold over 50,000,000 records worldwide. They are considered one of the Big 4 of thrash metal, alongside Metallica, Slayer, and Anthrax.

*Rust in Peace* is Megadeth’s best-remembered album, and perhaps the last great release of the thrash metal era (thrash permanently lost popularity when grunge broke in 1991). The album’s title comes from a bumper sticker that Mustaine had seen while driving which said “May all your nuclear weapons rust in peace”. Multiple songs play on the idea of nuclear war. The lyrics of the opener, “Holy War…The Punishment Due”, come from a misunderstanding during concert in Northern Ireland where Mustaine unknowingly supported the IRA on stage and incited a riot. The music and solos on the album are very technically proficient, leading some to consider it a work of technical thrash metal. The cover feature’s the band’s mascot, Vic Rattlehead, in front of a group of late 80s world leaders.

Thrash metal was one of the first important genres of underground metal in the 1980s. Black Sabbath created heavy metal in the 1970s, and the genre had mostly run its course by the mid 70s. In the late 70s, a group of bands known as the New Wave of British Heavy Metal (NWOBHM) came out of nowhere to keep the genre alive. In the mainstream, metal would eventually become identified with shitty hair metal acts like Def Leppard and Bon Jovi. In the underground, however, the NWOBHM sound was combined with hardcore punk to create thrash metal, a harsh, driving form of metal. Thrash would form the basis for most underground metal to come. Metallica is by far the most important thrash group, Megadeth was the Wario to Metallica’s Mario, Slayer was the harshest and most forward-thinking, and Anthrax was the most punk.

# Hargrove's review

I agree with Aidan on a lot of the substantive points, but my overall impression of this record is lower because I don’t care much for the vocals and because I find the lyrics even worse than he does. While this does not sound like the “Platonic ideal” of a thrash metal record, it does sound exactly like what you might expect from the genre. The guitars are quite good, and a highlight throughout. They cut through every other aspect as sharp as a knife, with the jagged edges of riffs drawing blood as they pass. I particularly enjoy “Hangar 18”, which is more than 50% guitar solos. I’m not typically impressed by technicality, but the complexity of what was being played is not lost on me, and I must admit it sounds good. The drums are consistently good; driving (with that double kick), but never ever overpowering. The opener “Holy Wars…The Punishment Due” is worthy of its acclaim and face-melting in its execution. It wasn’t my favorite song on the album, but certainly one I’m consistently impressed by on each listen. My favorite song would have to be “Lucretia, which begins with one of the best guitar lines on the album. I also enjoy the vocals here, which I haven’t on much of the rest of the record. And the more personal lyrics are more interesting to me than the fantasy elsewhere. I read a lot about how “Tornado of Souls” had the greatest guitar solo in metal history, but I wasn’t all thaaaaaaaat impressed. It’s a cool solo, and a cool song, but it doesn’t knock my socks off. My biggest issues with this album are the vocals, the lyrics, and the production. I can’t really articulate why I don’t like the vocals, but I just don’t. They’re not particularly harsh, but they just sound annoying. The lyrics, on the other hand, I can articulate why I don’t like. Aidan made much ado about how dumb the lyrics to *Slow, Deep and Hard* were, but I don’t mind that at all. That album realized it was stupid as fuck, and it played that role perfectly. On the other hand, the vague platitudinal political gesturing here bugs me, and the more fantastical settings are jumbled. As for the production, I just think it’s too clean. I have the same issue with Metallica, so it’s just a fact of thrash metal, but I think this style of music could do well to be a little more lo-fi. Still, I enjoyed this record. I don’t want to give the impression that I didn’t. This was a positive listen, and there were multiple songs I enjoyed.

Favorite tracks: Holy Wars…The Punishment Due, Hangar 18, Lucretia, Rust in Peace…Polaris

Least favorite track: Poison Was the Cure
