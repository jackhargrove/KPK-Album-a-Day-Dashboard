# Write-up

Metallica is a metal band formed in 1981 and based in San Francisco which, at the time of this album, consisted of vocalist and rhythm guitarist James Hetfield, lead guitarist Kirk Hammett, bassist Cliff Burton, and drummer Lars Ulrich. The most successful metal group of all time, Metallica pioneered thrash metal, the original style of extreme metal, in the 1980s before hitting the mainstream in the early 90s with a more accessible heavy metal sound. In 1980, Danish third-generation tennis prodigy Lars Ulrich moved to Los Angeles at age 16 to train professionally in the sport. After just missing the cut for an elite tennis team, he decided to pivot to playing drums instead. In 1981, Ulrich placed an ad in a newspaper seeking out other musicians who were fans of Iron Maiden. Guitarist James Hetfield responded to the ad; the two became friends and formed Metallica five months later, the name taken from one of Ulrich’s friends who considered using it as the name of a metal fanzine. Another ad recruited guitarist Dave Mustaine as the band’s third member. Bassist Ron McGovney rounded out this original lineup, and Metallica played its first gig in Anaheim in March 1982. Metallica recorded a track for the first edition of *Metal Massacre* in 1982, an influential compilation series that helped popularize LA’s extreme underground metal scene.

Metallica kicked out McGovney in late 1982 and replaced him with Cliff Burton. He only agreed to join the band if it moved to the San Francisco Bay Area, which it did in 1983. That same year, Metallica signed to indie label Megaforce Records and went to Rochester, New York, to record its debut album. Shortly before recording, Mustaine was kicked out of the band for being a violent junkie douche and was replaced by guitarist Kirk Hammett the same day. Hammett flew out from SF to Rochester for recording, while Mustaine was left to his own devices to take a bus back to California; not long after, Mustaine founded Megadeth, which would itself become one of the most famous metal bands ever. Mustaine has forever held a grudge against Metallica and Hammett in particular. Metallica’s debut, *Kill ‘Em All*, released in 1983. It made the band famous within the underground metal scene and eventually charted in 1986. Metallica released its second album, *Ride the Lightning*, in 1984. It became the band’s first record to chart, hitting number 100 on the Billboard 200, which earned Metallica a contract with major label Elektra. Many fans also cite it as the group’s best album to this day.

In 1986, Metallica released its third album and major label debut, *Master of Puppets*. The album was recorded in Denmark over a period of four months. It features longer songs with dynamic structures and perfect technical execution, a tendency the group began developing on *Ride the Lightning* but which did not come to fruition until *Master of Puppets*. Multiple tracks include references to David Bowie, including a riff from his track “Andy Warhol” in “Master of Puppets” and taking the title of “Leper Messiah” from a lyric in “Ziggy Stardust”. The instrumental “Orion” was used by DJ Shadow as a sample in *Endtroducing.....* on the track “The Number Song”. *Master of Puppets* was a major success, peaking at number 29 on the Billboard 200 and staying on the chart for 72 weeks despite releasing no music videos nor any single until four months after release. It was Metallica’s first record to receive a certification, going Gold by the end of the year; as of 2026, it has been certified 8x Platinum.

Given the commercial dominance of cleanly produced, pop-oriented hair metal groups, this was a staggering success for an unapologetically aggressive and progressive metal group like Metallica. The album received positive reviews upon release and is now a perennial contender in discussions of the best metal albums of all time. Fans of Metallica are often torn between this album and *Ride the Lightning* as the band’s finest. *Master of Puppets* launched thrash metal as a genre into the public consciousness, paving the way for the breakthroughs of Slayer, Megadeth, and Anthrax (collectively referred to as the “Big Four” of thrash). The album’s virtuosic technicality, relentless speed, hardcore punk aggression, power dynamic-focused lyricism, and progressive song structures set the stage for virtually all extreme metal to follow.

Metallica capitalized on the success of *Master of Puppets* with nonstop touring. The band opened for Ozzy Osbourne on his US tour, helping introduce the sound of thrash metal to fans of 70s heavy metal. Metallica also became known in the press for the antics and drinking habits of its members, resulting in the nickname “Alcoholica”. In Fall 1986, Metallica went on tour in Europe. Sadly, tragedy struck: the tour bus crashed, killing bassist Cliff Burton. The band was devastated and considered disbanding but ultimately decided to continue. With the blessing of Burton’s family, Jason Newsted joined Metallica as bassist. Metallica released its fourth album, *…And Justice for All*, in 1988. It peaked at number six on the Billboard 200 and was certified Platinum within three months of release. It rounds out the thrash metal period of Metallica’s early career as the band’s most complex and progressive release. “One” is considered by many to be the group’s greatest track. Having reached just about the maximum level of commercial success any band who made extreme music could hope to reach, the band decided to pivot toward a simpler, cleaner sound with pop-oriented songwriting. The result was 1991’s *Metallica*, commonly referred to as *The Black Album*, a record that left thrash behind. Bolstered by the top 40 hit “Enter Sandman”, *The Black Album* became unimaginably popular; as of 2026, it has sold 20,000,000 copies in the US alone, making it the bestselling album of the Nielsen SoundScan era (new tracking standard introduced in 1991). It has spent more than 800 weeks on the Billboard 200, second only to *The Dark Side of the Moon*.

In the 35 years since, Metallica has done just about everything it possibly could to alienate its original underground metal audience. The band has released seven albums since *The Black Album*, with the most recent coming in 2023; all were panned by fans. Most continued further into the pop metal direction with the notable exception of 2011’s *Lulu*, an indescribably bizarre and experimental collaboration with Lou Reed. In 2000, Ulrich became the face of the music industry’s crusade against file sharing site Napster, making him deeply unpopular with a generation of young music listeners. Newsted left the band on bad terms in 2001; Robert Trujillo replaced him on bass permanently in 2003. The members of Metallica have all experienced major degradation of their technical skills on their respective instruments as time goes on, most famously Ulrich’s inability to play the high-speed double kick drum parts that he himself had pioneered. Despite all of that, Metallica’s work in the 80s is so beloved that they have endured as *the* metal band, the first group anyone thinks of in the genre. Metallica also remains a Bay Area institution, performing during the 2015 NBA Finals for the Warriors, before Super Bowl 50 was held in the 49ers’ stadium, and at the opening of San Francisco’s Chase Center NBA stadium in 2019. As of 2026, Metallica still tours and is potentially working on a twelfth studio album.

# Hargrove's review

Though I had never actually listened to *Master of Puppets*, this was one of those albums where I recognized half of the songs on my first listen. “Battery” was on the main setlist of *Rock Band 2* and I remember playing it many times as a child (as well as my dad explaining to me the other definition of “battery” which means the act of hitting someone, which is especially funny to me now that I’ve read the lyrics and am pretty sure that it’s being used metaphorically in the sense of like a battery of cannons). “Welcome Home (Sanitarium)” was referenced by my dad numerous times when we visited the Exploratorium in San Francisco. And, of course, I knew “Master of Puppets”, the most recognizable song from this era of metal. We’ve heard a lot of metal music at this point, including some albums that I like more than *Master of Puppets*. But, damn, no other metal album really comes close to the level of songwriting found on this record. From the complex and shifting structures, to the sheer power of the riffs, to the elegance and tasteful placement of the guitar solos, to the catchy scream-along vocal melodies, to the suffocating claustrophobia of the lyrics, tracks like “Battery”, “Master of Puppets”, “Welcome Home (Sanitarium)”, “Disposable Heroes”, and “Leper Messiah” are compositionally elite. With these songwriting skills, it’s not difficult to see how Hetfield and Ulrich would eventually give into the temptation to churn out chart-toppers. On this album, however, those powers are used for good to create the perfect vessels for an album this blisteringly fast, relentlessly aggressive, and unapologetically progressive. There was no world in which *Master of Puppets* could possibly have competed for the title of the most brutal or punishing metal record on the list; we’ve had more than 40 years of nonstop innovation in the field of metallic unpleasantness. Instead, this record still stands out even after my ears have been worn into little nubs thanks to the brilliance of its execution. In addition to the songs I listed previously, I also quite like “Orion”, a psychedelic instrumental journey that helps keep the sound from getting stale. Despite all the praise I’ve heaped so far, this record is held back by “The Thing That Should Not Be”, a dreary anchor on the momentum of the album’s first half, and a mildly disappointing closer in “Damage, Inc.”. Without those two tracks, this album would be damn near perfect. Even so, I had a ton of fun listening to Metallica this weekend, and have six tracks to take with me for my liked songs playlist.

I love the acoustic guitars that open “Battery”. They have a bit of a Spanish flavor that reminds me of the acoustic and classical guitars used on Agalloch’s *The Mantle*. This intro gives you a taste of the moments of beauty to come buried within the power, which itself starts hard when the electric guitars kick in. The sheer forward momentum of this track just blows me away every time I listen to it. Though I’m not usually a big guitar solo guy, I generally like the solos on this album, and this track has a great one. “Master of Puppets” is a masterpiece that would be worth the price of admission on its own even if the rest of the album was terrible. I love the abruptness of the missing fourth beat every fourth bar, as well as the visceral and evocative lyrics. I get so giddy every time the music drops out as Hetfield screams “MASTER, MASTER”. And how beautiful is that first solo? It’s so delicate and frail compared to the sheer force of the track around it. But my favorite part is the song structure, the way it  goes from taunting to lamentful to angry. It’s basically a perfect track. Sadly, I don’t love “The Thing That Should Not Be”. The individual mini0builds don’t really work for me, and Metallica just doesn’t work as well at such a slow tempo unless it’s a power ballad. Speaking of, “Welcome Home (Sanitarium)” improves on the previous track in every way. I love how the frail complexity of the riff disorients me, the perfect tension build in both the vocals and the lyrics, and just fun it is to sing along to the cathartic chorus. Add to that the gorgeously mournful guitar solo and the simmering heft of the bridge, and you have a certified banger on your hands.

“Disposable Heroes” returns to terminal velocity after two slow tracks. Every drum kick sounds like a gun going off. The guitar riffs turn on a dime, creating distinct sections that are all great. And there’s yet another memorable chorus. I wanted to like “Leper Messiah” because of the Bowie reference and, thankfully, I did. It’s the most brutal track by far, just constantly smashing you over the head with the riffs. Those descending riffs in particular sound like the end of the world. I’m glad the energy remains so high this deep into the album. “Orion” is a unique concept for this style of record and they pulled it off well. Most of the sections are interesting, but my favorite has to be the one that starts around 4:15. I’m glad the album ends on a fast song, but “Damage, Inc.” unfortunately just sounds kind of bland. The vocals bug me, particularly the way Hetfield lingers on some words. The chorus is much sloppier than any other track too. It’s not the worst ending this album could conceivably have had, but it leaves me wanting more.

I was able to dedicate much of this weekend to listening to thrash metal, and I have some opinions. First, I was able to give two listens each to *Kill ‘Em All*, *Ride the Lightning*, and *…And Justice for All*, Metallica’s first, second, and fourth albums. *Kill ‘Em All* had a couple of decent tracks but it felt half-baked overall. *Ride the Lightning* is a stunning leap forward, honestly about on the same level as *Master of Puppets*. *…And Justice for All* has very high highs, particularly in the title track, but the tracks get too long and winding for their own good. In my opinion, *Master of Puppets* > *Ride the Lightning* > *…And Justice for All* > *Kill ‘Em All*. Additionally, I re-listened twice each to Slayer’s *Show No Mercy* and Megadeth’s *Rust in Peace*. Additionally, I gave two re-listens to Slayer’s masterpiece *Reign in Blood* and threw in two listens to Anthrax’s *Among the Living* to round out the Big 4. I did not like Anthrax at all. It was just middling hardcore punk with more distorted guitars. I enjoyed *Rust in Peace* more than I remembered; there are quite a few good tracks, though I’m still not crazy about Mustaine’s vocals, and it’s a touch too slick for my taste. And while I love the idea of the ultra-fast, ultra-raw Slayer being my favorite of the Big 4, I think I have to give the nod to *Master of Puppets* over *Reign in Blood*. Overall, *Master of Puppets* > *Reign in Blood* > *Rust in Peace* > *Show No Mercy* >>>>> *Among the Living*. And for band rankings, my heart says Slayer > Metallica > Megadeth > Anthrax, but my brain says Metallica > Slayer > Megadeth > Anthrax. Finally, for my tier list I debated between putting this album in high A or low S tier. Ultimately, the albums I felt most similar about were PiL’s *Metal Box* and Dinosaur Jr.’s *You’re Living All Over Me*, both albums full of music I had been around my whole life but had never listened to, and both with track listing containing or dud or two but with a high enough quality outside of that to make up for it. These records sit at the bottom of S Tier, so I’m putting *Master of Puppets* there too.

Favorite tracks: Battery, Master of Puppets, Welcome Home (Sanitarium), Disposable Heroes, Leper Messiah, Orion

Least favorite track: The Thing That Should Not Be
