# Write-up

Dumile Daniel Thompson was a rapper and producer who went by many names, including Daniel Dumile, Viktor Vaughn, King Geedorah, and, of course, MF DOOM. He was perhaps the most important figure in underground or alternative hip hop of all-time; Q-Tip, founder of legendary early 90s alternative hip hop group A Tribe Called Quest, once called him “your favorite rapper’s favorite rapper”. Dumile was born in 1971 to a Trinidadian mother and Zimbabwean father who lived in the US but were on vacation in the UK. As a result, Dumile was not a US citizen, a fact that will become important later on. Nevertheless, he grew up in Long Island and was for all intents and purposes a native New Yorker. He was raised a member of the Five-Percent Nation, an offshoot of the Nation of Islam affiliated with many New York rappers. Dumile, under the name Zev Love X, formed a rap group called KMD with his brother DJ Subroc and they released their debut in 1991. They recorded a follow-up in 1993, but Subroc was tragically hit by a car and killed at age 19 and the album was shelved. DOOM became depressed and stopped making music, spending his time from 1994-1997 wandering the streets of Manhattan. In 1997, he came back to take his revenge on the music industry. He began performing at open mic nights with a face covering resembling the mask worn by Doctor Doom in the Fantastic Four comics. He would turn this persona into the famous supervillain of hip hop: MF DOOM.

DOOM’s solo debut, *Operation: Doomsday*, released in 1999. It introduced some qualities that would become trademarks: heavy sampling from cartoons, a characteristic relaxed and wordy flow, and the characters of MF DOOM and King Geedorah. While MF DOOM would become his primary persona, King Geedorah would be an alter ego based off of a Godzilla monster. The album received good reviews and is now a classic. He followed it up in 2003 with *Take Me to Your Leader*, a full album in the King Geedorah persona. Also in 2003, he released the *Vaudeville Villain* album under the new persona Viktor Vaughn (Doctor Doom’s “real” name was Victor von Doom), a record some argue is his finest. In 2004, DOOM released his first collaborative album, a record with producer Madlib titled *Madvillainy* and released under the name Madvillain. This record is often cited as the greatest hip hop record of all time and we will be listening to it on this list. Very soon after, he released the weaker *VV:2* under the name Viktor Vaughn. Near the end of 2004, he released his second album as MF DOOM, *MM..FOOD*. It’s a concept record that features numerous food-based samples, puns, and metaphors. It’s commonly cited as his best solo work, particularly the tracks “One Beer” and “Rapp Snitch Knishes”.

After having one of the most productive year-and-a-half runs in hip hop history, DOOM  released the album *The Mouse and the Mask* with producer Danger Mouse in 2005. It would be the peak of his mainstream popularity. Unfortunately, everything was downhill from here. He would release one final solo record in 2009 to mixed reviews. In 2010, he left the US to tour internationally, and was denied entry when he tried to return. He would spend the rest of his life in the UK. He also collaborated with Radiohead’s Thom Yorke in 2010, who was a huge fan. For the rest of the 2010s, he released collaborative albums to middling reviews. He died on October 31, 2020 at age 49. His wife kept it a secret until December 31, 2020, and didn’t reveal the cause of death until 2023 (negative reaction to a blood pressure medication). Though the end of his career was uneventful, his peak was higher than perhaps any other hip hop artist. He will always be remembered as rap’s faceless supervillain.

# Hargrove's review

I’m a huge fan of *Madvillainy* (it was my top album of 2024, but I’ve been listening to it consistently since high school) but I’ve never much explored MF DOOM’s other work. I had heard “One Beer” from this album through internet osmosis, but that’s about it. There are just so many highly regarded albums and I didn’t really know where to begin. Thankfully, this list is great for forcing me to listen to things I’ve long meant to. Unsurprisingly, this album is very good. It doesn’t nearly touch *Madvillainy* for me, which is nearly unmatched in its cohesion and flow as a rap album. But there are some absolute bangers here. It’s also nice to hear DOOM explore an idea for the length of a full song, as most tracks on *Madvillainy* are under two minutes long. I really respect it when someone commits to a bit, and DOOM is on the food pun thing hard here. It really doesn’t get old for me. I appreciate how sparing the features are, and when they do happen they’re usually punchy and well-placed. While I enjoy the TV show samples, it does go a little overboard with them. They’re generally relevant and curated, but it’s just too long. I think this album would be sleeker and tighter without the sound collage in the middle. But really, I’m not that against it. It gives a ton of personality and elevates the album far beyond just a collection of songs. DOOM just went a little overboard is all. When you have tracks as strong as “One Beer”, “Deep Fried Frenz”, “Kon Karne”, and “Rapp Snitch Knishes”, though, I’m willing to forgive minor transgressions. Overall, this is a fun, focused, and banger-ful document of a giant in his prime.

“Beef Rapp” starts with like six different TV show samples in a row, giving you  aheads up for what you’re getting into right off the bat. The actual song itself is very good, classic DOOM. It’s one of the few that uses a cartoon music sample as the primary part of the beat and DOOM’s verses have zero fat to trim. The “we need food sample” is also brilliantly used. Like many other songs on this album, it doesn’t have a hook, which I actually kind of like. It creates the feeling of forward progression, all gas no breaks. It doesn’t surprise me that “Hoe Cakes” is one of DOOM’s most popular tracks, but I find the beatboxing and the “super” sample to be kind of obnoxious. I don’t hate it, and I see myself listening to it and putting it on playlists, but unfortunately the grating elements hold back what is otherwise a good track and make it my least favorite on side 1. “Potholderz” is so smooth, relaxed, and laid-back. Count Bass D gives some banger guest bard. Good track. “One Beer” is definitely the best song on the album. I do not understand how this didn’t make it onto *Madvillainy*, it’s straight up better than many of the songs there. It’s a brilliant use of a vocal sample, something that’s difficult to do, and the drums are also stellar. “Take it from a dude who wears a mask like a ‘tarded helmet” gets me every time. “Deep Fried Frenz” is another standout track. I love the Whodini electro sample. DOOM paints a grim picture of isolation so effectively with his lyrics. “I checked the dictionary for the meaning of friend/It said, ‘Person one like to socialize with/Sympathizer, helper’ and that’s about the size of it” is a particularly effective bar for me. I also think the skit at the end is an example of one that’s very well-placed since it tells the tragic origin story of DOOM that makes him so lonely.

The four track sound collage in the middle has its moments, but unfortunately I think the album would be better off without it. “Poo-Putt Platter” and “Fillet-O-Rapper” are both completely forgettable. “Gumbo” is actually pretty good though. The “edible wrappers” stuff is pretty funny and I love the “eat more chitlins”. “Fig Leaf Bi-Carbonate” is also pretty good. I love the crescendo in the superhero music and also the *Sesame Street* Grover sample. It actually feels like a real, full track. But still, could’ve done without all of this.

“Kon Karne” starts the second section of real songs on a high note. It has such a sad, urban, nocturnal feel to it, particularly in the delicate piano. DOOM’s lyrics are a heartful tribute to his dead brother. I really like the Princess sample at the beginning of “Guinnessez” because her voice sounds like such an East Coast stereotype to me. The rest of the track though is definitely my least favorite. It’s not bad, but it slows things down far too much and goes on and on for like five minutes. It just lacks energy. “Kon Queso” doesn’t have much that stands out in particular, but it’s just a good rap song. I definitely see myself listening to it casually. “Rapp Snitch Knishes” is obviously a fantastic song. That guitar sample is like a bolt of electricity wriggling through the entire song. Mr. Fantastik also lays down some BARS, and provides a really strong hook to an album that’s otherwise largely devoid of them. I do think “One Beer” is better and don’t quite understand why this seems to be the consensus “best” DOOM solo song, but it is very good. “Vomitspit”, much like “Kon Queso”, is just a good track without much more elaboration necessary. “Kookies” is a hilarious song filled with incredible masturbation and porn metaphors. My personal favorite is “I’m kill’t over spilt milk”.

This is a very good album. It’s full of tracks that I’ve added to my liked song playlist. It’s not *Madvillainy*, but not many albums can be.

Favorite tracks: Beef Rapp, Potholderz, One Beer, Deep Fried Frenz, Kon Karne, Kon Queso, Rapp Snitch Knishes, Vomitspit, Kookies

Least favorite track: Guinnessez
