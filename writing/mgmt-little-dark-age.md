# Write-up

MGMT is a duo formed in Middletown, Connecticut in 2002 consisting of lead vocalist and multi-instrumentalist Andrew VanWyngarden and multi-instrumentalist Benjamin Goldwasser. VanWyngarden and Goldwasser met as freshmen at Wesleyan University, originally using the name The Management. They shortened the name to MGMT in 2005, the same year they released the EP *Time to Pretend* and toured for the first time as openers for Of Montreal. MGMT signed to major label Columbia Records in 2006, who set them up with a producer who had previously worked with the Flaming Lips and Sleater-Kinney. This resulted in *Oracular Spectacular* in 2007, the band’s debut album that contained the indie synthpop mega-hits “Time to Pretend”, “Kids”, and “Electric Feel”. The success of these singles made MGMT popular for the remainder of the 00s decade, allowing them to tour with Radiohead and even open for Paul McCartney. However, MGMT’s second album, 2010’s *Congratulations*, signaled a major shift away from the group’s signature synthpop sound toward psychedelic rock, which was not terribly chic at the time. The album sold well but received mixed reviews as the band’s popularity slowly dwindled. Undeterred, MGMT continued in this sonic direction with *MGMT* in 2013, this time selling a little worse and receiving worse reviews. The duo went on hiatus in 2014 with plans to return once it had found a new sound. VanWyngarden moved to Queens, while Goldwasser moved to Los Angeles.

After four years away from music, MGMT released the single “Little Dark Age” in late 2017. VanWyngarden and Goldwasser had continued collaborating long-distance in the time since *MGMT* but faced writer’s block. They brought in Patrick Wimberly, formerly a member of Chairlift with Caroline Polachek, to help guide songwriting. Wimberly encouraged MGMT to bring in other musicians for jam sessions. The most fruitful of these was with Ariel Pink, whose hypnagogic pop sound greatly influenced the album; he received a writing credit on the song “When You Die” and played synth on the opener “She Works Out Too Much”. After finally writing enough songs, MGMT entered the studio with the same producer from *Oracular Spectacular* in November 2016, the month Donald Trump was elected President of the US. The duo’s shock and dismay at this political development heavily influenced the mood of the album. Sonically, the album largely returns to MGMT’s synthpop roots, but with a healthy dose of psychedelia left over from the previous two albums. VanWyngarden described it as “Apparently, we were more inspired to write pop music after evil took over the world”. *Little Dark Age* released in early 2018 and was considered a return to form by many. The album peaked at #35 on the Billboard 200, still decent but the lowest peak of any album by the band to that point.

In 2020, two years after this list was created, the title track “Little Dark Age” experienced a resurgence online. Unfortunately, it was primarily used in the background of far-right political edits, ironic given the track’s anti-Trump lyrics. MGMT has aggressively forced right-wing politicians to remove videos where they use the track, including Rishi Sunak in the UK and ICE’s Twitter account in the US. After *Little Dark Age*, MGMT’s contract with Columbia Records expired and the duo has gone independent. The duo released its fifth album, *Loss of Life*, in 2024 to decent reviews but abysmal sales. Allegedly, MGMT began recording its sixth album in late 2025, but it has yet to release.

# Hargrove's review

Man, do I love synthpop, and, man, do I love MGMT’s singles. The late 2000s indietronica aesthetic was one of the first that I ever fell in love with; tracks like “1901” and “Lisztomania” by Phoenix evoke feelings for me that I can’t find anywhere else. Of course, MGMT’s “Time to Pretend”, “Kids”, and “Electric Feel” define that era better than anything else. I liked them so much that I actually listened to *Oracular Spectacular* all the way through many, many times despite the fact that none of the deep cuts are any good. *Congratulations* and *MGMT* passed me by, leading me to believe as a teenager that they were something akin to a one-hit wonder, a flash in the pan synonymous with my mental idea of the year 2007. However, in early 2020, I was persuaded to give *Little Dark Age* a few spins. It was decent! I thought the title track, “She Works Out Too Much”, and “TSLAMP” were all brilliant and have listened to them many times since. Perhaps I was programmed by *Oracular Spectacular* to just assume I needed to pick out the few good tracks and leave the rest behind. Now, having listened to the other seven tracks again for the first time in six years, I’ve found two more that I’ve been severely missing out on: “When You Die” and “One Thing Left to Try”. At its best, *Little Dark Age* borrows the oppressive embrace of synthwave but without the nagging feeling that it’s been done before. Sure, you’ve heard these synth timbres and the drum programming in a million songs of the early 80s, but you’ve never heard compositions that use them in exactly this way. This album’s five golden tracks keep a delicate balance at around 75% synthpop and 25% psychedelia, a glimpse into an alternate universe 80s that had the self-consciousness of the 60s and knew just how scary its excesses could sound. Sadly, the other five tracks on this album just don’t do much for me at all. “Me and Michael” is a special case, a track in the same vein as the five I love but that unfortunately doesn’t stack up in strength of composition. But “James”, “Days That Got Away”, “When You’re Small”, and “Hand It Over” can’t keep their balance on the tightrope this record walks. They abandon the towering, interwoven synths that are necessary to this album’s dialectic, privileging antithesis over thesis. Oh well. *Oracular Spectacular* primed me for this, at least. And the five tracks that are good are *really* good.

I love “She Works Out Too Much” to death. I have listened to it so many times over the years. It captures the exact vibe of an 80s workout tape, twisting it into a way to explore the anxiety of modern life’s optimization. The synths are beautifully intricate. The chorus has been stuck in my head for years. Amazing track. “Little Dark Age” is a deeply unsettling banger. The sparse verse sections build up such a tense atmosphere before the warm embrace of synths fill the vacuum for the chorus. I don’t know whether the composition or arrangement of this track is more perfect. Furthermore, it has a certain statuesque, stately, Renaissance elegance to it. “When You Die” sounds straight up like an Ariel Pink song; it could easily be on *pom pom*. I love that album and mean this wholeheartedly as a compliment. I particularly enjoy the rapid fade-in to the vocals at the beginning of each verse and the organic motorik beat. It also has one of those classic Ariel Pink melodies that feels like you must have heard it somewhere before but just can’t place it. I’m not crazy about “Me and Michael”, which makes me sad. It has the same vibe as the bangers surrounding it on the first half of this record, but it just feels a little lifeless by contrast. I’ve found it slightly more grating with each listen. Thankfully, next comes “TSLAMP”, another favorite of mine. I love its offbeat swagger, 80s drums, snappy writing, catchy melodies, and female vocals. I also relate to the lyrics just a little too much for comfort. “James” marks the point where the album starts to lose the sheen of its first half. It sounds more like generic psychedelia and, for me personally, MGMT’s sound doesn’t work without the synthpop. I can’t keep my attention focused on this track no matter how hard I try. “Days That Got Away” has an interesting instrumental with an off-kilter drum pattern and a progressive structure. Sadly, the vocals on it bug me too much to be able to like the track overall. “One Thing Left to Try” is the gem buried in this album’s second half. I love the atmosphere, as thick as it was on the title track. There’s something terrifying about it, just positively apocalyptic. And the vocals are so sharp and jagged. I like it. “When You’re Small” is the only real competition to “James” for my least favorite track. It has some admirable qualities: the shifting instrumentation under the choruses, the somber atmosphere, the strings in the breakdown. Fuck, does it feel pointless though. It’s all trite lyrical observations and claustrophobic music. “Hand It Over” is undeniably a nice track, but a disappointing finish to the album. It’s quite pretty and well-constructed, sure, but would it kill them to inject a little energy here at the end?

It was really hard to choose between high B Tier and low A Tier for this record. Ultimately, the album it most reminded me of was *Violent Femmes*, another record with a few transcendentally good tracks interspersed in even measure with filler. I’m putting *Little Dark Age* right above it because though “Blister in the Sun” is the best song on either album, the average quality of *Little Dark Age*’s good tracks is higher. It’s also right below another album I have similar feelings on, *Mellon Collie and the Infinite Sadness*. In that case, I feel like the best songs there are about on par with the best here, but the average deep cut on *Mellon Collie* was much better. These three records form the top of B Tier.

Favorite tracks: She Works Out Too Much, Little Dark Age, When You Die, TSLAMP, One Thing Left to Try

Least favorite track: James

# Aidan's review

The rare 2010-ish indie album that I hadn't heard in full, this has some great singles. "Me and Michael", "When You Die" and the title track are all well written, catchy singles. "She Works Out Too Much" is basically a below average Ariel Pink idea executed well. "TSLAP" is solid, and the rest of the album is mostly forgettable. This is basically the standard for every synth pop album we've had, really solid singles, and the remainder coming out somewhere between fine and barely tolerable. I'll give this album points for having 3 amazing songs, while most of its peers top out at 2, and the supporting cast of tunes are about average for the genre. It's also interesting to see the next step of pop evolution after Ariel Pink, before it got crossbred with the output of Oneohtrix's samples and James Ferraro's sound effects. Fun, light fare.
