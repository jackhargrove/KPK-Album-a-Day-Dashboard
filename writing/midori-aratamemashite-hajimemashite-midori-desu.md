# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

After being so nonplussed by Les Rallizes Dénudés last week and Fishmans a year ago, I was not terribly excited to do another Japanese album that’s big in certain internet circles. On first listen, it was clear that the more direct and aggressive style here appeals to me more than either of those albums, but still it didn’t fully connect. It wasn’t until “5拍子 [5/4 Time]” on the back half of the album that my ears really perked up (NOTE: the English translations aren’t on Spotify, so I had been referring to this one as “the one in 5/4”. Imagine my surprise when I look up the translations). Gotō’s vocals are certainly unique and interesting, sure, but this was the song that made me realize this album’s strongsuit: the instrumentalists are damn talented! I assumed that jazz-punk would be kind of a gimmick that leans much more heavily on the punk side of things, but the best songs on this album are just straight up good jazz. Iwami plays the double bass louder and better than just about anyone I’ve heard on any “real” jazz album. Hajime’s piano steals the show on multiple tracks, using repetition to turn loose, swingy playing into something hypnotic. And Kozeni’s cymbal-heavy swing provides a bright spot even in the weakest tracks. While Gotō’s vocals can be hit or miss for me, her heavily feedbacked shoegaze guitar adds the perfect atmosphere. When everything comes together, it works, and it works damn well. The biggest issue is that most of my favorite songs are on the back half of the album. It feels like the first half has the instrumentals following Gotō’s vocals, playing aggressively when she’s yelling and being annoying when she’s doing bullshit. But it gets into a groove on the back half, really starting with the bass solo at the beginning of “ちはるの恋 [Chiharu’s Love]”, where the instruments take the lead. From there, Gotō’s vocals feel much more balanced, making for a much more enjoyable experience. Sadly, the album goes too far with the long, improvised final instrumental, ending an album that, for all its faults, was consistently high-energy on a real dud. Still, despite its unevenness, I had fun with this album. I can’t stop listening to “5拍子 [5/4 Time]”, and there are a few other tracks I really liked as well.

“スキ [Love You]” exists for the most part as a bait-and-switch lead in to the second track. I want to like “ゆきこさん [Yukiko-San]” because its highs are admittedly quite high. I love the melodic interludes and can get into the “DESTROY” screams. But the structure just doesn’t flow at all. The disparate parts are roughly assembled in such a way that feels choppy. The deranged rambling parts also put me off. “かなしい日々。[Sorrowful Days.]” has a screaming part that’s a bit less annoying and a stellar showing from the piano, so I’m putting it on my favorite tracks list instead. The double bass shows its power for the first time on “お猿 [The Monkey]”, but otherwise I do not like this song at all. The vocal melody is just so annoying. I like “根性無しあたし、あほぼけかす [I Am a Coward, Stupid, Idiot, Useless]” a bit more, but it has this same issue. The cutesy vocals and the baroque piano just don’t mix, making the whole less than the sum of its already not rgeat parts. The bass solo at the start of “ちはるの恋 [Chiharu’s Love]” marks a shift, eventually coalescing into a wonderful motif to form the song’s base. It’s rare to hear a bass sound so good even in the most legendary jazz albums. The swinging groove of the piano sounds great as well, and I like Gotō’s mannish vocals as she sings about (I think) being molested by a friend’s dad. Weird stuff. The piano takes over for the bass in “ひみつの2人 [The Secret Couple]”, starting the track loose before tightening into a wonderful loop. This song is a favorite for the masterful piano alone. The album peaks with “5拍子 [5/4 Time]”. I was not expecting this album to have probably a top 10% track from the whole list so far, but here we are. The bass and piano combine to make the 5/4 swing lilt and flow, inhale and exhale. Gotō comes in with her most beautiful vocal part on the album, a “la la la” line that perfectly complements the dreaminess of her shoegaze guitar. I felt some trepidation about “ハウリング地獄 [Feedback Hell]” at first, as it takes some time to coalesce despite the catchy scat singing from Gotō. However, the second the first breakdown section hits, the piano makes everything come together into something really beautiful. It’s really like shoegaze jazz. Super cool track. Tragically, the album ends on the total dud of “無欲の無力 [No Desires; No Power]”. An album this high energy deserved a better closer than 6 minutes of noodling.

We haven’t had an album this uneven in a while. The highs and lows could not be further apart. Man, those highs are nice though.

Favorite tracks: かなしい日々。[Sorrowful Days.], ちはるの恋 [Chiharu’s Love], ひみつの2人 [The Secret Couple], 5拍子 [5/4 Time], ハウリング地獄 [Feedback Hell]

Least favorite track: お猿 [The Monkey]
