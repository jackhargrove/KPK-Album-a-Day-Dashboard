# Write-up

Modest Mouse is an indie rock band formed in Issaquah, Washington outside Seattle in 1993 originally consisting of vocalist, guitarist, and songwriter Isaac Brock, bassist Eric Judy, and drummer Jeremiah Green. Brock was born in 1975 and raised in Montana and Oregon by a commune of cultish Christian hippies who lived in trailers. His family parked its trailer in Issaquah, Washington in 1986, where he lived until briefly moving to DC in 1992. He eventually settled into a shed next to his mother’s house in Issaquah in 1993, where he founded Modest Mouse at age 18 with Green and Judy. The band took its name from a passage of Virginia Woolf. Despite their adjacency to the then-famous Seattle music scene of the early 90s that spawned grunge, Modest Mouse had nothing to do with that crew. The group released its debut EP in 1994 with the help of Beat Happening’s Calvin Johnson and shortly after recorded *Sad Sappy Sucker*, an album intended to be their debut but which was shelved for years. Instead, *This Is a Long Drive for Someone with Nothing to Think About* was released as Modest Mouse’s debut LP in 1996. The band’s unique fusion of slacker rock production, Midwest emo angst and swirling guitars, rural white trash aesthetics, and Brock’s pointed lyrics earned the group a cult following.

Modest Mouse released its second album, *The Lonesome Crowded West*, in 1997. It’s a semi-concept album largely concerned with the encroachment of suburban sprawl on the empty rural areas of the Pacific Northwest where Brock grew up. The album further defined Modest Mouse’s unique sound, now even adding influence from hip-hop in the form of turntablism. The record was produced by Calvin Johnson of Beat Happening, whose sister Nicole also sings on a few tracks; however, the tracks “Teeth Like God’s Shoeshine”, “Doin’ the Cockroach”, and “Cowboy Dan” were all recorded by Phil Ek, who had worked on Earth’s second album immediately prior and went on to produce Built to Spill’s *Perfect from Now On*, Duster’s *Stratosphere*, and provide engineering on Unwound’s *Leaves Turn Inside You*. The album sold 60,000 copies, a success for a truly independent band, and helped define the post-“alternative rock” sound of indie rock in the late 90s and early 2000s.

After the relative success of *The Lonesome Crowded West*, Modest Mouse signed to a major label in 2000 and released its third album *The Moon & Antarctica*. Though the band was accused of selling-out, particularly after “Gravity Rides Everything” was used in a Nissan commercial, the album received even greater acclaim than the one before it. It introduced more sophisticated and clean production techniques, giving the music a space rock sheen. Fans often debate whether *The Lonesome Crowded West* or *The Moon & Antarctica* is their best album (I’m on team *The Moon & Antarctica*).

The band moved to Portland, Oregon, in 2004. For better or worse, those concerned about Modest Mouse’s commercial shift were vindicated when the lead single “Float On” from the band’s fourth album *Good News for People Who Love Bad News* became a genuine hit in 2004, charting at #68 on the Billboard Hot 100. As a result, Modest Mouse is often credited (or blamed, depending on who you ask) with ushering in the next wave of indie rock to the mainstream in the mid-2000s after the decline of the post-punk revival. Johnny Marr, former guitarist for the Smiths, joined the band in 2006 and played on its 2007 album *We Were Dead Before the Ship Even Sank*. This album reached #1 on the Billboard 200 and contained another hit single, “Dashboard”. Since this peak, the band has released two more albums and numerous EPs, each of increasingly dubious quality. Modest Mouse is still together and actively touring; Aidan and I saw them live in DC just last year. However, Green died of cancer in 2022 at age 45, while Judy left in 2012, leaving Brock the sole founding member in the band.

# Hargrove's review

Holy FUCK do I love Modest Mouse. Easily one of the greatest bands of all time. It’s one of those beautiful musical coincidences where an actual genius lyricist sets his songs against a distinctive, inventive, and sublime musical sound. Isaac Brock would be a great poet, and these songs would be brilliant instrumentals, but by the grace of God the two are inseparable. Like everyone else, I had known (and loved) “Float On” from growing up, but it wasn’t until I was 16 that I listened to *The Moon & Antarctica* and completely fell in love. I think that’s close to a perfect album, and it remains my favorite Modest Mouse album to this day. Not long afterward, I got into *Good New for People Who Love Bad News* and, of course, *The Lonesome Crowded West*. They were one of the first bands I loved so much that I knew three full albums by heart. My family actually flew to Boise, Idaho in late 2017 to watch them perform a concert with Built to Spill (and it ruled). In the ten years since, songs like “Tiny Cities Made of Ashes” and “Cowboy Dan” and “Doin’ the Cockroach” and “Ocean Breathes Salty” and “Bukowski” and “3rd Planet” and “The View” and “Dark Center of the Universe” have remained in heavy rotation for me. I’ve well-established that *The Moon & Antarctica* is my favorite, but, man, *The Lonesome Crowded West* is hot on its heels. Without being an out-and-out concept album, it feels easily more thematically cohesive than almost any other record I’ve ever heard. From the lyrics to the swirling and melancholic quiet parts to the searing loud parts, everything about this record makes you feel the intensifying ennui of the obsolete ruralite. It captures the death of the Wild West better than any work since *The Searchers*. Isaac Brock is kind of like a reverse empath; seldom few writers have his talent for forcing you to feel exactly what he feels. It’s a singular mix of hopelessness, fear, rage, nostalgia, claustrophobia, and indignation that affects me in some way each listen. And then there are the songs themselves, just banger after banger after banger. “Doin’ the Cockroach” into “Cowboy Dan” into “Trailer Trash” is an easy contender for the best three track run of all-time, but there are at least seven more tracks on the album that are almost just as good. Yeah, “Trucker’s Atlas” drags on a little long, and “Jesus Christ Was an Only Child” is a dud, but this is a remarkably consistent album given its length. And I don’t mean consistently above average, I mean consistently stellar. It’s one of the best albums of all-time. Just go listen to it.

I’m going to try to be sparing in the number of lyrics I cite because when I was taking notes I typed up at least 10x as many quotes as I have for any other album. Just know that I think every song on this album has essentially perfect lyrics. The album gets off to a crazy strong start with “Teeth Like God’s Shoeshine”, slamming the gas pedal firmly on the floor without any acclimatization. There are so many varied and cool sections to this track, but I especially love when it shifts to 6/8 just before the two minute mark. I associate that meter with lilting waltzes, so it blows my mind to hear it on such a scorcher. Brock forces me to understand his worldview, whether his unending captivity with From the top of the ocean, yeah/From the bottom of the sky, goddamn/Well, I get claustrophobic” or his righteous indignation with “Oh, if you could compact your conscience/And sell it, save it for another time/You know you might have to use it”. It’s just a wonderful opener. When I was younger, I didn’t love the lyrics on “Heart Cooks Brain”, but it has such an open and airy vibe to it that I’ve gotten over it. The turntablism not only adds a genre-bending hip hop flair, but it adds to the lethargic vibe. It’s a great track to zone out to. “Convenient Parking” was the first track from this record that I ever fell in ove with. It perfectly executes the quiet verse/loud chorus dynamic, with the rumbling riff in the quiet parts keeping the tension. The relentless repetition of the lyrics enhances the monotonous images of driving, while catchy screaming makes the choruses orgasmic. Banger city over here. “Lounge (Closing Time)” is a progressive cut like the opener, starting with a peppy riff and vocals but certainly not staying there. I love the great builds that dissipate into slowcore. Still a perfect record so far. Unfortunately, “Jesus Christ Was an Only Child” derails things a bit. It’s mercifully short, but it’s too annoying to work as a shake-up interlude. It also suffers from falling between the long, drawn-out “Lounge (Closing Time)” and the intensity of the next track.

“Doin’ the Cockroach” begins with one of my favorite line of all time: “I was in heaven, I was in hell/Believe in neither, but fear them as well”. Brock’s attitude toward religion so closely matches the way I think about things. The verses display a masterful control of tension, Brock shifting between perfect restraint and full anarchy every four bars. There’s a spindly post-hardcore guitar riff and an explosive banger of a chorus. Once it gets into its full groove, it becomes a hardcore equivalent to dance-punk. And it also kicks off the three track run of a lifetime as the somehow even better “Cowboy Dan” follows. What a fucking song. “He didn't move to the city, the city moved to me/And I want out desperately” is a line that has haunted me for a decade and will continue for decades more. And that visual of Dan trying to shoot God! It also somehow immediately tops the previous track for “most tension building guitar riff ever”. More than just Grammy-worthy, Brock’s vocals are Oscar-worthy, selling the story of Cowboy Dan completely. I used to resent the slow section, but I now appreciate it as both a breather and an intensifier for the final third, one of the most rewarding moments in music history. I love love love love love this song. Despite the scorching fire of those two barnburners, the tender ballad that is “Trailer Trash” might take the cake for most intense. It’s just achingly beautiful. It made me cry this morning while walking to work, specifically the “And I know that I miss you/And I'm sorry if I dissed you” line that I interpret as aimed at his parents. The extended guitar outro leaves you to stew in it for two minutes while the bass line conjures nostalgia from the ether in the same way as “1979”. This is just an unbelievable sequence of tracks.

The next three tracks are much more low-key, but that doesn’t mean they’re bad! “Out of Gas” features one of my favorite one-liners in “Opinions were like kittens, I was giving them away”. Beyond that, there’s a catchy chorus with good wordplay, great drums, it’s just a good track to have around. “Long Distance Drunk” has interesting percussion, but otherwise it’s only okay. There are maybe some complaints I could articulate about “Shit Luck”, but, I mean, come on. What a fantastic injection of energy. It’s a rocking good time that gets the blood flowing. What more can you ask for? “Trucker’s Atlas” starts strong, particularly the drums, but the issue is that if I could choose any song on this album to stretch to 10+ minutes, it would not be this one. It’s still pretty good, but I would prefer if it were normal length. “Polar Opposites” proves that Brock can write a perfect pop song too because fuck it, why not. “It's the same on the weekends as the rest of the days” hits on what I found to be one of the most demoralizing parts of unemployment, while “I'm tryin', I'm tryin' to/Drink away the part of the day that I cannot sleep away” is a depression line for the ages. It works so beautifully and I love it so much. “Bankrupt on Selling” rivals “Trailer Trash” for most tender, heart-wrenching ballad on the album. Obviously I’m not someone who is inherently moved by an anti-capitalist message, but Brock’s just so good at transmitting the way he feels. It’s so downtrodden, so broken, so sad. “Well, I'll go to college and I'll learn some big words/And I'll talk real loud, goddamn right I'll be heard/You'll remember the guy that said all those big words/He must've learned in college”. It gets to me. “Styrofoam Boots/It’s All Nice on Ice, Alright” ends the album on a high note, seamlessly fusing an acoustic jaunt with a ripping closer. This thing starts an accelerando with just 50 seconds left in the album. Who does that? It rules.

I like this album so much that I didn’t even once bitch about it being nearly the exact maximum length of a CD. I had planned to put this in S Tier to represent that *The Moon & Antarctica* would be in SS Tier were it on the list, but I cannot in good conscience put this below SS Tier. It’s just that good.

Favorite tracks: Teeth Like God’s Shoeshine, Heart Cooks Brain, Convenient Parking, Lounge (Closing Time), Doin’ the Cockroach, Cowboy Dan, Trailer Trash, Out of Gas, Shit Luck, Polar Opposites, Bankrupt on Selling, Styrofoam Boots/It’s All Nice on Ice, Alright

Least favorite track: Jesus Christ Was an Only Child
