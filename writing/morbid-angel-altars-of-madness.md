# Write-up

Morbid Angel is a death metal band formed in Tampa, Florida in 1983 which, at the time of this album, consisted of vocalist and bassist David Vincent, guitarist Trey Azagthoth, guitarist Richard Brunelle, and drummer Pete Sandoval. The band was founded by Azagthoth with drummer Mike Browning. Morbid Angel was one of the first bands in the Florida death metal scene that helped incubate the genre throughout the 80s and popularize it in the 90s. Early on, Morbid Angel became notorious in Tampa for its “gruesome stage antics”. The band recorded a debut album *Abominations of Desolation* in 1986 but chose to shelve it until 1991. Shortly afterward, Vincent and Sandoval joined the band.

Morbid Angel released its first studio album, *Altars of Madness*, in 1989. It includes new recordings of multiple tracks originally intended for *Abominations of Desolation*. The band set out to make the most brutal, evil, destructive metal album ever released up to that point and, by most accounts, they succeeded. This impulse came from an intense competition with other artists in the Florida death metal scene like Death. Along with Death’s early work, *Altars of Madness* helped introduce many of the hallmarks of death metal, including death growls, extremely fast and technical playing, blast beats, sudden tempo shifts, and Satanic lyrics. Retrospectively, *Altars of Madness* is recognized as one of the first records to have truly set death metal apart from thrash metal, and as one of the best death metal albums ever released.

*Altars of Madness* made waves in the underground metal scene at the time, allowing Morbid Angel to spend the next two years touring with other cutting-edge artists. Morbid Angel released its second album, *Blessed Are the Sick*, in 1991. It received similar acclaim to its predecessor and earned Morbid Angel one of the first major label deals in death metal. Brunelle was kicked out of the group in 1992 for substance abuse issues; instead of replacing him, Morbid Angel just decided to become a trio. *Covenant*, Morbid Angel’s third album and major label debut, was a massive commercial success. It sold 150,000 copies in the US, spawned two music videos which received constant airplay on MTV (and a feature on *Beavis and Butt-Head*), and earned the group a tour with Black Sabbath. This album was the peak of the death metal genre, starting its dominance in the mainstream metal scene of the mid-90s. The band’s fourth album, 1995’s *Domination*, slowed tempos down and polarized fans. Though it still sold decently well, Morbid Angel was dropped from its label. Vincent left the band in 1996. Since then, Morbid Angel has released five more studio albums, the most recent coming in 2017. Vincent left Morbid Angel in 1996, rejoined in 2004, and left again in 2015. Sandoval left the group in 2013 after converting to Christianity. Vincent and Sandoval both perform to this day as members of spin-off band I Am Morbid. Brunelle died in 2019. Morbid Angel still exists and is touring, but Azagthoth is the only remaining member who played on *Altars of Madness*.

# Hargrove's review

Just a few months ago, I had never actually heard a death metal album in my life. I had never listened to much metal after Metallica before we started this project, and then, by chance, Aidan’s album-picking algorithm saved most of the death metal for near the end. But when we finally listened to Death in April, I was really impressed. I liked that album (*Human*) a lot, and I liked the next Death album I listened to (*Symbolic*) even more. This also happened to coincide with when I started lifting weights, which was just a match made in heaven. I have listened to Death quite frequently since then and checked out some other death metal records not on the list. I still firmly place doom metal and derivatives (stoner metal, sludge metal) as my favorite metal subgenre, but death metal has become a close second. Anyway, all of that is to say that I was pretty stoked to listen to Morbid Angel’s *Altars of Madness*. And, thankfully, it did not disappoint. This is a super cool album. I love the way that it can shift gears at the drop of a hat. The song structures are far and away my favorite part of the album. Morbid Angel can play blazingly fast, and Morbid Angel can play slow with just as much power. Most songs on this album (the good ones, anyway) constantly shift between these two modes effortlessly. I have to give major props to drummer Pete Sandoval. His kick drum is the guiding light that ties every second of this album together. Sometimes it plays slowly and syncopated, while other times it attacks you with such force and ferocity that it sounds like one sustained note. The production on the kick drum is just amazing too. It sounds like it’s piercing deep into my ear with each thud. Additionally, I liked how the drums so smoothly shifted between the skank beats of thrash and the blast beats of death metal. Skank beats create so much bouncy energy (they are originally from hardcore punk, after all) while blast beats communicate sheer, dominating power. That Sandoval moves between both with ease emphasizes the tempo shifts even more. But that’s enough about the drums. Vincent delivers a perfect death growl, not too clean but not too abrasive either. My biggest complaint about this album has to do with the guitars. To be clear, this album contains some incredible guitar work, particularly the riffs on “Immortal Rites” and “Maze of Torment”. However, there were more than a few tracks without memorable riffs that I took away from me. Moreover, I’m just not a fan of the soloing on this album. Thankfully the solos aren’t too terribly frequent or long, but they never move me much at all. These nitpicks with the guitar mean that I prefer Death’s *Human* to this album, as it has much of what I liked here plus insane riff after insane riff. But still, what I’m looking for with this kind of music is power and complexity, and I found both here. Damn good album.

The reversed music at the beginning of “Immortal Rites” is a sick way to kick things off. Immediately after, the best riff on the entire album begins. This is a great track throughout, but my favorite part is the slow, chugging section right in the middle. What can I say, I am a sucker for slow metal music. I like the way that the guitars grind back and forth like rusty machinery on “Suffocation”, but the riff immediately stands out as less interesting than the one on the previous track. The drums are a highlight, but that’s true of every track, and this one’s just decent. I love the dissonance of the guitar part over the chorus of “Visions from the Dark Side”. This song also exemplifies that masterful combination of slow power chugging with an unbelievable onslaught of blast beat drums that I was raving about in the first paragraph. “Maze of Torment” opens with a riff almost as good as the opener’s. The rapid-fire vocals are a nice change of pace, while the drums lay down a perfect blend of thrash and death metal styles. Additionally, this is one of my favorite tracks lyrically. It’s a scary description of Hell! “Chapel of Ghouls” has all of the good elements I’ve been talking about with the addition of an amazing breakdown. The drums sound so brutal even at a minimal tempo. The slow drumming on this track reminds me a lot of the drumming on Swans’ *Filth*, similarly brutal and mechanical despite not being fast. I also like the Church-like voices in the background. “Bleed for the Devil” starts off blazingly fast, but I’m not crazy for it. It’s got a much less complex structure than the other tracks, mostly just sticking to maximum speed. As a result, it’s my least favorite track. “Damnation” is fine, but it has entirely too many guitar solos. There’s a decent riff in there somewhere, it’s just that the solos prevent there from being as many full distinct sections as I would like. I find the lyrics of “Blasphemy” hilarious in how straightforwardly edgy they are. It’s literally “Try this one weird trick that God doesn’t wan you to know about to go to Hell instantly”. On top of that, I love the tremolo picking on the guitar during some parts of the track, and, again, the drums are just absurd. The album closes on a high note with “Evil Spells”. It’s every bit as good as the first few tracks, showing off the amazing shifts from low tempo to high tempo. What a banger finish.

I’m very excited to explore more death metal once we finish this project in a couple of months. I have been impressed with the genre so far.

Favorite tracks: Immortal Rites, Visions from the Dark Side, Maze of Torment, Chapel of Ghouls, Blasphemy, Evil Spells

Least favorite track: Bleed for the Devil

# Aidan's review

I remember listening to Illmatic and feeling that this was what I had been thinking of when I thought of the platonic ideal of hip hop. The same goes for this and death metal. The song "Immortal Rites" is about rites to gain immortality, the song "Blasphemy" sees the band blaspheme the Holy Ghost and "Chapel of Ghouls" is about ghouls attacking a chapel. It's straightforward, yet enjoyable, with one guitar putting enough of a melody to make the songs fun while the other guitar hammers out some of the wildest licks imaginable. I do wish there was some cleverness to this album, and I think that if I listened to it earlier I would have hated it, but having listened to a lot of metal, it was a fine listen. I wish I could give some deeper insight, but it's partly a consequence of this album's success that every other death metal album we've done sounds a lot like it. If you like death metal, you'll like this album.
