# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

I’ve been excited for this record because as far as I can tell, it’s the only truly classical music on the list. Yeah, yeah, Glenn Branca is kind of classical, and Popol Vuh if you squint. But Feldman was a contemporary of John Cage. That’s the real deal! Overall, I liked *Rothko Chapel* quite a bit, but I couldn’t get into *Why Patterns?* at all. Really, I’m just a little confused why this of all things was chosen as the token classical album. I know that very few classical composers have their music performed, recorded, and packaged into albums, but Steve Reich’s *Music for 18 Musicians* is right there. That’s an absolute banger! Terry Riley also has some cool stuff, particularly *In C*. But it wouldn’t be fair to blame *Rothko Chapel + Why Patterns?* for this list’s omission of *Music for 18 Musicians*. They’re not actually rivalrous goods. I do think it serves as a good point of comparison though. While Feldman wasn’t actually a minimalist per se, you can clearly see a lot of shared ideas. He makes use of silence quite extensively throughout both pieces, only inserting sound where it absolutely needs to be. I find this approach to minimalism (as a philosophy, not necessarily a genre) less rewarding than Reich’s, who sets glacial changes in dynamics atop walls of droning, unchanging sound. Maybe I just have ADHD, but that works better for me. Still, I found a way into *Rothko Chapel*, even if I couldn’t for *Why Patterns?*.

It took a while to get into, but I enjoyed *Rothko Chapel* eventually. It’s so eerie, just like a horror movie score, with the voices setting the tone, the percussion generating tension, and the viola cutting through like a knife when it wants to. At times, it sounds more like Eno ambient than it does classical music to me (well, the other way around, to be fair). “Rothko Chapel 1” is one of the better parts of the composition. I particularly like the tympani roll into the vocal crescendo near the end of this part. “Rothko Chapel 2” continues a lot of the same ideas, while “Rothko Chapel 3” hits a lull by focusing on only the vocals. The composition gets really good with “Rothko Chapel 4” once the viola takes over. It cuts through the silence with such power. The very best, however, was saved for last with “Rothko Chapel 5”. I liked this final segment so much that I added it to my normal liked songs playlist. It starts with a beautiful celesta that leads into the viola playing the clearest and prettiest melody so far. It’s just gorgeous. It’s almost Medieval sounding, or like high fantasy. It’s a super cool few minutes. Unfortunately, I just cannot crack *Why Patterns?*. It’s austere, cold, and impenetrable. I enjoy the flute, but I cannot for the life of me tell what I’m supposed to get out of the piano and the glockenspiel. I kind of understand what’s happening conceptually, the independent repetitions, but I don’t enjoy it much at all while listening.

I would place *Rothko Chapel?* on its own into low B Tier. *Why Patterns?* drags it into C Tier. Still, this was an awesome change of pace from the other stuff we’ve listened to. I continue to be amazed by the breadth of music on this list.

Favorite tracks: Rothko Chapel 1, Rothko Chapel 4, Rothko Chapel 5

Least favorite track: Why Patterns?
