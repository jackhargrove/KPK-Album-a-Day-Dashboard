# Write-up

Mr. Bungle is an alternative metal band formed in Eureka, California (the far north of the state) in 1985 which, at the time of this album, consisted of vocalist and keyboardist Mike Patton (credited as Vlad Drac), guitarist Trey Spruance (as Scummy), bassist Trevor Dunn, tenor saxophonist Clinton McKinnon (as Bär, pronounced “bear”), alto and baritone saxophonist Theo Lengyel, and drummer Danny Heifetz (as Heifetz). In high school, Spruance was kicked out of a gothic metal band and Patton and Dunn were kicked out of Metallica cover band. They combined to form a death metal outfit named Mr. Bungle, the name taken from a character featured in HBO’s *The Pee-Wee Herman Show*. The band’s sound incorporated more styles than just death metal, however, including speed metal, thrash metal, and hardcore punk. The group added ska and funk to the sound, inspired by California new wave and alt rock bands like Oingo Boingo and Camper Van Beethoven. To this end, Mr. Bungle added saxophonists McKinnon and Lengyel. Mr. Bungle played a few shows in small town northern California and recorded a couple of demo tapes, but it never went anywhere. The first demo caught the attention of San Francisco alt metal band Faith No More, who invited Patton to become its lead singer. He moved to SF, though he remained a member of Mr. Bungle. In 1990, Faith No More broke into the mainstream with “Epic”, a Billboard top 10 hit that popularized rap metal. Faith No More became one of the most important groups of this genre and one of the major influences on nu metal (much to Patton’s chagrin). Seeing Patton’s success, the other members of Mr. Bungle finally left Eureka for SF and the band signed to a major label.

In 1991, Mr. Bungle released its self-titled debut album. Only one man was cut out for the job of producing these jazz- and funk-obsessed alt rock weirdos: John Zorn. *Mr. Bungle* features a broad range of styles, mixing metal riffs with ska, funk, free jazz, and even circus music. However, the album is often placed into the box of simply “funk metal”, the earliest major style of alternative metal which included the Red Hot Chili Peppers (with whom Mr. Bungle had an ongoing feud stemming from intense personal animosity between lead singer Anthony Kiedis and Patton), Rage Against the Machine, Primus, and Jane’s Addiction. The album features a number of samples, taken from sources including *Blue Velvet*, TV commercials, and various video games. The album received a polarized reception in the press, but it sold fairly well and has retrospectively been cited as an influence by major metal bands of the late 90s and early 00s.

Mr. Bungle continued as Patton’s more experimental and transgressive side project while Faith No More became one of the most popular bands in the world. Four years after the debut, Mr. Bungle released *Disco Volante* in 1995, an even more adventurous album that allowed the band to embark on its first world tour. Lengyel left the band around this time; unrelatedly, he was convicted of murdering his girlfriend in 2024 and is currently serving a life sentence in prison. Mr. Bungle returned with its third album, *California*, in 1999. Featuring much simpler and straightforward compositions than their first two records, it was a critical and commercial success. The band went on tour with the metal newcomers it had inspired, including System of a Down. The band broke up in 2000 because its members were involved with too many other projects. In 2019, Mr. Bungle reunited around a core of Patton, Spruance, and Dunn, with Anthrax guitarist Scott Ian and Slayer drummer Dave Lombardo joining as well. During reunion show, the band primarily played covers of punk and metal songs, disappointing fans. The band released its fourth album, *The Raging Wrath of the Easter Bunny Demo*, in 2020. As the name implies, it is a re-recording of the band’s first demo tape. As of 2026, Mr. Bungle is still touring.

# Hargrove's review

This album has intrigued me ever since Cooper pointed it out on the day we started this project and I’ve had to wait a year and a half to hear how it sounds. Well, it’s certainly an interesting one. It very much did not disappoint in that regard. *Mr. Bungle* combines many of the traits I most enjoy in music, such as groovy bass lines, heavy guitar riffs, and bizarro comedy, with some of the traits I most despise, like schizophrenic compositions, overly dramatic vocal delivery, and a tendency for the tracks to go on a few minutes too long. More succinctly, no album that I can recall has played jumprope with the line between delighting and infuriating me. Luckily, I was able to adjust to the unique presentation after a few listens and found five songs that I really enjoyed. Unluckily, acclimating to the sound wasn’t enough to resolve all of the issues I had, particularly my compositional complaints. On an atmosphere level, I enjoyed much of what I heard. Though it doesn’t sound exactly like anything else, the Oingo Boingo influence is palpable, and I enjoyed hearing that circus ska applied to metal. But from a bird’s-eye view, I was never able to get past the fact that I simply couldn’t listen to this album passively. Don’t get me wrong, I’m not opposed to music that demands attention, and in fact it’s practically a pre-requisite for me to really fall in love with an album that I can listen to it as my primary focus and enjoy the duration of its run time. My issue with *Mr. Bungle* is that, though it works decently well on an active listening level, it’s one of the only albums I’ve ever heard in my life that I’ve been unable to passively listen to as well. Whether I love it or hate it, I’m able to identify activities and actions that any given work can comfortably play in the background of pretty well. I cannot for the life of me think what I could do that *Mr. Bungle* could properly soundtrack. I found myself reluctant to start new listens, something that usually only happens with albums I really dislike. However, that wasn’t the case here; instead, my hesitation was due to the fact that I knew the next hour and 15 minutes of my life would be *Mr. Bungle*. I couldn’t hike around Tahoe or drive anywhere or even properly scroll the internet while it was playing. All of this is to say, I liked this album enough for it to land in at least low B Tier, but the overarching limitations of its style prevent me from putting it higher than that.

*Mr. Bungle* begins with an immaculate four track run of oddball bangers. I adore the squishy textures of “Quote Unquote” that make the funhouse vibe even more unstable and misproportioned. It’s actually a pretty novel use of one of music’s oldest tropes, the ska skank beat. The chorus is also super catchy, while the keyboard glides like it’s being played by Manzarek. I’m also a fan of the jazzy interludes which feel like they’re giving a glimpse into a grim reality in the distance of the haunted carnival. “Slowly Growing Deaf” gives a taste of a funky groove before the hypnotic slow section begins. Between the swirling keyboard and Patton’s killer crooning vocals, I can’t get enough of it. Then the next section introduces one of the crunchiest, most grinding guitar riffs of the whole album to remind you that this is metal. Thankfully, all of the individual sections of this track are quire good because it does start to push the limits of compositional disjointedness that I can stand. The outro collage is, uh, interesting, to say the least. I do actually like the Colonel Sanders sample a lot and I don’t dislike the “other” sample enough to stop this track from being a favorite. “Squeeze Me Macaroni” is my favorite track on the album. It’s the purest distillation of the record’s vibe into one straightforward song. There’s no shortage of inventive wordplay in the lyrics, which Patton delivers with gusto. Essentially, it’s an absurdist funk track, and I love it. “Carousel” rounds out the great first four. It’s the most Oingo Boingo sounding and also the most ska sounding. I love the rollercoaster “oh oh oh” vocals, easily the catchiest part of the album. It’s just a lot of fun and, as the shortest track, knows how to not wear out its welcome. “Egg” starts out so well but becomes one of the first real downturns on the album. The first four minutes have such bouncy momentum, particularly in the off-kilter rhythm of the chorus. The track starts to wear thin around the four-minute mark and only gets worse from there. This is where *Mr. Bungle* completely loses perspective compositionally and descends into pure chaos. I hate the “There’s no place like home” section that makes me want to kill myself with every false end. The field recording reminds me of *Jackass* but just serves to lengthen one of the lowest momentum sections of the album. The first four minutes save it from being my least favorite track though.

“Stubb (A Dub)” is fine. The more melodic vocal sections remind me a lot of my one listen to *California*, which also in turn reminds me a lot of Poppy’s nu metal revival albums like *I Disagree*. There is very much a world where I list “Stubb (A Dub)” as a favorite track. Unfortunately though, the record lost my trust on matters of compositional whiplash with “Egg” and this track darts around too much too soon. “My Ass Is on Fire” is one of the more straightforwardly heavy tracks with its dominating riffs, screamed vocals, and tremolo guitar. The combination of the conventional doing with the continued disjointed structure just doesn’t do it for me. Add to that the finish two minutes where Patton screams “Redundant, redundant, redundant” until my ears bleed, this is my least favorite track. “The Girls of Porn” brings us right back to top-tier quality. In addition to being as good as the first four tracks in every way, the laidback funk grove was sorely needed to help the album find a center again after all the chaos. I also love the concept of using a style like funk, the stereotypical music backing classic pornos, to explore the dark side of modern porn. Patton tops it all off with a truly inspired vocal performance. What a sleazy, unsettling track, and it all works thanks to that wonderful slap bass. I enjoy the riff that opens “Love Is a Fist”, a decent track buried at the end here. It’s not perfect; I don’t care for Zorn’s solos, nor the horns that dart from ear to ear. It’s definitely my favorite track of the ones not listed as favorite tracks, but it doesn’t clear the elevated bar needed for a track coming an hour into this album. I’m not crazy about the plodding pace nor the stupid voice Patton affects on “Dead Goon”. The lyrics are funny enough, but it’s unfortunate for this album to go out on such a scattered note instead of finding a way to bring it all together at the end.

As an album with a tough-to-crack presentation with real gems buried within, this reminds me a lot of Midori and Faust. Therefore, I’m slotting it between the two of them in the lower half of B Tier.

Favorite tracks: Quote Unquote, Slowly Growing Deaf, Squeeze Me Macaroni, Carousel, The Girls of Porn

Least favorite track: My Ass Is on Fire

# Aidan's review

In my first foray into this list during COVID, I listened to their third album, California, which came out 8 years after this one, and rather liked it. A lot of the same stuff is present in this one, the wild shifting of genres and the affinity for catchy horns. But it is also abundantly clear that California was made by serious musicians in their early 30s and this was the product of a bunch of guys in their early 20s. By far the worst offender is "Squeeze Me Macaroni" which the previous track transitions to with overlaid samples of a outtake of a KFC ad and someone taking a loud shit. The song itself is a fairly catchy with funk influences but consists of the crudest sexual puns with food imaginable. It's just not for me. "The Girls of Porn" is second on my shit list but it's not quite as bad. It's infuriating because the band has a knack for cool stylistic mashups and catchy melodies. Their songs that aren't quite as crude like "Quote Unquote" and "Egg" are totally fine. They also have picked up the habit of including lengthy unfunny samples. Really was hoping to like this record and there's stuff to like, but the lyrics on several songs annoy me to no end.
