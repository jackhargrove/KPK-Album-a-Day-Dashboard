# Write-up

Murmuüre is a the name of a metal project of a French man who goes only by Felix. Self-taught, Felix learned to play the guitar by playing along with Metallica's "Seek and Destroy". He started out playing in a punk band until, while touring in Germany, he suffered hearing damage, which drove him to electronic music. When he got bored with that, he started Murmuüre as a way to reconnect with the louder music of his youth. The project's name comes from "Ars Goeita", a 17th century work on mysticism. Among the lists of demons in that book was one called "Murmuur". Felix added an "e" to be unique and an umlaut because it was a metal project. Murmuüre features melodies from several films, including The Wicker Man and L'Adieu Au Soleil. Felix enjoys David Bowie and Coil, and does not plan to release any more music tied to Murmuüre.

# Hargrove's review

It’s become a cliché in my reviews at this point, but it is genuinely remarkable that this deep into the project, there are still records like this one that sound unlike anything I’ve ever heard before. Upon first listen, I hardly recognized *Murmuüre* as metal; sure, there were distorted guitars, but the primary instrument was the synth, the drums played slowly, and the overall thrust of the music was clearly ambient. The more I listened, the more I came to understand this record’s place as a black metal album. It conjures the same cold, desolate, abandoned landscapes of artists like Paysage d’Hiver or Burzum or Ulver. And yet, it does so with a completely different approach. I enjoyed the album’s commitment to obscurity, creating is own mystique with the foreignness of the song titles and the epic movie score samples. As an ambient record, I found myself enjoying it more than average. It’s a genre where I’ve had trouble finding albums I truly love, and, though I wouldn’t say I love this album, the suppressed black metal edge certainly made it more amenable to my tastes. On the other hand, I didn’t like how hard it was for me to distinguish between the tracks. All of the music kind of blends together, obscuring any feeling of change or forward progress. At under 30 minutes, *Murmuüre* is an incredibly short album, which helps with the lack of sonic diversity. Still, it would have been nice to have more fully formed tracks. But as a soundscape, I enjoyed listening to this and entering its bizarre little world. It’s like a cursed artifact from some kind of damned alternate dimension.

I like “Primo vere” as an opener. I’m a big fan of the distant horn sound, which I believe is the sample taken from *The Wicker Man*. It’s like a bugle signaling to a far away group. I love the moment when the drums come in, as well as the complex drum beat that continues throughout the track. The distorted guitars create a constant white noise that builds the atmosphere. I’m a big fan of the static electricity textures on “Reincarnate” created by the guitar clipping in and out. The 6/8 time signature works really well with this sound, hypnotic and tumbling. And the main riff is very good. The little electronic horns in “Torch Bearer” remind me of a movie but I can’t place it. It’s not one of the films listed as a sample source. Anyway, I really like that sound, but this track is short and doesn’t do much else to distinguish itself. I found myself generally enjoying “Amethyst” in my earlier listens, but for whatever reason I was not enthralled during my final listen. I like the wall of sound, the drum beat, and the final minute, but it just didn’t move me. “L’adieu au soleil” is kind of all over the place. It’s just a collage of the noises of the album. The buzzing flies that begin “Disincarnate” capture very well a certain vibe that the album had been giving off. I like the plinking bleeps and bloops set against white noise feedback. It finishes the album on a high note.

Overall, I’m not entirely sure what to make of this record. As it has a distinctive sound which I like but don’t love, I’m sticking it in C Tier next to *Geogaddi* and *Pacific*.

Favorite tracks: Primo vere, Reincarnate, Disincarnate

Least favorite track: L’adieu au soleil

# Aidan's review

An atmospheric black metal album that's basically all transitional sections, this takes a lot of albums I was lukewarm on, and strips out the parts I didn't like. The result is surprisingly cohesive, and rather pleasant. It's also an extremely scattered project here, Felix, the man behind Murmuüre, seems to have just thrown stuff at the wall. But a surprising amount of it sticks, and in a 29 minute runtime, the lack of cohesion doesn't affect it that much. The movie samples are an excellent choice, especially for a genre that I generally think of as cinematic, and the flies before the last track a particularly cool choice. It's a muddled mess, but it's charming enough to earn "creative" rather than "scattershot". Enjoyable, cool, album. It's cooler on paper than it is in practice, but that's just because "cassette released album by some random French guy" sounds extremely cool. Fun album.
