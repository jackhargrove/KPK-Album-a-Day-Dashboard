# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: my bloody valentine (all lowercase, herein referred to as “mbv”) are an Irish band consisting of lead creative and guitarist Kevin Shields, drummer and sampler Colm Ó Císóig, vocalist Bilinda Butcher, and bassist Debbie Googe. The band formed in the mid-1980, and they took their name from a Canadian horror film. Originally a part of the post-punk and indie rock scenes in the UK, they began to develop their own unique sound across their early EPs and their debut studio album *Isn’t Anything* in 1988, which received much critical acclaim.

Hoping to follow the success of their previous album, mbv began recording their sophomore record in February 1989 with funding from their label Creation Records. However, bandleader Kevin Shields had difficulty writing the music and became nearly impossible to work with. The album took 2 and a half years to record, moving between 19 different studios because Shields kept taking issue with each. Shields also took almost complete creative control; bassist Debbie Googe was sidelined from production entirely, drummer Colm Ó Císóig was suffering from a long-term illness so he recorded drum lines to sample instead of playing them live, and Bilinda Butcher was used only for her vocals. On this record, Shields effectively invented an entirely new way of playing guitar he termed “glide guitar”, where he makes extensive use of the whammy bar to create a vibrato sound. With all of the chaos of production, it is rumored the album cost Creation Records $700,000 in 2025 money and nearly bankrupted them. The album, named *loveless*, finally released in November of 1991 to immense critical success but limited commercial success.

Shortly after the release of *loveless*, mbv broke up. Kevin Shields went completely cuckoo bananas, and became a recluse for years. The members went on to play in other well-regarded groups such as Yo La Tengo and Hope Sandoval & the Warm Inventions. mbv reunited in the mid-2000s and finally released a third album in 2013. *loveless* has endured as mbv’s signature record, and the defining record of the shoegaze genre, so named because bands in the shoegaze movement remained motionless while performing on stage and stared at their feet. As typified by *loveless*, shoegaze features heavily distorted instrumentation, extensive use of guitar feedback, ethereal melodies, and loud walls of sound. This list features multiple other shoegaze albums, including Carms’ favorite, *Souvlaki* by Slowdive. There is also a blackgaze album on this list (*Sunbather* by Deafheaven), a genre that is a mixture of shoegaze and black metal.

# Hargrove's review

It’s been a little while since the last time I listened to this (I know for sure I haven’t listened to it since Chappell Roan has been famous), so it was really cool to rediscover some of the sounds in this album. I remember the first time I listened to it, on the drive to San Mateo for my aunt’s second wedding in Spring 2017. They just got divorced last year. This album completely washes over you in a nonstop wave of sound, with the omnipresent feedback, the dreamy vocals, and the undulating melodies. It’s a complete dreamscape, with motifs entering and leaving at will, sometimes midway through a song, sometimes in multiple songs. This is another example of an album with a great cover that pairs with its music; I can hear the pink and the black, and of course the guitar that barely looks/sounds like a guitar. However, I must admit I did find myself liking this a little bit less than I remembered. In particular, the undulating melodies in a few songs got a little old for me. It’s also not a particularly long album, but I do feel like it could be shortened by 10 minutes or so. Still a great record, and I very much still enjoyed it, but maybe my mood was off today.

One of the highlights of *loveless* for me is that it has the perfect opener and the perfect closer. The opener, “Only Shallow”, only gives you a few drum beats before you’re hit with the wall of sound that won’t dissipate for nearly 50 minutes. It’s almost a mission statement for the album, and one of the most direct songs. I never really thought of this track as harsh until Aidan said so today; I can see it, but I just found the use of the feedback so enjoyable and hypnotizing that it never even occurred to me. And the closer, “Soon”, takes every experiment and exploration from the rest of the album and packages it into the perfect pop song. The melody is catchy, and the guitar is just the right balance of present but not obnoxious. Two elite songs, and some elite album sequencing.

Other highlights of the album for me include: “Loomer” which has this really nice rumble effect coming from the feedback that gives it an earthier feel than any other song. “To Here Knows When”, which has a recurring sampler keyboard line that is flashy, glittery, and overall really enjoyable, and some of my favorite vocals. “When You Sleep”, which has my favorite example of the undulating melody, especially because it cycles downward, as well as a very nice chord structure during the verses. “Sometimes”, which is a masterclass in building tension; the guitar is restrained, but it combines with the sampler keyboard at the end to really build, and has a nice vocal melody.

My least favorite track is probably “Touched”, it’s so short and not very pleasant, seems like a completely half-baked idea. I also didn’t love “I Only Said” today despite having liked it in the past. Something about the recurring melody just rubbed me the wrong way and I got pretty tired of it. “Blown a Wish” sounds nice, but I wouldn’t have minded if it were left on the cutting room floor.

While I’ve enjoyed it more on previous listens, I did still really enjoy today’s listens, and I think this record is very deserving of its stature. Despite my complaints, there is something evocative about listening to all the ideas here, even if they are somewhat scattered. It’s also just pleasant, and there was never really a time where I wished I was listening to something else. It was worthwhile revisiting, and I’m excited for my next revisit already.

Favorite tracks: Only Shallow, Loomer, To Here Knows When, When You Sleep, Sometimes, Soon

Least favorite track: Touched
