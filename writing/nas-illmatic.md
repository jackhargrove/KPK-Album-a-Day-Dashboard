# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Nasir bin Olu Dara Jones, better known by his stage name Nas, is a rapper from New York City. Nas was raised by his musician father and his postal worker mother in the Queensbridge housing project (government subsidized low-income housing) in the Long Island City neighborhood of Queens. Nas dropped out of school after finishing 8th grade, and he was taken in by the Five-Percent Nation, an offshoot of the Nation of Islam that had a massive presence in New York at the time and was very influential on the development of hip hop. Nas began rapping as a teenager in the late 1980s, first under the name Kid Wave and later under the name Nasty Nas. He was signed by Columbia Records in 1992 (at age 18) and leased his first single, “Halftime”, which would later appear on *Illmatic*. Also in 1992, MC Search took Nas under his wing and began introducing him to other NY hip hop artists. From 1992-1994, Nas became very well-known in New York hip hop circles, often compared to legendary rapper Rakim, but he still hadn’t released a debut album.

When Nas announced that he would be releasing his debut album, those in the know immediately set expectations very high; prominent hip hop magazine The Source called Nas “the second coming” in an interview before the album’s release. Despite these sky-high expectations, *Illmatic* still managed to shatter every last one.*Illmatic* became probably the most critically acclaimed album in hip hop history up to that point. Nas’ relaxed delivery and highly complex rhyme schemes were a significant departure from popular rappers prior, who would often follow a basic AABB rhyme schemes and shout as they rapped. Nas’ lyrical themes of poverty, drugs, and violence, but also of artistic integrity were also lauded, as were the simple but meditative and sample-heavy beats he rapped over. The Source gave *Illmatic* 5 microphones (a perfect score) despite their strict policy of never giving a debt album a perfect score. In spite of this, *Illmatic* was initially a commercial failure; it peaked at number 12 on the Billboard 200. Eventually, however, it was certified gold by 1996 and platinum in 2001. Nas’ second album,*It Was Written…* received strong reviews as well, but his subsequent albums were received worse and worse as time went on. He was involved in a highly publicized beef with Jay-Z in the early 2000s, and he’s been having a career renaissance with his King’s Disease album trilogy in the 2020s, but he would never again reach the heights of *Illmatic*. To be fair, almost no one else ever did either.

# Hargrove's review

I kind of hate reviewing records that I’ve heard a trillion times, and this is one of those. It’s pretty obviously a fantastic record (sorry Carms). Especially with how much of a naked contrarian I am, it kills me to praise this album to the degree that I’m about to. And I’m about to. This might be the cleanest debut album ever, of any genre. And that’s especially impressive for rap. Many rap debuts are littered with stupid ass songs the artist has been collecting for years, and, especially for this era, replete with retarded skits. Not *Illmatic*. This album is fucking sleek. Not only are there no skits, there isn’t a filler track on it. Nas is one of the greatest lyricists of all time, both in the sense of the atmosphere his words create, and in the sense of how his flow and rhymes contribute to the rhythm. These tracks are fucking *smooth*. Nas sounds cooler than cool no matter what he does. He’s relaxed, he’s chilling, he’s spitting bar after fucking barn without reprieve. And the beats are immaculate. I love boom bap; it’s simple, it’s direct, and, more than anything else, it’s direct. The beats actually elevate Nas’ raps above a level I thought was conceivable. His rhymes are meditative, and the repetitive piano beats provide a clean, nocturnal canvas for him to jizz his art onto our faces. Fuck.

“N.Y. State of Mind” is one of the finest examples of the hip hop genre ever released. The treble intro is syncopated and iconic. The bass piano line is relentless and sleek. Nas sounds confident, mature beyond the his 20 years at the time of recording. In control of his destiny. “I don’t sleep, ‘cause sleep is the cousin of death”. I’ve said that a million times. Other artists would kill for a track like this once in their career, and this is the opening song on Nas’ debut album. I don’t think I’ve ever heard a flow match a beat so well. “Life’s a Bitch” is a banger in an entirely different sense. Probably the smoothest track overall. AZ is jazzy and sounds great. The chorus is bleak in a way only rap knows how to be; life truly is a bitch, and we will die. The bass on the verses is elite. I’ve mentioned it a few times, but the piano sample in “The World Is Yours” is catchy as hell. As is the chorus. The verses just sound so unresolved. I feel like the world truly is mine. It is underrated that I never know when the next rhyme will come; it could come at any point in the line, and it will flow like a motherfucker. “Halftime” sounds like a straight up A Tribe Called Quest song (even more than the track that actually has Tip), and I love that for it. I could see this on *The Low End Theory*. It demonstrates to me how Nas combines the best qualities of mainstream and alternative rap. Also he says “faggot”, and that reminds me of what Carms is

“Memory Lane” kind of blows. Not really. Really. Not really. Really. Not really. Really. It’s alright. Kind of a static track. The vocal sample is fine, I guess. Nas sounds great, but not any better than he does on any other song. Overall it’s good, and I wouldn’t call it filler, but it doesn’t impress me. “One Love”, on the other hand, might be the best on the album. Tip’s production is KILLER. No, I’m not salty that there’s no ATCQ album on the list. That being said: listen to *The Low End Theory*. Listen to *The Low End Theory*. Listen to *The Low End Theory*. The beat is so SMOOTH. It’s AMAZING. SO GOOD. ONE LOVE. “One Time 4 Your Mind” is mellow, and it does its job. Unfortunately kind of forgettable, but the hook is great. “Represent” has a great beat. It’s a nice two bar loop, and it really builds over that interval. Nas also knows exactly how to match his flow to an uncertain beat like this one. Hook is very slightly underwhelming. That being said, the closer “It Ain’t Hard to Tell” *more* than makes up for it. What an unbelievable instrumental. It samples one of my least favorite Michael Jackson songs (“Human Nature”) but it makes me remember it fondly. Alternates from straight boom bap to very flowery and with uncommon instrumentation. Just an incredible track.

An album I really wish I could hate. Do you know how much white boy cred I would get by saying “it’s aight, I’ve heard better”? So much. But it’s tight, it’s nice, it’s smooth, it’s clean. It’s kind of a crazy scenario where Nas was the first ever to rap this relaxed and, 30 years later, no one has ever even come close. He sounds unbelievable. And the beats are just PERFECT. Just a great triumph to point to when I espouse how great minimalism is. FUCK.

Favorite tracks: N.Y. State of Mind, Life’s a Bitch, The World Is Yours, Halftime, One Love, Represent, It Ain’t Hard to Tell

Least favorite track: Memory Lane
