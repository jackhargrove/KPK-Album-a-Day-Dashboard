# Hargrove's review

My dad loooooooooooves Neil Young, so I’ve absorbed a lot of his music through osmosis my entire life. Neil Young played at West Point on his Ragged Glory tour with openers Sonic Youth and Social Distortion when my dad was a cadet there. My dad loved both the openers, but thought Neil Young was boring old guy soft rock and intended to leave the concert when Sonic Youth finished. A friend convinced him to stay, and he’s been in love with Neil Young ever since. I think “My My, Hey Hey (Out of the Blue)” is one of the best songs I’ve ever heard, and “Cinnamon Girl”, “Down By the River”, “Heart of Gold”, “Old Man”, and “The Needle and the Damage Done” were all songs I liked. However, I had never listened to any Neil album other than *Everybody Knows This Is Nowhere* and I didn’t know a single song off of *After the Gold Rush*, which is pretty crazy. I was a little nonplussed by it at first, but with each successive listen (a great thing about these short albums is you can listen so many times) I came to really like it. There are a few songs that are transcendently good and very few that are even marginally bad. It never overstays its welcome. Young’s warble is an acquired taste, but he does a great job of writing songs that suit it well. And speaking of the songwriting, he just has such an amazing way with words. This is a singer-songwriter record that is an inch wide and a mile deep, providing so much to dig into from so little. It is definitely in the upper quartile of folksy singer-songwriter albums we’ve heard.

“Tell Me Why” is just fantastic. It stands up to how good “My My, Hey Hey” is. It’s so pretty. I love the guitar in the chorus as well as the vocals. And I don’t care if the lyrics are “meaningless”, they go together so well and they make me feel something, god damn it. Great, great track. I’m not crazy about “After the Gold Rush”. I think it’s a good song, but it doesn’t resonate with me like the rest of the album does. The lyrics do nothing for me and the instrumentation is so spars. Its best quality is its catchiness. “Only Love Can Break Your Heart”, on the other hand, is awesome. It is a beautiful little waltz with the message that loneliness isn’t causing you pain, it was actually the love. “Southern Man” is a banger and I love hearing that rock n roll after all the folk. The chorus is so intense, so fiery, so furious. “Till the Morning Comes” might be a little tonally inconsistent coming right after, but I don’t know, I like it. It’s a sweet ditty. “Oh, Lonesome Me” is certainly in the lower tier of this album, but that bridge is powerful enough for me to see some value in it. Young’s voice sounds just fantastic on “Don’t Let It Bring You Down”. His warble is perfect for this song. I also love the vivid urban imagery in the lyrics and the intense build into the chorus. “Birds” has a nice chorus, but overall it’s too sentimental and sappy for me. It’s my least favorite track. “When You Dance I Can Really Love” is a late rocker and I think it does its job well. “I Believe in You” is fine, but it drags a little bit as we approach the album’s end. “Cripple Creek Fairy” is fun and pretty but I unfortunately don’t think it sticks the landing. That being said, how do you end this album?

I liked this a lot. I have a lot I need to dig into with Neil. When I get the chance, he’ll be my next discog deep dive. It’s not perfect by any means, but it’s pretty damn good.

Favorite tracks: Tell Me Why, Only Love Can Break Your Heart, Southern Man, Till the Morning Comes, Don’t Let It Bring You Down, When You Dance I Can Really Love

Least favorite track: Birds
