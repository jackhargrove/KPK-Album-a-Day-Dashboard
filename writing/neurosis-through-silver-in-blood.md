# Write-up

Neurosis was a sludge metal band formed in 1985 in Oakland, California, whose lineup on this album consisted of guitarist and vocalist Scott Kelly, guitarist and vocalist Steve Von Till, keyboardist Noah Landis, bassist Dave Edwardson, drummer Jason Roeder, and visual artist Pete Inc. They are one of the major pioneers of the post-metal genre. Kelly, Edwardson, and Roeder formed the original incarnation as a crust punk (metal-influenced anarchist hardcore punk) band in 1985. As their lineup shifted over the next 10 years, they released four albums. 1987’s *Pain of Mind* and 1990’s *The Word as Law* were both unremarkable hardcore punk outings. 1992’s *Souls at Zero*, however, saw the creation of Neurosis’ signature style: atmospheric sludge metal. Introducing new experimentation, longer song structures, expanded instrumentation, and sampling, *Souls at Zero* is now considered the first album to fall under the broad post-metal umbrella. It was mixed by Jello Biafra, hardcore punk legend formerly of Dead Kennedys, and released by his label Alternative Tentacles. 1994’s *Enemy of the Sun* continued down this path.

In 1996, Neurosis released *Through Silver in Blood*, their mainstream breakthrough and most loved album. Citing influence from Black Sabbath and Swans, they turned the slow tempos, heavy atmospheres, and sprawling song structures up to 11. It received great reviews from national press and is today considered among the greatest metal records of all time. It was also their first album to be accompanied by an album from Tribes of Neurot, Neurosis’ ambient side project. They subsequently went on tour with famous groove metal group Pantera. Since then, Neurosis has released six albums, all produced by Steve Albini. Scott Kelly began a frequent collaboration with progressive sludge metal band Mastodon, providing backing vocals in six of their albums. Jason Roeder joined stoner metal band Sleep (whose album *Dopesmoker* we listened to) after their original drummer retired in 2010. Neurosis released their final album in 2016. In 2022, they announced on Facebook that Kelly had been fired in 2019 after discovering he had been domestically abusing his family. The band has been on hiatus since. In 2025, Roeder appeared to have some kind of mental breakdown and left both Neurosis and Sleep.

Sludge metal was developed by hardcore punk bands in the mid-80s. As a genre known for both its blistering speed and desire to piss off its audience, some hardcore bands began to play at extremely low tempos to disappoint fans. Black Flag’s 1984 album *My War* had an entire side devoted to long, slow tracks inspired by Black Sabbath. This style became known as “sludge metal” and became big in Seattle in the late 80s. Seattle sludge group the Melvins were particularly influential on their friend Kurt Cobain, whose band Nirvana merged sludge with the melodic stylings of alternative rock to create grunge. In the early 90s, a huge sludge metal scene cropped up in New Orleans that helped birth southern metal. And while Berkeley’s 924 Gilman Street was fostering a pop punk scene that would produce both Green Day and the Offspring, a sludge metal scene including Neurosis formed concurrently. Journalists coined the term “post-rock” in the mid-90s to describe alternative bands like Tortoise, Bark Psychosis, and Stereolab, who were using rock instruments to make music that defied basic rock conventions and structures. It wasn’t long before journalists wanted to use the term “post-metal” as well. Neurosis’ focus on atmosphere and long songs made them perfect for the title. Industrial metal group Godflesh, whose leader Justin Broadrick would later found Jesu, were also deemed post-metal. Since then, post-metal has grown into a large and diverse umbrella genre, encompassing other atmospheric sludge groups like ISIS, Cascadian black metal groups like Agalloch, and the shoegaze fusion genres of doomgaze (Jesu) and blackgaze (Deafheaven).

# Hargrove's review

Damn this album was awesome! I was a big fan of this one. I’ve been getting a little tired of the sheer number of black metal records in the metal section, so a little sludge metal was exactly what I needed to shake things up. I really enjoy most of the low tempo metal stuff I’ve heard (Black Sabbath, *Dopesmoker*) and it just worked for me again here. Everything sounds so heavy on *Through Silver in Blood*. Not just powerful, or loud, or brutal, even though it was all of those things, but *heavy*. It feels like I’m being crushed under the weight of thousands of pounds. The screamed vocals are more pleasant than the screaming on other records, likely because they typically remain in the mid-range of pitch instead of being annoyingly high or low and muddy. The drums are fantastic, focusing on the toms instead of the cymbals to create a thumping, tribal sound. When the drums do leave the toms, it’s often for clanging metallic sounds that remind me of the drumming on *Filth*. This complements the numerous industrial elements as well, another part of the album I enjoyed. The harmonics and chord structures are consistently cool as well, leaving everything dissonant and unresolved. The guitar riffs are perfectly tailored to the slow, plodding pace of the music. The way that the guitar uses arpeggios reminds me of John Lennon on “I Want You (She’s So Heavy)” or Robert Fripp on “One More Red Nightmare” but even slower and more hulking. There was also an interesting incorporation of ideas from European folk music, like the bagpipes at the end of “Purify” or the Medieval melody that opens “Aeon”. The song structures are executed perfectly, almost never feeling like the songs linger too long even when they stretch past 10 minutes. My two biggest complaints about the record are the lyrics, which read like an alphabet soup of war and apocalypse imagery, and the closing track, which is overall not a bad song but probably the only sonf that would make the album better if it were cut. In the grand scheme of things, these are pretty minor complaints. This was one of the better metal albums we’ve listened to.

“Through Silver in Blood” starts with industrial noises and then slowly layers on tribal drums, a super muscular guitar riff, and an exotic harmonic structure that reminds me of Bowie’s “Blackstar”. It’s just a great, sludgy track that keeps me entranced for 12 whole minutes. “Rehumanize” isn’t the most interesting interlude in the world, but it’s short and not intrusive. “Eye” has some of my favorite guitar work on the whole album, especially the riff that alternates between 4/4 and 3/4. Every song is unsettling and off-balance, but this kicks it up a notch and packs it all into just 5 minutes. “Purify” is the best tack on the album. It has this slow, doomed-out intro that eventually gives way to hulking arpeggios from the guitar, punctuated by waves of noise from the whole ensemble. Once the vocals begin at around 5:00, it enters a new phase, where raining tom tom drums and wailing guitar feedback rule the day. It eventually builds into this orgasmic loud section before dissipating into beautiful bagpipe music in the last two minutes. “Locust Star” is similar to “Eye” in that it packs in much of what makes this album great into a normal song length. It also has some of my favorite percussion. “Strength of Fates” stands out for the majority of its runtime being quiet, but honestly it works pretty well. It feels almost like a folk song for the first seven minutes. It’s a refreshing sound at this point in the album. “Become the Ocean” is another forgettable but inoffensive interlude. “Aeon” starts with the piano playing a Medieval melody in the high register that reminds me a lot of the first section of “Stairway to Heaven”. The first half takes a little too long to develop, but the build in the second half is so rewarding that I don’t even mind. “Enclosure in Flame” isn’t a bad song by any means, but it’s over-reliant on the seven note scale played by the guitar that forms the backbone of the entire piece. It ends up feeling static, especially in comparison to the dynamism of the rest of the album, and adds 10 less-than-stellar minutes to an album that was already on the long side. I think it should have been cut, but it’s also not the end of the world.

I’m sad this is the only sludge metal we have on the list, but at least it’s a good one. I hope to explore more of the genre the next time I get a chance.

Favorite tracks: Through Silver in Blood, Eye, Purify, Locust Star, Strength of Fates, Aeon

Least favorite track: Enclosure in Flame
