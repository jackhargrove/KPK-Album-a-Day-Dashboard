# Write-up

Neutral Milk Hotel was nominally a quartet, but In The Aeroplane Over the Sea is universally seen as the project of the frontman, Jeff Mangum. Mangum started making music in high school with 3 other friends under the imaginary label Elephant 6.  After high school, they ended up making the imaginary label into a real one, with the bands The Olivia Tremor Control, The Apples in Stereo and Mangum's own Neutral Milk Hotel forming the basis for the label. They took after the psychedelic rock of the 60s, with the Beach Boys standing foremost among their inspirations to the point that they named their recording studio Pet Sounds. All the bands overlapped members, producing and backing each others’ albums. Working mostly alone, with Elephant 6 member Robert Schneider producing, Mangum put out the decently well-received record On Avery Island, which gave him the confidence and street cred to work on his sophomore album with a full band backing him.

In The Aeroplane Over The Sea was heavily inspired by The Diary of a Young Girl by Anne Frank, which had a profound emotional effect on Mangum, as well as Lewis Carroll's Through The Looking Glass (Alice in Wonderland for the philistines among us), but isn't a proper concept album. Schneider continued as producer, while the remainder of the band were a motley crew whom Mangum had picked up on tour for On Avery Island. Julian Koster on bass was from NY, and he picked up drummer Jeremy Barnes from Chicago, while Scott Spillane met Mangum while working at a pizza restaurant Mangum ate at on tour. Mangum insisted on lo-fi production against the wishes of Schneider, who wanted something closer to The Beach Boys. Schneider achieved the signature warm sound of the album without any effects pedals, instead simply maxing out each individual instrument on a cassette tape. The resulting album was relatively well received, the most positive reviewer, Pitchfork gave it a 8.7/10. And though it generated enough buzz to get a tour, it didn't really make waves.

The ensuing tour was a sort of disaster, as Mangum behaved chaotically on stage, throwing members across the stage, while the band was so broke they couldn't afford hotels. This, combined with the high expectation after releasing a successful album, overwhelmed Mangum; he had a breakdown, locking himself in his house and preparing for Y2K, shunning publicity. Without its leader, the band broke up. As the internet became a thing, the albums reputation rose from decent to divine as message boards including, /mu/, trumpeted the album as the pinnacle of what music could be. In every other version of the /mu/ list, this album is separated from all the other essentials in the top right corner, with its cover art double the size of all other albums.

# Hargrove's review

For whatever reason, at the very beginning of COVID I rediscovered this album. Senior year of high school and freshman year of college had not been fruitful times for me artistically, I was overly focused on other things (college apps, social stuff, school, you know the things). But in March of 2020, I put this on again within a week of moving back home. And that’s when I had that realization I had above about how I wish I could care about things as much as Jeff Mangum. I listened to it almost obsessively. I contemplated how strange it was to be anything at all.

That, combined with me picking up Film History IV: 1990-Present as a fourth class the pass/fail term, and general COVID free time, made me dedicate myself to trying to care about things as much as possible. I might have been the only person in the world to get less online during the early pandemic. I listened to NMH followed by a productive time of new album discovery (particularly finally completing Death Grips’ discography summer of 2020), I got into foreign film for the first time (with Kieślowski’s *Red* being the inciting film in that medium, so the second most important contributor after ItAOtS), and I watched *The Sopranos* for the first time. I also started creating art for the first (and only) time. My first year seminar required me to write multiple plays, and when I took Film 1 in 20F I had to produce and edit short films. I also got really into cooking for a while.

All of this paid off. I started having really vivid dreams almost every night. They got more and more creative, and I got better at remembering them the next day. I also found my attention span greatly increased. That allowed me to get really into longer and more challenging artists like Godspeed You! Black Emperor and Swans. It also allowed me to get into longer movies; I mention *Sátántangó*, the 7.5 hour Hungarian film, quite frequently as a joke about how douchebag pretentious I am, but I watched in September 2020 as part of this and it came the closest to destroying me afterward of anything I had previously experienced. I was caring about things more.

And then, at the end of 2020 and beginning of 2021, I slowly became pre-occupied. Rush was heating up, and eventually I went back to campus. I started to use Twitter a lot more and developed bad screen time habits once again. I stopped having vivid dreams as frequently. I didn’t have time, or rather: I didn’t *make* time to watch film and listen to music and certainly not create anything like I once did. I think this happens to pretty much everyone at one point or another, and all we can do is give it our best shot.

But for a time, I cared about things to about as great as capacity as I ever will. And that was entirely inspired by *In the Aeroplane Over the Sea*. For a beautiful moment in time, Neutral Milk Hotel made me care about art again.

There’s very little I can say about the music itself that hasn’t been said, so consider that my review.

Favorite tracks: King of Carrot Flowers Pt. 1, King of Carrot Flowers Pts. 2 & 3, In the Aeroplane Over the Sea, Two-Headed Boy, Holland, 1945, Communist Daughter, Oh Comely, Ghost, [untitled], Two-Headed Boy Pt. 2

Least favorite track: The Fool

# Aidan's review

I know that "they couldn't make this anymore" is a popular refrain among those who have some borrowed nostalgia for the unremembered past, but it does truly describe this album. It's incredibly sincere in a way that we've all been inoculated against as a result of thousands of hours of exposure to the internet. No one with "good taste" would make an album about being in love with 14 year old Anne Frank and trying to rescue her in a time machine. The Inner Cop of Burroughs is now an Inner Commentator, allowing the World Wide Web to scorn things it hasn't even seen. The irony is that this album is only known because of that same internet. The collective Inner Commentator has lessened the appeal of the album some (it's down to #35 OAT on RYM) but Aeroplane still holds a place in a lot of hearts of a lot of music fans, including my own. Even among the other Elephant 6 albums with similar production and lyrics, they really don't come close to how warm and sincere this sounds.
