# Hargrove's review

For whatever reason, at the very beginning of COVID I rediscovered this album. Senior year of high school and freshman year of college had not been fruitful times for me artistically, I was overly focused on other things (college apps, social stuff, school, you know the things). But in March of 2020, I put this on again within a week of moving back home. And that’s when I had that realization I had above about how I wish I could care about things as much as Jeff Mangum. I listened to it almost obsessively. I contemplated how strange it was to be anything at all.

That, combined with me picking up Film History IV: 1990-Present as a fourth class the pass/fail term, and general COVID free time, made me dedicate myself to trying to care about things as much as possible. I might have been the only person in the world to get less online during the early pandemic. I listened to NMH followed by a productive time of new album discovery (particularly finally completing Death Grips’ discography summer of 2020), I got into foreign film for the first time (with Kieślowski’s *Red* being the inciting film in that medium, so the second most important contributor after ItAOtS), and I watched *The Sopranos* for the first time. I also started creating art for the first (and only) time. My first year seminar required me to write multiple plays, and when I took Film 1 in 20F I had to produce and edit short films. I also got really into cooking for a while.

All of this paid off. I started having really vivid dreams almost every night. They got more and more creative, and I got better at remembering them the next day. I also found my attention span greatly increased. That allowed me to get really into longer and more challenging artists like Godspeed You! Black Emperor and Swans. It also allowed me to get into longer movies; I mention *Sátántangó*, the 7.5 hour Hungarian film, quite frequently as a joke about how douchebag pretentious I am, but I watched in September 2020 as part of this and it came the closest to destroying me afterward of anything I had previously experienced. I was caring about things more.

And then, at the end of 2020 and beginning of 2021, I slowly became pre-occupied. Rush was heating up, and eventually I went back to campus. I started to use Twitter a lot more and developed bad screen time habits once again. I stopped having vivid dreams as frequently. I didn’t have time, or rather: I didn’t *make* time to watch film and listen to music and certainly not create anything like I once did. I think this happens to pretty much everyone at one point or another, and all we can do is give it our best shot.

But for a time, I cared about things to about as great as capacity as I ever will. And that was entirely inspired by *In the Aeroplane Over the Sea*. For a beautiful moment in time, Neutral Milk Hotel made me care about art again.

There’s very little I can say about the music itself that hasn’t been said, so consider that my review.
