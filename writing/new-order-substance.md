# Write-up

Picking up where we left off yesterday: Ian Curtis killed himself in May 1980, shortly before the release of Joy Division’s second (and now final) album, *Closer*. The remaining members of the band (guitarist and keyboardist Bernard Sumner, bassist Peter Hook, and drummer Stephen Morris) decided to continue making music together, but no longer wished to continue using the Joy Division name out of respect for Curtis. Instead, the reformed band chose the name New Order. Sumner assumed singing duties, but the group still felt like something was missing. In late 1980, they added Morris’ girlfriend, guitarist and keyboardist Gillian Gilbert, and their original lineup was set. New Order released their debut single, “Ceremony”/“In a Lonely Place” (both intended to be Joy Division songs), and their debut album, *Movement*, in 1981. These continued the dark and guitar-driven post-punk sound of Joy Division, but used synthesizers and electronic drums to a surprising degree. Later that year, New Order visited New York City, where they all got really into Italian disco and other forms of electronic and dance music. They released the single “Everything’s Gone Green”/“Temptation” in late 1981, beginning a major shift away from rock music to music that was electronic and designed for dancing (man I wish there was a name for that). In 1983, New Order released their second album *Power, Corruption and Lies* which is on this list. This album made heavy use of synthesizers, and helped define the signature synthpop sound of the 80s. Immediately after, they released the single “Blue Monday”, a major dance hit that would become the best-selling 12” single of all time. New Order would continue to release club banger after club banger (most notably “Bizzare Love Triangle”) for the rest of the 80s, helping teach the punk kids it was okay to like dance pop. New Order is still together today, albeit without bassist Peter Hook, and they’re doing a world tour this year.

*Substance* is a compilation released in 1987 that consists of all of New Order’s singles released up to that point. Legend has it that the owner of their record label, Tony Wilson, had just bought a car with a CD player and decided he wanted to have all of their hits on CD. It was released in 3 versions: a 12 track double LP with (almost) all of their A-sides, a 24 track double CD with all of their A sides followed by all of their B sides, and a 29 track double cassette with all of the above and more. For whatever reason, the songs “Temptation” and “Confusion” were re-recorded for the compilation instead of using the original single versions.

New Order has proved extremely influential on the development of dance music as a genre. They popularized a signature mechanical drum sound that they took from the German krautrock bands of the early 70s and particularly from German early synthpop group Kraftwerk. New Order was highly influential on the synthpop sound of 80s popular music. They also were a major influence on house music, which developed in Chicago in the mid-to-late 80s and was the start of EDM. Finally, New Order inspired many future underground and punk-descended groups to put down the guitars and pick up synthesizers.

# Hargrove's review

WOW I was surprised by how much I enjoyed this compilation. Really, genuinely blown away. My dad loves New Order so I’ve had a general familiarity with them for a long time, but really the only songs I could have named before this were “Blue Monday” and “Bizarre Love Triangle”, although to be fair the latter has been one of my favorite songs since forever. I was not prepared for how much this album would just be banger after banger after banger after banger though. I respect “Ceremony”, but I think it and “Everything Gone Green” are kind of weak intros, but starting with “Temptation” it’s just 10 tracks of nothing but straight fucking heat. This compilation is like if you took a stereotypical “sound of the 80s” and distilled it into only the parts that I like. The drum programming is CUCKOO BANANAS, just a syncopated sonic assault on my ear drums with clinical precision. Peter Hook’s bass is even better than it was with Joy Division, bouncing around nonstop. The layering of sound is really well-done, just fantastic production that always sounds cold but full. The hooks and melodies are so catchy that they’ve been competing with each other to see which can stay in my head the longest over the past few days. The synth work is brilliant and icy, blending in with the rest of the band as if it were a real instrument. Really my only major gripe is that there isn’t the structure, sequencing, cohesion, and diversity you get from a planned-out studio album. I guess I also wish the lyrics were a little more interesting but like, it’s dance music, come on. Really dug this.

“Ceremony” is a nice post-punk track, and RIP Ian Curtis, but it’s just not what I’m looking for from a New Order song. I’ll be fair though, the guitar work is very pretty, and the drums have cool effects. “Everything Gone Green” also doesn’t get me moving, and it doesn’t even have the qualities I like about “Ceremony”, so it’s definitely my least favorite track. We start cooking with gas though on “Temptation” and we do not look back. It fades in, and immediately we have some catchy vocals and a jangling, disco guitar. The drums are fairly conventional rhythmically, but that sound is so loud and crisp. The chorus is one of my favorites, and this is the first song where I think Sumner comes into his own as a vocalist. “Blue Monday” is “Blue Monday”, it’s a smash hit and that status is deserved. The drum machine is just driving in an almost militaristic fashion. The synth bass line is so bouncy that even with headphones I feel like my body is about to be vibrated out of my bed. The higher synths are so varied and diverse, giving so much character. Hook’s bass is noodling randomly for some reason, and there’s an explosion. The vocals drone on dispassionately for one of my favorite vocal performances in a dance song. “Confusion” continues with the stellar drums, and now the bass synth is so in tune with the drums that they sound like a combined maximalist rhythmic attack. The synth melody is brilliant, and I love the chorus. Off-kilter, funky, and fresh. “Thieves Like Us” is the closest thing to a downturn, but I really do enjoy the melodrama of the gliding synth and the dissonant descending synth. I think it also incorporates the post-punk sound of their past in an interesting way. It sounds like a rainy street at night in the big city.

“The Perfect Kiss” is absolutely my favorite track on this compilation. Love it, love it, love it, love it, love it. There’s a stretch at the end where they keep layering more and more noise as the melody continues to climb, and every time it’s played it grabs my attention completely. Positively mesmerizing. The techno synths in the main part of the song are almost glitchy, the vocals are expressive while remaining cold, and the drums are vibrant in a way they haven’t been before. “Sub-culture” is ANOTHER great song, the bouncy bass synth is superb. The chorus is one of the best as well, they climb to a much higher note than I expect. “Shellshock” has an extended glitchy intro, but once the drum machines kick in it becomes one of the grooviest tracks yet. I really like the use of stereo in this one, with how much is going on already it makes it even more overwhelming. “State of the Nation” brings back guitar in a major way, with some serious disco going on. The chorus is also well done. “Bizarre Love Triangle” was already a personal favorite of mine, and it remains so. That the same song can have two melodies so good as the primary synth melody and the vocals in the chorus is astounding. The bass is also phenomenal here. “True Faith” works surprisingly well as a closer. I think it’s a very well structured and paced song, and I really enjoy the descending vocals on the chorus.

Maybe I was just a little starved for a rhythmic dance pop album, but I had so much fun listening to this. Many songs are getting worked into my rotation.

Favorite tracks: Temptation, Blue Monday, Confusion, The Perfect Kiss, Sub-culture, Shellshock, Bizarre Love Triangle, True Faith

Least favorite track: Everything Gone Green

# Aidan's review

This album was a frustrating listen, and I’m grading on a curve because I really love a lot of songs by New Order. On one hand, there’s some amazing hits on here, Ceremony, Blue Monday, Temptation are all amazing. New Order was never really an album band, and by reducing their work to singles, you get a better idea of what they are capable of. On the other hand, even having made roughly half of their great songs by now, there’s a decent number of songs that just aren’t up to snuff on here. I cannot fathom why Age of Consent didn’t make it onto this album, and Confusion and Thieves Like Us drag a bit. New Order always has relied on being catchy enough to cover up their typically pedestrian lyrics, and they fall flat at times on the album. I’m also attached to the original versions of songs like Bizarre Love Triangle and I don’t think it’s served well by adding a 2 minute dance break. The great songs are extraordinary, but the entire concept of a singles compilation already takes away from the purpose of listening straight through and the inconstancy of the album just adds to that. It’s a pretty good album, but I don’t feel any affection for it as an album qua album, and when I want to listen to New Order, I’m just going to queue up the singles I like. This album is just someone doing that for me and doing a worse job.
