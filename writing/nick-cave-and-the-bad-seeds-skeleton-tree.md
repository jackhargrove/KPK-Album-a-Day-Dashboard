# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

I have never been able to get into Nick Cave, and unfortunately eight listens to *Skeleton Tree* this week (and one listen to *Let Love In*) didn’t help one iota. I had actually listened to this album before only a few years after it came out. It was after my grandma died in 2019, a year in which I found much solace in the track “Real Death” from the Mount Eerie album *A Crow Looked at Me*. I had read that legendary alternative artist Nick Cave had released a similar album with *Skeleton Tree* in 2016 and I gave it a listen. It was not for me at all. I’ve also always kind of found “Red Right Hand” annoying. I don’t know, man. Cave’s gravelly, emphatic voice just doesn’t work for me at all. Though I like Leonard Cohen, I almost always consider “sounds like Leonard Cohen” to be a negative trait in a singer: Nick Cave, Tom Waits, Bill Callahan, etc. It’s just so difficult to make that kind of voice not sound hokey or forced. To make matters worse for *Skeleton Tree*, Cave pairs his vocals with a unique, distinctive atmosphere in the instrumental. I get precious little out of this atmosphere. It’s too minimal, too austere, too cold. I dislike the vocals and the instrumentals separately, and yet together they’re even less than the sum of their parts. And while there are a few decent one-liners on this album, I found the lyrics scattered and unfocused overall. I wanted Cave to pick a lane between metaphor and direct expression of grief, but instead he was all over the place. But what really made this album brutal for me to listen to was just how bad I felt about disliking it! It feels awful to listen to a man bare his soul and be completely unmoved. I dreaded pressing play on this album more than I have for any album in quite a while. I enjoyed “Girl in Amber” and “Anthrocene”; otherwise, I don’t care for this record at all.

“Jesus Alone” contains two very good lines in “You believe in God/But you get no special dispensation for this belief now” and “You’re a distant memory in the mind of your creator. Outside of that, I found the lyrics an impossible nut to crack and, frankly, not rewarding to decipher. The instrumental’s empty and the vocal melody emptier. It’s not a great start. “Ring of Saturn” has “I thought slavery had been abolished/How come it's gone and reared its ugly head again?”, but I like the track as a whole even less than the opener. The little oh oh oh oh oh oh in the background just sounds silly. It’s my second least favorite on the album after “Distant Sky”. “Girl in Amber” is, by far, the best execution of this album’s ideas. I like how much less gravitas Cave displays in his vocals. He sounds so desperate, especially when his vocals outpace the instrumental to the point of becoming off-beat. I also appreciate the powerful swells in the string for the final section, and the line “I used to think that when you died you kind of wandered the world/In a slumber till you crumbled, were absorbed into the earth/Well, I don't think that any more” destroys me. The title of “Magneto” drives me crazy. Why is it named after the X-Men guy? More importantly, though I like the concept of taking a downbeat folk song and backing it with nonstop tape sounds, I just feel nothing while listening to it. “Anthrocene” works for me because of the frenetic instrumentation. I like the piano chords that don’t seem to follow any sense of time or rhythm. They’re impressionistic, and the atmosphere is apocalyptic. There’s an almost free jazz quality to the chaos of the instrumental. “I Need You” certainly has an impassioned vocal performance from Cave, but it sounds awful to me. He sounds creaky and out of breath. I strongly dislike “Distant Sky”. The ping-ponging back and forth between Cave’s growl and the female soprano gives me whiplash. I find neither pleasant to listen to. And the instrumental is soooooooooo sloooooooooow. “Skeleton Tree” isn’t the greatest song in the world, but it’s better than much of what precedes. The piano sounds pretty and the chords are nice. The line “I call out, I call out/Right across the sea/But the echo comes back empty” stands out to me. Still, my feelings toward it are more tolerance than enjoyment.

I was hoping to avoid this result, but *Skeleton Tree* goes straight to D Tier. Low D Tier, too. Maybe one day I’ll see what other people see in Nick Cave. Today is not that day. I even found my one listen to *Let Love In* grating.

Favorite tracks: Girl in Amber, Anthrocene

Least favorite track: Distant Sky
