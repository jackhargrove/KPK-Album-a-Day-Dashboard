# Write-up

Nick Drake was born in Burma (Myanmar if you're woke) in 1948 to two British parents and moved to England, where he would live the rest of his life, when he was 3. He was introduced to music at a young age, being taught piano, clarinet and saxophone by his mother and forming his first band at the age of 16. When he got into Cambridge, he decided to spend the 10 months between acceptance and attendance abroad. He first went to France, then Morocco. During his time abroad, he began playing guitar and smoking marajuana, developing a heavy habit that would be with him his whole life. When he got to Cambridge, he had lost his passion for sports, and preferred to spend his time alone smoking weed. During his time at Cambridge, he was known by his peers as bright but unmotivated, and he quickly found himself less interested in his major of English literature than the folk music that was developing in Britain and across the pond. While playing in local clubs, Drake met Robert Kirby and Joe Boyd, who would become his two biggest collaborators. Boyd, whose own small label was owned by major label Island Records (who would go on to sign King Crimson, Roxy Music and Sparks), managed to secure a record contract for Drake. His first album, Five Leaves Left, was recorded while skipping lectures at Cambridge. Boyd produced, and after disagreements with Richard Anthony Hewson (who worked with Herbie Hancock and the Beatles), Kirby was brought in to arrange the album. The recording process was difficult and delayed, and when the album was released, it was promoted poorly, sold poorly and had middling reviews.

Before recoding his second album, Drake dropped out of Cambridge with 9 months left on his degree, against the wishes of his father. Around that same time, he was prescribed antidepressants, which, combined with his marajuana habit, caused him to become "a "hunched, disheveled, figure, staring latently". He started playing in public less and less, and lived off only his contract stipend of £20 week ($415 today).  His second album, Bryter Layter featured a jazzier sound and contributions from VU album John Cale, but was met with the same response as the first, poor sales and middling reviews. Drake refused to promote the album at all, and the lack of success made him turn even more inward, a condition that was made even worse when Boyd moved to the US.

Drake's final album, Pink Moon, was produced by John Wood, who had engineered Bryter Layter and without the knowledge of Island Records. Recording sessions took only two days, and featured only Drake on vocals and guitar, minus the title track, which features his piano playing overdubbed. He dropped the album off with the receptionist at Island Records after awkwardly standing in their office for half an hour. Drake viewed it as his most personal album, and when it also failed to sell, he grew depressed, angry and bitter. He moved back in with his parents, and even began to struggle to play up to his own standards while working on his fourth album. Island stopped paying his meager income, and in November of 1974, he committed suicide at age 27.

He remained obscure in the years immediately following his death, with only a box set being released in 1979. Yet several artists began citing him as an influence, from Kate Bush and Robert Smith to REM. He got his first biography written by a Danish author in 1986, and by the end of the decade, he was seen as a minor notable figure in folk music, viewed as a modern day romantic hero. His music hit the mainstream when Volkswagen used Pink Moon in a commercial, which combined with the newfound ability to share his music on Napster, gave him popularity of the kind he had never known in his lifetime. Fast forward to the present day, Drake is a well recognized folk musician, being featured in The Royal Tennenbaums and Garden State, and earning his own tribute album featuring Liz Phair, Feist and Radiohead's drummer.

# Hargrove's review

This is a very good folk album. I think I got really into Nick Drake around Thanksgiving 2018, and the data shows that the last time I listened to his music was 4 or 5 listens of this album in March and April of 2020. It’s really remarkable how effective it is at establishing an atmosphere and sustaining it over its entire run time. This runs the risk of getting stale, but *Pink Moon* avoids that fate by keeping itself to a tight 28 minutes, as well as through the strength of its songwriting. Nick Drake sounds like an alien. I had never one the proper research until today, and I always assumed he had some unique accent because of his time spent in Myanmar as a young child. Turns out, he had a very posh Received Pronunciation British accent, and he just sounds completely unique. It’s unbelievable to me that he was only 24 (my age btw) because he sounds so mature. He’s so stoic as he sings about such sad emotions. Drake’s guitar work is the perfect complement to his voice: perfectly simple, hauntingly beautiful, creates an additional texture without taking away from the stark minimalism or use of space. This album does so many things well, but one of its strengths is also its biggest issue, that being the lyrics. I think the lyrics on this are quite good; they strike a great balance between the opaque and the direct, mixing genuinely felt emotions with intelligent metaphors. However, for this style of music, I want the lyrics to resonate with me just a little bit more. In this particular style, I find myself much more affected by Elliott Smith or Phoebe Bridgers. Still, as I’m writing this I’m listening to “Parasite”, and there’s something about that almost-howl in the chorus that’s so heartbreaking. So perhaps as I continue to age, it will resonate with me more. For now though, I con only peer into Drake’s profoundly depressing life from the outside.

“Pink Moon” is simply one of the greatest songs ever written. The vocal performance is just incredible and that melody sticks with you for hours and hours and hours. Then lyrics are so simple but so vague, they beckon you in for analysis but good luck getting further than anyone else. The piano after the little bridge section is perfectly restrained. What a song. “Place To Be” continues the great instrumental and atmospheric work, but with more direct lyrics which are some of the most affecting on the album. Another banger. “Road” is fine but nothing exciting. The song’s structure is so basic and the lyrics don’t excite me much at all. Worse, the off-kilter rhythm in the guitar line between the verses messes up what has been an  immaculate flow through the album to this point. The song has many redeeming qualities, but it’s the worst on side 1. “Which Will” is a great song with a totally depressive vibe (shocker, I know). This song is the first that really demonstrates how resigned to life Drake is. It also has a notably strong melody. “Horn” is a fine interlude but nothing worth writing home about. “Things Behind The Sun” is perhaps my favorite on the album. The Belle and Sebastian comparison is not an original one, but it really does sound like “Expectations”. The song has its verses in a minor key and its chorus in a major key, which is a very noticeable and enjoyable effect. The lyrics are an insightful statement on the mind of an ignored artist. “Know” is my least favorite track. The guitar line is too choppy and repetitive and the song itself is underdeveloped. “Parasite”, however, is a full return to form. This song is dripping with self-loathing and intelligent imagery. I love the main guitar line, and the chorus is very well-delivered. “Free Ride” has weak verses but a strong chorus. The lyrics veer a little bit into “too cynical” category, but I’d be lying if I said they weren’t at least a little bit relatable. I’m conflicted on this song, so it misses out on “Favorite tracks” status. “Harvest Breed” is tense and ominous, but much like “Know” it’s underdeveloped. “From The Morning” is the perfect closer. It really reminds me of “Say Yes”, the closer to Elliott Smith’s brilliant *Either/Or*. Both are upbeat and optimistic endings to crushingly depressing folk albums. Both are achingly pretty.

This album is such an oddity. It’s as weird and depressing as the story of Nick Drake’s life. I think there are certainly better folk albums out there, but this deserves to be a staple of the genre. If nothing else, it shows just how much depth can be expressed by a man and a guitar.

Favorite tracks: Pink Moon, Place To Be, Which Will, Things Behind The Sun, Parasite, From The Morning

Least favorite track: Know

# Aidan's review

Nice, solid folk album. The sparse instrumentation, combined with the melancholy lyrics and Drake’s tragic life story makes this album feel intensely personal. Still, it rarely really stood out to me. I wish the sparseness of the album didn’t bleed into the lyrics as much as it did, because when Drake writes more elaborate lines like on Parasite and Things Behind the Sun, it works great. But as it is, the guitar outshines the vocals, creating an album that coasts by on an excellent aura while not doing anything particularly compelling. As good as the guitar is, it’s also pretty receptive, the individual melodies are incredibly impressive but when there’s only one or two per song, combined with the often lackluster lyrics, I too often found myself apathetic towards the individual songs. I grew less and less favorable on this album with repeated listens, its simplicity may be beautiful, but it lacks anything to really dig into. The general sound is good enough that I would enjoy any song played individually, but as an album it’s too one note.
