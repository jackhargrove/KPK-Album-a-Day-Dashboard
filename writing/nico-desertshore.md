# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

228 albums in and this list still occasionally introduces me to music that sounds like nothing else I’ve ever heard. Of Nico’s three tracks on *The Velvet Underground & Nico*, the creepy, trance-like, ritualistic “All Tomorrow’s Parties” is easily my favorite. On *Desertshore*, Nico (and the Velvet Underground’s John Cale) isolate exactly what made that song so unsettling and expand it to album length. They correctly identify that Nico’s austere, cold, and unmistakably European vocals combine better with Medieval classical music than they do American rock or folk. In so doing, they tapped into a feeling of ancient horror that can only emanate from the forests of the Brothers Grimm, similar to the fairy tale terror of Norwegian mountains in that Ulver record we listened to. This album is fucking scary, man. The harmonium drones create this thick, unrelenting atmosphere that’s more sopping wet comforter than warm blanket. I was not kidding when I said it freaked me out to listen to it while I walked around at night! The album only lets up twice, first with the thinner a cappella of “My Only Child” and later with the melancholia of “Afraid”. The former is my least favorite track, while the latter shines individually despite how out of place it feels on the album. Otherwise, the album keeps its monolithic nature throughout. I was skeptical going in that a folk album could truly have inspired the swirling post-punk atmospheres of gothic rock, but no longer. This was a cool, alien listen. It’s not one that will enter my heavy rotation, but certainly one that I will continue to think about.

*Desertshore* throws you into the deep end immediately with “Janitor of Lunacy”, a terrifying entryway into the album’s sound. Nico’s songwriting shines here, providing one of the stronger melodic lines. The performances are great as well, both Nico’s severe vocal performance and Cale’s tidal wave of vibrato drones. “The Falconers” is my favorite track on the album. The instrumentation feels more dynamic and impressionistic than the droning opener, flitting around at the drop of a hat. Though the piano section near the end creates some respite, this is otherwise the album’s scariest track. “My Only Child” is undeniably creepy, and I like the chorus, but I sincerely miss the thickness of the instrumentation, resulting in it being my least favorite track. On the other hand, the voice of a child in “La Petit Chevalier” notches up the scary factor enough on its own to make this short interlude one of my favorite tracks. What can I say, I’m a sucker for French. Next, we have somehow the second track on this list to be titled “Abschied”. Cale’s viola steals the show here, creating a nice counterpoint to Nico’s vocals. I also appreciate the introduction of German lyrics. “Afraid” contains the best lyrics on the album, particularly the line “You are beautiful, and you are alone“. It also has a relatively conventional song structure and an atmosphere more melancholic than haunting. It’s a very good song so I’m putting it on my favorites, but I do unfortunately think it disrupts the flow of the album. “Mütterlein” brings back the horror movie atmosphere in spades; those trumpets at the beginning sound like they herald the entrance to Hell. It’s the best track since “The Falconer”. “All That Is My Own” ends the album on an as yet unexplored Medieval feeling, that of the festival or fair(e). However, I don’t get the sense that we’re reveling in something positive. It’s a good track, but I find the abrupt fade out ending unsatisfying.

This album is cool enough to justify the top half of B Tier, but I don’t see myself returning to it enough to justify A Tier or higher. I’m very happy to have added this album to my sonic lexicon, though.

Favorite tracks: Janitor of Lunacy, The Falconer, Le Petit Chevalier, Afraid, Mütterlein

Least favorite track: My Only Child
