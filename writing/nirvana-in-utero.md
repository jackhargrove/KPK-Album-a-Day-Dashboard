# Write-up

Nirvana was an alternative rock/“grunge” band formed in 1987 in Aberdeen, Washington which consisted of guitarist and vocalist Kurt Cobain, bassist Krist Novoselic, and drummer Dave Grohl. One of the best-selling musical acts of all time, it was also among the most culturally relevant, popularizing the alt rock that would define the 90s with their breakthrough in 1991. After the punk movement of the 70s fizzled out and gave way to new wave and synthpop, the guitar rock that had defined the previous two decades was forced underground. New subgenres formed, united by their descent from punk rock, DIY ethos, and lack of success. This included hardcore punk, thrash and extreme metal, noise rock, and jangle pop. The more melodic, poppy proponents of these genres found an audience on college radio by the mid-80s, earning the moniker “college rock” or “alternative rock”. In the small towns of western Washington, the style of choice was sludge metal, a fusion of the slow tempos and heaviness of doom metal with the aggression of hardcore punk. While the sound had been introduced by California band Black Flag, Washington band the Melvins led the local scene. Cobain and Novoselic met in 1983 as high schoolers watching the Melvins practice. They started a band in 1987 that became Nirvana in 1988 with Chad Channing on drums. Nirvana signed to Seattle indie label Sub Pop, whose owner popularized the term “grunge” to market its signees, and released debut album *Bleach* in 1989. Though it’s quite sludgy, songs like “About a Girl” revealed Cobain’s pop sensibility. *Bleach* was a modest success, selling 40,000 copies and getting college radio play (my dad, West Point’s DJ at the time, was sent a copy). Channing left in 1990 and was replaced by DC-based drummer Dave Grohl. Inspired by Sonic Youth, Nirvana signed to a major label in 1990. Their second album, 1991’s *Nevermind*, featured cleaner production and a quiet-loud dynamic taken from the Pixies. The album smashed expectations, beating Michael Jackson for the #1 spot on the Billboard 200 as lead single “Smells Like Teen Spirit” hit #6 on the Hot 100. The music industry plundered Seattle to make stars of local bands like Pearl Jam, Soundgarden, and Alice in Chains, turning grunge into a global phenomenon. More broadly, Nirvana’s success opened the floodgates for underground bands, unleashing alt rock on the mainstream.

Cobain did not take success well. Nirvana began to self-sabotage, debuting “Rape Me” in 1992. They enlisted Chicago punk legend Steve Albini to produce their third album with a raw sound like he had the Pixies’ *Surfer Rosa*. It was recorded live in Minnesota. A friend of Albini’s played cello on “Dumb” and “All Apologies”. The label was unhappy with the result, but Cobain stood by it. Ultimately, they got R.E.M.’s producer to remix the singles “Heart-Shaped Box” and “All Apologies” for a cleaner sound but otherwise released the album with Albini’s production. Despite Cobain’s best efforts, 1993’s *In Utero* was another smash success, debuting at #1 on the Billboard 200 and going 6x platinum. It received positive but mixed reviews at the time. In retrospect, many consider it Nirvana’s best work.

Cursed with fame he did not want, a heroin addiction, and a tempestuous relationship with his wife Courtney Love, Cobain began experiencing mental and physical health issues. On April 5, 1994, he shot himself with a shotgun and died. He was 27. In a cruel twist of fate, Cobain’s suicide only made Nirvana more popular, if that was possible. But out of respect, Novoselic and Grohl ended the band in 1994. Grohl went on to form successful band the Foo Fighters. Nirvana was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2014 (coincidentally the year I visited), playing its first show in 20 years with artists including St. Vincent and Lorde on vocals. Today, Nirvana remains one of the most popular bands in the world, introducing teenagers everywhere to alt rock.

# Hargrove's review

I get a little protective over Nirvana because I think they get put in a bad spot sometimes. Unlike the Beatles, Michael Jackson, Madonna, etc. (their true peers), they have to bear the cross of “alternative rock”, meta-commentary beyond the music as to the moral standing of popularity. No one on earth who like those other artists feels shame at their success, nor does any potential fan feel put off by alleged inaccessibility. Nirvana, by contrast, will never get adequate credit from underground hipster purists who can’t shake the feeling they “sold out” or at least didn’t deserve their success, while they look less and less inviting to new fans of catchy music as the context of 90s “alternative” in the mainstream gets ever smaller in the rear-view mirror. As a fan of both sublime pop and alternative aesthetics, Nirvana had a seminal effect on me as a child that carries through today (NOTE: I am by no means trying to imply that I am niche for liking Nirvana. It’s just that “music snob annoyed by Nirvana’s existence” and “pop/rock fan who thinks Nirvana is whiny” are both types of guys I personally interact with 100x more than “16 year old who buys Nirvana shirt at Target”). As I’ve mentioned previously, *Nevermind* and *MTV Unplugged in New York* were both extremely important records for me as a 12-year-old. I I eventually moved past them and hadn’t listened to either in a great long while. I did, however, fall in love with *In Utero* in 2024. It’s just a really stellar album. I feel like the general view for people in my set is that there’s a tradeoff: *Nevermind* has the better songs but *In Utero* the cooler aesthetic. I don’t know that that’s true. “Serve the Servants”, “Heart-Shaped Box”, “Dumb”, “Pennyroyal Tea”, and “All Apologies” are as good of pop bangers as Nirvana ever wrote. And with each of these being supplemented by raging punk tracks, *In Utero* is just a joy of an album to listen to. As cringe as Foo Fighters can be, it’s easy to forget how much of a goddamn great drummer Dave Grohl is. So propulsive, so bouncy, none of these songs would sound the same with a different drummer. And then there’s Kurt’s delivery, impassioned and tortured over some of the most blistering guitar lines he ever wrote. I really like this album. It’s easily my favorite from Nirvana. And I think anyone who gets a kind of instinctive “cringe” feeling about Nirvana should swallow their pride and really give *In Utero* a chance.

The album starts with a banger in “Serve the Servants”. It’s been said, but “Teenage angst has paid off well/Now I'm bored and old” is truly a funny line to start this particular album with. I love the aimless wildness of the guitar riff, the generally resonant lyrics, and the super catchy chorus. It’s everything you want in a Nirvana song. “Scentless Apprentice” debuts the second kind of track you’ll find on this album, the harsh loud screamers that contrast with the pop bangers. It’s one of the stronger tracks of this second type, particularly the guitar leading into the chorus screams and Grohl’s drumming. It’s not one I come back to much on its own, but it’s important for the album’s character. “Heart-Shaped Box” is just a phenomenal, phenomenal pop song. It does *Nevermind* better than any track on *Nevermind*. It’s unbelievable how well the verses build into the choruses, and how catchy both are. I just adore the main guitar line and the drums. It’s maybe the best use of quiet/loud ever in alt rock, Pixies included. And the lines are great too: “Hey, wait, I got a new complaint/Forever in debt to your priceless advice” works great as a chorus, and “Throw down your umbilical noose so I can climb right back” always stood out to me. It’s hard for me to put into words how good this track is. “Rape Me” is an interesting one. Its use in *Succession* is one of my all-time favorite TV needle drops, making it hard for me to separate the song out on its own. It’s intriguing how similar the guitar sounds to “Smells Like Teen Spirit”, as well as the incongruity between the edginess of the lyrics and the catchiness of the song. It’s such a simple song, but it really sticks in your head. I really like the verses of “Frances Farmer Will Have Her Revenge on Seattle”, but the chorus is just okay. Still, it’s catchy enough to be a favorite of mine.

“Dumb” is gorgeous. I love the sparse guitar sounds that underscore the verses, the angelic cello, the catchiness of the vocal melody, the darkly sweet sentiment, and the overall softness that gives a break from the rest of the album. Great, great track. I don’t think about “Very Ape” much but honestly I’ve really been liking it this week. It’s high energy and catchy. My favorite part of “Milk It” is the way Grohl jams on 4 during the verses. I also love the little chuckle before Kurt’s wail at 3:15. Otherwise, it’s only okay. “Pennyroyal Tea” is yet another banger. Very few albums have so many bangers that they can be as evenly spaced throughout the album as they are here. “I have very bad posture” cracks me up every time in the short first verse before the song breaks into this really grand, anthemic chorus. I appreciate the shoutout in “Give me a Leonard Cohen afterworld/So I can sigh eternally” as well. One of Cobain’s greatest qualities was his extensive music knowledge and good taste. My least favorite track is “Radio Friendly Unit Shifter”. It’s forgettable and goes on for nearly five minutes. “tourette’s” is a great character song for the album. I love how insane Kurt sounds when he yells “hey”. It’s a fun track before the tender ending. “All Apologies” is potentially my favorite Nirvana song. It’s full of great lines, from “What else could I say?/Everyone is gay” to “What else could I write?/I don't have the right”. “All in all is all we are” is one of history’s great discography enders. I adore the swirling, circular guitar riff that breaks into the cathartic chorus. The cello is used to perfection once again, while Cobain gives his strongest vocal performance.

This is just an album where it’s hard for me to find many flaws. Nirvana is no longer one of my very favorite artists of all-time, but I’ll always have soft-spot for Cobain and company, and this is definitely my favorite work of theirs.

Favorite tracks: Serve the Servants, Heart-Shaped Box, Rape Me, Frances Farmer Will Have Her Revenge on Seattle, Dumb, Very Ape, Pennyroyal Tea, All Apologies

Least favorite track: Radio Friendly Unit Shifter
