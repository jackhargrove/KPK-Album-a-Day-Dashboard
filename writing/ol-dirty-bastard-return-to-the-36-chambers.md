# Write-up

Ol’ Dirty Bastard (real name Russell Jones, often referred to as ODB) was a rapper born in 1968 and raised in Brooklyn. He was one of the original three members of the Staten Island-based hip hop group Wu-Tang Clan, along with his cousins RZA and GZA. The three began making music together in the late 80s before adding six more members to form Wu-Tang Clan in 1992. He took his rap name from an old Chinese Kung Fu movie titled *Ol’ Dirty and the Bastard*. After their 1993 debut album *Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers)* released to great success and a revitalization of East Coast hip hop after years of West Coast dominance, the members all set out to make solo albums with RZA’s production. The first of these was Method Man’s *Tical* in 1994, which hit #4 on the Billboard 200 chart but doesn’t get much recognition in retrospect. The second was ODB’s *Return to the 36 Chambers: The Dirty Version* in 1995, followed the same year by Raekwon’s *Only Built 4 Cuban Linx…* (a fan favorite that I’m surprised is not on this list) and GZA’s *Liquid Swords* (on this list).

ODB was a complex man who in his personal life did some good things and did some bad things. He was a lifelong drug addict who had a real soft spot for crack. He did multiple stints in prison after earning fame and riches, for offenses including assault, robbery, and criminal threatening. He was once charged with attempted murder, though the charges were dropped; that being said, FBI files released after his death revealed that they suspected him of being involved in multiple gang murders. He also escaped from a rehab facility in 2000 and was a fugitive from the law for over a month. He was diagnosed with schizophrenia in 2003, which likely contributed to his worsening behavior. At the same time, he once saved a 4-year-old girl from the wreckage of a car crash and secretly visited her in the hospital as she recovered. As time went on, his drug use, criminal lifestyle, and weight gain weighed on his health. He was much less involved on Wu-Tang’s second album *Wu-Tang Forever* in 1997 than he was on their debut. He released his second solo album *[REDACTED] Please* in 1999 with reduced RZA involvement, with the slack being picked up by a young Pharrell Williams. This album was not well-received or overall successful, though the single “Got Your Money” was a major hit. Though his life was in shambles by then, he recorded a third album *A Son Unique* in 2003; it was never released. He died in late 2004 at age 35 as a result of mixing cocaine with opioids. He is to date the only member of Wu-Tang Clan on their 1993 debut to have died.

# Hargrove's review

I’ve always said ODB is my favorite member of Wu-Tang Clan, but admittedly I’m not a huge Wu-Tang fan, nor had I listened to any of the solo albums (or any Wu-Tang related album other than their debut) until we listened to Ghostface Killah’s *Supreme Clientele* a few weeks ago. Really, I just liked how distinctive ODB sounded compared to the more uniform hardcore rap style of the others on *Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers)*, as well as the his raunchy comedy. Well, *Return to the 36 Chambers: The Dirty Version* has both of those things in spades. It’s a really fun album. ODB’s flow is completely unpredictable, not only shifting from song to song and verse to verse but sometimes within individual bars. When I got really into BROCKHAMPTON as a teenager, Merlyn was my favorite member because he also had this quality, but I can see now that he must have been really influenced by ODB. He sounds like he has no idea how he’s going to say something until he says it, and yet it always works out. It’s jazz rap conceptually, though not at all sonically. Lyrically, this album is full of memorable quotables, hilarious scenarios, and complete non-sequiturs. ODB makes the zaniness of both his flow and his lyrics work with his complete commitment to every bit. He has great acting skills, putting them to use by bringing the perfect level of intensity to every delivery. RZA’s production is decent. There are a few tracks where the production stands out, but for the most part it just serves as a backdrop to ODB’s deranged rapping. The features are spare and tasteful, a refreshing quality for a 90s Wu-Tang record. By far this album’s biggest problem is its lack of catchy hooks. You have “Shimmy Shimmy Ya”, but otherwise there aren’t many lines I come away singing to myself. It’s also a little too long (even on the LP version) and the quality drops off significantly toward the end. Still, this is by far my favorite thing I’ve heard from Wu-Tang. It’s just a good time.

“Intro” might be a little too long to kick off an album, but fuck, it’s a funny and well-acted skit. You don’t get a lot of those in hip hop. The progression of “Yeah, I love the girl but I had to cut the bitch off/Yeah the bitch died, I killed the bitch” cracks me up, while “But the pussy was good, yeah the pussy was good, oh shit, the pussy's good” through tears is hysterical. “Shimmy Shimmy Ya” is a total banger. It’s simple, but so so fun. The minimalist piano beat provides just the right amount of motion for ODB to go crazy on. “Baby C’mon” is the perfect filler track, conveying ODB’s charisma despite lackluster songwriting. I’m more than okay with that though because it’s so early in the record. “Brooklyn Zoo” doesn’t move me as much as it seems to move others. ODB does go crazy on the verse, but the shouted hook is meh. It’s just not as fun as the rest of the album. “Hippa to da Hoppa” combines outrageously scatological bars (“Rhymes come stinky like a girl’s poo poo”? Really?) with one of the smoothest, chillest beats I’ve ever heard. ODB gives an insane second verse on “Raw Hide”, and Method Man kills it as well. “You bound to catch AIDS or somethin’/Not sayin' I got it, but [REDACTED], if I got it, you got it, what?” is an all-timer from ODB. On “Damage”, ODB and GZA do something I’ve never really heard before: instead of trading bars back and forth, they kind of merge into one two-headed rapper. I like it if only for the ambition, not to mention the hilarious“ Try to Jew me for a rhyme and I change to Hitler/Go out like a Nazi”.

“Don’t U Know’ is another genuinely outrageous track. ODB captivates me completely as a storyteller, even if the story involves him getting head in Sex Ed. The final verse is batshit: “My knees buckled, heart startin' to drop/My dick grew to a size that my nerves couldn't stop” and “Oh, I begged, I begged/‘Easy on my balls, they're fragile as eggs’” could only come from a genuinely twisted mind. It’s like horrorcore but really, really stupid. “The Stomp” is fine, nothing great nothing bad. “Goin’ Down” is a complete clusterfuck (complimentary) between the unique opening, the realistic argument, and the random “Somewhere Over the Rainbow”. Truly, no one did it like Dirty. “Drunk Game (Sweet Sugar Pie)” is a real miss. We did not need a 4 minute soul track here. On “Snakes”, I find it really funny how they’re doing a hardcore posse cut and then ODB just comes in singing Jim Croce. Otherwise, it’s a decent but forgettable track. The concept of “Brooklyn Zoo II (Tiger Crane)” is interesting and ambitious: ODB comes in with lyrics from earlier on the album but with an entirely different flow, then it splices together samples from the rest of the album, then there’s a live part. I don’t think this experiment was successful, but I do respect it. “Proteck Ya Neck II the Zoo” is a fine if slightly dull posse cut. I do like “Cuttin’ Headz” quite a bit even if it’s kind of a weak closer. It’s a nice lo-fi back and forth between ODB and RZA. It’s infectious and fun.

This album has its flaws, but ODB’s personality and charisma just shine through no matter what. I wish there were more individual tracks that I wanted to save or add to playlists, but I can’t deny that this was an enjoyable album experience.

Favorite tracks: Shimmy Shimmy Ya, Baby C’mon, Hippa to da Hoppa, Raw Hide, Damage, Don’t U Know, Goin’ Down, Cuttin’ Headz

Least favorite track: Drunk Game (Sweet Sugar Pie)
