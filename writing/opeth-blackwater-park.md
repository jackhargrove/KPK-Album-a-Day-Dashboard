# Write-up

Opeth is a progressive metal band formed in Stockholm, Sweden, in 1990 which, at the time of this album, consisted of vocalist, guitarist, and songwriter Mikael Åkerfeldt, guitarist Peter Lindgren, bassist Martín Méndez, and drummer Martin Lopez. Opeth was founded by David Isberg as a death metal band, the style of metal most popular in Sweden at the time. The name “Opeth” comes from “Opet”, a fictional city in the novel*The Sunbird*. One day, Isberg asked Åkerfeldt to replace the band’s bassist, a move that caused the other members to all leave. The band cycled through various members, with Peter Lindgren being the third to stick. In 1992, Isberg left the band he founded, leaving Åkerfeldt as frontman. Under Åkerfeldt’s direction, Opeth moved away from the aggressive hallmarks of death metal like blast beats, working in more delicate elements like acoustic guitars and harmony. These innovations made them a part of the progressive metal genre, which took the approach of prog rock and applied it to metal. Opeth released its debut studio album, *Orchid*, in 1995 to positive reviews but not much attention. *Morningrise* followed in 1996. The resultant tour earned them a record deal for US distribution in 1997. That same year, Lopez and Méndez (both Uruguay natives who lived in Sweden) joined the band, solidifying the lineup found on today’s album. Opeth released its third album, *My Arms, Your Hearse*, in 1998. The album takes its name from a line in the Comus song “Drip Drip”. It was Opeth’s biggest budget album to date and received significant acclaim. *Still Life* followed in 1999 (though it was not released in the US until 2001), garnering further positive reviews. The band was not given much time to write nor rehearse for that album, an environment the members enjoyed.

In 2000, Opeth returned to the studio despite having no new material in the hopes they could recreate the environment of their previous album. These sessions became Opeth’s fifth and most loved album, *Blackwater Park*, released in 2001. The album takes its name from a German prog band that Åkerfeldt was a fan of. Opeth’s previous releases caught the attention of Porcupine Tree frontman Steven Wilson. Porcupine Tree is a prog rock band from the 90s that helped spearhead a prog revival. Wilson agreed to serve as producer for *Blackwater Park*. Though it did not chart, the album helped popularize Opeth in the US, selling nearly 100,000 copies. “The Drapery Falls” and “Still Day Beneath the Sun” were released as singles. Critically, the album received universal acclaim, even from outlets that are not metal-focused. Many likened it to a metal version of King Crimson or Pink Floyd.

After touring for *Blackwater Park*, Åkerfeldt had the idea for Opeth to record two albums simultaneously, one heavy and one mellow. The heavy album, *Deliverance*, released in 2002, while the mellow album, *Damnation*, released in 2003. Both were successful, with the latter marking Opeth’s first appearance on the Billboard 200. Opeth moved to a larger label in 2005 and released its eighth album, *Ghost Reveries*. Considered another masterpiece, this album debuted at 64 on the Billboard 200, signaling a break into the mainstream for Opeth. Lopez left the band in 2006 after a series of anxiety attacks while on tour. Lindgren followed in 2007, disliking Opeth’s fame. Opeth continued to grow more successful; 2008’s *Watershed* hit number 23 on the Billboard 200, while the band hit its peak at number 19 with 2011’s *Heritage*. *Heritage* also found the band leaving metal behind for a pure prog rock direction and was also their first record for some time to receive only mildly positive reviews. 2014’s *Pale Communion* matched the peak of *Heritage* at number 19 on the Billboard 200 but received even more tepid reviews. Since then, Opeth has released three more albums, the most recent coming in 2024. As of 2026, Opeth is still going, with Åkerfeldt and Méndez both still members.

# Hargrove's review

I had never heard of this band, put apparently my dad is a fan of Opeth’s earlier stuff from the mid-90s and he was playing it a lot around the time I was born. I was a bit apprehensive about the progressive metal label given my issues with so many prog rock albums and artists, but I was hopeful that the metal element would inject the edge that I find lacking from a Pink Floyd. Instead, this record certainly displays many of the hallmarks I dislike from mainstream prog rock: smooth production, wanky instrumental sections, predictable structural shifts, and contradictory soaring pop choruses. And yet…I actually liked this record a good bit? Maybe it was in fact the death metal edge that made it work for me, but in all honesty my favorite tracks (“Bleak”, “Harvest”) are some of the least edgy. Don’t get me wrong, Åkerfeldt has a fantastic death growl and I enjoyed a lot of the heavier sections, but man, I’m a sucker for those early 2000s, melancholic to the point of near-cheesiness clean emo vocals. I very much got the sense that this was a Hot Topic band (though I could be wrong on that), but it was in the best way possible. The album merges the cold, gloomy atmosphere of Scandinavian metal with tastefully tight 10-minute compositions and an emo pop aesthetic sensibility. Though the later tracks fall into heavier, sharper hard rock riffs that undermine the delicate balance, the first five tracks truly kick ass. Quiet-loud-quiet may be overdone, but I really loved the way the tracks traded acoustic guitar and electric guitar back and forth. Similarly, the growled vocal/clean vocal split worked for me here as well as it did on Agalloch’s *The Mantle*. This album actually reminded me of *The Mantle* in a lot of ways, sharing the same similarities that progressive rock has with post-rock. I may be over-indexing on genre, but Agalloch’s post-black metal and Opeth’s progressive death metal each extract the best elements of their respective genre fusions, predictably landing on comparable ground. If I have any complaint that applies to the album in its entirety, it’s that the lyrics were only okay. They were pretty generic Scandinavian metal murder and domination and suicide stuff. Thankfully, Åkerfeldt’s voice inserted the grim emotionality in a way the words themselves couldn’t. Overall, this was a solid listening experience that had me adding numerous tracks to my playlists.

The first note on “The Leper Affinity” comes in fucking LOUD. No matter what my AirPods were set to, I had to turn them down every time the guitars came in. I liked that a lot. I find the blast beats in this album complement the grandiosity of the music. This opener sounds more identifiably death metal than the others, lingering on the heavier stuff before giving way to the progressive inclinations. For that reason, I think it was a good choice of opener. Once the soft section begins, I loved the pretty singing and the chords that shift from evil to sorrowful. In addition to being gorgeous, the piano at the end leads in perfectly to “Bleak”. I’m a big fan of this track’s slower drum groove, as well as the fantastic main guitar riff. The verses sound so evil and yet so catchy, sticking in my mind even as I listen to the other tracks. Steven Wilson’s vocals on the chorus work for me. They’re even more obnoxiously emo, and it’s just a great melody. It’s a strong track from beginning to end, structured fantastically with a great contrast between heavy and soft and a few truly beautiful moments. Then begins “Harvest”, my favorite track on the album. I love the use of acoustic guitar. It walks the line of becoming cheesy but the melodies are just too good for it to fall flat. This applies to the verses, certainly, but it applies tenfold to the chorus, where the melody falls apart in an intentional avoidance of an explosive climax. Starting with the second chorus, the swirling, almost psychedelic instrumental elements kick it up a notch further. “Mourners lament, but it’s me who’s the martyr” was one of the few lyrics to stick out to me on this album. “The Drapery Falls” begins with an orgasmic shoegaze riff, kind of like the guitar is wailing. It fills up the aural space so nicely. Even when that drops away, the guitar makes ominous and threatening wails in the distance behind the acoustic verses. I love the great guitar breakdown with three bars of 5/4 followed by a bar of 6/4, right around the same time as the hulking growl section. The final instrumental section leaves me beyond satisfied when the shoegaze guitar returns.

I was hesitant about the opening acoustic section on “Dirge for November” on my first few listens because the vocals do cross the line into being a bit hokey. However, that doesn’t go on too long and afterward there’s a beautiful acoustic guitar section, and, of course, the face-melting electric section. The heaviness that occupies the second half of this track is one of my favorite sections on the whole album, just pounding and noisy and relentless. I’m not a fan of “The Funeral Portrait” at all. For whatever reason, it loses that indelible metal quality for me and becomes just Rush-style progressive hard rock. The main guitar riff screams old frat party to me instead of desolate Scandinavian wilderness. “Patterns in the Ivy” is a nice, if insubstantial, acoustic instrumental cut. It’s good for helping me get back into the atmosphere after the last track. It also reminds me of some video game music I’ve heard for some reason, perhaps “Midna’s Lament” from *The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess*. “Blackwater Park” is the track that everyone seems to really love from this album, but unfortunately I don’t really get it. It’s fina and all, but it takes so long to find firm footing. The overall outline of the lyrics seems like it should be interesting, but none of the verses really do anything for me. And like “The Funeral Portrait”, the riffs are more heavy than they are evil, prioritizing power over malevolence.

It falls off a bit in its final third, but still there’s a lot to love on this album. It’s not consistent enough to make it into my absolute top tier of metal albums, but it’s not terribly far behind.

Favorite tracks: The Leper Affinity, Bleak, Harvest, The Drapery Falls, Dirge for November

Least favorite track: The Funeral Portrait
