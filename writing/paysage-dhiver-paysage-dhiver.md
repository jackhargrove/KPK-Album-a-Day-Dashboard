# Write-up

Paysage d’Hiver is the solo project of Wintherr (real name Tobias Möckl), an ambient black metal artist from a city near Bern, Switzerland. “Paysage d’Hiver” is French for “Winter Landscape”, though Wintherr is from the German-speaking part of Switzerland. He began this solo project in 1997 and has released 13 albums since, with the most recent coming out in 2024. Every album tells the continuing story of a lone wanderer trapped in the frigid realm of Paysage d’Hiver. The album covers all depict the lone wanderer in various landscapes. Wintherr cites Varg Vikernes’ project Burzum as his primary inspiration, particularly in the use of lo-fi production to create a cold and menacing atmosphere. Today’s album is Paysage d’Hiver’s fifth and usually considered by fans as the best, though recent releases have consistently received praise. The titles of the three tracks translate from German to “World of Ice”, “Frozen Breath”, and “The Way” in order. It was originally released as only 200 cassette tapes, but was subsequently made available online. The website that used to host all of Paysage d’Hiver’s releases describes this album as “reflect[ing] this world (Paysage d'hiver) and the most fundamental path that leads through it.” There is just really not that much information out there on this guy. On Spotify, he added this self description: “Paysage d’Hiver slowely [sic] came into existence somewhere in the ‘90s, with its first release in 1998. It represents a very personal diary, a soul journey if you will. Timeless, limitless.” Under the name Wroth, Wintherr also fronts Swiss black metal group Darkspace. Of the 15 metal albums we’ve done so far, 5 have been described as “atmospheric black metal”, so clearly it is a well-liked genre among online metalheads.

# Hargrove's review

Now that there are fewer records ahead of us that there are behind, my brain is naturally starting to ask what each new record brings to the table that another one hasn’t already. Thankfully, I’m still finding new things to enjoy in just about every listen. But for genres like jazz and metal where my prior experience is limited, things can start to feel a little same-y. This is especially true for “atmospheric black metal”, a genre that 4-5 records we’ve listened to fall under. My opinions on those records range from “decent” to “very very good”, so I’m really not complaining. Still, Paysage d’Hiver is very open about the significant influence he took from Burzum (particularly *Filosofem*), and that influence can be found in just about every element of this album’s sound. Most glaringly, there’s the over-the-top lo-fi production, blending everything into a staticky soup. Paysage d’Hiver also takes his guitar riff style from Burzum, playing minimalist, dissonant sequences of 3-6 notes again and again and again. Wintherr even gets his atmosphere from the freezing cold textures and soundscapes but together by Burzum’s Vikernes. I am not in any way accusing Wintherr of plagiarism, as this kind of influence is the lifeblood of all types of music. Rather, I want to compare this record directly to *Filosofem* to judge which I would want to listen to when I’m in the mood for frigid ambient black metal, and also to highlight the new ideas that Wintherr introduces. *Filosofem* excels with its riffs, many of which feel timeless and randomly get caught in my head. I also have to give Burzum the points for atmosphere, as even though *Paysage d’Hiver* literally depicts a “Winter Landscape”, there’s something inherent to Burzum’s chords and riffs that just feels colder. As for the lo-fi production, it’s a wash; I think both artists use it to great effect. So, what sets *Paysage d’Hiver* apart? I really enjoyed the instrumental solos that Wintherr put deep in the mix as the album progresses. If you focus really hard, you’ll hear lonely violins, desolate trumpets, and icy piano lines buried in the slush of production. I find these really beautiful, both in the texture they add as well as how they complement the atmosphere. I also found the use of drums interesting; they’re produced so poorly that it removes their percussive quality, allowing them to blend in with the rest of the ambience. I’m not a huge fan of the way this album is structured, however. The songs are too long to create memorable personalities for themselves, but too short to be an epic a la “Dopesmoker” or “Flood”. That being said, there are some segments that are just beautiful. the last three minutes or so of “Gefrorener Atem” comes to mind.

It’s quite difficult to distinguish between the tracks, but I’m going to try. This structure also ensures that I’ll prefer the earlier tracks to the later tracks, which is unfortunate but just the way it is. “Welt aus Eis” is the obvious pick for best track just because it introduces the sound. Highlights for me include both the violin and trumpet solos in the first half, the guitar arpeggios of the sparse section near the middle, and the transition to the violin around the 13:15 mark. It’s a good track, one that I think kind of conveys all of the album’s strengths in just a third of its runtime. “Gefrorener Atem” substitutes the double kick drum beat of the other two tracks for something more militaristic and driving. The guitar gets worse compared to the opener though, just trading two sustained chords back and forth for 12 minutes. However, the last few minutes are just gorgeous. They’re so meditative in a way that none of the other atmospheric black metal we’ve heard has been. “Der Weg” suffers from coming last, and is the least memorable track to me. I don’t love the aggressive opening after the beauty at the end of the previous track. On the other hand, I like the introduction of the piano, and the way the track slowly dissolves and dissipates the longer it goes on.

Overall, there have been much better atmospheric black metal albums, and there have been worse. It’s pretty cool, definitely worse than *Filosofem* but probably better than Ulver.

Favorite tracks: Welt aus Eis

Least favorite track: Der Weg
