# Hargrove's review

Pere Ubu is a band I’ve been kind of curious about for years thanks to LCD Soundsystem’s “Losing My Edge”. It’s one of the bands in the long list near the end, but it stands out both because of the unusual name and the fact that it’s mentioned twice. I guess it didn’t pique my interest enough for me to listen on my own, though. They just had kind of an intimidating, important but obscure vibe. Upon my first two listens to this album, I really disliked it. I’ve noticed that a lot of the really early, art school punk stuff has this 50s revivalism I just do not care for. I hear it in Patti Smith, I hear it in Suicide, I even hear it in the Ramones, and I do not like it each time. It’s certainly present in Pere Ubu, as exemplified by the sing-along chorus of “Non-Alignment Pact”. As I kept listening, a few songs did grow on me, and I’m now at peace with the album. It’s very similar to suicide for me in that I both understand and appreciate the intentions of the sound, but that I get very little out of half the actual tracks. *The Modern Dance* is a little worse than *Suicide* too because it has tracks that are outright annoying instead of just forgettable, and also because it doesn’t have anything as unique as “Frankie Teardrop”. Still, I found things I liked throughout the record, particularly in “The Modern Dance”, “Laughing”, “The Real World”, and “Humor Me”. I like when it leans into more of a British post-punk sound, particularly in the bass lines. The experimental flutes and synths are great too. I also like when they balance the avant-garde stuff and the basic rock stuff. I find David Thomas a grating vocalist with little charisma, but he’s actually used well on a few of those tracks I listed. Overall, I’m glad I was able to find some stuff I liked, even if the record has kinda bad vibes.

While I love the bass guitar on “Non-Alignment Pact”, I cannot stand the song itself. I don’t like the chorus and I don’t like the vocals. “The Modern Dance” is much better. The repeated “merdre, merdre” line gets stuck in my head something fierce, and I love how foreboding the slow sections sound when juxtaposed with the vocal sections. I can’t believe I’m saying this, but I also like the dolphin inflection in Thomas’ voice. “Laughing” is pretty good too. I like the weird flute things a lot, particularly the way they clash with each other. Then it moves seamlessly into a rocker, demonstrating what Pere Ubu can offer at its best. “Street Waves” has a great swinging ride cymbal that helps make it a decent but not stellar track overall. I absolutely hate “Chinese Radiation”, which has two distinct sections that both suck. What a needless track. I don’t care for “Life Stinks” either. Thomas sounds obnoxious without even sounding original; he just rips off Damo Suzuki on disc 2 of *Tago Mago*. I like the industrial cadence of Thomas’ vocals on “Real World”, as well as the cool use of synth. The fake laugh after “Cindy makes a bad joke” sticks with me. I like the dark and soft vibe of “Over My Head”, but the song doesn’t do much for me overall. At least it’s not outwardly annoying. “Sentimental Journey” is an interesting atmospheric piece, and the shattering bottles are certainly jarring. I have some respect for it but it’s not for me. The album ends on its strongest track with “Humor Me”. Thomas’ vocals sound great here, as do the guitars. “Oh it’s a joke/Well then humor me” is a fantastic line, especially as a mission statement to end on.

This was a very, very meh album. With early punk, it often feels like “influential” is inversely correlated with “good”. There are some true gems, but they tend not to be the pillars of the genre.

Favorite tracks: The Modern Dance, Laughing, The Real World, Humor Me

Least favorite track: Chinese Radiation
