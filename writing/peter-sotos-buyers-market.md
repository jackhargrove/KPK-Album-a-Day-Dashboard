# Hargrove's review

Along with the Whitehouse album, which also features the signature contributions of one Peter Sotos, Aidan and I have been absolutely dreading this record for well over a year now. And let me tell you, it is NOT a fun listen. Like I do with every album, I got through five full listens, five full hours of real-life human misery, sadness, and despair. On the one hand, it wasn’t quite as fucked up as I had built it up to be; outside of 3-4 clips (mostly in the opening track “Children”), there was very little here that couldn’t or wouldn’t appear on NBC’s *Dateline* or a true crime podcast. On the other hand, I absolutely fucking hate NBC’s *Dateline* and true crime podcasts. I have never understood the entertainment element of the worst parts of the human experience. I am by no means against art and education about terrible things. In fact, I think it’s vital to discuss these topics and give them the weight they deserve. It’s the popcornification of it all that I cannot stand. With *Buyer’s Market*, I have such trouble getting into Sotos’ head that it makes the intent of the project unclear to me. I certainly don’t think it’s meant to be popcorn true crime, that’s for sure, but I also don’t know exactly how seriously he takes it all. I am interested in the artistic angle of it all, and I will discuss my thoughts in the track-by-track paragraph below. As an album, however, it was a miserable experience to get through. I do not plan to ever listen to it again after I hit send on this review and am freed from my (self-imposed) obligations. For that reason, I will not be rating it very highly.

I am naming “Children” my “favorite track” at the bottom because I have to pick one and it’s the track that most affected me emotionally. The clip of the toddler girl with the dolls is one of the toughest to get through on the album. It almost makes it worse that the toddler is so nonchalant and matter-of-fact about the whole thing while the adult social worker can barely get through it. The next clip that sticks with me is the girl with the thick Boston accent talking about her father groping her and her whole family getting mad because she won’t “giver her own father a hug anymore”. Beyond just the obvious sadness of the story, I remember it because the girl is a charismatic storyteller, a fact that makes me uncomfortable. This happens again on the extended clip of the mother who ends by revealing that her son’s “penis had been severed”, something that shocked me the first time I heard it. It kind of sucks that I’m most affected by the pain of people who are good at storytelling! And of course, the clip of the toddler crying at the end when she’s being sent to her dad’s place is by far the most heart-wrenching clip on the album. I really wish I had never heard it. Before listening to “McMartin”, I had been familiar with the Satanic panic, but I didn’t know anything about McMartin preschool or it being a hoax. This made for a very different experience on listens one and two compared to later listens after Aidan filled me in. I do have to give Sotos credit for expertly weaving in the two quotes from (presumably) Ray Buckey discussing the failure of the system and how the parents put both him and their children through hell. It’s a weird feeling to have so. much contempt for the adults talking in the clips on this track as I have nothing but sympathy for the speakers elsewhere.

I placed “Trash” as my least favorite track because it feels the least focused of any of the tracks. The others each have some kind of theme, while this one feels more scattered. There are certainly some tough clips, particularly the one with the woman on the verge of tears talking about throwing up after being forced to ingest semen, but it doesn’t feel like a self-contained work like the others do. “Bundy” fucks me up because Ted Bundy is so ingrained in popular culture that my brain stores him in the same part that it stores TV shows and movies, so hearing this pain and heartbreak fucks me up. The mother of a victim saying I keep asking myself why did he do it, why did he pick on her. She would have been his friend if he had given her a chance. I've said from the very beginning that somewhere there was some mother with a hurt deeper than mine, and I'd rather have my daughter than that son” was absolutely heartbreaking. I’m also interested in why this track places such an emphasis on the death penalty. Ultimately, I come away pondering how one person can destroy the lives of so many. “Victims” is centered around the families of murder victims broadly instead of only victims of sex crimes, giving it a unique perspective. More clips than usual seem to be taken from TV programs, leading to lots of melodramatic background music that puzzles me a bit.

I really wanted to avoid doing so because it feels like a copout, but I’m placing this near the very bottom of my ranking and tier list. I just don’t see myself ever wanting to listen to it again, though I do believe it has some artistic merit. In fairness, I imagine that Sotos would be delighted with the difficulty I had stomaching it.

Favorite tracks: Children

Least favorite track: Trash
