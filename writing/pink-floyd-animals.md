# Hargrove's review

Try as I might, I just don’t think I’m ever going to love Pink Floyd at this point. I had listened to *Dark Side of the Moon* prior to this project, and listened to *Wish You Were Here* and *Meddle* as part of the project, so this is my fourth Pink Floyd record. I like *Wish You Were Here*, but I’m not crazy about the other two at all (though it’s been a while since I’ve listened to *Dark Side*). That being said, I’ve known my whole life that *Animals* is my dad’s favorite Pink Floyd album, so I really gave it my all to give this a fair shot. And it’s just…it’s alright. It’s fine. I’m not very excited about it. It has many admirable qualities, but just never really rouses me at any point, a consistent criticism I have of Pink Floyd’s music. All three of the main tracks had the potential to be decent, but they’re honestly just inconsistent across their runtimes. “Shine on You Crazy Diamond” is my gold standard for long suites from Pink Floyd, and none of these tracks do it as seamlessly as that one does. Whether it’s the “stone” breakdown in “Dogs” or the vocoder Bible verse in “Sheep”, every track has its significant lulls. “Pigs (Three Different Ones)” is better in this regard, but Waters’ self-assured smugness in his vocals really turns me off. And I’m really trying to separate that from my opinion of Roger Waters in real life outside of the music. I don’t find his vocals as grating on “Sheep”. It’s something about “Pigs (Three Different Ones)” where it just sounds like he’s talking downwards. I don’t know. Given the relatively even highs and lows between all three tracks, it was hard to slot them into favorite and least favorite. “Dogs” felt like the one most impacted by dead space so it drew the short straw, but honestly my opinion on all three is very similar. And even if they didn’t have these lows, even if these tracks were all seamless suites, they would only be above average because they just don’t make me feel all that much. I have begrudgingly come to enjoy *Wish You Were Here* because the pain and regret over their lost friend does punch me in the gut at times on that record. The “Remember when we were young/You shone like the Sun” after 8 minutes of intro, every last line on “Wish You Were Here” (Waters really does have a talent for lyric writing), and Gilmour’s weighty, wailing guitar. Setting aside the fact that I think Waters’ analysis of the world on this record is quite shoddy, I was just never going to be particularly drawn into a grand political manifesto from Pink Floyd. That’s not the type of art I enjoy generally, much less from a band that I already find lacking in its ability to connect with me emotionally. I acknowledge this as a good record, and will stick it into C Tier for its craftsmanship. But if I’m ever looking for a record to help me process some emotion, or just to get me to feel anything at all, it will never be this one.

I liked the idea of bookending an album with a composition split in half on *Wish You Were Here*, and I still like that fundamental idea here. However, I’m not crazy about this particular composition. It’s simple but not poignant, feeling trite compared to the music sandwiched between. I like the first section of “Dogs” quite a bit. The acoustic guitar line sets the stage for a long-ass track perfectly and I love Gilmour’s vocals. I also think the dog metaphor is the most interesting of the three animal metaphors. A line like “Yeah, and in the end you'll pack up, fly down south/Hide your head in the sand/Just another sad old man/All alone and dying of cancer” actually does get to me a little bit. However, I don’t love the solos in this section; they’re not terrible, but not transcendent like you would hope for them to be. Then, there’s the “stone” section, which completely derails the track for me. If it stopped before that, it could be almost as good as “Welcome to the Machine” for me, but, alas, it does not. I wish the vocals didn’t shift to Waters for the second half, but it’s fine. Overall, this track reminds me of “Close to the Edge” by Yes in its construction, and I would just much rather listen to Yes instead. I like the echoing guitar part at the beginning of “Pigs (Three Different Ones)”. The song has a nice, sleazy swagger to it that I admire. I also have to admit that the “haha, charade you are” refrain is an earworm. However, Waters just sounds so smug in his verses, like he’s sneering. And to be fair, he proudly is sneering, but I don’t find that a particularly pleasing quality in a song, much less one condemning elitists. The breakdown is even more pointless than in “Dogs” but it’s shorter, and still there’s a real pull to the base track. It’s almost funky, with a certain James Brown quality to the way it carries itself. The guitar solo at the end is my favorite on the album. “Sheep” is carried by its relentless bass line. It has so much forward motion thanks to that bass and it knows when to get loud as just the right moments. It’s my favorite track overall, but it could really do without the vocoder Bible section. And I always respect a good unhappy ending. My thoughts on “Pigs on the Wing 2” are the same as my thoughts on “Pigs on the Wing 1”.

I really just wish I liked this more than I do. It has so much going for it. But I walk away feeling largely unchanged from before I first listened to it on Monday. Sorry, Dad.

Favorite tracks: Pigs (Three Different Ones), Sheep

Least favorite track: Dogs
