# Hargrove's review

I’ve listened to this like 8 times since Monday trying to get something out of it and I’m really just not. I don’t hate it. It sounds fine. I don’t even understand the hate toward “Seamus”, it’s just blues and the only honkey who should do the blues is John Lennon and maybe Bob Dylan, but it’s like a fine blues. There’s a certain dreamy quality to side 1 that isn’t unenjoyable to space out to. But it does not grip me. As for side 2, it is genuinely pretty cool to see Pink Floyd develop their famous sound in real time if only as a historical artifact, but there’s just not much for me to go back to in “Echoes”. It’s good, but it’s nowhere near as dynamic as something like “Shine On You Crazy Diamond”, which I would much rather listen to. This paragraph pretty much sums up my thoughts, the next paragraph is me ranting about the Beatles rip offs.

Aidan has already pointed out how “Echoes” borrows a lyric from the Beatles’ “Across the Universe”, and beyond that they also use the same cadence a bit in the verses, but the worst offender is actually all of side 1. Side 1 is insane in how much it’s just “Floyd does the Beatles”. And I’m not someone who even really cares about plagiarism, if you can ape someone’s style and do it better that’s fine by me (as Bowie said, “It's not who does it first, it's who does it second.”). But for one, they just don’t do it as well or as naturally as the Beatles did, and for two, it’s *uncanny* how much it specifically incorporates what the Beatles were obsessed with in 1968, 3 years prior. In 1968, during sessions for the White Album, Paul McCartney was getting really into a traditional English genre called “music hall”, and he wrote more than a few songs that John and George despised and famously called “granny shit”. These songs include like “Martha My Dear”, “I Will”, “Honey Pie”, and later on *Abbey Road* “Maxwell’s Silver Hammer”. This style had a certain theatricality, grandiosity, and pleasant quality to it. On the other hand, John Lennon was getting obsessed with the blues, most obviously seen on “Yer Blues” and later “Don’t Let Me Down”, but it can be seen all over John’s work in the late 60s. It is insane to me how much of side 1 is like a combination of music hall and blues influences. “San Tropez” stands out to me as a particularly music hall song (especially the piano), and “Seamus” stands out to me as a particularly blues song. “Fearless” is like a straight combo of the two, with the melodic vocals and the bluesy guitar. And apropos of nothing, the guitar on “A Pillow of Winds” sounds very much like “Dear Prudence”. Like I said, it’s not a huge problem for me, it’s just INSANE how obvious it is.

Favorite tracks: Fearless, San Tropez

Least favorite track: Seamus
