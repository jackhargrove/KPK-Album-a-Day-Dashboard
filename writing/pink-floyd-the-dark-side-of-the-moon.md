# Write-up

Our fourth and final album from Pink Floyd in our survey, chronologically, The Dark Side of the Moon (henceforth referred to as "TDSotM") was Pink Floyd's eight album and the 2nd of the four we have listened to. Work on TDSofM began in December 1971, a month after the release of Meddle. The band met to prepare for a tour, and Waters proposed that they write a concept album regarding Syd Barrett's mental health issues as well as the band's own struggles, and perform it on the tour. The band has already written previous songs about these topics, so the members approved of the idea and let Waters record some demo tracks. Many of the tracks came from reworking old ideas, including scores for films various members of the band had written. They first performed a version of the music in late January of 1972, and proceeded to refine the ideas in it over the next year touring and recording. Work on the album was interrupted by work on their 7th album, a soundtrack for La Valleé, a French film about a woman meeting an unconnected tribe in New Zealand. The album was titled "Obscured By Clouds", and the band rushed the album, wanting to get back to working on TDSotM and not wanting to make too good of an album as to overshadow their main work.

"Brain Damage", "Eclipse", "Any Colour You Like" and "On The Run" were the last songs to be finished. The album was heavily produced, featuring the usage of double tracking, flanging (laying the same sound with a tiny delay), and panning. The famous intro to "Money" used recordings of Waters throwing coins into a mixing bowl. The father of Naomi Watts, Peter, who also happened to be the band's manager, features several times on the album, laughing on two tracks and saying "I can't think of anything to say" on "Brain Damage". A variety of people were interviewed at Abby Road studio where the band recorded to get recordings for the album, including Paul and Linda McCartney. The McCartney's contributions were not used, and the band thought they were "trying too hard to be funny". The album's iconic cover was created by a contracted design firm and chosen by the band out of several options.

The band was extremely happy with the final result. Their label, Capitol Records, went all out to promote it, trying to reverse the poor sales of Meddle and get their money's worth out of the band, with TDSotM being their last record on the current contract. The band would sign their next contract with Columbia Records. Through a combination of heavy promotion, an iconic cover and good music, the album was the best selling album ever when it was released and it now the 4th best-selling album ever, behind Thriller, Back in Black and The Bodyguard. The album has been covered numerous times, and Waters himself has made his own solo remake.

# Hargrove's review

After well over a year since our second Pink Floyd album, our third and fourth came back-to-back to force me to reckon with the canonical band I fuck with least. I’m pretty Pink Floyded out after these two weeks, but I’m doing my best to not let it influence this review. Thankfully, *The Dark Side of the Moon* is the Pink Floyd album I was most familiar with prior to this list and that I have the strongest pre-conceived notions toward. My dad put this on my first MP3 player as a kid, where I became enamored with “Breathe (In the Air)”, “Time”, and “The Great Gig in the Sky”. I found “Money” interesting, but it never resonated with me on the same level. I found “Us and Them” difficult to get through, gating off most of side 2 from my 9-year-old critical gaze. Now, as a 25-year-old who has listened to this record about ten times since Monday, I have to say: I was pretty fucking bang-on with my track-by-track assessment as a kid. As much as Roger Waters bugs me, I’ve always had to give him his flowers for his way with words, and the cleverness of his lyric-writing is on full display on this album’s first side. Moreover, the deep focus on life and insanity is much more in line with the subject matter I want to hear about than was the confused politics and sociology of *Animals*. “Time” is my favorite Pink Floyd song lyrically apart from “Wish You Were Here”, while “Breathe (In the Air)” contains a few stunningly beautiful passages of its own. “Don’t be afraid to care” became a real mantra for me as a pre-teen, and it still comes to me even now. Musically, these side 1 tracks have first-rate vocal melodies as well. No track stands out to me more than “The Great Gig in the Sky”, where Clare Torry’s vocals captivated me as a child and still captivate me now. Those belts came from somewhere deep within the heart. But once this album gets to “Us and Them”, it falls headfirst off a cliff. I really dislike this track, from its runtime to its dreary verses to its jarring, overwrought choruses. The two that follow it are some of Pink Floyd’s weakest ever musically. The closer is fine, but nothing approaching the heights of side 1. So in effect, *The Dark Side of the Moon* has one above average side and one below average side. This isn’t the profile you want for the fourth-best selling album of all-time! Ultimately, I think that’s what ends up driving me crazy with Pink Floyd. It’s the FOMO! With every Pink Floyd album I listen to, I can feel the pull. I can hear the expert craftsmanship, the feats of composition, the cohesive concepts. I can see exactly what it is that people like about it, but, try as I might, I just can’t like it on some deep level. It sucks. I wish I just liked it. Oh well.

“Speak to Me” isn’t much of a song, but it sets up the album’s concept very well, and the concept is one of this record’s best qualities. The bass-drum-as-heartbeat is a super cool sound effect. The album starts in earnest with the smooth, peaceful “Breathe (In the Air)”. Waters’ lyrics are truly fantastic, particularly “For long you live and high you fly/And smiles you'll give and tears you'll cry/And all you touch and all you see/Is all your life will ever be”. I also enjoy the vocal melody quite a lot. Like the opener, “On the Run” is a sound collage. Unlike the opener, I fucking hate it. It kills any momentum created by the very good previous track. The keyboard line drives me nuts. This track just feels like a tremendous waste of time. The clock sound effects on “Time” are really well-done, and the lyrics are my favorite on the album. They’re a moving representation of how quickly life can get away from you. Gilmour delivers one of the only guitar solos on the album to perk my ears up. I’m also a fan of the reprise of “Breathe (In the Air)”. “The Great Gig in the Sky” opens with the best interview response on the album, signaling that this track is going to be pretty good. I love Clare Torry’s vocals here. They’re the main hook of the track, but that’s more than enough. I strongly respect the choice to include such an out-of-left-field track on this album. It feels like more of a risk than anything on any other Floyd album I’ve heard except maybe “Echoes” on *Meddle*.

I respect “Money” but I don’t love it. I have to hand it to them for turning a 7/4 bass line into a wildly successful pop song, and what a bass line it is. However, I find the vocal melody a bit overdone. Maybe it’s more blues-y than I feel it deserves to be? I don’t know for sure, but I do know that I’m just not crazy about it. Then, the song goes off into guitar solo and saxophone land, eventually even just becoming 4/4. It’s sadly not my thing. I strongly dislike “Us and Them”, the point at which the album completely falls apart for me. It’s slow, long, sentimental, and just not all that interesting. Even when the loud choruses come in it doesn’t feel like a release, instead coming out of nowhere with maximal volume. This track is the definition of wallpaper. “Any Colour You Like” is just as bad, an aimless instrumental track. It sounds like proggy keyboard and guitar effects put over a generic rhythm section. Fortunately, the good lyrics return on “Brain Damage”, with “The lunatic is on the grass” deserving of its stature as a line. Unfortunately, I really hate this track’s instrumental. The guitar tone and hi-hat on the verses both bug me, then, much like “Us and Them”, I find the crescendo into the choruses jarring. “Eclipse” is my second favorite on side 2 after “Money”. There’s a strong drum groove and undeniable build in the vocals. It also has a great closing line and final interview quote. It’s not really my jam, but this album does a great job executing its concept.

*Wish You Were Here* is untouchable as my favorite Pink Floyd album, while *Meddle* remains, by far, my least favorite thing I’ve heard from them. *The Dark Side of the Moon* is roughly comparable to *Animals*; I prefer the lyrics on the former, but the music on the latter. After some thought, *Animals* wins for me between these two, but they’re not terribly far apart.

Favorite tracks: Breathe (In the Air), Time, The Great Gig in the Sky

Least favorite track: Us and Them

# Aidan's review

The best part of this album is its cover. That's nothing to be ashamed of, it's a great cover, but I just don't feel this record. The production is very flashy, the spoken word samples do a nice job of setting the mood, the jingling coins on "Money" and the alarm clocks on "Time" are all well and good, but it's easily the least focused concept album of the three we've heard. "Eclipse" is a compelling and appropriately grand closer, but the entire album feels that it has a tremendous amount of vamping, even by the standards of the Floyd. The vamping is even pretty good, it's just that the actual songs feel so space-y. I kept waiting for the album to have some real emotion, and there's just flashes on "Time" and "Eclipse". Animals had real hatred for Thatcher and Whitehouse, Wish You Were Here had real mourning for Syd, this is mostly half baked philosophical musings. Might be rating this lower because of Floyd fatigue, but this album just felt like it was sitting there most of the time I was listening to it. They're excellent musicians, but I never really felt like their grandiosity managed to tug at my heartstrings. The end result feels closer to a movie soundtrack than an album, it needs something else to help evoke emotion. Dark Side of the Rainbow is probably really good for this reason.
