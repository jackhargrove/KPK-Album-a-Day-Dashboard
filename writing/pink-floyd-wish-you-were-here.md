# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: While this album has multiple thematic ideas, the primary one is as a tribute to Syd Barrett. Syd Barrett founded Pink Floyd in the 1960s and was the lead singer and songwriter for their modestly successful first album. Unfortunately, he completely fried his brain on LSD and gave himself schizophrenia. In the late 1960s, Pink Floyd was forced to kick him out and he eventually moved back in with his parents and became a recluse.

Pink Floyd continued to grow after Barrett’s removal, now led by Roger Waters as the primary songwriter and with strong influence from lead guitar player David Gilmour. However, Barrett left a lasting mark on the band, and their 1973 album *The Dark Side of the Moon* was largely inspired by their experiences with Barrett’s mental illness. This album became wildly successful, and is today the 4th highest selling album of all time.

Coming off of that success, Pink Floyd still had Barrett on the brain and wrote *Wish You Were Here* about Syd Barrett as well. “Shine On You Crazy Diamond” is an epic, 25 minute tribute to Barrett, remembering his good years and lamenting his nervous breakdown.

While recording “Shine On You Crazy Diamond”, a man entered the recording studio whom no one recognized. After they finished recording the song, the band approached him and realized he was Syd Barrett. They hadn’t seen him in years, and he had shaved his head and gained a significant amount of weight. The old friend they had known was completely gone, and this was likely the last time any of them saw him.

Syd Barrett continued to live with his parents in Cambridge, England until his death in 2006. He never regained his sanity. He primarily focused on gardening for the rest of his life. His is one of the most tragic stories in rock music. Know what you’re getting into with psychedelics, people.

# Hargrove's review

I am not a huge prog rock guy. I tend to find its epic nature tedious and overwrought. That being said, I enjoyed this album much more than I enjoy *The Dark Side of the Moon*.

I like the soundscape that they build in “SOYCD (Pts. 1-5)”, and when the vocals come in more than 8 minutes into the song it feels so earned. In particular, props to David Gilmour’s guitar work in this song, it leads things along so well.

The title track probably contains my favorite lyrics I’ve ever heard from Roger Waters; “Did you exchange/A walk-on part in the war for a lead role in a cage?” is one of the most compelling takedowns of selfishness I’ve ever heard. I also really appreciate how in this song, as well as in SOYCD, they experience their genuine sadness at Syd’s absence without holding back their anger at his poor decision-making and behavior. And, of course, amazing and catchy guitar riff.

“Welcome to the Machine” sounds cool and space age, but the vocals are a little too strained. Synths are an awesome tool and they’re awesome here. Particularly the ominous and heavy synths that come in on the downbeat of every other bar in the second verse.

I don’t care for “Have a Cigar”, although “Oh, by the way, which one’s Pink?” is admittedly funny. “SOYCD (Pts. 6-9)” was fine but I didn’t like it as much as the opener. This album didn’t completely rid me of Pink Floyd phobia, but I feel like I have a better appreciation. I enjoyed the parts about Syd (“SOYCD” and “Wish You Were Here”) better both lyrically and instrumentally than I did the songs shitting on the music industry (“Welcome to the Machine” and “Have a Cigar”)

Favorite Tracks: SOYCD (Pts. 1-5), Wish You Were Here

Least Favorite Track: Have a Cigar

Rating: 7.5/10
