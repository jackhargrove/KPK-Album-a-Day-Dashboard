# Write-up

The above write-up for *Surfer Rosa* covers the history of the Pixies from beginning to end in its first two paragraphs. This write-up will focus entirely on the album *Doolittle*, their commercial breakout record. Though *Surfer Rosa* had been produced by legendary underground producer Steve Albini, the Pixies decided they wanted to go in a more commercial direction for their next album. They chose Gil Norton, who had worked on the single version of their song “Gigantic”. They began recording their follow-up to *Surfer Rosa* in Boston in late 1988. Bandleader and songwriter Black Francis had many song ideas, but most of them were very short. Norton continuously encouraged him to develop them further, which he sometimes did, but for the most part the songs on *Doolittle* are under three minutes each (Francis used Buddy Holly’s brevity as a guidepost). Norton would also frequently use production techniques to smooth out their harsh guitar sound, which the band was not entirely comfortable with. The result was an album that still had the underground intensity of *Surfer Rosa* but in a neater package. The lyrics continue on Francis’ themes of sex, violence, and depravity, but this time with quite a few Biblical references as well. The album’s cover was designed by Simon Larbalestier, who had designed the cover of their last album, as well as for many other 4AD bands like Red House Painters. It features a collage of references to the album’s lyrics, most notably a monkey who has gone to heaven. The lyrics of “Debaser” reference Luis Buñuel’s seminal surrealist 1928 film *Un Chien Andalou*, which you should all watch. “Here Comes Your Man” was the most pure pop song Pixies had released to this point, and is still among their most famous; for example, Tom sings it at karaoke in *(500) Days of Summer*. It was released as a single, along with “Monkey Gone to Heaven”. *Doolittle* perfected Pixies’ loud/quiet dynamic, a sound that would go on to define the 1990s after Nirvana ripped it off. Kurt Cobain described Nirvana’s breakthrough smash hit “Smells Like Teen Spirit” as his attempt to write a Pixies song, specifically referencing “Tame” and “Gouge Away”. Radiohead and Slowdive are other groups that explicitly named the Pixies as among their biggest influences. You can safely lump Weezer, The Smashing Pumpkins, Modest Mouse, and Pavement in there too. Though *Surfer Rosa* was popular in the underground and in the UK, *Doolittle* was Pixies’ breakthrough into the mainstream, getting endless play on MTV and ultimately going platinum. It was also the beginning of the end for the Pixies, however, as the irreparable rift between Kim Deal and Black Francis opened during its recording.

# Hargrove's review

I’ve almost been putting off writing this review because it would mean I’d lose my excuse to keep listening to this album. It is just so fun from beginning to end. Pixies are probably in my dad’s top 10 favorite bands of all time, and I was a little bit confused when we listened to *Surfer Rosa* and the only two songs I recognized were the two big hits. That’s because all the Pixies songs I heard over and over and over again as a kid are on *Doolittle*. I recognized every last song despite never having sat down and listened to this album before (though “Here Comes Your Man” has been one of my favorite songs my whole life and I listen to it very often). It is just genuine pop perfection, fifteen little bursts of manic energy, catchy as hell songwriting, and stellar arrangement. I’ve complained a bit about albums full of super short songs in the past, but it works to perfection here. No song overstays its welcome, and no song feels like it’s missing anything. It’s insane efficiency. Kim Deal’s bass was good on *Surfer Rosa* was pretty good but the improvement on *Doolittle* is astounding. The bass steals the show in multiple songs! Joey Santiago’s guitar interacts with the rest of the music in a way I’ve never really heard before. He’s often just playing his own melodies and snaking them through the rest of the song as he sees fit. Which brings me to what a feat of arrangement this is. On just about every track, you have Black Francis screaming and hollering while playing pure fuzz in his rhythm guitar, Kim adding harmonies and interjections in her soft voice that couldn’t sound more different than Francis’, Santiago inventing his own melody lines that go all over the place, and Lovering playing syncopated drum beats that I’d expect from hip hop. And yet, with all this bullshit going on, it’s hard to imagine a tighter, sleeker, more focused record. It’s masterfully controlled chaos. Credit to the production, obviously, but really it’s also a credit to the songwriting. None of this works if the songs aren’t all-killer-no-filler tactical nuclear bombs of pop ditties. Sure, some of the songs on the back half aren’t as good as the songs on the front half. But what a wonderful fusion of unbridled creativity and shiny velvety pop this album is. I love it.

“Debaser” is a crazy good song and an insane way to start off your sophomore record. The guitar melody and the main vocals are just so unbelievably catchy. It has the perfect amount of manic energy from Francis and angelic calm from Kim. The bass is just great. The lyrics are really cool and fit the delivery perfectly; who knew “Andalusia!” sounded so good when yelled by a schizo? So, so good. “Tame” has a bouncy ass bass line that steals the show. The grunting in this track is one of the few times on this album that Francis gets a little overboard for me but then Kim comes in and turns it into this cool breathing melody. The chorus is so fun and so loud. Love it lots. “Wave of Mutilation” continues the bangers with another unbelievable bass line. I love the fuzziness of the guitars in the verses and the chorus does it again with a perfect pop melody. The title is also really cool sounding. “I Bleed” has one of my all-time favorite Francis/Kim harmonies in its verses. Kim sounds like an angel and Francis is so animated. The bass line is a banger, the drums sound like a fucking breakbeat (seriously I’m shocked I couldn’t find anyone else talking about the hip hop drums on this album), and the bridge is eye-of-the-hurricane controlled chaos. “Here Comes Your Man” is one of my favorite songs. Talk about bubblegum cotton candy caramel rot-your-teeth-and-stain-your-fingers sugary pop perfection. Genuinely could have been a Beatles song. I like “Dead” for the dissonance of its guitar line alone. There’s also a great sunny quality on the bridge and I’m also a fan of the effect on Francis’ vocals.

“Monkey Gone to Heaven” continues an unbroken streak of great songs. The lyrics used to intrigue me as a kid, I was so drawn to them. Of course, they’re nonsense, but what a lovely shape that nothingness takes on. The strings are unexpected but so so nice and I can’t get enough of the open space and airiness of the verses. The guitar solo goes hard, as does the screaming delivery on “Then God is seven”. “Mr. Grieves” keeps things going hot, man. It introduces a fast/slow dynamic that mirrors the quiet/loud and executes it perfectly. It’s catchy too, and I love the theatrics of the ending. “Crackity Jones” is the first real miss on the album. I like the boom chuk drums, but it feels like it’s missing something, and Francis goes a tad bit too far. “La La Love You” is a great recovery though. It’s another one with a straight up breakbeat in the drums, but this time our wonderful drummer is killing it on the vocals too. The collage of different voices is perfect and then you have Santiago threading his guitar through everything without knocking anything off balance. This is definitely my favorite deconstruction of a love song I’ve listened to recently. “No. 13 Baby” is good but not great. It’s catchy, has another breakbeat in the drums, and the outro is actually nice all things considered, but I don’t appreciate it breaking up the brevity and efficiency. “There Goes My Gun” brings back the efficiency in spades though with great economy of lyrics. Kim kills it on vocals, Santiago kills it on the twangy guitars, Francis kills it on the melody. “Hey” brings back the extremely high quality of the opening few tracks. I can’t stop listening to that slinking bass line from Kim, the masterful chorus, the squealing guitars, the dynamic quality of Francis’ delivery. Wonderful song. “Silver” isn’t like stellar or anything, but it’s not as bad as people make it out to be. It’s got a really cool atmosphere. “Gouge Away” ends things on an impossibly high note. It’s got everything we’ve come to know and love: banger chorus, banger bass line, banger guitars.

I love this album man. I’m getting giddy just writing about it. There’s a lot of weird pop out there, especially these days, but there’s not much that indulges in so much earworminess and yet still maintains this level of eclecticism. A very rare record, and just one that makes me happy.

Favorite tracks: Debaser, Tame, Wave of Mutilation, I Bleed, Here Comes Your Man, Dead, Monkey Gone to Heaven, Mr. Grieves, La La Love You, There Goes My Gun, Hey, Gouge Away

Least favorite track: Crackity Jones
