# Write-up

The Pixies are an indie rock band from Boston who consisted of lead vocalist and rhythm guitarist Black Francis (real name Charles Michael Kittridge Thompson IV), bassist and backing vocalist Kim Deal, lead guitarist Joey Santiago, and drummer David Lovering. Along with Sonic Youth and Dinosaur Jr., the Pixies are considered one of the three founding bands of the indie rock genre, and their quiet-loud stop-start sound defined the alt rock boom of the 90s. Francis and Santiago met at UMass Amherst and formed a band together in 1986. They put an ad in a local Boston newspaper seeking a bass player who was a fan of both 60s folk pop group Peter, Paul and Mary and post-hardcore group Hüsker Dü. Kim Deal, an Ohio native who had recently moved to Boston, was the only person to respond to the ad. Though she only played guitar, she borrowed a bass from her sister and joined the band. David Lovering, a friend of Deal’s husband whom she had met at their wedding, was chosen as drummer. Santiago selected the word “pixies” randomly from a dictionary as their band’s name and they began playing local Boston gigs in 1986.

While opening for fellow proto-indie act Throwing Muses, the Pixies were discovered by producer Gary Smith, who had them sign to independent label 4AD and record their debut EP *Come on Pilgrim* in 1987. Someone from 4AD recommended they ditch producer Smith for Chicago post-hardcore pioneer Steve Albini. This resulted in their 1988 debut album *Surfer Rosa*, possibly the first indie rock album. In 1989, the Pixies found another new producer, Gil Norton, who produced their second album *Doolittle* to have a much cleaner, sleeker pop sound. *Doolittle* was their breakthrough both critically and commercially, getting significant MTV airplay. It is also on this list, and there is considerable debate as to whether *Surfer Rosa* or *Doolittle* is the better album. However, serious tensions between Francis and Deal began to form while recording *Doolittle*, which would only get worse as time went on. Deal felt stifled creatively by Francis, and she formed her own side project The Breeders, who would release the well-regarded *Pod* in 1990 and the platinum album *Last Splash* in 1993 with hit single “Cannonball”. Despite Deal growing more distant from the Pixies, they would release two more albums in 1990 and 1991 to middling reviews. After their fourth album, exhausted and with Deal and Francis no longer on speaking terms, the Pixies broke up in 1993. As the 90s progressed and more popular bands blatantly aped the Pixies sound, their legend grew, and they reunited in 2004 for a famous performance at Coachella. The band has continued since then, although Deal left in 2013, and have released 5 trash albums since 2014.

*Surfer Rosa* contains two of the Pixies most well-known songs: the anthemic “Gigantic” and the bizarre “Where Is My Mind?”, the latter of which has over a billion streams on Spotify and has been used in infinity movies and TV shows. Every song was written and sung by Black Francis except for “Gigantic”, which was co-written and sung by Kim Deal. The quirks and dynamics of *Surfer Rosa* would prove extremely influential on the indie rock sound, with the genre getting its name from the independent labels that the bands were signed to (4AD in the case of the Pixies). It was also influential on alternative rock more broadly, with Kurt Cobain citing it as his primary influence for Nirvana’s breakthrough *Nevermind*. The extreme quiet-loud dynamics and unique drum sound would be copied relentlessly throughout the 90s thanks to bands like Nirvana and The Smashing Pumpkins. Steve Albini’s raw production also inspired many artists including Nirvana and PJ Harvey to seek him out for their albums. Additionally, Albini’s decision to include studio banter gave the Pixies a strong personality. While “Gigantic” and “Where Is My Mind?” have both been covered ad nauseum, “Cactus” was covered by David Bowie on his 2002 album *Heathen*.

# Hargrove's review

The first (and only?) band where my parents are friends with one of the members! They are on a first name basis with Kim Deal and were in a documentary on the Breeders a couple years ago. I can’t remember if I’ve ever listened to *Doolittle* all the way through and I know I hadn’t ever listened to *Surfer Rosa* all the way through, but I’m familiar with many songs off both albums thanks to my parents’ love for the Pixies. I was very excited to listen to *Surfer Rosa*, and I thought it was pretty fun! There are a lot of imperfections with the record: side 1 is much stronger than side 2, the songwriting is much less tight and punchy than the songs I know off of *Doolittle*, and Black Francis can become a little grating after a while (thank God for Kim Deal balancing out the more self-indulgent aspects of his personality). However, the album really plays to its strengths by keeping both the album and the individual songs short, playing up its more endearing personality aspects, and never taking itself too seriously. Though I understand the recording sessions for subsequent albums were very unpleasant, you can just tell that they had a lot of fun making this record, and it translates well into the music. Everything is a little ball of energy, rough and unsanded around the edges, but with a core of pure sugar that exposes itself periodically. The guitar is fuzzy but catchy, the bass is prominent in the shaping the songs, the drums are bright and crisp, the lyrics are single-mindedly grotesque, and I really enjoy Francis and Kim singing over each other (though, I can see how, if you didn’t enjoy this effect, it would get really frustrating really fast). And when it comes time for a melody, a small burst of pure pop, it frequently delivers. This is by no means a perfect album, in fact it has many flaws, but it is a *really* enjoyable listen.

Side 1 of this LP has some fantastic songwriting, focusing more on the sweet than the noise. “Bone Machine” starts things off firing on all cylinders with one of the best guitar riffs on the album. The vocals on the chorus are fantastic, I love Francis and Kim’s harmonizing. It walks the quirky tightrope perfectly like much of the first half of this album; Francis’ vocal tics and yelling feel distinctive without crossing the line into annoying. The rhythm section is stellar, as it will be for the rest of the record. “Break My Body” continues much the same, retaining all the positive elements of the opener but with a different song. Very strong start to the album so far. “Something Against You” really plays up the surf sound, something I’m a sucker for (I love you Dead Kennedys). It feels a little more like an interlude than a full song, but it’s a banger nonetheless. “Broken Face” is maybe the weakest on side 1, with Francis crossing the line into annoying for the first time on the vocals. However, the rest of the song is quite strong and even catchy, despite being the noisiest thus far. “Gigantic” is a banger’s banger’s banger, Kim absolutely kills it on this song from front to back on both vocals and bass. She sounds like she’s having so much fun as she sings about watching another woman get fucked by a black guy with a gigantic dick. Indie pop perfection, and the best song on the album. “River Euphrates” closes side 1 with momentum, forcing the lilting chorus deep into your brain. Santiago’s guitar steals the show here, providing a strong exoskeleton around the chaotic vocals, and I really love the guitar line after the chorus. Really, really great side of music.

“Where Is My Mind?” opens side 2 as a continuation of side 1, a banger that’s catchy and you don’t understand why. On paper, I don’t think this song works; none of the individual elements are great on their own, and they seem far away from each other even when together. And yet, this song is an absolute earworm. I really have no idea how it works the way it does, but it just works. Unfortunately, the album is only downhill from here. “Cactus” is enjoyable but nothing special, about on par with “Broken Face”. I do enjoy the lyrics about an inmate asking for his girlfriend to send him a dress soaked in various bodily fluids, but I don’t find the song super catchy, nor do I think the loud sections are loud enough. It ends up being a somewhat nice Pixies take on country. Can’t complain, but not crazy for it. “Tony’s Theme” is where they kick the quirk up to 11 with a half-baked song structure and subject. This one was probably best left on the cutting room floor, but I can at least appreciate it adding to the throw-everything-at-the-wall atmosphere, and Kim sounds great. “Oh My Golly” is the first song to introduce Spanish, a sign that it’s an early Pixies song (Black Francis went to Puerto Rico in 1984 and many of the first songs he wrote were about that trip). It’s…not my favorite. The drums sound cool, but I kind of hate the vocals. Probably my least favorite track. I do like the studio chatter at the end though. “Vamos” continues the Spanish, but I find it a little more bearable here. Santiago goes crazy with the discordant guitar solo, and the rhythm section plays in a very cool, circular manner. I think this is a good track, if a bit noodly. “I’m Amazed” starts with a pretty hilarious bit of studio banter, and what follows is a nice tight noisy pop song. One of my favorites on the back half, a short gem. The album ends on a similar note, short but sweet. Very good lead guitar work on “Red as Brick”, and neat work with the drumming. Overall, side 2 is super inconsistent, and you can tell it’s where they put their earlier, rougher songwriting.

*Surfer Rosa* is an album of contradictions; quiet and loud, stop and start, melody and noise, funny and annoying, Kim and Francis. It’s very raw and rough around the edges. There are definitely times where I think “ehhhh, it’s alright” or “I maybe wouldn’t have included this”. But, more than anything else, it’s fun and brimming with ideas. It reminds me of *The Velvet Underground & Nico* in that way. It reminds me of it a lot, in fact. Noisy exteriors with charming interiors. Inconsistent but fun. That Francis guy really needs to get his incest obsession checked though.

Favorite tracks: Bone Machine, Break My Body, Something Against You, Gigantic, River Euphrates, Where Is My Mind?, Vamos, I’m Amazed

Least favorite track: Oh My Golly!
