# Write-up

Popol Vuh's history is heavily tied to German Director Werner Herzog. While studying briefly at Munich University, Herzog met Florian Fricke and hit it off. While in the university library, they found a Mayan manuscript that laid out the creation myth of the K'ich'e people. It was titled Popul Vuh. When Herzog started making movies, he cast Fricke in his very first one, Signs of Life in a small bit role. Later, Fricke would go on to edit the soundtrack for several of Herzog's films. A year after appearing in Signs of Life, Fricke founded the group Popol Vuh, which would be the main vehicle for his music. To start with, the band played electronic music and were among the first in the world to own a Moog synthesizer. Popol Vuh put out two albums that were already somewhat outside of krautrock, the growing German experimental rock scene that included NEU!, CAN and Faust. But while all of those bands utilized experimental and often technologically cutting edge techniques to create novel music, Popol Vuh eschewed the rhythmic tempo that most of the other band at least toyed with, instead focusing fully on an ambient, psychedelic sound.

In pursuit of music even more divorced from tradition, Fricke sold his Moog synthesizer, kicked out the other two members, then brought in Conny Veidt and others to play a new style, one without synthesizers. Hosianna Mantra was the first album in this new style. Korean vocalist Djong Yun sung, and instruments from the oboe to the tambura were used. The band were among the first to get called "ethereal" and took inspiration from world music, blending the foreign with Fricke's newly found Christianity to create something completely new. After Hosanna Mantra, Popol Vuh would put out 17 more albums, including the soundtracks for six of Herzog's films. Fricke died in 2001.

# Hargrove's review

This album is really pretty. It was definitely not what I was expecting, I thought they were some second-rate krautrock group. I actually complained to Aidan that they were on the list and Kraftwerk wasn’t to Aidan a couple months ago. But this is obviously not what your typical krautrock sounds like. I think the selection of timbres was really strong. Piano, oboe, tambura, harpsichord, violin, and soprano vocals all sound heavenly. And while the electric guitar might sound like an odd choice to complement those others, they somehow make it sound like a Spanish guitar in the way it’s played. The guitar has a real space rock feeling to it, like David Gilmour playing flamenco. I don’t usually get much out of “spiritual” music and that’s no different here. I do think the combined Christian and Hindu symbolism is cool, but it doesn’t inspire any awakenings in me. Another issue with the album is that side 1 is significantly, significantly better than side 2. Side 1 just has three heavenly, beautiful, meditative, transcendental ruminations in a row, while side 2 is a medley of kind of mid tracks. So at the end of the day, this is a pretty cool album but one that doesn’t get me particularly excited. I do very much see myself coming back to it and listening as music while I’m trying to relax or work, though.

“Ah!” starts things off wonderfully. That piano opening is just so pretty. And then I really like the descending piano line later on that defines the track for me. “Kyrie” is, by far, my favorite track on the album. The electric guitar gets more space to shine here than on the opener, and boy is it pretty. The oboe sounds just gorgeous when it comes in, weirdly reminding me of a theremin. And the chords are a little weird but very satisfying to me. Great track. “Hosianna Mantra” is also quite good. It has a driving pace unlike the rest of the album, reminding me of krautrock I’m used to. The oboe at the end is one of my favorite bits from the album. Unfortunately, the quality drops off pretty quickly for the side 2 medley. “Abschied” is an oboe-led piece, which I would think I would love, but it just doesn’t pull it off. It kills my vibe a bit. “Segnung” is probably the best on side 2 thanks to the electric guitar line near the end. “Nicht hoch im himmel” is not great until the last two minutes which have a focus on the vocals and gets quite dark. Both “Andacht” tracks are forgettable.

Overall, super cool record with a mixed execution. I’m glad that this deep into the list, we’re still getting records that sound like nothing else. I will say, I prefer this style of ambient to any of the electronic stuff we’ve listened to save Tim Hecker.

Favorite tracks: Ah!, Kyrie, Hosianna Mantra

Least favorite track: Abschied

# Aidan's review

Hard album to really write about because of how same-ish it sounds. It's pleasant throughout and works well to meditate, chill out or generally disassociate to, but that leaves little to actually review. I really like the general atmosphere, and the instruments accomplish their goal of feeling new and "ethereal". I don't know if I have a rational reason for liking this more than Biosphere but less than Stars of the Lid or Eno, but that's where it lands among the ambient works we've done. Fine, but not extraordinary.
