# Write-up

Portal is an avant-garde metal band formed in Brisbane, Australia in 1994 which, at the time of this record, consisted of vocalist The Curator, lead guitarist Horror Illogium, rhythm guitarist Aphotic Mote, bassist Elsewhere, and drummer Monocular. All members of the band keep their real identities anonymous and perform wearing robes and executioner’s masks over their faces. These outfits were inspired by legendary 1920s silent horror film actor Lon Chaney. Portal was formed in 1994 as a duo of The Curator and Horror Illogium. The band added bassist Werm and drummer Mephitic in 2002 to record its debut album, *Seepia*. Rhythm guitarist Aphotic Mote joined shortly after recording in 2003. Werm and Mephitic both left the group in 2005 and were replaced by Elsewhere and Monocular, respectively, ahead of Portal’s sophomore LP *Outre* in 2007. Portal had been disappointed by the sound of its debut and so set out to make *Outre* darker and more atmospheric. The album received good reviews upon release. Elsewhere and Monocular both left the group after the recording of *Outre*. Ignis Fatuus has played drums for the band ever since; Phathom Conspicuous briefly became bassist but was replaced by current bassist Omenous Fugue before recording an album. Portal has released five albums since 2009, with the most recent coming in 2021. The group’s fourth album, 2013’s *Vexovoid*, was a critical and commercial peak. It received positive reviews from *Pitchfork*, NPR, and even *The New York Times*. While Portal is most commonly identified as a death metal band, it incorporates heavy influence from black metal, as well as ambient and experimental music. Generally, Portal takes the vocals and structures of death metal and combines them with the foreboding atmospheres and raw production of black metal. Portal’s early lyrics revolved around Lovecraft’s Cthulhu Mythos, but quickly the group created its own cosmically horrific lore to sing about. As of 2026, Portal is still active.

# Hargrove's review

With a genre name like “dissonant death metal”, I was prepared for something quite brutal, and Portal did not disappoint with *Outre*. This entire album marinates in a soup of the muddiest, rustiest, tuneless guitars I’ve ever heard in my life. Each track has at least one riff you can pick out of the ooze, but the primary function of the guitar is to create this overbearing, suffocating atmosphere. On some tracks, the guitars howl like the wind, while in others they swirl like a tornado. While I appreciate the effect, this lack of diversity runs the risk of getting old fast; thankfully, Portal kept this album to a tight 36 minutes, just about the exact length I want to listen to it. While the guitars set the atmosphere, the drums lead the song structures and generate forward motion. Though they sometimes approach blast beats, the drums generally keep things mid-tempo and sometimes even get into doom territory. The aggression comes from the power of each beat instead of the speed. There are some sections where the percussion becomes metallic and clanging, reminding me a ton of Swans’ *Filth*. I swear that has to be one of the most influential albums of all time, just about every sufficiently heavy album reminds me of it in some way. This album’s greatest weakness comes from its vocals. I found the death growl fairly generic and the vocal lines not all that interesting. Additionally, though the impenetrability of the lyrics did add to the foreboding, alternate dimension atmosphere, I would have enjoyed the opportunity to follow the descriptions of the eldritch horrors a little more. Speaking of the concept, something this spartan, austere, and uniform will always have a hard ceiling for me, regardless of execution. Still, the execution and concept are both pretty damn good, and I came away from my listens impressed. While it of course has the tempo shifts and growls of death metal, the album functioned much more as black metal to me. Those raw, horrific, and unrelenting atmospheres are as frigid as anything to have ever come out of Norway.

The album starts with the cinematic “Moil”, a fantastic opener that grows into the awful din that permeates the remainder of the record. It reminds me stylistically of *Murmuüre* but far more terrifying and sinister. “Abysmill” combines guitars that sound like howling wind with mid-tempo but relentless drums and the muddiest production I’ve ever heard in my life. This track keeps a ton of tension instead of allowing any release yet, even slowing down to doom tempos at the end. It’s a great first full track. “Heirships” is the first track to bring serious aggression. The drums approach blast beat level while the guitars have moved on from howling wind to full-on storm. This track is a bit too choppy for me though in the way the drums change the rhythm. “Omnipotent Crawling Chaos” is even more aggressive than the previous track, but I prefer the way it’s done here with its pounding jackhammer drums and most dynamic guitar riffing yet. It’s also the first track to bring in the *Filth* percussion. This track has so much power apart from just the atmosphere. By contrast, “Black Houses” returns to a more simmering, seething sound. It’s my least favorite track because the guitars are less interesting and I dislike the way the slow sections are integrated into the composition. “Outre” sounds like when you film something outside while it’s super windy. It’s not musical enough to be a favorite track but it’s a solid interlude that adds to the terrifying atmosphere. “13 Globes” contains by far the most dynamic guitar line on the album. I mean, listen to all the different notes! It makes it stand out as one of the most memorable tracks. The melodic guitars also make it sound like a real culmination of the album. And I LOVE the doom section near the end. “Sourlows” starts super slow, continuing that doom vibe from the end of the last track. But after a while, it grows into something truly foul (complimentary). It’s like a storm of shit, the guitars whipping back and forth with such intensity. It’s a stellar conclusion to the album.

I feel similarly about this album as I do the Faust and Midori albums we listened to. It’s got a super unique concept with a few great moments even if the overall sound isn’t what I usually look for. As a result, I’m sticking it in low B Tier.

Favorite tracks: Moil, Abysmill, Omnipotent Crawling Chaos, 13 Globes, Sourlows

Least favorite track: Black Houses
