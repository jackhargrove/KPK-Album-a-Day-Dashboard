# Write-up

Prince Rodgers Nelson aka Prince aka The Artist Formerly Known as Prince was born to a musical family in 1958 Minneapolis. He had a tumultuous home life, moving between the homes of his mother and stepfather, father and eventually his neighbors' basement. In high school he was a three sport athlete and trained in classical ballet. His first taste of the music industry came when he was still in high school, when Prince's cousin in law recruited him to play guitar on several tracks for his band, 94 East. Soon after graduating, he created a demo tape good enough for Warner Bros Records to sign a 19 year old Prince to a three album deal. His first album, "For You" was a lukewarm success, but his self titled second album was a huge hit, reaching number 3 on the charts and garnering a platinum certification.

With his next two albums, "Dirty Mind" and "Controversy", Prince began to establish the public persona he would be known for including racy lyrics, racy and flamboyant outfits on stage and song titles spelled like teenage girls would text 20 years later. With his fifth album, "1999", he (along with MJ) managed to break the color barrier on MTV. Around this time, he also formed The Time, a funk band managed by him and fronted by Morris Day. The band's debut album was Prince's in all but name, he played every instrument and wrote the lyrics and vocal melodies for Morris Day. The band would release two more albums with similar levels of control from Prince before splitting up in 1985. The band would reunite twice to tour and release and album, both times without Prince running things.

"Purple Rain" was Prince's sixth album as well as the soundtrack for a movie of the same name. The movie starred Prince as a Minneapolis musician in a rivalry with Morris Day. The movie was a commercial success and did well critically, with Siskel and Ebert championing it while some more modern critics have viewed it as misogynistic. The album was better by far, topping the charts for 24 weeks, with two singles hitting number 1 and two more breaching the top 10. Critical reception was positive and has only grown more positive over time. When Tipper Gore (wife of Al) heard her 11 year old daughter listening to the highly explicit single "Darling Nikki", she was so outraged, she campaigned to start labelling inappropriate music. Dee Snider, John Denver and Frank Zappa were called to testify in front of Congress and the now ubiquitous "Parental Advisory Explicit Content" label (aka the "Tipper Sticker")  was created and slapped on most good albums. Around the same time, Andy Warhol also created an artwork of Prince to accompany a Vanity Fair article. This piece would go on to be the subject of a Supreme Court decision in 2023 restricting fair use.

Prince would follow "Purple Rain" with "Around the World in A Day", a more psychedelic album that would fail to reach the heights of "Purple Rain". His next album, "Parade" would soundtrack his second movie, which he also directed. This film, "Under the Cherry Moon" was universally panned and faced stiff competition from "Howard the Duck" for Worst Picture at the Razzies. After "Parade", Prince disbanded his backing band, which had started out nameless before coming to be known as "The Revolution"

His first album as a solo artist, "Sign o' The Times" was a critical hit and sold decently well. The album saw him shift away from pop rock towards R&B. Prince also released an accompanying concert film, which was better than "Under the Cherry Moon". He would cancel his next album, "The Black Album", after it had already been printed when he had a "spiritual epiphany". He instead released "Lovesexy", a far more positive album and went on a lavish tour to support it. He would release several more successful albums in the early 90s, including "Batman", "Graffiti Bridge" and "Diamonds and Pearls". For "Graffiti Bridge", he made another film and for "Diamonds and Pearls", he created a new backing band, "The New Power Generation". In 1992, on the success of "Diamonds and Pearls", he resigned for a massive six album deal with Warner Bros, which quickly ran into issues.

His first album on the new deal, known as "The Love Symbol", featured the eponymous symbol as the title. The album was a success, but fell short of expectations. In retaliation for Warner Bros releasing his back catalogue, Prince refused to go by Prince, instead using the aforementioned symbol, "The Artist" and most famously "The Artists Formerly Known as Prince". He would also go out in public with "slave" written on his right cheek. He churned out his new few albums in order to get out of his record deal, but by this time, he was well past his creative prime.

In 2000, Prince was Prince again, and started releasing his music over the internet. He has a minor comeback with "3121" in 2005, which was his first album to debut #1. In 2007, he performed at the Super Bowl in Miami, where the Colts beat the Bears. It is widely regarded as the greatest halftime show ever. In April of 2016, he canceled his ongoing tour, citing illness. The details surrounding this time period are hazy and mired in lawsuits, but it seems probable that he OD'd on opioids and then entered a rehab program.

The next week, he was found dead in his home, overdosed on fentanyl in his fake Vicodin pills. Prince left a vast estate and no will, causing a massive legal headache as his sister and five half-siblings squabbled over the money, cars and mansion. Prince was known to be a eccentric and prolific philanthropist, and after his death the full extent became known, including supporting libraries, hackathons, a ballet theater in Chicago, and the Afghani branch of the Boy Scouts. He remains an incredibly influential figure in pop music, both for his music and for his stage presence.

# Hargrove's review

I got into Prince quite late (even factoring in that I was born 16 years after this album came out). When the Apple Music top 100 albums of all time list dropped in early 2024 and generated so much discourse, it made me realize that there were a few massive, canonical albums that I just hadn’t listened to at all. I actually don’t know how I made it that far without having listened to Prince given that my dad loves him, but I would have only recognized “Raspberry Beret”, “1999”, “Little Red Corvette”, and maybe “Kiss” at that time. Not even “When Doves Cry”. So I listened to *Purple Rain* in May 2024 and was pretty quickly blown away. Sometimes it’s difficult to crack the appeal of monumental classics right away, especially with the pressure to “get it” like everyone else has. An album like Stevie Wonder’s *Songs in the Key of Life* scared me off with it’s sheer magnitude the first time I put it on (though I’d like to revisit it now that I’m much better at listening to music). This wasn’t the case with *Purple Rain* at all. The second the instrumental kicked in on “Let’s Go Crazy”, I understood immediately the appeal. The explicit lyrics of “Darling Nikki” scandalized me even in the 2020s as much as it must have Tipper Gore. By the time I got to “When Doves Cry” for the first time, I was filled with regret for having deprived myself of it for over two decades. What makes this album so genius is the way it makes grandiosity feel so effortlessly simple. There are huge, overwhelmingly emotional moments on this album: the guitar solo that ends “Let’s Go Crazy”, the instrumental explosions of “Darling Nikki”, the indelible chorus of “When Doves Cry”, the anthemic refrain of “Purple Rain”. And yet, the actual building blocks never feel alien or inaccessible, though every musician on the album was clearly a virtuoso. The simple funk grooves, catchy synth melodies, and perfect pop hooks just combine to create endless, infectious momentum. It’s all a testament to Prince’s songwriting and charisma. It’s also just remarkably consistent, especially compared to Michael Jackson’s albums of the same period (I love MJ, but he had some filler). Sure, “Baby I’m a Star” isn’t my favorite song ever, but “The Beautiful Ones” is the only track I could really do without. This is just about as close to a perfect pop album as you can get. And what a joy it is to listen to.

While I love that there’s a grand, iconic opening to this grand, iconic album, I always just want the eulogy in the opening to hurry up so I can get into the banger that is “Let’s Go Crazy” faster. God, this song rules so hard. It has maybe the catchiest, most infectious hook I’ve ever heard, and it’s just two simple lines. Beyond that, I’m always taken aback by the sheer momentum of the drums, Prince’s guitar acrobatics, and the fascinating outro. This track is essentially the aural representation of fun. And almost as a cherry on top, “We're all excited/But we don't know why/Maybe it's 'cause/We're all gonna die/And when we do/What's it all for?/You better live now/‘Fore the grim reaper come knocking on your door” is a better verse than you’re going to hear on just about any other pop album, too. “Take Me With U” starts with a lovely intro where the drums roll from ear to ear. It’s mellower than the last track, blissful but with no loss of momentum. The strings are lovely, and the duet works better than any comparable R&B. Sadly, “The Beautiful Ones” takes things even slower, and it goes overboard. I find the vocal melody and synths a bit too cheesy. Prince rarely gets lost in 80s schmaltz, but he lays it on too thick here. I appreciate Prince’s passion in the climax, but it’s not enough to save the song. Thankfully, the sheer sexual tension of the intro to “Computer Blue” adds back in the edge I need to cut through the atmosphere. After a stellar synth melody and pulsating bass drum, the track morphs into an invigorating jam that puts the album right back on track. While I love every solo, what really makes it all work for me is the hazy, dreamy soundscape. I haven’t seen the film, but I can see this working well in that setting. Side 1 closes with my favorite track, “Darling Nikki”. How can you start a track with “I knew a girl named Nikki/I guess you could say she was a sex fiend/I met her in a hotel lobby/Masturbating with a magazine/She said, ‘How'd you like to waste some time?’/And I could not resist when I saw little Nikki grind”? It’s so gross, sleazy, sweaty, funky. I love it. Prince perfectly uses quiet in the verses to build unreal tension that leads to a series of unbridled explosions. It all leads to an absolutely bonkers breakdown with rumbling thunder from the bass drum and piercing synths.

If it weren’t for “Darling Nikki”, “When Doves Cry” would be my favorite track. What a fucking song. What happens when a perfect melody meets gut-wrenching lyrics meets the synth line of a lifetime? Pop perfection. I can’t help myself but sing along to “How can you just leave me standing/Alone in a world that's so cold?/Maybe I'm just too demanding/Maybe I'm just like my father, too bold/Maybe you're just like my mother/She's never satisfied/Why do we scream at each other?/This is what it sounds like when doves cry” every time I hear it. The bangers keep coming with “I Would Die 4 U”. Prince uses these grooves so well to create nonstop energy, and the hook is catchy enough. It just fucking works. “Baby I’m a Star” is still catchy and fun, but this one maybe gets a little too bouncy for my tastes without the songwriting substance to justify it. It’s still fine to pad out the ending a little bit, but the formula loses its sheen a little bit. “Purple Rain” is obviously a fantastic song, and certainly a favorite. However, I don’t get how it’s the consensus best Prince song. In my opinion, it’s the fourth best song on this album (after “Darling Nikki”, “When Doves Cry”, and “Let’s Go Crazy”, in that order). The chorus is anthemic and appropriately grand, Prince expresses crazy amounts of emotion in the verses, and the guitar work in the extended outro clearly rules. But, man, there are just tracks I’d sooner listen to on this album.

I didn’t want to overdo it, but naturally I need to compare this album to MJ’s contribution to our list, *Bad*. I like that album a lot and currently have it in low A Tier, which I still feel is appropriate. “Smooth Criminal” and “Bad” can compete with any track on here. But *Purple Rain* is just clearly a cut above. It’s so consistently good throughout without sacrificing any highs. It’s just that good.

Favorite tracks: Let’s Go Crazy, Take Me with U, Computer Blue, Darling Nikki, When Doves Cry, I Would Die 4 U, Purple Rain

Least favorite track: The Beautiful Ones

# Aidan's review

Prince is really good at writing songs. He's not great at making movies and I'm not sold on basically any of his aesthetic preferences. But those flaws are completely irrelevant to how groovy "When Doves Cry" is. "Dig if you will this picture" is a baffling opening to a song, but Prince pulls it off with ease. No other songs is quite as good, but there's no stinkers on here either. "Darling Nikki" makes quite an impression with its lyrics and stop and go guitar solos, and "thank you for a funky time" is quite funny, but I think it's more fun for the novelty than the actual song. "Purple Rain" is no "Jungleland", but it's a fitting closer for the album and fully deserves the near 9 minutes. It's just really good pop rock. There was nothing that really moved me deeply, but I was quite happy listening to it throughout the week, both absentmindedly and intently. Hard to dislike this album.
