# Hargrove's review

I got into Prince quite late (even factoring in that I was born 16 years after this album came out). When the Apple Music top 100 albums of all time list dropped in early 2024 and generated so much discourse, it made me realize that there were a few massive, canonical albums that I just hadn’t listened to at all. I actually don’t know how I made it that far without having listened to Prince given that my dad loves him, but I would have only recognized “Raspberry Beret”, “1999”, “Little Red Corvette”, and maybe “Kiss” at that time. Not even “When Doves Cry”. So I listened to *Purple Rain* in May 2024 and was pretty quickly blown away. Sometimes it’s difficult to crack the appeal of monumental classics right away, especially with the pressure to “get it” like everyone else has. An album like Stevie Wonder’s *Songs in the Key of Life* scared me off with it’s sheer magnitude the first time I put it on (though I’d like to revisit it now that I’m much better at listening to music). This wasn’t the case with *Purple Rain* at all. The second the instrumental kicked in on “Let’s Go Crazy”, I understood immediately the appeal. The explicit lyrics of “Darling Nikki” scandalized me even in the 2020s as much as it must have Tipper Gore. By the time I got to “When Doves Cry” for the first time, I was filled with regret for having deprived myself of it for over two decades. What makes this album so genius is the way it makes grandiosity feel so effortlessly simple. There are huge, overwhelmingly emotional moments on this album: the guitar solo that ends “Let’s Go Crazy”, the instrumental explosions of “Darling Nikki”, the indelible chorus of “When Doves Cry”, the anthemic refrain of “Purple Rain”. And yet, the actual building blocks never feel alien or inaccessible, though every musician on the album was clearly a virtuoso. The simple funk grooves, catchy synth melodies, and perfect pop hooks just combine to create endless, infectious momentum. It’s all a testament to Prince’s songwriting and charisma. It’s also just remarkably consistent, especially compared to Michael Jackson’s albums of the same period (I love MJ, but he had some filler). Sure, “Baby I’m a Star” isn’t my favorite song ever, but “The Beautiful Ones” is the only track I could really do without. This is just about as close to a perfect pop album as you can get. And what a joy it is to listen to.

While I love that there’s a grand, iconic opening to this grand, iconic album, I always just want the eulogy in the opening to hurry up so I can get into the banger that is “Let’s Go Crazy” faster. God, this song rules so hard. It has maybe the catchiest, most infectious hook I’ve ever heard, and it’s just two simple lines. Beyond that, I’m always taken aback by the sheer momentum of the drums, Prince’s guitar acrobatics, and the fascinating outro. This track is essentially the aural representation of fun. And almost as a cherry on top, “We're all excited/But we don't know why/Maybe it's 'cause/We're all gonna die/And when we do/What's it all for?/You better live now/‘Fore the grim reaper come knocking on your door” is a better verse than you’re going to hear on just about any other pop album, too. “Take Me With U” starts with a lovely intro where the drums roll from ear to ear. It’s mellower than the last track, blissful but with no loss of momentum. The strings are lovely, and the duet works better than any comparable R&B. Sadly, “The Beautiful Ones” takes things even slower, and it goes overboard. I find the vocal melody and synths a bit too cheesy. Prince rarely gets lost in 80s schmaltz, but he lays it on too thick here. I appreciate Prince’s passion in the climax, but it’s not enough to save the song. Thankfully, the sheer sexual tension of the intro to “Computer Blue” adds back in the edge I need to cut through the atmosphere. After a stellar synth melody and pulsating bass drum, the track morphs into an invigorating jam that puts the album right back on track. While I love every solo, what really makes it all work for me is the hazy, dreamy soundscape. I haven’t seen the film, but I can see this working well in that setting. Side 1 closes with my favorite track, “Darling Nikki”. How can you start a track with “I knew a girl named Nikki/I guess you could say she was a sex fiend/I met her in a hotel lobby/Masturbating with a magazine/She said, ‘How'd you like to waste some time?’/And I could not resist when I saw little Nikki grind”? It’s so gross, sleazy, sweaty, funky. I love it. Prince perfectly uses quiet in the verses to build unreal tension that leads to a series of unbridled explosions. It all leads to an absolutely bonkers breakdown with rumbling thunder from the bass drum and piercing synths.

If it weren’t for “Darling Nikki”, “When Doves Cry” would be my favorite track. What a fucking song. What happens when a perfect melody meets gut-wrenching lyrics meets the synth line of a lifetime? Pop perfection. I can’t help myself but sing along to “How can you just leave me standing/Alone in a world that's so cold?/Maybe I'm just too demanding/Maybe I'm just like my father, too bold/Maybe you're just like my mother/She's never satisfied/Why do we scream at each other?/This is what it sounds like when doves cry” every time I hear it. The bangers keep coming with “I Would Die 4 U”. Prince uses these grooves so well to create nonstop energy, and the hook is catchy enough. It just fucking works. “Baby I’m a Star” is still catchy and fun, but this one maybe gets a little too bouncy for my tastes without the songwriting substance to justify it. It’s still fine to pad out the ending a little bit, but the formula loses its sheen a little bit. “Purple Rain” is obviously a fantastic song, and certainly a favorite. However, I don’t get how it’s the consensus best Prince song. In my opinion, it’s the fourth best song on this album (after “Darling Nikki”, “When Doves Cry”, and “Let’s Go Crazy”, in that order). The chorus is anthemic and appropriately grand, Prince expresses crazy amounts of emotion in the verses, and the guitar work in the extended outro clearly rules. But, man, there are just tracks I’d sooner listen to on this album.

I didn’t want to overdo it, but naturally I need to compare this album to MJ’s contribution to our list, *Bad*. I like that album a lot and currently have it in low A Tier, which I still feel is appropriate. “Smooth Criminal” and “Bad” can compete with any track on here. But *Purple Rain* is just clearly a cut above. It’s so consistently good throughout without sacrificing any highs. It’s just that good.

Favorite tracks: Let’s Go Crazy, Take Me with U, Computer Blue, Darling Nikki, When Doves Cry, I Would Die 4 U, Purple Rain

Least favorite track: The Beautiful Ones
