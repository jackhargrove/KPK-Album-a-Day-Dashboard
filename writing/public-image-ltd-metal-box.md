# Write-up

Public Image Ltd. (PiL) is a British post-punk band formed in London in 1978 who at the time of this album’s recording consisted of vocalist and pianist John Lydon, guitarist Keith Levene, bassist Jah Wobble (real name John Wardle), and a succession of drummers including David Humphrey, Richard Dudanski, and Martin Atkins. Since 1975, Lydon, under the name Johnny Rotten, had been lead singer of the pioneering UK punk band Sex Pistols. By 1977, the band was one of the most popular in the UK, and punk quickly dominated the country’s music scene. However, Lydon quickly grew frustrated with the fame and especially the image of the band kept by their manager Malcolm McLaren; as a result, he left the band in early 1978, causing them to break up. After a trip to Jamaica where record producer Richard Branson tried to get him to join American new wave band Devo, Lydon returned to London to put together a new band. He recruited Jah Wobble, an old school friend, to learn to play bass. Keith Levene, original guitarist of the other most important UK punk band the Clash, joined on guitar. Their first drummer was Jim Walker, who had responded to an advertisement. Lydon named the band Public Image Ltd. after a 1968 novel about an actress with a jealous husband. Lydon’s affinity with the novel was a thinly veiled outlet for his feelings about Sex Pistols manager Malcolm McLaren. PiL released its debut album *Public Image: First Issue* at the end of 1978. It was controversial at the time, but is now remembered for helping to kick off the post-punk era in British music.

The band was unstable (“PiL was the simple thing of four different people doing different drugs at different time”, according to Wobble), and Walker left in early 1979. The band struggled to replace him with a consistent drummer, resulting in their next album featuring contributions from three different drummers. That album, *Metal Box*, was released in its unique metal packaging in 1979. It was re-released the next year in more standard packaging as *Second Edition*. It further pushed post-punk forward, particularly with its heavy influence from dub reggae that would inspire the Clash and new wave. The track “Swan Lake” had originally been released as a single under the title “Death Disco”, but was renamed in honor of the melodic line taken from Tchaikovsky’s ballet of the same name. *Metal Box* is now commonly cited as one of the very best albums of post-punk. PiL toured the US in 1980, consistently antagonizing audiences to stir up controversy and trouble at shows.

Wobble left the band in 1980, going on to collaborate heavily with Holger Czukay and Jaki Liebezeit of CAN in the 80s, and Brian Eno and Evan Parker (saxophonist on *Machine Gun*) in the 90s. Lydon chose not to recruit a new bassist, and third album *The Flowers of Romance* in 1981 had no bass. Levene left in 1983, leaving Lydon as the only remaining original member. He took the band in a pop direction, capitalizing on the new wave, synthpop, and dance music styles of the 80s, earning a top 5 hit in the UK in 1983 with “This Is Not a Love Song”. PiL continued in this direction under Lydon and a revolving cast of sidemen until going on hiatus in 1992. Lydon reformed the band in 2009, and they’ve been playing ever since. Keith Levene died in 2022 at age 65.

# Hargrove's review

PiL is probably a top 20 all-time band for my dad. He glazes them to the point that I refused to listen on principle because he was so annoying, like with the Cure. Again like the Cure, he was unfortunately bang-on correct about them making great music. This album is really quite good. It’s definitely the best pure post-punk album I’ve ever heard, better than Wire or Gang of Four or Joy Division. And I quite like all three of those bands! Gang of Four feels like the most directly comparable because both take a similar dance-punk approach. I don’t want to pit them against each other, as I listened to *Entertainment!* earlier today and it is a damn good album. But I think what makes *Metal Box* work for me just that much more is that it doesn’t treat danceability like an academic exercise. I never think to myself “wow, that sure is an interesting way of incorporating a disco trope into punk”. Instead, I just feel it in the bass line, in the spacey synths, in the off-beat hi-hat cymbals. Everything just works on this album. Wobble’s bass lines obviously steal the show most of the time, turning simple, repetitive melodies into endlessly bouncy and energetic lines mixed above everything else. Levene’s guitar provides another important element. I’ve never heard anything quite like it. Instead of playing riffs or chordal rhythms, he goes for something much more impressionistic, kicking the treble up to maximum and playing shimmering arpeggios completely out of step with the bass rhythm. It’s like Johnny Marr without the jangle. And of course there’s Lydon, whose Sex Pistols snarl I worried would turn me off. Instead, he also opts for something more experimental, delivering surprisingly poignant improvised lyrics in a sprawling, unorganized, almost disinterested manner. While they’re not a highlight, the drums are surprisingly tight for an album that went through three drummers. My main complaint with this album is its length; the first half is entirely heater after heater after heater, while the second half is a bit more inconsistent. That being said, most of the second half is good too, so realistically I would maybe only want this album trimmed down to like 50 minutes. Otherwise, this album rules.

The album starts with “Albatross”, a 10 minute behemoth of a song with some choice words for Malcolm McLaren. While the track risks feeling aimless, instead it comes across to me as a hypnotic, expressionist masterpiece. There’s the entrancing bass line that sets the foundation, some truly unique guitar work from Levene, and lyrics that emotionally cut deep. What a way to start an album. “Memories” is another banger. This time, the bass line is even more driving, setting a relentless pace for the rest of the song. Lydon’s chilling vocals perfectly complement the scathing lyrics that seem to be aimed at himself. The lines “I think you're slightly late” and particularly “It's not the movies/And you're old“ hit me hard. The off-beat cymbals serve to heighten everything else, as does the constant switching between takes of differing production value. More than anything else, it’s just a tense song. It feels so tightly wound. The hits keep coming with “Swan Lake”. I did not go into this album expecting such a touching take on death of a loved one from Johnny fucking Rotten. The off-beat cymbals work as well here as they do in actual disco at making me want to move. I love not only the use of the Tchaikovsky melody but also the numerous ways that Levene just breaks it down into its constituent parts throughout the instrumental. And, of course, the bass line is as bouncy as ever. While the last three songs have been great, “Poptones” tops them all to take my top spot for the album. The bass line is just UNBELIEVABLE, especially when it modulates a half step higher. Levene plays shimmering high notes in a different rhythm entirely, while Lydon snarls sparingly and tastefully. Even the drums provide nicely crashing cymbals. The track is long, but it goes on and on in the best way. I always hope it will never end.

None of the other tracks quite touch the immaculate run of the first four, but many are still quite good. “Careering” has one of the better vocal melodies, as well as a super cool synth pad part that creates a strong, ominous atmosphere that gives a sci-fi experimental atmosphere to the whole track. While the takedown of the suburbs in “No Birds” may not be anything new, I do rather like Lydon’s writing here: “Well intentioned rules/To dignify a daily code/Lawful order standard views/This could be heaven”. Wobble’s bass line is so strong that it allows Levene’s guitar and Lydon’s vocals to become impressionistic to the point of just adding color. We are halfway through this double album and it’s still on an absolute heater. “Graveyard” is a bit short, but man, is it still good. “The Suit” is the album’s first real miss, and probably my least favorite track. Lydon comes across as a bit of a dick and that vocal melody sounds slightly annoying as well. What really damns the track is the fact that the bass line is just not that good either. “Bad Baby” brings things back a little bit. It’s not the greatest song in the world, but I do appreciate its more experimental textures, particularly with the synth. “Socialist” expands on this further, applying those experimental textures to an amazingly composition this time. The atmosphere on this track is so cool, approaching the cavernous echo of dub. I love the weird shape shifting manipulation of the cymbals, the driving and melodic bass line, and the off-kilter synthesized sounds. It’s a very cinematic track. “Chant” is my second-least favorite on the album because of how grating the “Mob! War! Kill! Hate!” chant gets over time. “Radio 4”, on the other hand, closes the album masterfully. I’ve never really heard a synth used in this, approximating waltz or even polka. Wobble just goes crazy on it as well, and the song stretches on for the perfect amount of time.

I was expecting to enjoy this album, probably stick it somewhere in B Tier. Instead, I’ve really become a fan of it. It’s so simple, yet so endlessly relistenable. I anticipate liking it even more as I continue listening, but only time will tell. Until then, I’m content saying it’s only pretty goddamn good.

Favorite tracks: Albatross, Memories, Swan Lake, Poptones, Careering, No Birds, Graveyard, Socialist, Radio 4

Least favorite track: The Suit
