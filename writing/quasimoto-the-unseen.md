# Write-up

Quasimoto is an abstract hip hop “duo” consisting of producer Madlib (real name Otis Jackson Jr.) and his high-pitched rapping alter ego Lord Quas. Madlib was born in 1973 in Oxnard, California, where he was also raised. He came from a musical family, as both his parents and younger brother (named Michael Jackson, but no not that one) are musicians and  his uncle is jazz trumpeter Jon Faddis. He learned how to take a sample from a vinyl record as a young child and got really into hip hop production. He was also completely obsessed with jazz, which would have a major impact on his production. In the early 90s, Madlib formed an underground hip hop collective with his brother and some friends in Oxnard named CDP (Crate Diggas Palace, crate digging = looking through crates of records for good samples). He also did freelance production for local hip hop groups throughout the 90s. In 1993, he formed the hip hop group Lootpack with Wildchild and DJ Romes. This group was discovered by LA-based DJ and producer Peanut Butter Wolf, who signed them to his independent label Stones Throw Records in 1998 and they released their debut album in 1999. Peanut Butter Wolf encouraged Madlib to rap more in addition to producing, but Madlib hated the sound of his deep voice. He began recording himself rapping very slowly and then speeding up the recordings, resulting is a very high-pitched voice. He built an entire character around this voice named Lord Quas; though he would eventually come to be fleshed out visually as a cartoonish humanoid creature with a long snout, he was initially “unseen”. Madlib recorded his debut record *The Unseen* in 2000, credited to Quasimoto. Throughout the 2000s, Madlib would become one of the central figures in underground rap production. He’s most well-known for his collaborative albums with other rappers, most notably *Madvillainy* with MF DOOM in 2004. *Madvillainy* is not only on this list, but considered by many to be one of the greatest albums of all time (it currently sits at the #7 highest-ranked album of all time on RYM). It also contains two features from Lord Quas. He also made collaborative albums with J Dilla (whose album *Donuts* is on this list and is a personal favorite of mine) and Talib Kweli in the 2000s and with Freddie Gibbs in the 2010s. He has also done production work for artists like Snoop Dogg, Erykah Badu, and De La Soul. He was a common fixture in Kanye West’s Hawaii compound during the recording of *My Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy*, but none of his production made it on the album; he did produce “No More Parties in LA” on *The Life of Pablo* in 2016. Madlib would release another album as Quasimoto in 2005, but it would not receive the same acclaim as much of his other work. Fun fact: his house burned down in the LA fires earlier this year!

*The Unseen* was Madlib’s first solo release and most listener’s first introduction to his unique sound. Famously, Madlib locked himself away and continuously tripped on mushrooms for a month while making this album. The result is a very psychedelic feel, with Madlib trading verses back and forth with his own alter ego and samples introducing new sounds regularly. This album is one of the first documents of the nerdy jazz-rap style that would define 2000s underground hip hop. Madlib names dozens of jazz artists who inspired him on the track “Jazz Cats Pt. 1”. He also reflects on his early 90s boom bap hip hop influences on the track “Boom Music”. Madlib says much of the atmosphere was inspired by the soundtrack of the 1973 French animated film *Fantastic Planet* (great movie highly recommend), which is sampled directly on “Come on Feet”. Blaxploitation film director Melvin Van Peebles is also sampled numerous times across the record. “Low Class Conspiracy” was used on the soundtrack of the video game *Tony Hawk’s Underground*. *The Unseen* was a huge critical success at the time, setting up Madlib for the insane run he would have in the 2000s.

# Hargrove's review

Yesterday we had psychedelic jazz, today we have psychedelic hip hop; what ever happened to good, old-fashioned psychedelic ROCK N ROLL?????? I’m a huge fan of *Madvillainy*, got into it high school and its been a consistent listen for me over the past 10 or so years. It was my sleep album for like 4 months of 2024, which is a high honor for an album (my sleep album for most of this year so far has been David Bowie’s *Low*, with The Cure’s *Pornography* and Talking Heads’ *Remain in Light* having brief stints). So I had warm feelings toward Madlib going into this, and I also had some familiarity with the Lord Quas character as he gets two tracks for himself on *Madvillainy*. Imagine my surprise when I found this album nearly impossible to crack on my first two listens. The style was uncannily familiar, so many elements I liked were there, and yet I couldn’t get into any groove! It wasn’t until my third listen that I finally started enjoying some of what I was hearing, and now at the conclusion of listen six my opinion has stabilized and I would say that I like this album. However, I don’t love it. I can picture myself listening to this album at release in 2000 and being absolutely blown away. It really is quite weird! The Lord Quas voice is really out there, the self-duets are weird, the samples are trippy and disorienting, it’s a very singular style. Or at least it was. Just a few years later, this style would be done many times, and much better at that. Typically, I’m deferential to chronology: I don’t like to ding something because I’ve heard later music drive it into the ground. Here, though, the stuff that is to come is just a complete improvement on the style in every way. There is pretty much no aspect of this album that is better than *Madvillainy*. It’s also just too long; 24 tracks, over an hour, it really outstays its welcome. But man, are the vibes good, and are some tracks really something. The following are some of the standouts, in my opinion. “Microphone Mathematics” is the first true banger of the album, I love the repeated sax sample that goes back and forth from ear to ear, the second verse is well-executed lyrically, and the rhythm section in the samples is sublime. “Basic Instinct” continues the heat off the strength of its bass line alone. After a few so-so tracks (including the most popular “Low Class Conspiracy”, which confuses me as it did not capture my fancy much at all), “Return of the Loop Digga” begins the strongest section of the album. I love its hook, which is almost arhythmic but somehow flows very smoothly, and the skit in the middle over some nice jazz is quite funny. “Real Eyes” is a candidate for my favorite track overall; the flute alone is just orgasmic, but the bass is also sooooo smooooth, the interplay between Quas and Madlib on the verses is perfectly done, and the lyrics are above average. “Come On Feet” is the competition for my favorite track. The *Fantastic Planet* sample is used so perfectly and it gives an amazingly eerie vibe. The story told in the verses is super evocative and inspires this tired, bleak, weary mood. And the vocal samples are expertly placed. Amazing song. “Boom Music” is a consistent banger, with the guitar sample and the bells (?) as highlights. “Green Power” is incredible and its vibe reminds me of “Frontier Psychiatrist” by The Avalanches. The bass line is bouncy, the xylophone (?) is awesome, and the turntablism is a welcome texture. “The Unseen” is a late album gem with its gorgeous vocal sample and delayed chord change. The album closes on a high note, with both “Axe Puzzles” and “Discipline 99 Pt. 1” as good as any other track. But man, does the quality overall taper off near the end. This album did not need to be this long. Could have done with at least 5 tracks being cut. I will close with this though: there is something infectious about the good vibes here. Madlib’s love for music is palpable, and I love it when others love music.

Favorite tracks: Microphone Mathematics, Basic Instinct, Return of the Loop Digga, Real Eyes, Come On Feet, Boom Music, Green Power, The Unseen, Axe Puzzles, Discipline 99 Pt. 1

Least favorite track: Phony Game
