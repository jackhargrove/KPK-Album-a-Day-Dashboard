# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Queens of the Stone Age is a hard rock group founded by Josh Homme in 1996 in Palm Desert, California (inland Southern California). While the only consistent member of the group is Josh Homme on lead vocals and guitar, the lineup for this album also consisted of Nick Oliveri on bass and lead vocals, Mark Lanegan on lead vocals (yes, three lead vocalists, they alternate between the songs), and Dave Grohl on drums (on the off-chance that you are not already aware, Dave Grohl was the drummer for Nirvana). Unusual for a rock album, there were also a significant number of featured musicians on this album, but I’ll get more into that in the next paragraph. Josh Homme grew up in Palm Desert, California as part of a well-to-do settler family. He started his first group, a heavy metal outfit named Kyuss, as a 14 year old in 1987; Kyuss’ 1994 album *Welcome to Sky Valley* is on the metal section of this list. Kyuss broke up in 1995, and Homme moved to Seattle for a change of scenery. While in Seattle, Homme became very involved in the grunge scene and started a new band named Gamma Ray. Gamma Ray became Queens of the Stone Age; despite being all male, they chose “Queens” instead of “Kings” because “rock should be heavy enough for the boys and sweet enough for the girls.” Homme moved back to Palm Desert in 1998 and Queens of the Stone Age released their self-titled debut album that same year to very little success or recognition. However, their sophomore record *Rated R* in 2000 had two modest hit single and put QotSA on the map. Their third album, 2002’s *Songs for the Deaf*, was a critical and commercial hit and turned the band into stars. QotSA released two more albums in 2005 and 2007 before taking a 6 year hiatus that culminated in Homme nearly dying from complications of a surgery. After recovering, QotSA released another album in 2013 to critical acclaim, with two more albums in 2017 and 2023. It should be noted that Josh Homme has struggled with drug abuse for much of his life, and the rumor mill has it that his life has spun out of control over the last 5 years or so.

*Songs for the Deaf* was the record that made QotSA famous and is still today regarded as their signature album. It’s a concept album of a road trip that starts in Los Angeles and ends in Joshua Tree in the California desert; the concept is told through consistent radio interludes that let the listener know which town they’re passing through, and the concept took heavy inspiration from the movies of David Lynch. Dave Grohl joined QotSA as an official member in 2001 and it was his first time drumming since Nirvana ended when Kurt Cobain killed himself in 1994. He left the group after the album was completed. The album also features many guest musicians, too many to name here but the most notable of whom is Dean Ween, guitarist for the band Ween whose album *The Mollusk* is on this list. Josh Homme describes the music on this album and subsequent QotSA albums as “rock versions of electronic music”, taking heavy influence from the hypnotic repetition of the trance genre.

Queens of the Stone Age are the most well-known band to come out of the Palm Desert scene in the 1990s. The Palm Desert scene, also known as desert rock, was a scene that pioneered stoner rock and stoner metal in the deserts of Southern California. The bands in the scene would get really high on weed and jam for a long time to make music. There was also a heavy focus on psychedelics, particularly LSD and peyote. The most notable music elements of desert rock are the loud and grinding guitar riffs, the sludge grooves (sludge metal is a genre that combines doom metal and hardcore punk), and the jamming.  Another well-known Palm Desert scene band are The Eagles of Death Metal.

# Hargrove's review

Disclaimer up front, album I’ve heard before, album I already really liked. I often don’t like the hard rock aesthetic for the same reason I don’t like the prog rock aesthetic, it just takes itself too seriously. However, maybe it’s the stoner aspect of things, but I never find Queens of the Stone Age to be guilty of that sin. One of the adjectives I chose to describe this album was “playful”, and I very much stand by that. The album opens with a hidden instrumental track that starts from silence, a literal “song for the deaf”. They constantly make fun of themselves throughout the album, the radio interlude where the DJ says they’ll never be millionaires comes to mind. The radio interludes in general are pretty funny and help prevent the music from becoming overbearing. The lyrics that describe pretty intense drug experiences are . I also really enjoy the concept, I think it’s one of the best executed concept albums of all time. I find the vast majority of concept albums to be lacking in the concept department; *Deltron 3030*’s story was a mess for example, or in *Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band* the Beatles drop the fake band conceit after two songs. This album, on the other hand, does a *fantastic* job of representing a drive into the desert at night. The album starts off with some fairly straightforward and tight pop rock songs like “No One Knows” and “First It Giveth” and “Song for the Dead” as we’re leaving LA. As we start to get deeper and deeper into the desert, the songs get a little bit more abstract, the structures a little looser, the sounds a little harsher, the concepts a little headier. We have left civilization and are forced to contemplate as the environment turns to sand around us. It’s a really interesting trajectory for an album; you typically expect to start lost and end up found. But here, you enter with your wits about you, and leave completely adrift.

The guitar riffs on this album are fucking fantastic. They really do put me into a trance as they repeat, they’re so loud and gritty that your mind has to contextualize them to keep listening. “No One Knows” is the archetypal example of their guitar work; the riff is relentless and bluesy, singlehandedly turning this song into a banger’s banger’s banger’s banger. Phenomenal drums from Grohl as well, and Homme’s voice is my favorite of the three vocalists. “Song for the Dead” is phenomenal, it’s the most varied track on the album by far as you get all these breaks with new riffs and noodles to enjoy, as well as call-and-response guitars in the middle. It’s also Dave Grohl’s finest performance on the album, although the drums are great throughout (Grohl is such a talented drummer, shame he’s a much less interesting bandleader). “The Sky Is Fallin’” has always been a personal favorite track of this album for me, and that disgusting guitar riff is very much the reason. Another Homme vocal performance, and the stop-start nature of the song just captures and holds my attention like no other. “Hanging Tree” is another favorite riff of mine, but what steals the show for me in this track is the vocal melody and chords in the chorus. It’s really unnerving to me and stands out for that reason. “Mosquito Song” is a hidden track tacked on to the end of the album and, as such, is our prize for making it to Joshua Tree. And what a disturbing prize it is. Dean Ween’s guitar (12-string I believe) sounds like nothing I’ve really heard before, it sounds almost baroque but Arabic at the same time. Homme’s vocals are heartbreaking, and the theremin that starts a third of the way through is haunting, as is the piano later. Compare this song to where we started with “You Think I Ain’t Worth a Dollar…” or “No One Knows” and you’ll see how far we’ve unraveled.

Thems above are my absolute favorites, but there are plenty of other great tracks. “You Think I Ain’t Worth a Dollar…” has just a killer riff to start the album, but Nick Oliver’s growls steal the show. I also love love love the pregnant pause. “First It Giveth” is probably my least favorite of the banger tracks on this album, but it’s still quite good. I feel like it gives an explicit focus to these Biblical vibes you get elsewhere in the album (we are in teh stone age, after all). “Go with the Flow” is heavy and driving, and another stellar performance from Grohl on drums. “Gonna Leave You” is a pretty deep-into-the-desert song, and its sonic concept is one of the more interesting as a result. I love them playing around with pop, especially the Beatles-y harmonies on the chorus. “God Is in the Radio” is so bluesy and so oppressive, I feel like that distorted guitar is smothering me in my sleep. “Song for the Deaf” has my favorite chorus, Homme sounds like he’s on the edge, desperate with nothing to lose. Great guitars too.

The main thing I don’t love about this album is the length. God damn the CD era for letting bands fit more than an hour of music on one disc. This wouldn’t be that much of a problem if I loved every song. Unfortunately, there are a few I don’t love. “Six Shooter” is just annoying, feels like a short interlude that’s there for shock value and I really hate it. “Do It Again” is just a little too anthemic for me, almost the hard rock equivalent of stomp-clap-hey, I don’t hate it but I could do just fine without it. I also don’t care for “Another Love Song”, the idea of it feels a little half-baked, and the chorus is uninspired. Overall though, I can deal with a few songs that are good but not great (except for “Six Shooter”, get it out of here).

Overall, what I really like about this album is the same thing I really like about Sleep’s *Dopesmoker*. This stoner shit is great at turning the low-stakes into the high-stakes. Both use drugged-out mania to turn life as a layabout into an epic tale worthy of crushingly loud riffs and gothic singing. But they’re also both great about reminding you that the stakes are actually low instead of earnestly trying to convince you that they’re high! Sleep achieves this with their weed portmanteaus (“weedian”, “Marijuanaut”), and QotSA does it with the radio interludes and self-deprecation. And both are just extremely evocative to me, they pull me into their worlds and I buy what they sell me wholeheartedly. These qualities are what separate Queens of the Stone Age from similar bands that I don’t care for, like the Foo Fighters. I love this album. I listened to *Rated R* (the QotSA album before this one) today and I loved it too.

Favorite tracks: No One Knows, Song for the Dead, The Sky Is Fallin’, Hanging Tree, Go with the Flow, Gonna Leave You, Song for the Deaf, Mosquito Song

Least favorite track: Six Shooter

# Aidan's review

There’s a lot going against this album, it’s coming between an album that I absolutely loved hearing for the first time and an album I’ve loved for years, and I did not have the most positive reaction on listening to it the first time I went through the list. That being said, I was pleasantly surprised, especially with the back half. The chugging guitars that start with a vengeance the moment the first radio skit ends certainly fit the tempo of a road trip, but as I found out with Dopesmoker, they’re not really my thing. No One Knows would sound great on a radio, the guitars are catchy, and it sounds great aurally, but the punchy guitars have been done six ways to Sunday by bands from the White Stripes to the Fratellis. Broadly, a lot of the songs lean on a riff or two, which makes them feel longer than they are (just like driving in LA traffic amiright hahahah). The skits are funny, (and authentic), but don’t lend themselves to relistening. The middle of the album really struggles with Six Shooter especially, but once we hit Go With The Flow, we’re out of the traffic and hit smooth sailing, with the great cuts of Do It Again and Mosquito Song all shying away from the chugging guitars of the first half, and doing more with lyrics. God Is In The Radio is stylistically similar to the first half, but I enjoyed it a lot more when the chugging guitars are one song long instead of four. I also found the lyrics more evocative than anything in the first half. Overall the album felt to me like an hour on the road, once is fine, but after repeats it started to grate a bit. Not horrible, but not something I think I’ll return to for a while.
