# Write-up

See above for the write-up I did on *Kid A* back in March. After the controversial release of *Kid A* in 2000, which saw both severe criticism as well as severe praise, Radiohead released its fourth album *Amnesiac* in 2001. It was largely a collection of unreleased tracks from the *Kid A* sessions, making the two albums spiritual siblings. *Amnesiac* is Radiohead’s most underrated album imo and it’s very worth checking out if you like *Kid A*. Before too long, the mixed reaction to Radiohead’s new sound became strongly positive. They remained one of the most acclaimed and commercially successful bands in the world. After the shock to the system of *Kid A*, Radiohead had only one card left to play: release some fucking dogshit. I’m kidding. It’s fine. It’s okay. It’s not great. Radiohead returned to the more firmly rock-grounded work of *OK Computer* with their sixth album, 2003’s *Hail to the Thief*. Except instead of being an intricately designed, eargasmic exploration of the paranoia of modern life, it was an overlong collection of so-so Coldplay stadium rockers about how much Thom Yorke hated George W. Bush. The singles “There There” and “2 + 2 = 5” are both quite good, very solid Radiohead work, but it’s really just kind of a bummer album. After their time working with electronic music, they just couldn’t find the right balance with their rock inclinations. Additionally, *Hail to the Thief* finished Radiohead’s six album deal that they had signed with their label EMI twelve years prior in 1991. They decided to take a break. Thom went off to record a solo album of electronica, while Jonny made some banger film scores for Paul Thomas Anderson and cameo’d in a Harry Potter movie. Clearly, they were at an artistic peak.

Radiohead began work on what would become *In Rainbows* in 2004, but they couldn’t get anything right. They went through multiple producers, and their own management even suggested they break up because things weren’t working. They finally broke through in late 2006 when they returned to Nigel Godrich, the man who had produced all of their albums since *The Bends* and is often considered an honorary member of the band. In reaction to the mistakes of their previous album, they chose to make *In Rainbows* a tight 45 minutes and feature personal, existential lyrics instead of political themes. They also sought inspiration in a wide variety of sources: the art rock of Lou Reed and David Bowie, the pop rock of the Beatles and Fleetwood Mac, the minimalism of Steve Reich (“Weird Fishes/Arpeggi”), the trance EDM of Surgeon, hell, even the electroclash of Peaches (yes, the “sucking on my titties like you wanted me” lady) on “15 Step”. Perhaps more than anything else, however, was the influence of krautrock bands like NEU! and Faust. Godrich got the band out of their funk with the idea to pre-record drums and bass lines and have them compose the songs on top of them, as if they were sampling themselves. This krautrock-inspired tape manipulation technique finally allowed Radiohead to perfect their hybrid of rock and electronica, synthesizing *OK Computer* with *Kid A*. They also finally succesfully recorded “Nude”, a fan favorite song they had been playing in concert since the 90s and originally attempted to record for *OK Computer*. As Radiohead finished up the album in 2007, a private equity firm purchased their label EMI and begged Radiohead to release the album on their label. Righteously disgusted, Radiohead instead decided on something radical: they would self-release *In Rainbows* online in pay-as-you-want format. If you want to pay $0, as ~60% of listeners did, you can. If you want to pay $10, you can. If you want to pay $100, you can. This first-of-its-kind model proved highly controversial, drawing praise from Jay-Z and Courtney Love but condemnation from Trent Reznor and Lily Allen. *In Rainbows* released in 2007 to considerable critical and commercial success.

After *In Rainbows*, Radiohead kind of dicked around for 4 years before recording their eighth album *The King of Limbs* in 2011. It was another radical stylistic departure, this time to experimental bleep bloop sampled and looped electronica. It is best forgotten. They dicked around for another 5 years, with Jonny catching the film bug and making score after score for Paul Thomas Anderson and the whole band writing music for the James Bond film *Spectre*. In 2016, they released their ninth and most recent album *A Moon Shaped Pool*. Similar to “Nude” on *In Rainbows*, they used this album to nail down recordings of some other old songs, including the legendary “True Love Waits” which was first played in concert more than 20 years prior in 1995 but had never been recorded for official release. It’s really good too. The whole album is. It’s kind of like a softer, sadder *In Rainbows*. Really sad. Since then, Radiohead has just dicked around for 10 years now. It’s gotten bad. Fans are so bored that they’ve gotten lost in a kind of a proxy argument about Israel and Palestine. Oh well. They got inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2019, and recently started playing concerts again. I’d love to see them. I hope they play “Creep”.

# Hargrove's review

This album is just so good. It has to be one of the most consistent albums of all time. The average song quality is quite high, there are no bad songs, and everything is packaged into a neat and tidy 40 minute single LP. It distills everything that makes Radiohead good into the perfect sales pitch. *OK Computer* was my first Radiohead love and one of the first full albums I got into in general. *Kid A* has some of my favorite individual tracks, like “How to Disappear Completely” and “Idioteque”, and helped me to embrace more electronic music than I previously had. I love both albums, though I definitely consider *OK Computer* superior. But even then, neither can touch *In Rainbows*. I’ve always been drawn to some level of moderation in music. My favorite artists, favorite albums, and favorite songs are often ones that take some new innovation, some experimental sound, and mold it into something that resembles the conventions of popular music. David Bowie is a great example of this: “It's not who does it first, it's who does it second, as he once said. While *In Rainbows* doesn’t perfectly fit into this exact mold, it has the same spirit. It perfectly combines Radiohead’s rock roots with their middle-period electronic experimentation. This is obviously not an observation unique to me, but man is it hard to dispute. No other album blends sounds so disparate this seamlessly. And the result is deeply human. It’s so warm, so lush, so dense. It’s a rainforest of an album. It’s damn good.

“15 Step” has got to be one of the greatest opening tracks of all time. What a crazy banger with such relentless, driving forward motion. I find that no music sounds anywhere near as fast as an uptempo song in 5/4 (see also: Sufjan Stevens’ “Come On! Feel the Illinoise!”). Jonny finds perhaps the most beautiful, heavenly guitar tone I’ve ever heard on this track, and it contrasts so perfectly with the cold electronic beat. For whatever reason I’ve always considered “Bodysnatchers” my least favorite track, but this week has reversed my opinion there in a major way. It continues from “15 Step” by keeping the pace high. I really enjoy the bridge vocal section, particularly “Has the light gone out for you?/Because the light’s gone out for me/It is the 21st century”. I was talking some shit about “Nude” a few days ago, but I’ve come back around on it again. It’s still not one of my very favorites, but that bass line is undeniable. It also has one of my favorite vocal deliveries from Thom; the disillusionment on “Don’t get any big ideas/They’re not gonna happen” is palpable. I do think the instrumental outro is a letdown though. “Weird Fishes/Arpeggi” is crazy, crazy, good. The rhythmic disconnection between the guitar arpeggios and the drums just sounds so good to me. It makes the arpeggios sound like they’re floating, endless. The song builds and builds into the perfect anti-climax when all but the arpeggios drop out as Thom delivers the chorus. In contrast to “Nude”, it then has a brilliant instrumental outro. Definitely the best song on the album. “All I Need” is one that doesn’t generally stick with me, but I was digging it this time around. The low-pass synth sounds so warm in a very Daft Punk way. The song pre-explosion is only so good but the explosion makes it all worth it, particularly those crashing cymbals.

I’ve always had a soft spot for “Faust Arp”. You know I’m a sucker for strings and shifting time signatures. The acoustic guitar sound sounds gorgeous on its own too though, and Thom’s vocal rhythm is infectiously catchy. “Reckoner” is almost on the level of “Weird Fishes”. The syncopated ride cymbal rhythm creates so much heft, while the guitar feels frantic but like it’s overextending itself. The instrumental intro uses these elements to create so much tension off the bat, then executes the drop out and build from the chorus masterfully. “Because we separate like ripples on a blank shore” makes for a fitting lyrical climax for the whole album, while the melancholy string coda dissipates the track’s sublime beauty. “House of Cards:” has definitely been my least favorite track on this series of listens. It’s still good, but it grinds everything to a halt in the same way that “Nude” does but without the same intricacy or tenderness of that track. “Jigsaw Falling Into Place” is another track that hasn’t really stuck with me in the past but I’ve really fallen in love with it here. It’s the final burst of high tempo drumming, plus I love the ascending guitar lines between the verses. The final verse is actually orgasmic; that synth line is ridiculous. I also love how vivid Thom’s lyrics about a night out are. I’ve always liked “Videotape”. The piano is relentless and just so sad, Thom’s lyrics are fantastic, the drums sound like a tape disintegrating, and I love that the album ends on a low note.

*In Rainbows* is the most solid offering from one of history’s most solid bands. There are certainly album I personally like more, but, as a consensus pick, I think it will have legs for a long time.

Favorite tracks: 15 Step, Bodysnatchers, Nude, Weird Fishes/Arpeggi, All I Need, Faust Arp, Reckoner, Jigsaw Falling Into Place, Videotape

Least favorite track: House of Cards
