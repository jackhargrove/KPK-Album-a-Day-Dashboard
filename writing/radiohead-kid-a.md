# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: “The greatest left turn in music history” - a famous quote typically used to describe this album, but one that, as I just discovered, doesn’t have a definitive original source.

Radiohead is a British rock band named after a lyric from a Talking Heads song and fronted by vocalist and lyricist Thom Yorke. Other members include guitarist and multi-instrumentalist (and primary composer) Jonny Greenwood, bassist Colin Greenwood, drummer Phil Selway, and effect guitarist Ed O’Brien. Radiohead originally found success in the grunge genre, with their 1993 debut *Pablo Honey* featuring the hit single “Creep”. By 1993, however, grunge had already become an artistically (and soon to be commercially) bankrupt genre, and Radiohead moved onto the next big craze: Britpop. Following in the footsteps of Second British Invasion bands Oasis and Blur, Radiohead released their take on britpop with 1995’s *The Bends*. This time, however, something was different; while nothing on *Pablo Honey* indicated Radiohead was anything more than a Nirvana ripoff, *The Bends* proved much darker and more interesting than their britpop contemporaries. Radiohead finally established themselves as a critical darling and cutting edge group with *OK Computer* in 1997, a deeply weird and glitchy arena rock record dripping with fear over the role of technology in society.

In 2000, it was discovered that Radiohead was ready to release their 4th album. This discovery was no thanks to Radiohead or their label; there were no singles released, Radiohead did no interviews or promotion, and the record was eventually leaked on the internet. The music contained on that album was a shock to everyone. A rock band had just released a record with 10 tracks, of which only 3 featured a guitar. Instead, the album was almost entirely electronic, in a time when electronic music struggled to find critical relevance thanks to the dominance of rock. Critics and fans were divided; this sound was not what anyone wanted out of Radiohead. But over time, it’s unique sound and risk taking have made it Radiohead’s most acclaimed album, and permanently cemented them as the first name people think of when they hear the words “art rock”.

# Hargrove's review

Radiohead’s third best album, but it’s no surprise to me that it’s the most common choice for their best. It was, of course, a massive risk taken by one of the biggest rock bands in the world, and they deserve some credit for that. But more importantly, it’s just so beautiful. From the second you hear the five descending synth notes that start “Everything in Its Right Place”, you’re entranced. There’s something so hypnotizing about these classically beautiful melodies being played on cold, empty synths. The album then takes you on a journey through cacophonous free jazz, heartbreaking depression, spiraling synth lines, an EDM mental breakdown, an ambient soundscape, and the worst song you’ve ever heard in your life (FUCK I HATE THE TITLE TRACK). The highs on this are nowhere near the highs on something like *OK Computer*, and the emotions aren’t as varied or interesting as something like *In Rainbows*, but man is this album great at cultivating and sustaining a deeply unsettling atmosphere.

“Everything in Its Right Place”, “Idioteque”, and “Motion Picture Soundtrack” are far and away the highlights for me. “Everything in Its Right Place” has the perfect synths, the perfect rhythm, and is the perfect tone setter. Just an iconic track that has been deservingly widely covered. “Idioteque” is quite possibly the pinnacle of all dance music; it’s driving, it’s glitchy, it’s off-kilter. It doesn’t just make you want to move your body in time, it makes you want to move your body into a mental institution. It is seriously one of the freakiest, most disturbing songs I’ve ever heard, and it gives me goosebumps every single time. “Motion Picture Soundtrack” is pure, distilled depression (somehow even moreso than “How to Disappear Completely”), and the second song on the list so far which has suicide note lyrics. The organ is haunting, and Thom Yorke has the vocal performance of his career. The “I will see you/in another life” climax is earth-shaking, heartbreaking.

Other great tracks include “The National Anthem”, which is just a spastic jazz breakdown that comes out of nowhere, being so out of place that it makes it feel like it’s in the right place. “How to Disappear Completely” is another phenomenal vocal performance from Yorke, but Greenwood’s ondes martinot steals the show; it is truly a unique instrument (only like 10 exist, I believe), and it gives the song an otherworldly longing. And “In Limbo”, which follows seamlessly from the end of the preceding “Optimistic” to give the weirdest of the few guitar parts on this album, a tumbling and hypnotic spiral of a riff.

All that being said, this is far from a perfect album. As I’ve mentioned, I have really grown to dislike the title track “Kid A”. It absolutely destroys the momentum of the album right after the bat, coming after the mood-setting opener and before the glorious chaos of “The National Anthem”. The synth line is not particularly good, and the vocals are annoying in the worst way. “Treefingers” is poorly-executed in my opinion, I love a good ambient soundscape but this one is not it for me. “Morning Bell” is overall solid, but it’s just overshadowed by “Idioteque” for me, although that is nitpicking.

Overall, this album is pretty damn good. I really think my ranking of the top 3 Radiohead albums just come down to personal taste for me; *In Rainbows* is emotionally powerful and. the perfect balance between electronic and rock, *OK Computer* has the most kickass songs, *Kid A* just doesn’t meet those personal criteria for me to put it as one of my all-time favorites (*In Rainbows* is in my top 10 all time). It is an album everyone should listen to though, such a great introduction to a band that’s given an unbelievable amount of great music over the years.

Favorite tracks: Everything in Its Right Place, The National Anthem, How to Disappear Completely, In Limbo, Idioteque, Motion Picture Soundtrack

Least favorite track (of all time): Kid A
