# Write-up

Royal Trux were an experimental rock duo formed in Washington, DC in 1987 consisting of boyfriend and girlfriend Neil Hagerty and Jennifer Herrema. Neil Hagerty was originally a member of punk band Pussy Galore, which coincidentally made a name for itself by covering the entirety of the Rolling Stones’ *Exile on Main St.* Hagerty left Pussy Galore to form Royal Trux with his girlfriend Herrema in 1987, and they released their self-titled debut in 1988. Shortly thereafter, they moved from DC to San Francisco, where they would record their second album, the highly experimental *Twin Infinitives*. Starting with their third album, Royal Trux abandoned the super unlistenable experimental rock to become a more palatable indie noise blues rock band. Their most well-liked releases in this mode are *Cats and Dogs* (1993) and *Accelerator* (1998). Despite never gaining any mainstream popularity or having anything resembling a hit, Royal Trux was actually hilariously signed to a major label for a short while. After Nirvana exploded the popularity of alternative rock in 1991, Virgin Records chose to sign Royal Trux because they had serious street cred among the artsy crowd, and they hoped it would attract more marketable alternative bands to Virgin. Royal Trux recorded two records for Virgin before returning to the more natural indie label, Drag City (Joanna Newsom’s label). They broke up (both band and relationship) in 2001, and had a brief reunion from 2015-2019 where they released one terrible album and one remix album led by Ariel Pink.

Although they never reached any success themselves, Royal Trux, and particularly their early work like *Twin Infinitives*, was very influential on the budding indie rock scene. Their wild risk-taking and deconstruction of rock norms has been credited by many more popular musicians in their space as being inspiring, leading to many comparisons to the Velvet Underground. In particular, Stephen Malkmus of indie forefathers Pavement, Will Oldham aka Bonnie

“Prince” Billy who took the cover photo for *Spiderland*, Thurston Moore of Sonic Youth, and MGMT have all cited Royal Trux as an inspiration. *Twin Infinitives* is Royal Trux’ most challenging and daring album, made when both Hagerty and Herrema were taking drugs (in particular LSD) very regularly.

# Hargrove's review

I really, really wanted to like this one because Aidan was being such a complete and utter little bitch about it. Also because they’re from DC and the song names reference various places here (Florida Avenue, New York Avenue bridge). Not only did I give it 6 full listens, I also gave two listens each to two of their later, more melodic albums, *Cats and Dogs* and *Accelerator*, in the hopes that they would help me “get it”. While I did actually enjoy both of those, man did it not help here. This album is just a complete mess, not dissimilar to *Trout Mask Replica* or Sun City Girls. Its fans (and it certainly has those) like to say it’s a deconstruction of rock music, but I just don’t see it. I don’t think you could piece something real back together using this album as parts. Their later stuff, sure; I can see how *Cats and Dogs* takes the masculinity of rock to its extreme. And maybe they needed to make this stuff to get there. But it really just does not inspire good feelings. I don’t love the overuse of feedback. While feedback can be awesome when used right, I feel like every song having a base layer of feedback that everything else is built on precludes more interesting and exploratory soundscapes. I also think more evocative lyrics would have helped add some sense to the senselessness. Some songs have elements that I enjoy (like the pew pew sounds on the opener), but it’s very rarely enough to carry a full track. Thanks to my sincere efforts, I was able to find a few songs that I did, on the net, enjoy. “Jet Pet” was the first one to consistently pique my interest; the vocals are so annoying that they loop back around into being pleasingly psychedelic, the guitar is successfully hypnotic at points, and I like all the dumb little accoutrements they add to the sound. “(Edge of the) Ape Oven” is 15 minutes and takes up a whole side, so thankfully it’s actually pretty decent. It employs a lot of interesting guitar textures, particularly the tremolo part during verse 3. The percussion is also unique and spontaneous but still rhythmic, and it’s fun to get lost trying to follow it. “Lick My Boots” has an almost recognizable and complex rhythm, and I enjoy it for that reason (“Funky Son” has a similar rhythm but ruins it with annoying noodling). The closer, “New York Avenue Bridge”, is potentially the best song on the album thanks entirely to the piano part. It’s a genuinely pretty melody, but on with enough eeriness and edge to match up with the rest of the record. The only truly irredeemable part of this record is side 2 (“Chances Are the Comets in Our Future” -> “Yin Jim Versus the Vomit Creature” -> “Osiris”), but none of it is particularly enjoyable. I really did give it my best shot though, and I’m glad I found some parts I enjoyed.

Favorite tracks: Jet Pet, (Edge of the) Ape Oven, Lick My Boots, New York Avenue Bridge

Least favorite track: Chances Are the Comets in Our Future
