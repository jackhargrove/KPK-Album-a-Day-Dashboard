# Write-up

Ryo Fukui was a jazz pianist born in 1948 and raised in Sapporo, Japan. He first learned to play accordion at age 18, followed by teaching himself piano at age 22. He moved to Tokyo around that time to pursue a career playing jazz, though he struggled to find success. Fukui released his first album, *Scenery*, in 1976 while still living in Tokyo. The album features a standard jazz trio, with Fukui on piano, his brother Yoshinori Fukui on drums, and Satoshi Denpo on bass. “It Could Happen to You”, “I Want to Talk About It”, “Willow Weep for Me”, and are all covers of old American jazz standards. “Autumn Leaves” is also a jazz standard, but given its French author it is sometimes considered the most important non-American standard; Cannonball Adderley played it on *Somethin’ Else*, which we listened to last year. “Early Summer”, the album’s most celebrated track, was written by Fukui’s Japanese jazz contemporary Hideo Ichikawa. Fukui composed “Scenery” himself. The album uses a 50s hard bop and cool jazz sound that was very out of date by 1976 following nearly two decades of free jazz and jazz fusion. Together with its foreignness, this caused the record to receive little attention at the time. However, Japanese jazz experienced a major resurgence on the internet in the 2010s, with Fukui’s *Scenery* in particular becoming the most well-known album of this rediscovery. Fukui went on to release a second album in 1977 before focusing on live shows in Tokyo throughout the 80s. In the 90s, he began to travel to Europe and America, where he met prominent jazz artists and began releasing live albums. In 1995, Fukui returned to his native Sapporo and opened the Slowboat jazz club, a regional music hub. Fukui died of lymphoma in 2016. He was 67 years old. His widow continues to operate the Slowboat club.

# Hargrove's review

If my memory is correct, this record is our third to feature only a jazz trio (piano, bass, and drums), the most barebones setup you need to make jazz music. The other two were Vince Guaraldi Trio’s *A Charlie Brown Christmas* and Bill Evans Trio’s *Waltz for Debby*, both good to great records. While that was a good sign, I’m also very skeptical of Japanese records on this list that I hadn’t already heard of because this was compiled by 4chan and, well, you know. After listening to this record for quite a while, I’m still a little confused. “Early Summer” is a driving monster of a track, genuinely exhilarating to listen to each time from beginning to end. The rest of the album, while often quite nice, never comes close to reaching that same level of imagery. I have trouble reconciling this. Where the fuck did “Early Summer” come from? It doesn’t even really sound anything like other jazz I’ve listened to, let alone the hyper palatable cool jazz sound of most of the record. I’m dumbfounded. At any rate, the other tracks on this album are fine. I don’t hate any of them, but they also lack any real standout moments. This has to be the most vanilla jazz album I’ve ever heard, which isn’t entirely a bad thing. Sometimes it’s nice to just hear some good playing to set the mood. But I found myself really missing that feeling of freedom and transgression. I felt a little boxed in at times, especially in the syrupy sentimentality of “I Want to Talk About You”. Still, I enjoyed listening to it more than plenty of other jazz records. Even without “Early Summer”, this was perfectly passable.

“It Could Happen to You” has a nice piano intro. I like how weighty the left hand of the piano sounds, while the right hand feels so delicate and soft. I also appreciate how it slowly works into the swinging rhythm. It’s a nice track, certainly in the top half of this album, but it does breeze right by. I don’t love “I Want to Talk About You”. It slows things way down, and while there are some nice things happening on piano, the track itself just feels overly sentimental. There’s something too sweet to this slowness when I like my slow jazz to feel sleazy. “Early Summer” rules and I just need to know where the fuck Fukui pulled this out of. The intro starts things slow, but still there’s so much energy in the fluttering movement of the right hand. Once the main riff starts, it picks up steam and never looks back. Fukui’s hands just sound so heavy, which is the most impressive quality of the album to me when it occurs on occasion. The drums go insane here too. That swing is impressive in the ultra fast middle section, and Fukui keeps up! It’s a super sweet track nestled in the middle here that I can’t stop thinking about. “Willow Weep for Me” slows things back down again to the feeling of the first two tracks, though this time it’s bluesy and loose. I could take it or leave it. It’s not as soulful as I’d like it to be. I like Fukui’s version of “Autumn Leaves” much better than Cannonball Adderley’s. The piece itself is very well-composed, which helps a lot, but I think the jazz trio emphasizes that beautiful melody instead of soloing. Unfortunately, “Scenery” slows things down to the point of near irrelevance to close the album. Yeah, it’s pretty, but it’s a little sad to go out with this little energy. At least it picks up in the final minute or so with Fukui’s heavy keystrokes

I am glad to have listened to this record, but I don’t feel changed by it. It was nice and breezy.

Favorite tracks: It Could Happen to You, Early Summer, Autumn Leaves

Least favorite track: I Want to Talk About You
