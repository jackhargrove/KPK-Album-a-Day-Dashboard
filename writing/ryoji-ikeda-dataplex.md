# Write-up

Dataplex is barely music. Its creator, Japan's Ryoji Ikeda, is more commonly referred to as a sound artist, and more commonly creates instillations in museums. He's done shows in JFK and at the Park Avenue Armory, and releases minimalistic electronic music in this vein solo and in collaborations such as "Cyclo.", which is with a German artist, Carsten Nicolai. Dataplex is incredibly minimalistic techno, which pushes the boundary of what sound and music, with extreme pitches, sounds that are designed for oscilloscopes rather than human ears, and a closing track which was designed to temporarily break CD players (this album was released back when CD players were still used). This is one of if not the most obscure album on this list, so there's not really any other info out there.

# Hargrove's review

This album takes one admittedly really cool sonic concept, and then proceeds to do very little that’s actually interesting with it. The microsounds are fascinating and completely unlike anything I’ve ever heard before. They’re harsh, but in a way that is completely dissimilar to any abrasive noise album I’ve ever heard. Their duration is so short that it gives them an intense percussive quality, giving me the feeling that the pressure has shifted in my ear canals. At age 24, I pretty much just assumed I was never again going to hear a category of sound that I had never heard before. I was wrong. Unfortunately, the vast majority of this album just wastes them. Ikeda largely refuses to fashion these exhilarating building blocks into fleshed-out structures. Ironically, the album as a whole is assembled masterfully; the type of composition shifts fluidly across the album. It can be broken up into broad groupings. The first stretches from the opener, “data.index”, through track 8, “data.googolplex”. This section is pure, freeform noodling with the microsounds. Super, super uninteresting beyond the first listen. Ikeda did not at all need to spend this many tracks showing off his new sounds. It’s just masturbatory. Tracks 9-12, from “data.microhelix” through “data.syntax”, however, form by far the most enjoyable section. This 4 song stretch is exactly what this album should have been the entire time, and I would have given this at minimum B Tier if it were. Not only are these the first tracks that I would describe as actually being musical, but they damn near nail the kind of music that should be made with this sound. They’re glitchy, they’re harsh, they’re daring, but man do they have a strong rhythm. And, most importantly, that rhythm comes from the microsounds themselves. Remember that percussive quality I mentioned earlier? That forms the beat of each of these tracks. The completely unique class of sound is being used to make music that only it can make. It’s euphoric and super enjoyable. Unfortunately, “data.telex” is a one-off non-musical interlude that separates this small run from the dreariness of the rest of the album. Tracks 14 and 15, “data.flex” and the aptly named “data.reflex”, make up what I dub the “bland techno” section. These two tracks effectively shift the genre to bog-standard generic IDM with microsound flourishes. The actual backbone of these tracks are normal tones! Especially coming almost immediately after the coolest part of the album, these are just dreadful. Tracks 16-18, “data.convex” through “data.vortex”, start a new mode: ambient. These tracks are more consistent than the nonmusical noodling of the beginning of the album, allowing them to take on a musical quality, but wow do they not stick in my head at all. A complete neutering of the core sound. Track 19, “data.matrix”, is by far the most streamed track. It belongs to a section of its own: “bland techno (reprise)”. This is just “data.flex” yet again, but stretched out to 10 minutes. I will admit that it’s better than both “data.flex” and “data.reflex”, but it has the same fundamental issues for me. The closer, “data.adaplex”, repreises the ambient mode, but its textures are adequately daring for me to prefer it to the others in this mode. Overall, an album that, whether out of integrity or stubbornness, refuses to become anything. I ended up finding it very hard to rank. I knew it had to be D Tier, but I ultimately placed it very high up in D Tier because it was obvious that it would leave a much longer lasting and powerful impression on me than Fishmans or Shabazz Palaces. So it gets second place in D Tier after *The Smile Sessions*. And now my ears are ringing.

Favorite tracks: data.microhelix, data.superhelix, data.minimax, data.syntax

Least favorite track: data.googolplex

Least favorite track that’s actually musical: data.flex

# Aidan's review

This album is a gimmick, but it's a hell of a gimmick. My first listen I was blown away at how far the envelope was pushed with the sounds. I highly recommend listening to this album, especially the opening section, once with headphones one. However, I cannot recommend that you listen to it more than once. There's just not that much there beside the weird sensation of hearing a noise you've never heard before, and one the novelty wears off, it just sounds like someone is trying to play Darude Sandstorm on a turntable by moving the needle back and forth on a stationary record. I really wish this was better because it's genuinely one of the coolest ideas for a record on this list, and I think it's honestly good if you view it as a work of art and not a record. But as music, it really falls short, it's not really repayable, and after its bag of tricks is gone, the album just gives up and stops doing new things in favor of just minimalist techno. My final impression was very tired, but I can't ignore how wowed I was listening to it for the first time.
