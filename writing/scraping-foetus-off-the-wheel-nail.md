# Write-up

Scraping Foetus Off the Wheel was one of the many variant names for Foetus, the solo project of Australian industrial musician JG Thirlwell (full name James George Thirlwell). Thirlwell was born in 1960 and raised in Melbourne. After a short stint at Melbourne State College, he moved to London in 1978 and played synths for post-punk band PragVEC. After that band broke up, Thirlwell established Foetus (first under the name Foetus Under Glass) in 1981. Thirlwell was a prominent member of the UK post-punk and industrial music scenes, frequently collaborating with other artists as both a producer and a musician using pseudonyms such as Clint Ruin and Frank Want, alter egos that Thirlwell also used for Foetus projects. On the post-punk side, Thirlwell worked with Sonic Youth, Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds, The The, and Lydia Lunch. On the industrial side, Thirlwell collaborated with Coil, Einstürzende Neubauten, Nurse with Wound, and Current 93. Thirlwell was also a frequent collaborator with Swans, who straddle the two genres, and also worked on multiple Jarboe solo albums.

The Foetus project released its debut album *Deaf* in 1981 using the name You’ve Got Foetus on Your Breath. It was one of the first ever examples of industrial rock, a rock subgenre that takes its aesthetic cues from industrial and was popularized by Nine Inch Nails in the 90s. Thirlwell further pioneered this genre on Foetus’ second record, *Ache*, in 1982. Thirlwell moved to New York City in 1983, where he has lived ever since. Foetus released its third album *Hole* in 1984, the first to use the Scraping Foetus Off the Wheel name. *Nail*, the fourth Foetus album and second released as Scraping Foetus Off the Wheel, was released in 1985. Allmusic has described it as “possibly the best Foetus album”. Since *Nail*, Foetus has released eight studio albums. The most recent, *Halt*, was released in 2025. After 1988’s *Thaw* (released under Foetus Interruptus), every album has just been released using the name Foetus. Thirlwell has continued to frequently collaborate with other members of the post-punk and industrial scenes, including a supergroup with Nick Cave, Lydia Lunch, and Marc Almond (of Soft Cell) named The Immaculate Consumptive. He has also written the score for multiple adult animated shows including *The Venture Bros.* and *Archer*.

# Hargrove's review

I’ve been looking forward to this one for a while because industrial rock is one of the genres I most consistently enjoy. Nine Inch Nails, Big Black, Swans, just inject that shit straight into my veins. I love it when machinery is used as percussion, when metal scrapes against metal, when grinding noise lets me become entranced with simple dance beats. Sadly, I did not like this album at all. After my first two listens, I straight up hated it, giving it a good chance of ending up in F Tier. I do not care for Thirlwell’s vocals at all. Most of the time, he sounds like Weird Al Yankovic doing a Tom Waits impression to sing a Nine Inch Nails song. I like all of those artists in isolation (well, jury’s still out on Waits), but fuck does it sound annoying when put together like this. It’s especially bad when he’s being theatrical. While that creates a near-constant problem, the actual compositions themselves are flawed in potentially an even more fundamental way. Of the 10 tracks that make up this album, six are “real” songs while the other four are interludes. Those six songs all employ a progressive compositional style, with multiple sections that completely change the melody, beat, and chords. Though this could have been a cool thing, the different sections are just too short, making everything sound rushed and disjointed. Add to that the frantic, maximalist instrumentation and you have pure chaos. It’s just not a pleasant experience to listen to most of these songs. They feel manic, sure, but with no direction. The six-minute runtimes mix with the frantic pacing to make tracks that simultaneously feel too long and too short. A third, much smaller issue I have with this album is that it’s sequenced poorly. Why is have four of the six “real” tracks play in a row and then follow them immediately with back-to-back interludes? Use the interludes to break up the meat of the album! Anyway, I eventually came to appreciate more of the album over time. I really do like what Thirlwell is going for, the atmospheres and timbres and energy. Individual tracks that I don’t love have great sections, like the “hot time in the old town tonight” closer on “Pigswill”. And I even realized that I like the closer, “Anything (Viva!)”, without qualification. Still, even with that slight warming, I have no desire to save any track apart from the closer. I am sad that this album did so little for me.

*Nail* begins with the Elfman-esque “Theme from Pigdom Come”. It’s dark and cinematic, but it doesn’t feel like it really introduces many of the album’s ideas, and as a composition it’s only okay. While I still find the overall style off-putting, “The Throne of Agony” is undeniably one of the stronger compositions on this record. It minimizes the busy theatrical distractions of the other tracks, particularly in the powerful chorus. I also love how the chorus slowly gets faster with each repetition. It’s too long, and doesn’t need so many transitions to reach the build effect, but I admire the execution. I’m going to mention “!” for the sake of thoroughness, but it’s a four-second sound effect. I like the idea of what’s happening in “Pigswill”, but it’s so disjointed without the constant groove of “The Throne of Agony” to keep it on track. I do like the closing section a good bit, but it doesn’t make up for the preceding four minutes. “Descent into the Inferno” is a tough one for me because it aesthetically appeals to me less than the rest of the album, but I have to admit that its execution is just about flawless. It’s like a show tune with the “skibbly doo bop” stuff. I can’t stand it. But it clearly fulfills Thirlwell’s exact vision. I like the whispered singing at the start of “Enter the Exterminator” when its simply backed by crashing objects and tapping percussion, but it wears thin quickly when the whispers last the entire length of the track. Ironically, though my main complaint is that this album has too much going on, this song somehow has too little going on. At least the others left an impression. “DI-1-9026” is the best track since “The Throne of Agony”, though the “ram ba lam” stuff tests my patience. The repetition of the title gets stuck in my head, there’s a consistent drum part to anchor the track, and there’s a lot of momentum in the vocal line. I’ll take it. “The Overture from Pigdom Come” is not as good as the instrumental opener and is placed poorly within the album. As a proud sicko, “Pricvate War” is my favorite interlude. I LOVE THE SOUND OF METAL. Finally, at the end of the album, there’s one track that I like enough to want to listen to it again. I love the metallic percussion of “Anything (Viva!)”. The focus on machinery and clanking serves the vocals well, making them much more bearable than anywhere else. I also like how Thirlwell’s interest in classical music is actually integrated into this song instead of quarantined as an interlude. By the end, it turns into a hypnotic behemoth.

This is the most bummed out I’ve been to not like something in a while. I’m placing it right above Quasimoto in D Tier because that album made me feel the exact same way.

Favorite tracks: The Throne of Agony, DI-1-9026, Anything (Viva!)

Least favorite track: Enter the Exterminator
