# Write-up

Sd Laika was the stage name of Peter Runge, an electronic musician from Wisconsin. I have no clue how you’re supposed to pronounce “Sd”, but whatever. Runge used multiple stage names in his career, but the Sd Laika moniker is the most well-known. Runge released his first music in 2012 via a London-based label known for a genre called “grime”. He released his debut full-length album *That’s Harakiri* in 2014 to critical excitement. The album cover comes from the Japanese short film*The Cage*, and the album’s title refers to the Japanese practice of ritual suicide. Pioneering electronic musician Aphex Twin named Sd Laika as his favorite new artist, going so far as to sample Sd Laika’s music on tour in 2017. Björk would also sample his music. Despite all this buzz, Runge would drop off the face of the Earth after the album’s release, issuing only three singles in 2015 and one single in 2018 for the rest of his career. He died in 2023 of unknown causes.

Although *That’s Harakiri* might not be considered grime strictly speaking, it is heavily influenced by the genre. Grime is a style of EDM that evolved from the UK garage scene (garage is a form of house music), particularly the 2-step style that also inspired dubstep. Grime would combine this garage base with Jamaican styles like dancehall and raga, as well as with hip hop. In fact, most grime music features a rapper, although the style of instrumental defines the genre. British rapper and producer Dizzee Rascal is the most celebrated practitioner of the style. Grime was only ever popular in the UK, and its heyday was the early 2000s. While grime was a predecessor to *That’s Harakiri*, the album is seen as an example of the UK bass and deconstructed club styles that developed in the 2010s. UK bass (sometimes just called “bass music” or “bass”) developed in the late 2000s in the UK as an outgrowth of dubstep. It combines the stuttering, complex drums and dark atmosphere of dubstep with a heavy bass sound (as an aside: one notable derivative of UK bass was the sickeningly sweet sound of SOPHIE dubbed “bubblegum bass” that would eventually turn into hyperpop). Deconstructed club was a scene within UK bass that developed through the internet in the early 2010s. Like IDM, deconstructed club took forms of dance music and played with them to make them more intellectual and less danceable. In the case of deconstructed club, an abrasive post-industrial sound to popular EDM genres on the 2010s in the UK including grime, UK bass, and dubstep. For context, Coil is an example of a prototypical post-industrial band. Other major deconstructed club artists include SOPHIE, Iglooghost, and Arca.

# Hargrove's review

Weirdly, this has been one of my favorite electronic albums so far. It’s quite short and only about half of the tracks are good, but I really like them. Maybe it’s just that I like industrial sounds and this album is recent enough for abrasive noises to be genuinely abrasive. Whatever the reason, there are five songs on here that are among my favorite purely EDM songs we’ve heard so far: “Great God Plan”, “Gutter Vibrations”, “Remote Heaven”, “You Were Wrong”, and “Don’t Know”. “Great God Plan” has fantastic plasticky percussive noises that sound like a film clapperboard underneath a melancholy melody. “Gutter Vibrations” has such a chaotic medley of sonic textures that I could see it being a complete mess, but not only does it pull it off, it matches the energy with a wonky, off-kilter rhythm. “Remote Heaven” starts unremarkably, but by the second half it’s become a really interesting percussive track. This little machine-sounding synth plays a crazy rhythm made even more complex by its interplay with the bass drum. “You Were Wrong” has a super creepy main melodic line, especially when it solos for a while. Then, it adds in-your0face drums and a ringing synth over the top to create a haunting soundscape. And “Don’t Know” has a standout bass line that dominates the rest of the track. I also like its high-pitched, tinny melody and the stabs of synths at weird rhythmic moments. All of these are just fun to listen to. “Meshes” is decent, especially the sonic assault at the end. The opener “Peace” is fine, especially the squealing synths. “It’s Ritual” is fine if forgettable. “Precressing” and “I Don’t” are both a little bit annoying and “Peaked” is mercifully short. It’s an inconsistent album for sure, with five songs I’d call good, three I’d call mid, and three I’d call bad. There’s also not much of a grand statement or overarching theme to it, which is why I’m keeping this review concise. But for someone who’s been struggling to get into the EDM albums, it had a high hit rate of songs that I can enjoy like I do in any other style of music. Not bad at all. For a fusion of industrial and EDM I’d still rather listen to The Knife’s *Shaking the Habitual*, but this will do in a pinch.

Favorite tracks: Great God Plan, Gutter Vibrations, Remote Heaven, You Were Wrong, Don’t Know

Least favorite track: Peaked
