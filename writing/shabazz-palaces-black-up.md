# Write-up

Shabazz Palaces is an experimental hip-hop duo from Seattle consisting of MC Ishmael Butler (aka Palaceer Lazaro) and Zimbabwean multi-instrumentalist Tendai Maraire. Butler was born and raised in Seattle by a father who was a professor of history at UVA (he’s Gotti fr). He dropped out of college in 1989 and moved to Brooklyn, where he formed jazz rap group Digable Planets and experienced mild commercial success in the 90s. He moved back to Seattle in 2003. Maraire was born to a notable traditional musician in Zimbabwe and I guess moved to Seattle at some point for some reason. The two formed Shabazz Palaces in Seattle in 2009 and self-released two EPs. They became the first hip hop act to be signed to Sub Pop, a legendary record label who signed many of the famous alt rock and grunge bands of the 90s including Nirvana, and released their debut *Black Up* in 2011. The album was a unique and experimental work, both recalling the jazziness of early 90s underground hip hop and foreshadowing the glitchy abrasive sound that would define 2010s underground hip hop. Shabazz Palaces have released 7 albums since, with the most recent coming in 2024, but none have achieved success in underground hip hop in the same way as their debut. Maraire also left the group at some point so now it’s just Butler. Notable 2010s experimental hip hop artists including Thundercat, Flying Lotus, and clipping. have all featured on Shabazz Palaces albums.

# Hargrove's review

Did not care for this one. I thought it was dreadful on first listen, but now after 5 listens I’ve been able to find some parts that I enjoy and my overall reaction is more “meh”. There’s just a weird tension in this album where the beats are glitchy and almost industrial in shape, but the timbres they use aren’t harsh or abrasive like you get with industrial hip hop. Add to this Butler’s 90s backpack rap vocals and you get an album that doesn’t know if it wants to be A Tribe Called Quest or Death Grips. Yes, this came out the same year as DG’s debut, so it’s not like they were inspired by DG or anything. I just mean it as 2011 was where the glitchy, industrial direction of 2010s hip hop began, and it feels like this album has one foot in the past, one foot in the future, and can’t figure out how to stay upright. One of the best examples of this is “Endeavors for Never (The Last Time We Spoke You Said You Were Not Here. I Saw You Though.)”, where the beat is constantly shifting and unbalanced, but you have this pretty R&B woman crooning. Another example is “A Treatease Dedicated to the Avian Airess from North East Nubis (1000 Questions, 1 Answer)”, where the beat is super jittery but the lyrics are dreamy and the flow is smooth jazz rap. These were my two least favorite tracks. A lot of the other tracks are just kind of uninteresting, with overly minimalist beats playing unchanged and with Butler’s voice that I find kind of annoying. I did like “Youlogy”, which has a really well-done 3 section structure. Each part is distinct and good. This is one of the only tracks where the feeling of the rapping is as schizo as the beat, the drums are great in sections 1 and 3, and the flow is stellar in section 2. I also liked the closer “Swerve… The Reeping of All That Is Worthwhile (Noir Not Withstanding)”, which has a super bouncy drum beat with a nice melody in the instrumental. It also has one of the only good hooks in the album, and I enjoyed the R&B woman coming back with a sultrier vibe. All in all, I found myself just wishing this album would commit one way or the other, either be nice and smooth or destroy my ear drums.

Favorite tracks: Youlogy, Swerve… The Reeping of All That Is Worthwhile (Noir Not Withstanding)

Least favorite track: Endeavors for Never (The Last Time We Spoke You Said You Were Not Here. I Saw You Though.)
