# Write-up

Slayer is a thrash metal band formed in LA in 1981 consisting of guitarist Jeff Hanneman, guitarist Kerry King, drummer Dave Lombardo, and bassist and vocalist Tom Araya. Slayer is one of the “Big 4” of thrash metal, alongside Metallica, Megadeth, and Anthrax. We’ve already listened to Megadeth’s *Rust in Peace*, but as a refresher: thrash metal is an early style of “extreme metal” that developed in the early-to-mid-1980s. It was primarily based off the sound of New Wave of British Heavy Metal bands like Iron Maiden and speed metal bands like Motörhead, but introduced ideas from hardcore punk like extreme speed and aggression. Metallica was the most successful thrash group and defined the sound. Megadeth was created by Metallica’s original guitarist Dave Mustaine after he was kicked out of the band, and Megadeth was like the Wario to Metallica’s Mario. Anthrax was the only one of the Big 4 from the East Coast (Queens), were the most similar to hardcore punk, and were the least successful. Slayer found themselves somewhere in between: more punk than Metallica and Megadeth, less punk than Anthrax. More successful than Anthrax, less successful than Metallica and Megadeth. They carved out their own niche and unique sound for the scene, and formed the basis for the development of death metal with their 1986 album *Reign in Blood*.

Slayer formed in 1981 to play covers of Iron Maiden, Black Sabbath, and Judas Priest songs at parties in Southern California. They used Satanic imagery from the very beginning and styled their logo to look like it was carved with a knife by a serial killer. In 1983, they played a gig at a club where they were discovered by a representative for Metal Blade Records. They were given a contract to make an album but no budget, so they used Araya’s savings from his job as a respiratory therapist and a loan from King’s father to make their 1983 debut, *Show No Mercy*. The record sold 20,000 copies, which isn’t a lot, but Metal Blade Record’s average was 5,000. The production was incredibly lo-fi, but the music was unprecedentedly hard and fast for metal. It would go on to heavily influence death metal, as well as alternative metal band System of a Down (on our list). The songs “The Antichrist”, “Die by the Sword”, and “Black Magic” would become some of Slayer’s signature songs.

Slayer toured relentlessly after releasing their debut. They released their second album, *Hell Awaits*, in 1985. This record was a modest commercial success, and it caught the attention of Rick Rubin, legendary hip hop producer and founder of Def Jam records. They became the first non-hip hop act to sign to Def Jam, and Rubin produced their third album, *Reign in Blood*. This album is by far considered Slayer’s finest, and is among the most well-regarded metal albums of all-time. Slayer remained cool until around 1991, when thrash in general declined in popularity and Metallica sold out. Their first five albums are all considered classics of the genre, *Reign in Blood* in particular. Slayer’s extreme sound, profoundly disturbing lyrics (they were sued in the 90s after a group of teenage boys were inspired to ritualistically murder a female classmate by a Slayer song), and relative obscurity compared to Metallica made them perhaps the most influential of the Big 4. Death metal in particular owes its existence to *Reign in Blood*, but many black metal, metalcore (90s fusion of metal and hardcore punk), and even alternative metal bands owe a lot of credit to Slayer.

# Hargrove's review

In addition to listening to *Show No Mercy*, I listened quite a few times to Slayer’s third album *Reign in Blood* and I gotta say, vibes-wise Slayer is definitely my favorite of the big 4. There are two aspects of metal that I haven’t really gotten before that Slayer has made me understand: growled vocals and double kick drum. Araya’s voice isn’t perfect (some of his high notes on *Show No Mercy* are really cringe-inducing), but his primary growl sounds sick as hell. But the drums were a highlight for me across both albums. It’s a very similar style to the drums we’ve heard on other metal albums and I’ve always thought were a little over-the-top, but wow do they work so well with Slayer. While listening to just about every song, I found myself going “the drums here rule” at least once. I’m going to try to avoid letting *Reign in Blood* cloud my review of *Show No Mercy*, but it definitely gave me a positive view of Slayer as a band. *Show No Mercy* has really lo-fi production, and I think it’s too much at points. However, a complaint I’ve had about thrash in the past (particularly Megadeth’s *Rust in Peace*) is that it often sounds over-produced. While this is a little too far in the other direction, it’s refreshing and preferable in my opinion, and is part of what I like about Slayer’s vibes and sound so much. The guitars still sound like pure electricity, but there’s some edge and more of a punk attitude. Listening to Slayer has been a very positive experience for me.

The opener “Evil Has No Boundaries” is a pretty good introduction to Slayer’s sound. It’s well-written, the riff is compelling, the background vocals are cool, and the drums are top notch. “The Antichrist” is similar but with a riff I like less, though it has a good solo. “Die by the Sword” has the best solo on the album, and also the first half-time section that I’ve noticed is really common on both this album and *Reign in Blood*. The half-time mid-section sounds so heavy and even cataclysmic, it’s really well-done. I also like the droning chords in the rhythm guitar at points. “Fight Till Death” has my favorite riff. It’s a construction that I really like, an ascending 4-note line that consists of an arpeggio + the third. This is a construction that I think sounds so good I use it in the little GarageBand experiments I make. Drums are once again killer. The first section of “Metal Storm / Face the Slayer” heavy and awesome, and the riff in the second section has this nautical feel that I enjoy. Another very good track. It marks the halfway point of the album, and past this point the sameyness of the songs starts to take a toll. This album benefits from being short because its sound is not varied at all. “Black Magic” has a decent solo but I don’t understand why it’s the most notable song from this album. “Tormentor” sounds the most like a sped-up Iron Maiden song, particularly the chorus melody, but I can’t stand the high-pitched way Araya yelps “the NIIIIIIIGHT” in the chorus. It’s enough to make it my least favorite track. “The Final Command” is the best song on the second half, it’s blisteringly fast and I appreciate it for that on its own. The dueling solo part is cool and the guitar sounds like pure electricity throughout the entire song. “Crionics” has the best lyrics on the album, which saves it from how otherwise unremarkable the instrumental is. And the closer “Show No Mercy” is similarly fine but I’m really worn out by how similar the sound is song-to-song by this point.

This album is a rough debut album, and *Reign in Blood* is much better, but it’s still pretty damn good. It’s a fun, short listen that just fills me with energy and makes me want to throw up devil horns with my fingers. I was really pleasantly surprised with how enjoyable it was.

Favorite tracks: Evil Has No Boundaries, Die by the Sword, Fight Till Death, Metal Storm / Face the Slayer, The Final Command

Least favorite track: Tormenter
