# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Sleep is a doom metal group from San Jose, California that formed  in 1990. The lineup during their original incarnation consisted of Al Cisneros on lead vocals and bass, Matt Pike on guitar, and Chris Hakius on drums. Although doom metal was already an established genre when Sleep began, they were an important pioneer of stoner metal. They released their debut, *Volume One*, in 1991 to little commercial success, but it did establish them within the doom metal scene. Their 1992 sophomore record *Sleep’s Holy Mountain* was both a critical and (relative) commercial success that got Sleep signed to a major label and is considered the founding document of stoner metal. In 1996, Sleep began recording their third record. They decided to make the album consist of only one hour-long track, and the recording process was demanding: one month in the studio, one month at home rehearsing, one month back in the studio. When they presented this recording to their label, they refused to release it, claiming that an hour-long song was completely commercially unviable. Sleep fought with their label for years to no avail, and in 1998 the band broke up as a result. In 1999, an independent label released the first version of the composition, entitled *Jerusalem*. In 2003, another independent label released a longer version entitled *Dopesmoker*, the definitive version today. At least 3 more versions have been released since. Sleep has reunited twice, once from 2009-2019, and again from 2022-present.

Although some versions of the album have included other random songs to fill out disc length, *Dopesmoker* is a single, hour-long composition. Its sparse lyrics tell the story of a man who gets so high on weed that he hallucinates himself in the deserts of Israel during biblical times. The instruments attempt to place the audience in a similar drug-addled, hallucinatory state.

Here is a (brief) overview of metal. I will get more into each subgenre in the write-ups for applicable records. In the 60s, LSD and other psychedelic drugs began to have a major impact on rock music. Psychedelic rock became popular, and it mostly channeled the enjoyable and spiritual aspects of a psychedelic trip. However, in the late 60s, some bands started to make louder and darker music that were more representative of the negative aspects of LSD and “bad trips”, a genre that was called acid rock. In 1969 and 1970, three groups coined “the unholy trinity” released albums that would form the basis of “heavy metal”: Black Sabbath, Led Zeppelin, and Deep Purple. Their music featured a focus on guitar riffs, feedback, dark and Satanic imagery, and extreme loudness. All three groups were hated by critics, who coined the term “heavy metal” as a pejorative term to make fun of their music. However, it proved very influential on British groups of the 1970s like Iron Maiden, who solidified the sound. In the 1980s, metal split into two directions: in the mainstream, groups like Def Leppard and Bon Jovi played glam metal (a pop genre that is not represented on this list), whereas the underground developed extreme metal, which stripped nearly all of the blues influence of early heavy metal, replacing it with the virtuosity of European classical music, and established 5 major subgenres. Thrash metal bands like the Big 4 (Metallica, Megadeth, Slayer, and Anthrax) took heavy influence from hardcore punk and focused on fast tempos and harsh sounds. Speed metal (controversial whether this is separate from thrash) bands like Motörhead focused on extremely fast tempos, even moreso than in thrash, but had a more melodic sound. Death metal bands such as Cannibal Corpse and Death focused on extreme aggression, deeply distorted guitars, growling vocals, and grotesque imagery. Black metal groups like Venom and Bathory in the 1980s and Burzum and Mayhem in the 90s focus on shrieking vocals, atmosphere, and overall aesthetic (black metal is extremely popular in the Nordic countries and Eastern Europe, and also has a bit of a Nazi problem). And doom metal bands like Electric Wizard and Sleep (of today’s record!) focused on low tempos, low pitched guitars, heaviness, thickness, and attempted to induce a feeling of anxiety or doom in the audience. In the 90s, bands like Deftones and Alice in Chains combined alternative rock with metal to create alternative metal. In the late 90s and early 2000s, a much maligned genre known as nu metal became extremely popular, which combined metal music with hip hop (there is one album on the list that can be described as both nu metal and alternative metal - *Toxicity* by System of a Down).

# Hargrove's review

You know those tracks where you’re like “damn I hope it isn’t over soon” and it has like 58 minutes left?

Wow this album blew me away. I’m halfway through my 6th (!) and final full listen right now, and I’m worried I’m going to end up overrating it because of how much I’m feeling it right now. I had not heard this at all before we did this, and I am LOVING it. Have never been a metal guy, but wow does this spin my gears in just the right fashion. Literally the only complaint I can think of right now is that the vocals, while fine, don’t fit the overall atmosphere for me in the way I would like. Otherwise, it’s all positives. I don’t even think it’s too long! It’s both captivating in a focused listening, and great in an ambient listen while not giving it my full attention. Really, really good stuff.

In my opinion, this album does the best job of building atmosphere of anything we’ve listened to so far. I just get completely lost in the music and the dreamscape whenever I put it on. The lyrics, as sparse as they are, are genuinely pretty cool I’m not even going to lie. I had never thought of the Biblical holy land as a good place for a surreal and nightmarish ordeal, but it works so well. I will also say, as someone who generally has anxiety-ridden and unpleasant experiences with weed, the music *perfectly* captures what it feels like when I’m high. Just constant tension building, very little relief or climax. I’ve never had a strong hallucination or anything, but I have felt long fucking guitar drones.

Speaking of the guitar drone, it’s really what makes this album. In an active listen, it ensures that the tension is relentless. In a passive listen, it gives you a baseline by which to sense when the music slightly progresses. And in the select few moments where the guitar goes on an extended solo, it absolutely explodes (as I was typing this I hit the guitar solo at 38:50 and I want to come). The drums are also exceptional throughout. When they finally come in at around 2:30, you really feel the composition get its feet under itself. I know I say this a lot, but the cymbal work is *excellent* (I’m starting to think the fast track to an essential album is just having a drummer who knows his way around cymbals). And whenever the music does get a little monotonous, the vocals come in to provide a nice change of pace, albeit one that does disrupt my dreamscape a bit.

Man did I *love* this album I love the quiet parts (e.g. ~20:00 — ~23:00, ~41:00 — ~43:30), I love the loud parts (you know the ones). And no matter what volume you have it set at, those loud parts feel *loud*. Also, as a throwaway point, the cover looks dope. It’s such a coherent composition, but it does so many interesting things with texture throughout. There is genuinely something very tactile about it, like reading braille vs. reading writing. I feel like I run my fingers over the sounds and feel the music.

Favorite tracks: Dopesmoker

Least favorite track: Dopesmoker
