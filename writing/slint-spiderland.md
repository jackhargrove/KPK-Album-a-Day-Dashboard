# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Slint was formed by a group of high schoolers in Louisville, Kentucky in 1986. Their first album, *Tweez*, was produced by Steve Albini, a legendary Chicago punk and hardcore artist who I would guess was the producer for more albums on this list than anyone else. The album was about as commercially successful as you would imagine an album recorded by a bunch of Louisville teenagers would be. After its release, all the members went off to college, but they would still play together as Slint when back in Louisville on breaks.

Slint recorded *Spiderland* in Chicago in 1991, with Brian Paulson as producer instead of Steve Albini. All four members of the group were now in their early 20s. They named it *Spiderland* because they believed the guitars sounded like long, thin spider legs. The day after recording, Brian McMahan, Slint’s lead singer, checked into a mental hospital for depression. The band broke up not long afterward. When *Spiderland* released, there was almost no promotion because the band no longer existed, and it initially received little attention. However, over time, *Spiderland* developed a cult following and is now considered by some to be one of the most important musical works of the 1990s.

*Spiderland* launched two, potentially three, genres. The first is post-rock, a genre characterized by its use of rock instruments and aesthetics to make longer, slower, more experimental works. The second is math rock, which is defined by its experimentation with complex rhythms and time signatures. Later in the 90s, math rock combined with post-hardcore to form Midwest emo, which ultimately resulted in the plain old emo we know today. In this sense, *Spiderland* can also be seen as a forerunner to emo.

# Hargrove's review

I already let the cat out of the bag earlier that this is one of my favorite albums of all time. I’ve been listening to this album for a third of my life at this point, and it still sounds just as unsettling as when I was 16 years old. Yeah, it also influenced a lot of other music that I like, but more than anything it just sounds so good. Every time I listen, I’m transported into this hypnotic, nightmarish world of angular guitar riffs, whispers-turned-screams, and cryptic poetry. Even when I look at the cover art, it feels like those smarmy young men are stalking me, ready to pop out of the image and kill me. Everything about *Spiderland* is so unsettling in the best way.

My favorite track is “Washer”. I’m a big fan of repetition, as, when done well, it can put me into a trance like state. Here, the repeated guitar line achieves that effect masterfully. Although the guitar riffs in pretty much every track are angular, it’s most pronounced on “Washer”; when I listen, I can see the loop-de-loops in my head so clearly. I’ll use this opportunity to explain what I mean by “angular”: an angular riff is one in which the successive notes go up or down steeply, usually more than a third. If you were to transcribe an angular riff to sheet music, and then connect the notes like connect-the-dots, it would result in really sharp angles. This is what makes the guitars on this album sound so thin and spindly and circular, and the effect is so good. All of this is not even to mention the story told in “Washer”, a crushing suicide note. One of the greatest songs of all time.

The first two tracks are amazing, and I’m grouping them together because they’re the two that really play with time signatures. The groove is constantly changing, especially in the loud section of “Breadcrumb Trail” where it switches between 12/8 and 15/8 seemingly at random. You can’t feel comfortable if you can’t even predict how long the phrase will last! Also in “Breadcrumb Trail”, the story about a carnival reminds me of Ray Bradbury’s novel *Something Wicked this Way Comes*. Super creepy. “Nosferatu Man” I think shows the hardcore roots more than any other song, the loud sections are just so harsh.

“Don, Aman” and “For Dinner…” are probably my least favorite tracks, but that’s like saying a handjob is my least favorite way to orgasm. “Don, Aman” has the most interesting and down-to-earth poem of the album, narrating an anxious misanthropes night at a bar. The guitar strumming does such a good job of building tension, and the chord changes are so ominous. And the climax, the distorted guitars, the return to a whimper; it’s so worth it. “For Dinner…” leans more on the quiet part of quiet-loud than any other track, but the guitar is great.

“Good Morning, Captain” is the perfect closer. The guitar intro is so creepy, it sets the tone perfectly. And then the bass, for reasons I can’t describe, sounds rusty to me. Like the strings have rusted. The bass sounds loose but the guitar sounds tight. The story of the shipwrecked captain and the little boy genuinely strikes fear into my heart. The final screams are one of the most affecting loud sections, and the final drone sends Slint on their way.

A perfect album. An album that I have no idea how it even exists. It’s been 8 years since the first time I heard it, but it still felt so fresh when I started it today.

10/10

Favorite tracks: Breadcrumb Trail, Nosferatu Man, Washer, Good Morning, Captain

Least favorite track: For Dinner…
