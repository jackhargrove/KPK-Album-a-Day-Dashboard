# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

There are some really high highs on this album. After learning that “When the Sun Hits” is top 40 all-time on RYM, I have the strong urge to be contrarian or to pick a different song, but no, it deserves its placement. No word other than “sublime” can adequately describe it. But I’ll get to that later. There are some other amazing moments on this album. “Souvlaki Space Station” is one of the coolest uses of shoegaze guitar techniques I’ve ever heard. “Alison” is as ethereal as any pop banger has ever gotten. “Dagger” is gorgeous and heartbreaking. “40 Days” and “Here She Comes” are also quite good. Where the album loses me a bit is in its slow songs. They’re all pretty downtempo, but there are four that really strike me as feeling a little aimless: “Machine Gun”, “Sing”, “Altogether”, and “Melon Yellow”. After Carms praised “Sing”, I gave it a few more dedicated listens and it’s definitely now my favorite of these, but still not what I’m looking for. “Melon Yellow” is an absolute slog and definitely my least favorite track. “Machine Gun” and “Altogether” both have their moments, but they’re just not for me. Still, 6/10 favorite tracks with 4 of those being particularly striking is good for any album. I understand the mbv comparisons with both the guitar techniques and the shared scene, but outside of that *Souvlaki* and *loveless* just do entirely different things for me. I don’t think they should be pitted against each other as much as they are. With *loveless*, you’re constantly attacked with a lush but loud wall of sound, and the enjoyment comes from slowly picking out the gorgeous pop songs from underneath. With *Souvlaki*, you’re getting the aching beauty of youth surrounded by otherworldly atmosphere. I ultimately think I enjoy both albums to a similar degree, but I don’t think they should be seen as competitors or substitutes.

“When the Sun Hits” is the perfect shoegaze song. The instrumental and the lyrics are both perfect, and they combine to form something greater than either individually. I have never heard something that captures the wistfulness of your early 20s so perfectly. The quiet/loud dynamic is done more tastefully and to much greater effect than any grunge song. The verses build up a, frankly, unbelievable amount of tension, and the chorus explosion feels like I’m hearing it for the first time every time I listen. “It matters where you are”. What a line, what a delivery. Pure yearning. My one complaint is that it ends on a fade out when I want the instrumental to keep going and building for another 5 minutes. “Souvlaki Space Station” is also amazing, and the music itself gives me that outer space feeling referenced in the title. The guitar pulsating is out of this world. I appreciate getting Goswell’s take on the breakup here since so many other songs are Halstead-centric. The back half of the song does what I wish “When the Sun Hits” did where it just grows and builds into a monster. “Alison” is a banger of an opener, and I love the lyrics about this drug addict girl. Halstead’s vocals are gorgeous, especially on the chorus, and the guitar washes are some of the best on the album. This song makes me feel like I’m falling slowly through a thick, warm fluid. “Dagger” closes the album on a similarly strong note. You know shit is going down when that acoustic comes out. The song is very well-written, and it’s positively heartbreaking. I would also be this sad if I fumbled Rachel Goswell. That soft but sharp piano in the background adds so much to the cold atmosphere, and the bass that comes in halfway through is great as well. “40 Days” is pretty enough to work its way onto my favorite tracks. I love the warbling feedback, I love the dueted vocals, the underlying song itself isn’t that strong but I really enjoy listening. The guitars on “Here She Comes” are just so pretty as well. I like this one because of how effectively it pulls you into Halstead’s world of cold isolation.

That’s all just 60% of the album though, and the rest of it doesn’t measure up for me. “Sing” has Goswell’s vocals and the haunting ringing of the guitar going for it, but there’s so little motion. It’s just not what I’m looking for. That being said, it does get better near the end when the guitars get wavy and kaleidoscopic, but everything is still too ethereal and passes me by. I liked this one better with every subsequent listen, and I have hope that trend continues. “Machine Gun” has a great chorus, but I do not love Goswell’s vocals on the verses. It’s too downtempo overall, and it suffers from coming right after “Alison”. “Altogether” similarly dashes momentum after “When the Sun Hits”. I appreciate the nod to The Beatles with the “yeah yeah yeah” melody, but it ends up being too distracting. Just something that doesn’t grip me about this song. “Melon Yellow” is the absolute nadir of the album, it’s a real slog at the end. It feels like Slowdive’s half-assed take on gothic rock. Even when things get loud at the end I just don’t care, and it fizzles out.

In my opinion, *loveless* is more consistently good, but *Souvlaki* has the highest highs by far. I know I said I don’t think the two can be directly compared, that’s my general read. Many songs I have and will come back to, but with some small but noticeable flaws as an album. Still, very enjoyable day of listening. I would put my feelings on this as very similar to *Surfer Rosa* by the Pixies.

Favorite tracks: Alison, 40 Days, Here She Comes, Souvlaki Space Station, When the Sun Hits, Dagger

Least favorite track: Melon Yellow
