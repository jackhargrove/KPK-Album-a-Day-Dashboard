# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

This album is a really weird mix of like *Bitches Brew* and Jethro Tull. It’s a bunch of druggy white Brits making a jazz fusion album. And this is much more actual jazz fusion than I possibly could have imagined. Knowing what I knew about Soft Machine and the British prog scene, I assumed the jazz elements would be subordinate to the prog elements, like the jazz sections on “21st Century Schizoid Man”. But these white boys really play! And they also add a whole bunch of flute! Overall, *Third* has a great vibe to it and is a consistently enjoyable listen. However, it’s also much too long, both at the album level and at the individual track level. Some of these tracks, particularly “Facelift” and “Moon in June”, feel stretched out solely for the purpose of taking up an entire album side. There’s a lot of tape manipulation stuff, which I find in experimental rock to often be aimless cargo culting of the avant-garde and minimalist composers who pioneered the technique. That’s true here somewhat, but actually I was mostly impressed with how well they did the tape manipulation stuff. Those interlude sections on “Out-Bloody-Rageous” sound like pure sex! Another highlight for me was Robert Wyatt’s bass guitar playing. It’s hard to pinpoint exactly what it was, but man do I just love how free and loose that bass sounds. It’s never doing anything too out there, but it always has both swagger and an element of unpredictability. Sadly, Wyatt’s vocal skills are nowhere near his bass playing, and he sounds a little silly singing on “Moon in June”. I liked this album a lot better than I thought I would, but it’s still got its issues. I’d like to condense it down, but while I think there’s not enough material for a double album, I also think there’s too much material for a single album. And that just about sums it up for me: more than I expected but not quite as much as I’d like.

The album unfortunately opens with its weakest track by far, which really gave me the wrong impression on my first listen. “Facelift” starts with experimental noise stuff that’s a little bit interesting, particularly for 1970, but takes way too long to give way to real music. It’s over five minutes of noodling, albeit not the most offensive noodling I’ve ever heard. Once the sax melody starts, the track reminds me of Faust a lot. I really wish we had listened to this and Zappa before doing all of the krautrock. Along with the slow start, my biggest issue with this track is how disjointed it is. It constantly switches between different melodies, different instruments, and different performances. I can’t get into a groove with it at all. “Slightly All the Time” kicks off with a cool bass line, then a nice, breezy sax part. This track is so smooth. And when it suddenly shifts into double time, I love that too. In the second slow section, there’s a rhythmic part played by the piano in 9/8 that features four dotted eighth notes followed by three eighth notes that I like a lot. I don’t know that this track needs to be 18 minutes, but it’s frequently a great listen. “Moon in June” has a lot going for it. The drum rhythm is interesting. The vocal melody builds quite pleasingly. The bass steals the show once again. But, fuck, does Wyatt have kind of a feeble voice. It just doesn’t do it for me. I like the instrumental section in the midsection best, particularly the bass line and the violin solo. And I would be remiss not to mention the driving yet dynamic rhythm in the drums. Sadly, the track ends on an extended spacey tape part that goes on way too long. I know I just complained about tape bullshit, but the tape bullshit on “Out-Bloody-Rageous” is actually really really good. At five minutes, the drums start, queuing more great sax playing. The uneven rhythm throws me off in a good way, and whatever the melodic instrument is sounds so interesting. It slips back into cool tape stuff before settling into a slow section with a different but still completely unstable rhythm that I find hypnotic. It ends on the coolest tape shit yet, with this shimmering piano loop.

I enjoyed listening to this while it was on and have less strong feelings now that it’s not. I really wanted to put this in B Tier, but there’s no way I could justify putting it above Yes a the top of C Tier. Oh well.

Favorite tracks: Slightly All the Time, Out-Bloody-Rageous

Least favorite track: Facelift
