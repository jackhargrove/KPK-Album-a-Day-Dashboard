# Write-up

SOPHIE (she originally went by the stage name “Sophie Xeon” but nobody really uses that anymore) was an electronic music producer and DJ born in England in 1986. She was one of the most influential electronic musicians of the 2010s as her bubblegum bass style set the stage for hyperpop. Although it is commonly believed that SOPHIE was raised in Glasgow, Scotland, she was in fact raised in England. Her father introduced her to the thriving UK rave scene of the 90s while she was a kid. She honed her craft as both a producer and DJ in the band Motherland, which played across Europe in the late 2000s. Her first commercial music release was a remix she made in 2012 that was put out by Mexican Summer, the American indie label that brought you Ariel Pink and Weyes Blood. Around that same time, SOPHIE met A. G. Cook and Danny L. Harle; the former would found PC Music the next year and the latter would become one of the first signed artists. I’ve written about PC Music before, but as a refresher, they were a British music label founded in 2013 whose artists made experimental EDM and pop music that would develop into hyperpop. Though SOPHIE is heavily associated with PC Music and collaborated with PC Music artists frequently, she never signed with the label. SOPHIE released her debut solo single “Nothing More to Say” in 2013, now signed to a British label for artists from Glasgow (likely why people thought she was Scottish). The track was mostly conventional electro house, but it already included elements of her sugary sound, heavy bass, and bouncy drums. Later that year, she released her second single, “BIPP”. This was the first SOPHIE solo track I ever heard. It’s this really bizarre EDM track with a prominent repeated vocal part that didn’t really resemble any existing electronic music. It was a huge critical success, with Pitchfork in particular taking notice and putting it on their year-end list. Her next single, 2014’s “Lemonade”, is even weirder. It’s a bouncy, slippery, squelching vocal EDM track about drinking piss in a BDSM context. The true lyrical meaning is hidden though, as evidenced by McDonald’s choice to use it in a 2015 ad campaign. Though I’ve noticed that in retrospect other SOPHIE songs from this era are better remembered, at the time before this album was released, “BIPP” and “Lemonade” were the two go-to SOPHIE tracks. All of SOPHIE’s singles were released together in 2015 as the compilation album *Product*.

In 2015, middling one-hit wonder pop star Charli XCX announced she would be making a collaborative EP entirely produced by the eclectic, avant-garde SOPHIE. The media found this move baffling for both parties. Why would this plastic pop star throw her career away for some weirdos? Why would SOPHIE ruin her underground art cred? The resulting EP, 2016’s *Vroom Vroom*, polarized critics. Pitchfork, an early SOPHIE champion, gave it a 4.5, though they have since re-reviewed it. However, it proved to be a seminal, essential release for the budding internet-obsessed alt-pop scene. SOPHIE would go on to be a frequent producer for Charli XCX, working on both *Number 1 Angel* and *Pop 2* in 2017. Also that year, SOPHIE, who had been presenting as a cis man, officially came out as transgender. She began working with more mainstream artists, including making a beat for “Yeah Right” by Vince Staples feat. Kendrick Lamar, and an unreleased Lady Gaga collaboration. She announced that she would be making her debut studio album, which released as *Oil of Every Pearl’s Un-Insides* in 2018. SOPHIE was famously private in the early part of her career, never showing her face or voice, but this album featured a picture of her on the cover and she was in the music videos. It remains her most acclaimed project and made her the first openly transgender artist to receive a Grammy nomination. She released a fully remixed version of the album in 2019 to further acclaim. Afterward, she laid low for a couple years, but nothing seemed off. However, in January 2021, it was suddenly announced that she had died at age 34. This was shocking; it took time for details to emerge, and many people assumed suicide, but ultimately it was revealed that she drunkenly fell off her third-story balcony in Greece while taking a picture of the moon. A tragic, tragic accident. Artists as major as Rihanna and FKA Twigs commented on her death, proving her reach. Vince Staples was and is still completely beside himself, considering her the only true genius he’s ever met. Charli XCX dedicated her 2022 album *Crash* TO SOPHIE’s memory, and the track “So I” from *Brat*expresses her regret that she didn’t spend more time with SOPHIE when she was alive. Despite SOPHIE’s family’s effort to deface her memory with an ill-conceived posthumous album in 2024, SOPHIE has left a legacy on pop in the 2020s that is perhaps greater than any other artist’s.

Though there were many terms invented in the 2010s to try and describe SOPHIE’s music genre, the one that stuck was “bubblegum bass”. UK bass was the style of EDM that emerged in the wake of dubstep in late 2000s Britain that emphasized a deep bass sound. Deconstructed club was an industrial and cerebral EDM genre to emerge from UK bass. SOPHIE made deconstructed club music that had a sickeningly sweet and plastic feeling akin to bubblegum pop. UK bass + bubblegum pop = bubblegum bass. Though artists like 100 gecs took the bubblegum bass sound and turned it into hyperpop, it would be improper to call SOPHIE’s music hyperpop. SOPHIE was particularly known for her use of synthesized sounds that mimicked grating metallic and rubbery materials, a well as her unrelenting industrial approach to EDM. Throughout the 2010s, it was often remarked that SOPHIE’s music sounded like it was from the future, and the influence her sound has had on 2020s pop bears that out. For my money, her collaboration with Charli XCX on *Vroom Vroom*was her overall best work, but she has so much great music to explore. I wish she was still around to make more.

# Hargrove's review

This is the most recent album we’ve listened to, and the first one I remember listening to immediately when it was released. By 2018, I was super engrossed in the PC Music/bubblegum bass scene (I can’t remember if we were saying “hyperpop” yet). My introduction to that whole sound was Charli XCX’s *Vroom Vroom*, which I got to about a year and a half late. When I did it hear it, though, it absolutely floored me. It sounded like nothing I had ever heard before. It was unmistakably pop, but man was it harsh on the ears. The synths were metallic, percussive, and loud. That was my introduction to SOPHIE. To this day, she is the progenitor of all of this for me. I give credit to A. G. Cook for facilitating so much innovation through his founding and leadership of PC Music, but as a visionary, SOPHIE had no equal. At some point I listened to *Product* and added “BIPP” and “Lemonade” into my regular rotation. When *Oil of Every Pearl’s Un-Insides* came out, it was an event that united all the different forums I was on. The popheads loved “Immaterial” and “Faceshopping”, the indieheads loved “Is It Cold in the Water?”, the experimentalheads loved “Whole New World/Pretend World”, the hip hop heads liked her sense of rhythm and the fact that she had worked with Vince Staples. For me, the soul of this album will always be “Ponyboy”. I wasn’t sure about “It’s Okay to Cry” at first (I like it now), but the second the bass synth hits in “Ponyboy” my face was melted clean off. Holy shit that song bangs. It’s just so aggressive. It turns pop music into a full on assault of the senses. My dad used to make fun of me for listening to too much pop, and I played this album to scare him. When “Ponyboy” came on at full volume in his home office, it scared our dog so much he ran out of the room. In addition to “Ponyboy”, “Faceshopping” and “Immaterial” are eternal bangers that will never fall out of my rotation. “Is It Cold in the Water?”, “It’s Okay to Cry”, and “Whole New World/Pretend World” are songs that I very much like and respect. “Infatuation” and “Not Okay” are both fine, and “Pretending” is a bit of a drag. This album certainly falls into a bit of a rut in its midsection. But the highs are so goddamn high. While listening yesterday, I realized that I had never really given the lyrics their due. Every song treats concepts of change and identity with an astonishing level of frankness and thoughtfulness. As cliché as it sounds, I really love that SOPHIE was always herself or, at least, got there eventually. It’s how she built something out of nothing with her music. Her premature death is genuinely ione of the biggest losses for music this century.

“It’s Okay to Cry” is a song that I respect more than I actually like. It’s a good song, don’t get me wrong, but what I really enjoy about it is how it doesn’t prepare people for “Ponyboy” in any way. It’s a pretty pop song with beautiful lyrics and a decent build. It’s good. “Ponyboy”, however is sublime. It fucks so unbelievably hard. It rattles my brain around my skull every single time I hear it. That bass synth is just relentlessly. It’s like a jackhammer. The lyrics are hilarious, the instrumental bridge is transcendent, and I love the soulful vocals on the “he is just a pony” section. What a behemoth of a song. “Faceshopping” keeps the quality going. The metallic sounds just straight up give me a hard-on. SOPHIE made a pop song that sounds like two metal bars scarping against each other. The chorus is catchy, the lyrics hit, and there’s another fantastic bridge. “Is It Cold in the Water?” is one that it took me a little while to get into. This was the cool guy favorite when this album first came out but I didn’t quite understand why. Though I still think there are better songs, I’ve come to like it a good bit. I like how the instrumental slowly becomes all-consuming without actually getting all that loud. The lyrics are simple but moving, and the vocals are just so expressive. “Infatuation” is still good, but definitely not up to the same quality as the first third of this album. The “I wanna know” repetition is super catchy and I like the simulated scuzzy guitar in the back half. It’s not quite as iconic to me as the other songs though. “Not Okay” is the shortest track on the album but is kind of a cool interlude. I like the stop/start synth experimentation over that bass tone. It’s fine, nothing crazy. The concept of “Pretending” is quite cool but a 6 minute ambient track is not what this album needed right at that moment. Definitely a miss for me. “Immaterial”, on the other hand, is such a motherfucking BOP. It’s my second favorite after “Ponyboy”. That percussive synth just bangs and bangs and bangs. It’s crazy danceable, has a wonderful chorus, and has just beautiful lyrics. “You could be me and I could be you”. What a stellar song. “Whole New World/Pretend World” is the perfect closer. The first half is so focused and aggressive. Just complete industrial intensity. Those abrasive synths come down like lightning strikes. I always get the “I looked into your eyes/I thought that I could see a whole new world” stuck in my head. The second half is the superior ambient experiment to “Pretending” if only because it’s placed at a better point in the album.

At the end of the day, SOPHIE was a singles artist, so it’s shocking that she put out an album that’s this consistently quality. It’s far from perfect, but its best songs are so damn good, and it never gets too bad at any point. I think if she stuck around she had a better album in her, but this is not a terrible one to leave behind. I’m probably ranking this higher than it strictly deserves, but sue me. This sound resonates with me.

Favorite tracks: It’s Okay to Cry, Ponyboy, Faceshopping, Is It Cold in the Water?, Immaterial, Whole New World/Pretend World

Least favorite track: Pretending

# Aidan's review

Going from "It's Okay to Cry" to "Ponyboy" is one hell of a shock to start off an album, and is a good showcase of the range of SOPHIE. This album runs the full gamut from borderline ambient pieces to normal pop songs to hammering industrial bass lines. The former two are done extremely well, and while "Ponyboy" is a cool song, "Whole New World" outstays its welcome, and causes the album to end on a bit of a sour note. I really wish there were more lyrical sections, because the songs that have them like “Immaterial” and “Is It Cold In The Water”Iwork really well. The production is obviously excellent, with fantastic watery synths that sound like nothing else I've heard. While the variety is impressive it too often works against the album, as it's not quite good enough to allow the mood to shift as much as it does. It ends up feeling more like an audition tape, with a bunch of extremely promising songs that don't coalesce into anything more than the sum of their parts. Sort of fitting for a career that ended far too early.
