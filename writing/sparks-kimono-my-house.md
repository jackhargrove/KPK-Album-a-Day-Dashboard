# Write-up

Sparks are an American rock duo composed of brothers Russell and Ron Mael. Russell is the frontman and has vocal duties, while Ron plays keyboard and acts as a straight man on stage, in contrast to his brother. The brothers grew up in Los Angeles and came of age when the Doors and Beach Boys were playing in local clubs. They joined their first band in 1967. Russell was 16 and Ron was 21. Their main influences were the British rock bands of the era, including Pink Floyd, the Who and the Kinks. They later formed their own band as a duo, called "Halfnelson" and a year after they had joined their first band, they managed to get signed by Albert Grossman, Bob Dylan's manager. Their first album was a failure, and the pair renamed themselves "Sparks", a shortening of "Sparks Brothers", itself a play on "Marx Brothers". Their second album was more successful, allowing the band to relocate to England and sign with Island Records.

The Sparks had always relied heavily on session musicians to record their music, and they recruited a new cast upon moving across the Atlantic. They hired Adrian Fisher, Martin Gordon, and Norman "Dinky" Diamond on guitar, bass and drums, respectively and began recording their third album, Kimono My House. The title appears in their song "Hasta Mañana Monsieur", and is a pun on "Come On-a My House", a song made famous in the 50s by Rosemary Clooney, the aunt of George Clooney. The brothers had long flirted with different styles, but Kimono My House saw them hone in on the glam rock style that was popular in London at that time, with T. Rex's Electric Warrior, Roxy Music's For Your Pleasure and Bowie's Ziggy Stardust all having been released within the prior 3 years. The other major shift in their sound for this album was Ron Mael shifting from an electric piano (electronic parts moving to make sounds) to an electronic piano (sound is completely from electronics).

The album was a smash hit in the UK, especially the lead single, "This Town Ain't Big Enough for Both of Us", a western-inspired piece that hit number two on the UK charts, the best charting song the Sparks ever put out. The album hit 4th on the UK charts. Their success did not carry over the Atlantic, with the album topping out at the 101 spot. Kimono My House has been enormously influential, with Björk, Steve Jones, Morrissey, John Frusicante and Thurston Moore all citing it as influences. Sparks would release two more albums in England, "Propaganda" and "Indiscreet", which are both seen as among their best. They then return to Los Angeles, recorded one meh album, then embraced the then-emerging Italian disco sound on N°1 in Heaven, which would heavily influence synth pop and Joy Division's hit "Love Will Tear Us Apart". They decamped for France for a bit, and continued to release roughly an album a year through the 80s, to declining commercial success. They had a comeback in the 90s after a short break before shifting towards classical music influenced pop in the early 00s, which yet again revitalized their career. They are still making music and touring to this day, with the actress Cate Blanchett featuring on their latest album.

# Hargrove's review

I first learned of Sparks in 2021 when the documentary *The Sparks Brothers* released on streaming. Neither my dad nor I had ever heard of this band, and yet here was this documentary where every famous person we’ve ever respected sang their praises. Naturally, this generated some excitement; that we might potentially discover a fantastic band we had never heard of was great news! But wow was the music just kind of annoying. Ever since, I’ve only associated Sparks with this episode in my life instead of as like a real band with real music. Putting aside that baggage, I entered *Kimono My House* with a fresh mind. And wow is it annoying. There’s just so much to hate about this record and this band. I don’t care for Russel Mael’s shrill voice at all. The faux-British accent drives me insane. The music is overwrought to the point of absurdity, making Emerson, Lake & Palmer seem understated by contrast. The songs are written with a hammy theatricality that borders on farce. “Talent Is an Asset”, “Complaints”, and “In My Family” is easily the worst three track run so far on the project, absolutely dreadful from top to bottom. And you know what the worst part is? Three tracks on this album are fucking amazing. Everything that I’ve said so far describes “This Town Ain’t Big Enough for the Both of Us”, “Here in Heaven”, and “Thank God for Christmas”. But somehow, these are unmitigated bangers. They’re just so well-written, from the vocal melodies to the guitar riffs to the truly unique lyrical scenarios. It’s so goddamn annoying! How do you even review this? We’ve had plenty of albums with a few great tracks surrounded by filler. That’s easy enough. Basically a form review for me at this point. But an album that swaps the mid filler for genuinely horrendous dogshit? That’s a puzzle! Even as I’m writing this, I don’t really know what to do with it. Hopefully I’ll have made up my mind by the time I get to the final paragraph.

How is “This Town Ain’t Big Enough for Both of Us” so good? I haven’t been able to remove it from my head all week. What an immaculate pop song! The verse vocals wind around like a melody from classical music. I can’t get enough of that guitar riff after the first line of the chorus. And everything is so tight and clean and shiny. Sadly, this is a certified banger. “Amateur Hour” is my fourth favorite track, but it doesn’t reach anywhere near the heights of my top three. The lyrics don’t interest me at all and really just come across as an incompetent handling of the topic. The vocals are catchy and the guitar lick decent, certainly, but the lyrics and the repetitive chorus hold it back. The Sparks sound pairs nicely with 6/8, but “Falling in Love with Myself Again” has an even weaker chorus than the last track. I also don’t understand what it’s trying for lyrically at all. “Here in Heaven”, on the other hand, takes a genuinely intriguing lyrical concept and knocks the execution out of the park. I can’t think of anyone else who would write a song like this. The amazing energy on the verses and fun chorus make it banger number two. “Thank God It’s Not Christmas” brings banger number three in quick succession. Again, the conceptual framing of the lyrics is so bizarre, but it just works. I also love the guitar intro, the way the arrangement slowly builds, and the melody that twists around so unpredictably. All three of verse, pre-chorus, and chorus are amazing in completely different ways. That is the last praise I will award this album.

“Hasta Mañana, Monsieur” eases the album into the really bad stuff. Lyrically, this is just the Beatles’ “Michelle”, no? But, like, clunkier and too clever by half. At this point, the songs also settle into a formulaic rut with little to differentiate them musically. It’s far from the worst track, but I do not like this song. “Talent Is an Asset” is much less far from being the worst track. I strongly, strongly dislike it. The bells are annoying as shit, the melody doesn’t hit even a little bit, and the only thing stupider than the lyrical concept is the lyrical execution. What a deeply aggravating track, with the outro worst of all. Just shut up, dude. “Complaints” isn’t quite as bad, but it’s still rancid, from the annoyingly choppy rhythm to the beyond awful lyrical concept to the stop/start guitar. It has zero redeeming qualities. “In My Family” rounds out this run by taking the album to a new low. While the last two were overly cutesy and faux-clever, this one is just plain stupid, which, honestly, is an improvement. What’s not an improvement is the obnoxious wine of the vocals. Any of these three tracks could be my least favorite, but this one just never ends and takes the cake. Unlike the last few, “Equator” actually has a decent lyrical concept. With better execution, it could have been as good as “Here in Heaven” or “Thank God It’s not Christmas”. Sadly, it’s just much worse in every way, though better than the last four. The incessant repetition of “equator” creates an awful foundation around which to build a song. At the end, it drags on and on and on, which is such a great encapsulation of this album.

Ultimately, I’m sticking this in my “fundamentally flawed” zone in D Tier alongside The Magnetic Fields, Mos Def, De La Soul, and Zappa’s *Uncle Meat*. I’m putting it at the top of this grouping because it’s highs are higher than any of them, but I can’t in good conscience call it a good album.

Favorite tracks: This Town Ain’t Big Enough for Both of Us, Here in Heaven, Thank God It’s Not Christmas

Least favorite track: In My Family

# Aidan's review

Sparks have the same talent as Ariel Pink for uncanny synth-driven melodies. My favorite song on here is "Amateur Hour", which deftly makes light of first time lovers without being cruel. "Here in Heaven" is a clever play on Romeo and Juliet, while the big hit, "This Town..." is just plain catchy. Most of the songs on here are catchy, mildly clever, but ultimately don't make a real impact. They've got plenty of creativity, who else would think of Einstein's childhood friends as a song topic. And I'm partial to the self-skewering intellectualism of "Hasta Mañana Monsieur". But once you get the joke of the songs and enjoy the melody once or twice, they wear thin. Fun for a listen or two, but nothing to write home about.
