# Write-up

Tom Jenkinson, better known as Squarepusher, is an electronic musician from Chelmsford, England. As a child, he learned to play organ at a local cathedral, and he formed a thrash metal band at age 12 in 1987. He also picked up bass guitar. In 1991, at age 16, he developed an interest in electronic dance music. The popular styles at the time were house, acid house, hardcore, and techno. Jenkinson in particular was obsessed with drum breaks, or sections of funk/disco/jazz songs where all instruments stop except the drums. The most famous drum break of all time is called the “Amen break”, which can be found here. This particular break has been sampled too many times to count. Drum breaks are best known as the foundation of hip hop, which was created in the early 70s when New York DJs began using two records to play drum breaks in funk records indefinitely. Though breaks are most associated with hip-hop, EDM developed concurrently with hip hop in late 70s/early 80s New York, and “breakbeat” refers to styles of EDM that use drum breaks. By the 90s, the most prominent form of breakbeat EDM was drum and bass (DnB), a very high tempo form of EDM with complex drum breaks and dominant bass lines. DnB evolved from jungle, which evolved from breakbeat hardcore, which evolved from very early hip hop. Jenkinson began releasing music as Squarepusher in 1995 at age 20, pioneering a subgenre of DnB known as drill and bass (extremely fast, complex, technical, and with a cerebral feel). Drill and bass is often considered a part of the IDM (intelligent dance music) phenomenon of the 90s, which included a broad array of artists like Aphex Twin and Autechre whose main similarity was that they made artsy EDM that was impossible to actually dance to.

Speaking of, Aphex Twin was an admirer of Squarepusher’s early singles and used his clout to get him a record deal with Warp Records. Aphex Twin was so involved that he personally curated and structured Squarepusher’s 1996 debut album *Feed Me Weird Things*. The album was heavily influenced by jazz and was a commercial and critical success in the UK. For his second album, Squarepusher wanted to move away from the jazz sound that was so popular in the mid-90s although, paradoxically, Herbie Hancock would be cited as one main influences for his next album. *Hard Normal Daddy* was released in 1997 to positive but muted reviews. The album is insanely fast and schizophrenic and features heavy influence from funk. Squarepusher was also interested in older video game console music at the time, inspiring him to use 8-bit figures on the cover. Though it received some criticism at the time, it has been in retrospect considered Squarepusher’s masterpiece, the progenitor of drill and bass music, and one of the greatest IDM albums of all time (at least according to Pitchfork).

Squarepusher would go on to have a long and successful music career. He has released 13 albums and countless EPs and singles. Like other IDM acts, he consistently reinvents his sound. He still releases music today.

# Hargrove's review

This did end up growing on me. I think it benefitted from my weird schedule this week where I had more days to give it a listen before I wrote my review. It’s still not my cup of tea, but I respect and understand it more now. On my first few listens, I just didn’t know what to make of it at all. The drums are so fast that sometimes they’re used as a continuous sound. The melodies range from techno to funk to video game. Everything is schizophrenic. I don’t see myself really ever sitting down to enjoy this as an album on my own time, but there are a few tracks I can see myself working in here and there. This could maybe be an elite workout album too. Still, my enjoyment is largely at a distance. I don’t feel like I’ve fully immersed myself into the electronic sound like I have with metal since we started doing this. I think I generally prefer more house type stuff than I do techno or DnB.

“Coopers World” kind of sounds like it could be in *Law and Order*. It shows the funk influence more than many of the other tracks and the drums are also relatively tame. Some of the stuff in the beginning can get annoying, but overall I’m a fan of this one. I’m also a fan of the next track, “Beep Street”. This is the platonic ideal of what I think the music on this album should sound like. I really like that sustained marimba/xylophone/vibraphone sounding synth that pops up occasionally on other tracks. It’s used harmonically and I love the timbre. The drums are impressive without being overbearing. “Rustic Raver” is not one of my favorites. I do not care for the watery synth, especially when I’ve been loving the colder, more solid sounds. When it gets kind of metallic by the end I like it more, but this is a miss. “Anirog D9” climbs nicely, but it’s just a short interlude. I really do not like “Chin Hippy”. There’s too much focus on drum wizardry while everything around it is annoying as fuck. Bad track. “Papalon” is a bit too long, but it pulls it off. It gets back to a similar vibe as the first two tracks, which I liked. That marimba synth is back. The beginning and the end are my favorite parts, but I also liked the slow and funky mid-section. “E8 Boogie” is just as long, but it does nothing for me. It’s static, takes up a lot of time, and there’s nothing particularly impressive. “Fat Controller” is a borderline favorite track but just barely missed my cut. The synth has a real techno element to it, and it’s a very rough sound but I like it. It feels much tighter and more focused than the previous track. “Vic Acid” is nice and short, and I think it’s pretty great. I could see it being great workout or work grinding music. Great melody. “Male Pill Part 13” is the best of the longer tracks. The funky bass line is a highlight and I really like the chord progression. Drums are also perfect all the way through. “Rat/P’s and Q’s” sounds like two interludes spliced together, but I think both are alright. I like the staccato thing that both have going on. “Rebus” is a let down closer, just a short atmospheric piece.

This album is not my favorite, but it’s the exact kind of thing I wanted to discover from this list: music that sounds nothing like the music I typically listen to. It was overall pretty fine.

Favorite tracks: Coopers World, Beep Street, Papalon, Vic Acid, Male Pill Part 13

Least favorite track: Chin Hippy
