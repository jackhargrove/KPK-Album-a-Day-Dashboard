# Write-up

Steely Dan is an American band founded in 1971. The band was founded by and controlled by the partnership of Donald Fagen and Walter Becker from its founding in 1971, its reunion in 1993 and until Becker's death in 2017. The band started at Bard College in New York, where Fagen heard someone playing so well on the electric guitar, he thought it had to be a black guy. It was Becker, and the two began playing together in local groups, one of which included famously cantankerous former SNL star Chevy Chase. After graduation, the duo moved to Brooklyn to try to work in the Brill Building, the city's songwriting hub. They got some interest, writing for a low budget Richard Pryor film, having Barbara Streisand record one of their songs and backing Jay and the Americans (known for their hit Come A Little Bit Closer) on tour, but they weren't exactly making it. The titular Jay Black called the duo "the Manson and Starkweather of rock 'n' roll", and a reputation for being difficult to work with would stick around the two. Their fortunes changed when they got picked up by ABC Records to work as songwriters. When they found ABC musicians unable to perform many of their compositions, they got a few other musicians to form their own band to record them. They named their band after a mechanical dildo from William Burrough's incredibly (but not legally (on appeal)) obscene novel Naked Lunch.

Their first album (and my personal favorite), Can't Buy A Thrill, was a success, with its singles, "Do It Again", "Dirty Work" and "Reelin' In The Years" charting and gaining airplay on radio. While Fagen sung on most of the band's songs, he suffered from stage fright, so band member David Palmer sung on stage at first, but Becker and their producer, Gary Katz preferred Fagen's vocals and persuaded him to tough it out. The miffed Palmer later left the band. The band released two more albums, Countdown to Ecstasy and Pretzel Logic, but tensions between the duo and the rest of the band were growing. The two were perfectionists to rival Kubrick, which aggravated the members who would have to record upward of forty takes and were increasingly sidelined in favor of session musicians. Additionally, Fagen and Becker disliked touring (partially due to Fagen's stage fright) and preferred to focus on just writing their songs. Most of the members left, leaving Becker and Fagen in complete control of the band. They went on to release Katy Lied (featuring the track "Doctor Wu", which we've heard on Double Nickels on the Dime) and the guitar heavy The Royal Scam (featuring "Kid Charlemagne", a sample of which forms the basis for Kanye's "Champion").

Aja was the band's sixth album. The album featured many prominent studio musicians, including Wayne Shorter, who has a sax solo on the title track. The album was their biggest commercial success, aided by the two using a manager for the first time. Like most Steely Dan albums, the album is known for its excellent production done by Gary Katz along with engineer Roger Nichols and is often used to test audio systems. Fagen and Becker would go on to release one more album together, Gaucho, before becoming embroiled in personal and legal troubles regarding copyright infringement and the death of Becker's girlfriend. The two put the band on hiatus, Becker became an avocado farmer in Maui and Fagen started a solo career. The two reunited in 1993 and released their acclaimed comeback album, Two Against Nature in 2000. They released one more album, Everything Must Go, before settling into just touring. Becker died from cancer in 2017, leaving Fagen as the sole official member.

Steely Dan are now often referred to as "yacht rock", a typically pejorative label that arose in the 2000s due to a parody web series of the same name. Characterized by high production values, a cast of studio musicians, jazz/soul influences, a slow tempo and complex, sardonic lyrics (often using the word "fool), other emblematic bands include Kenny Loggins, Toto, The Doobie Brothers and Hall & Oates. The retrospective genre was a popular target for criticism in the 2010s for perceptions of being "too white" and appropriating and black musical traditions to make easy listening radio pop, but has come back around to being more critically respected in recent years, and is less of a pejorative now.

# Hargrove's review

This is a fun one for me because my mom’s least favorite band of all time is Steely Dan. Yes, I usually talk about my dad, but this time it’s my mom. Her two favorite bands are the Grateful Dead and Jane’s Addiction. If you mention Steely Dan to her, she’ll say something along the lines of “so overproduced, I can barely listen to them”. What’s funny is my dad doesn’t even like the Dan that much, but he turned me onto them at a very young age. In fact, *The Royal Scam* was the first vinyl record I ever owned. I used to fall asleep to side B on repeat, “Green Earrings” forever seared into my brain. I really don’t know why he gave it to me. Must have been to spite my mom. Anyway, I adore *The Royal Scam*, and have meant to listen to *Aja* for some time. It’s pretty good! “Deacon Blues” rivals anything on *The Royal Scam*, and aside, the other tracks are pretty good as well. I typically like things with studio imperfections, but the dedication to absolute perfection here strikes me as a labor of love. I’m a little bit unmoved by the jazziness; I happen to know from my prior experience that even the most guitar-focused work of the Dan sounds smoother than anything I’ve ever heard. But, fuck, when these songs work, they’re unbelievable. It’s just unmatched movement, fluidity, and smoothness from these tracks. The songwriting moves me more than any overdubbing could. They’re just fucking nice with it.

I love the melody to “Black Cow”. In particular, the “tomorrow” part of “One of these surely will screen out the sorrow/But where are you tomorrow?” titillates me. Overall, this song is only decent. But, and this might sound stupid, but the fact that it only kind of bangs makes it the perfect opener for me. It eases you into the unbelievably funky world of Steely Dan. It’s just good, man. It’s just good enough. Drink your big black cow and get out of here. “Aja” is super interesting as by far the jazziest song they have. It’s just a banger, whaddya want me to say? Those solos drive me crazy. And “Here at the dude ranch above the sea” is hilarious. “Deacon Blues” might be the best song they’ve ever done. I love it so much. It’s stuck in my head ad infinitum. It’s simply so goddamn good, the best track by a country mile. “So useless to ask me why/Throw a kiss and say goodbye”, “Learn to work the saxophone/I'll play just what I feel/Drink Scotch whiskey all night longAnd die behind the wheel/They got a name for the winners in the world/I want a name when I lose/They call Alabama the Crimson Tide/Call me Deacon Blues” is among the best written choruses we’ve heard. “Peg” is super catchy as well. Steely Dan is great insofar as they just don’t sound like anything ever else. “Peg” reminds me the most of *The Royal Scam* in the way it’s just a catchy pop banger. “Home at Last” is decent. but honestly it’s kind of a chord progression merchant. It’s a super nice vibe-y track, but I would never listen to it on its own. It’s nice, it keeps me going, but that’s all it is. “I Got the News” has a shitty piano (complimentary), but it is a bit annoying. It distracts from the rest of the song because it’s too choppy. Definitely my least favorite track. “Josie” is decent, and “She prays like a Roman/With her eyes on fire” is a great line, but it feels a little over-the-top here at the end.

Overall, this is just a super lovable album. And yet, it’s not *The Royal Scam*. Will I ever be able to shake that? Probably not. Will I be getting down to “Deacon Blues” for the rest of my life? Certainly definitely. Good feelings.

Favorite tracks: Black Cow, Aja, Deacon Blues, Peg

Least favorite track: I Got the News

# Aidan's review

My admiration for Steely Dan is partially out of nostalgia, it was among the standards in my uncle's garage when he would work on a car or furniture, and Can't Buy A Thrill has been my go-to for accompaniment to DIYing. This is without a doubt their most accomplished instrumental album, Wayne Shorter is dong a sax solo for heaven's sake, and the production is as immaculate as its reputation. "Deacon Blues" is a strong contender for the best Steely Dan song, and perfectly embodies all of what I love about the band, an cryptic ode to the gentlemen losers of the world drifting through city jazz bars. The chorus is so good, "I want a name when I lose" is pure poetry. Unfortunately, I feel like the rest of the album goes too far in favor of brief, mood-establishing lyrics as opposed to the edge their best lyrical songs have. There's nothing that is lyrically as clever as the Watergate allegory of "Kings", the anti-Lennon "Only a Fool Would Say That" or the kiss-off of "Reelin' In The Years", and neither are there catchy vocal melodies to match "Doctor Wu", "Night by Night" or "Kid Charlemagne". It's still a great album from a great band, and I don't distain anyone who has it as their favorite work from Steely Dan, but it's not exactly what I love about them.
