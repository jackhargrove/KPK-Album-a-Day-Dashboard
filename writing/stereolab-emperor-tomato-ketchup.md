# Write-up

“Lounge Music for Marxists” was how Pitchfork summed up one of Stereolab’s earlier album and the absurd epithet accurately sums up the band. Based around the core duo (and until 2002, romantic pairing) of Tim Gane and Lætiea Sadler, the band was formed after Gane met Sadler while on tour in France for his previous (also left wing) band McCarthy. After McCarthy ended, Stereolab was born, drawing its name and sound from what was musically groundbreaking in the 50s and 60s. The group set up their own label but only found success after buying into the bogus capitalist system that’s destroying youth culture (per major label signer Thurston Moore) by signing with major label Elektra Records. Their sophomore album and major label debut, Transient Random-Noise Bursts with Announcements, was an underground hit both in Gane’s native UK and across the pond, featuring drone and krautrock influences.

Their fourth album, Emperor Tomato Ketchup was their most critically acclaimed and featured a postmodern take on pop, with the inspirations from 60s French pop and tape loops resulting in a poppier sound. Our second instance of something being named after an obscure experimental Japanese film (after Godspeed You! Black Emperor), this album took its name from a scandalous Japanese film of the same name which featured a hypothetical future in which children rule over and abuse adults in what can be described as “Japanese Saló”. Lyrically, the band drew on Surrealism and Situationism, an obscure brand of socialism that influenced the Paris 68 protests and pretty much every punk band around that time, before completely fading out. While situationalist texts are to put it kindly, abstruse, it broadly is a more Marxist influenced version of anti-mass media and consumerism that was a common American lefty trope in the 80s. Situationalism called for the creation of “situations” which exposed the underlying flaws of capitalism. Lyrics like “What is society based on?/It’s built on blood!” reflect both the revolutionary leanings and artistic aspirations of a movement that left only echoes in art.

The band followed up ETK with their most popular album, Dots and Loops, which featured a sound inspired by Brazilian Bossa Nova. In all, they have (as yet) put out 13 mostly well received albums. The band took a hiatus in 2009 and reformed in 2019.

# Hargrove's review

I wish I had listened to this when I was 17, when I would have been much more open to the style. The issue with getting really into a genre/sound/scene when you’re a teenager (in my case, 90s and 00s indie) you cherish the bands you were into but, once saturated, you get a little tired of it. I think I would have really really fallen in love with this when I was first getting into music, but as is I think it’s only okay. I did like it, but I wasn’t super wowed, and it wasn’t foreign enough to make up for that. Paradoxically, there are more songs I see myself returning to on this than some albums I liked better, but that’s more a testament to the pop structures and melodies (not a bad thing!) making it easier to work into playlists and whatnot. The one big gripe that I had, and that will recur in the review, is that I HATE THAT STUPID WATERY SYNTH THAT KEEPS APPEARING. Super annoying. Otherwise, the hooks are catchy pop goodness, the loops are interesting, the vocals are angelic, and everything is pretty laid back. Bleep bloop meets indie. It’s aight.

I enjoyed the opener “Metronomic Underground”, particularly the super catchy vocal line. The nonsensical baking vocals are great too. The guitar loop is energetic, syncopated, and one of the best on the album. It’s longer than it needs to be, but the ending cacophony feels very earned. I quite like the lyrics of “Cybele’s Reverie” once translated (really beautiful exploration of how everything feels new when young but boring when old) but I also love the flow of them before translation. The strings are so melodramatic, so up my alley. I also like the unorthodox song structure. “Percolator” closes out a great three song run to open the album with a crazy cool bass line and very staccato guitar. The song uses its 5/4 time signature perfectly, feeling completely uneven and off-kilter in every way. Unfortunately, this is the beginning of the annoying-ass synth, but at least it’s used sparingly and in a way that makes sense. The strings also inject the perfect amount of tension.

The album is very front-loaded, but there are some pretty good tracks on the rest of the album. “Spark Plug” would otherwise be a pretty mediocre track for me, BUT the sections where Lætitia’s vocals speed up and the keyboard kicks in at the end of each verse is so unbelievably good it outweighs everything else. “The Noise of Carpet” is the rockingest track and the clear “hit”. It just works super well honestly, I love being berated by the vocals and they do describe a pervasive and contemptible attitude within the underground. The drums are also perfect for the song. I have to include “Motoroller Scalatron” with my favorite tracks if only because the “What’s society built on” melody has been stuck in my head for the entire day. The 7/4 also gives it a kind of alien feel.

Unfortunately, a good number of the songs on this are nice in the moment but feel ultimately unsubstantial or forgettable. “Les Yper-Sound” has a cool glitchy ending, but outside of that, not much comes to mind when I think about it. “Olv 26” has great lyrics about the concept of heaven, but again, otherwise nothing really stands out for me. “Tomorrow Is Already Here” is by far my least favorite track, it again has one good quality (use of stereo at the beginning) but the keyboard is grating and repetitive, and the lyrics are dull. The title track “Emperor Tomato Ketchup” has an interesting use of vocal layering and a great melody in the second verse, but leaves me wanting more. “Monstre Sacre’ brings moodiness for the only time in the album, and there are great swelling strings, but it’s just so static. “Slow Fast Hazel” has some of the most angelic vocals on the album, but it keeps building and losing steam without going anywhere. “Anonymous Collective” has this awesome droning bass line, but the vocals leave me cold and it ends the album on a whimper, with a final random use of the synth to piss me off.

I do like this album, and I got 5-6 great songs out of it that I will continue to listen to. I appreciate the pop sensibility and the overall atmosphere. There are just so many songs though that introduce an interesting idea but don’t do much with it, don’t take it anywhere. You’ll notice with every song I wasn’t crazy about, I had at least one positive thing to say. This is both a blessing and a curse for this album, and an inconsistency that I’m still wrestling with.

Favorite tracks: Metronomic Underground, Cybele’s Reverie, Percolator, Spark Plug, The Noise of Carpet, Motoroller Scalatron

Least favorite track: Tomorrow Is Already Here
