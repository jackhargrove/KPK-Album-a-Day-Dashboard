# Hargrove's review

This was a record I’ve been looking forward to because I had never heard of Suicide before this list, but “synth punk” as a concept intrigued me and it was cool how early they were to the punk thing in general. Now that I’ve listened, it’s mostly just fine. It’s not revelatory to me at all, but I also don’t have anything specifically against it. “Frankie Teardrop” had been built up for me as this absolutely harrowing track, and it is pretty cool, but I just don’t feel what I think I’m supposed to. That feels emblematic of my reaction to this album as a whole. While I appreciate the minimalism of the synth lines, it also results in none of the instrumentals particularly standing out on their own. While I appreciate Vega’s intensity and commitment in his vocals, there are often points where the lack of substance underneath becomes glaring. But I never disliked listening to it, and I never felt reluctant to put the album on for another spin. It was just fine. Really, the biggest indictment is that I don’t have much more to say than that in this paragraph. For such an influential album, I don’t feel all that influenced.

Because the music on this album is kind of whatever, the best tracks are the ones that have other elements going for them. “Ghost Rider” has a certain flair and cool factor to it that I enjoy. The bouncy synth rhythm and Vega’s delivery make it a fun listen, and one where I can very much see what Springsteen saw in Suicide. I respect “Rocket USA” but it has decidedly less oomph to it than the opener. There’s not much more to it than the competing synth lines and Vega’s rambling. It’s fine. Compared to the staccato rhythms of the first two tracks, the synth on “Cheree” positively glides. Vega comes across terrifying as a lover boy. The twinkling synth line in the chorus is probably the most instrumentally distinctive part of the album. Definitely one of the best tracks. “Johnny” has a catchy synth line, but the whole thing feels a little vapid. I would pretty much never go out of my way to listen to it. “Girl” has more or less the same structure and subjetc as “Cheree” but it’s worse in every way. That makes it needless, and thus my least favorite track. I admit that “Frankie Teardrop” is genuinely quite cool, but it doesn’t exactly live up to expectations. I was much more terrified by, say, *Desertshore* than I was by this track. Still, I enjoy how the minimalist synths really let Vega’s insanity shine, while at the same time delivering an incessant pounding and rhythmic disconnect that heightens the overall intensity. The most fucked up (in a good way) part of this track is that it makes clear that Hell is real and Frankie will be tormented for eternity after his 20 years of torment on Earth. That’s an awful thought. The screams are also bloodcurdling. I like the descending synth line of “Che”, but honestly it just feels stupid to have a random track right after “Frankie Teardrop”. It also never really builds into anything.

This album is really just fine. Maybe the album I have the most neutral feelings toward so far in the entire project. It’s simply fine.

Favorite tracks: Ghost Rider, Cheree, Frankie Teardrop

Least favorite track: Girl
