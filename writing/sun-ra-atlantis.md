# Hargrove's review

After 18 jazz albums that flirted with but never gave in to complete freedom, we finally broke the dam to true free jazz two weeks ago with Peter Brötzmann’s *Machine Gun*. While it had elements I respected and even some that I enjoyed, it really was not my thing. Now, with our very next jazz album, we’re diving back into that whole free thing. Except, Sun Ra doesn’t really fall into the same tradition as all the other free jazz who can trace their sound back to Ornette Coleman. He was a true original, his sound coming from nowhere. And while I can dig that, it’s unfortunately still not really my thing. I did sadly form a bad first impression of this album through a mistake on my part: I put the wrong version of “Yucatan” on my playlist, recreating the later Impulse! release instead of the original Saturn release that Ra intended. That track listing absolutely destroys side 1 for me. By far the coolest element of this album is Sun Ra’s clavinet electric piano that puts the “space” in “space jazz”. When the Impulse! version of “Yucatan” runs directly into “Bimini”, that results in over 13 minutes of almost just drums, removing the album’s best element. The Saturn version reduces this to 7 minutes of just drums, which still blows, and “Bimini” is easily my least favorite track, but it’s a major improvement. All of that being said, however, the meat of the album clearly comes from the title track “Atlantis” which spans the entirety of side 2. It’s just…not appealing to me. It just sounds like disc 2 of CAN’s *Tago Mago*, which I didn’t want to listen to then. And that’s a huge dealbreaker for me.

“Mu” starts the album with a nice bouncy rhythm, smooth sax, and the *appropriate* amount of tribal drumming. I do actually like this particular style of free jazz a good bit, the kind that’s neither too frantic nor too loud. The jaunting of Ra’s clavinet takes me on a fun, roundabout journey. “Lemuria” is more of the same, introducing a good bass line. I’m not crazy about either of these two tracks or anything, but I do enjoy them. My favorite track is the Saturn version of “Yucatan”, titled “Yucatan I” on streaming. It starts with the only real groove on the album, created by the tribal drums. It’s largely in line with the sound of the first two tracks, but the groove makes it feel like more of a fleshed-out song. The Impulse! version of “Yucatan”, on the other hand, kicks off a brutal, brutal 13 minute stretch of nothing but a cascading avalanche of drums. Look, I really like drums. I really, really do. But I do not want to hear this. it completely arrests the momentum of the album and removes its best elements. “Bimini” is worse because it’s longer and comes later. I absolutely cannot wait for that track to end every time. “Atlantis” starts off promising with a controlled cacophony and an intriguing spaceship beeping noise. After a couple minutes, though, it jumps into *Tago Mago* disc 2 territory. I guess I didn’t give that record enough credit when we listened for its influence from free jazz. I’m not putting “Atlantis” as my least favorite track because I respect the ambition, but I don’t particularly desire to hear it again.

I think we have two more fully free jazz albums remaining. I’m hoping one of them can win me over. But maybe this style just isn’t for me. I don’t know.

Favorite tracks: Mu, Lemuria, Yucatan (Saturn)

Least favorite track: Bimini
