# Write-up

Sunn O))) is a drone metal band formed in 1998 in Seattle with two consistent members, guitarists and multi-instrumentalists Stephen O’Malley and Greg Anderson. The group takes its name from Sunn, a brand of amplifiers with a logo that resembles the “O)))” symbol in the band’s name. As such, the band’s name is simply pronounced “sun”. This name is also a reference to drone metal pioneers Earth, who have an album on this list. Sunn O))) has released nine albums between 2000 and 2019, with a tenth scheduled for release next month. Additionally, it has also released three collaborative albums, including *Altar* with Japanese drone metal group Boris in 2006 and *Terrestrials* with Norwegian black metal group Ulver in 2014. Sunn O))) also recorded two albums produced by Steve Albini, and put on a festival in 2015 that featured French group Magma. O’Malley is also a visual artist and has designed album covers for Burzum, Oneohtrix Point Never, and Thurston Moore of Sonic Youth, with whom he also made a collab album. Sunn O)))’s music is known for its lack of percussion or any kind of beat and their heavy soundscapes.

*Black One* was the fifth album by Sunn O))), released in 2005. The album features guest appearances from various metal musicians. Malefic of black metal project Xasthur sings on “Cursed Realms (Of the Winterdemons)” and “Cry for the Weeper”, plays guitar on “CandleGoat”, and provides screams from within a closed coffin on “Báthory Erzsébet”. Wrest of black metal project Leviathan provides voices on “It Took the Night to Believe”. Free jazz guitarist and frequent Jim O’Rourke collaborator Oren Ambarchi plays all instruments on opener “Sin Nanna”, plays “caveman drums” on “Orthodox Caveman”, and plays multiple instruments on “Cry for the Weeper” and “Báthory Erzsébet”. John Wiese of noise band Sissy Spacek provides unorthodox sounds on “Cursed Realms (of the Winterdemons)” and “CandleGoat”. Musician Matthias Schneeberger provides unorthodox sounds on “Cursed Realms (of the Winterdemons)” and “Báthory Erzsébet”. Sunn O))) members Greg Anderson and Stephen O’Malley contribute to every song but the opener, with the former credited as “Mystik Fogg Invokator” and the latter credited with a different name for each track starting with track 2: Taoiseach, MK Ultra Blizzard, Caveman Skillz, MK Ultra Blizzard (again), SOMA, and Drone Slut. “Cursed Realms (Of the Winterdemons)” is a cover of a song by Norwegian black metal band Immortal. The lyrics of “CandleGoat” are taken from “Freezing Moon”, the most popular track by the original Norwegian black metal band Mayhem from their debut album (the original song features Varg Vikernes of Burzum on bass). “Báthory Erzsébet” was dedicated to the memory of Quorthon of Swedish black metal band Bathory, who had died the previous year. The album was highly acclaimed, earning an 8.9 from Pitchfork.

# Hargrove's review

I have been looking forward to this one because the drone metal we had listened to in the past (mainly Boris) felt a little heavier on the metal than the drone, and my understanding was that Sunn O))) was the opposite. I was also intrigued by the cover art. Well, after listening, I was not let down on either point. This really is pure drone, just done with metal instrumentation, techniques, and aesthetic sensibilities. It is truly, truly ambient in a way that post-rock simply is not. As for the cover, the music absolutely sounds exactly the way the cover makes you feel. It is grim, colorless, terrifying, disorienting, and hopeless. *Black One* in deed. It’s up there as one of the most uncompromising albums we’ve yet listened to. Not once in its hour-plus runtime does it deviate from its mission. Given the heavy use of repetition, when it works, it really works. You can just let a particular riff or timbre sink in for quite some time. But when it doesn’t work, it gets uninteresting quickly. Still, I’m glad that it never really throws me any lifeline. The unrelenting nature of the sound makes me feel like I’m lost in an evil forest just as much as the growling or the booming guitars. For that reason, I feel really similarly toward this album as Aidan felt toward Swans’ *Filth*. I have to respect it as an artistic statement, but it’s opaque to the point that I’m still struggling to break through after seven listens. But it is undeniably a super cool record.

“Sin Nanna” opens the record with a sub-sub-bass foghorn drone. Just the very texture of it rattles me, an omen of the darkness to come. It’s a short track, really just setting the mood, but it does such a wonderful job that I simply must put it on my favorite tracks list. I love the sparse, clanging percussion and the creepy ass synth screams that warble around the foghorn. “It Took the Night to Believe” takes that energy and carries it into a full length track. I find the tremolo picking fascinating; I usually associate it with much more aggressive metal, but here it adds an extra tinge of unease. I also find the growled black metal vocals more interesting here as an ambient texture than I do in actual black metal. The tremolo guitar anchors everything else, keeping my hair standing on end for the entirety of this freaky, evil track. “Cursed Realms (Of the Winterdemons)” feels like a real swing and a miss. The lyrics from the original song are actually pretty cool, but you can barely make them out here. The chilly, windswept soundscape feels much thinner than the thick, ominous atmosphere of the first two tracks. And then it takes up 10 minutes. Its best quality is the staticky lost-transmission feel to the production. My ears welcome the sounds of “Orthodox Caveman” after the preceding track. The guitar is basically playing a pop riff at this point! It’s genuinely quite impressive how much this sounds like a high-point for the album considering how little is actually going on. The slight melodic movement in the guitar feels like a revelation, as does the rattling percussion near the end. “CandleGoat” starts interestingly with real motion in the melody, but unfortunately it ends up going in a direction I don’t love. There’s just not a ton that makes it stand out against the other tracks. “Cry for the Weeper” opens with a few minutes of pure drone, but the timbres are much softer than any previous tracks. That makes it all the more crushing when the guitars kick in around 4:00. A melodic guitar riff forms the basis for the rest of the track. While I like it enough to make this a favorite track, it does leave me wanting a little more. “Báthory Erzsébet” has my favorite lyric on the album: “For she hates you eternally with the ferocious lust that binds all that inhabit the waste and forgotten/The blissful loathing of you is now all that remains/Alone/Forgotten and damned”. It makes me shudder to even type it out. The track itself starts very strong, experimenting with production and electronics around this gong that emanates outward. I find this sonic experimentation exciting. Unfortunately, the second half focuses far too much on the vocals, losing my attention as the album comes to a close.

I would definitely prefer to listen to Boris’ *Flood* than I would this record, but there’s just something about it I can’t dismiss. In that way, it’s very similar to *Geogaddi* for me. I might see these guys in concert in a few weeks, and maybe that will fully sell me.

Favorite tracks: Sin Nanna, It Took the Night to Believe, Orthodox Caveman, Cry for the Weeper

Least favorite track: Cursed Realms (Of the Winterdemons)

# Aidan's review

It's hard to write a detailed review for a drone metal album, and if Pitchfork hadn't decided to ration their reviews like a methadone clinic, I would check out how they approached it. The first few times through this, I kept convincing myself that I had some psychic barrier that I needed to break through via exposure to enjoy the album, after finding myself irritated starting out before surprisingly enjoying the back half. It turns out the second half is just better, especially the lengthy "Cry for the Weeper". It seems odd for there to be such a notable gap in quality in a near-lyricless ambient drone metal album, but the swap from rapid strumming and normal black metal vocals on "It Took The Night To Believe" to the more feedback-heavy back half is a real difference. Solid ambient album once it got to the parts I liked, just wish it got there sooner. I think I would be more impressed by the sincerity of the vision on this album if I hadn't listened to a decent amount of drone music recently, but it's still not unimpressive.
