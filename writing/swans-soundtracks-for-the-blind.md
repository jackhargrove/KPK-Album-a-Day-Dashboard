# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: Swans was formed by Michael Gira in 1981 as part of the No Wave scene in New York. While discussing *Remain in Light*, we talked about New Wave, a movement that sought to make punk rock more commercial and accessible. No Wave was a rejection of New Wave, an attempt to make punk rock even less commercial and accessible. One of Aidan’s favorite bands, Sonic Youth, also came from this No Wave scene. In 1986, Jarboe joined Swans as the secondary creative force behind Michael Gira, and this set Swans on a path to more complex and less harsh music. On the songs with actual singing, Michael Gira is the male voice, and Jarboe is the female voice.

In 1996, Michael Gira decided that after 15 years as a band, Swans had run its course. He decided to make one final album to go out on a high note, which became *Soundtracks for the Blind*, titled as such because he saw it as a soundtrack for a movie that does not exist. He gathered various sound recordings made by Swans between 1981-1996, both live and in the studio, and put them together to form a cohesive experience. The first disc of the album is titled “Silver”, while the second disc is titled “Copper”.

Given the album’s grand scope, as well as the fact that it was the closer to Swans’ discography, led many to consider it their finest work. In 2010, Swans reformed (without Jarboe, although there’s no bad blood there) and have continued to receive significant critical acclaim for the albums released since, two of which are later on this list.

# Hargrove's review

What an insane and impossible to review album. I think I’ll focus on the four long tracks (“Helpless Child”, “Animus”, “The Sound”, and “The Final Sacrifice”) and then bring up the shorter tracks that stood out to me. Overall a really cool experience that’s not like anything else I’ve heard, not counting Swans’ other records. I had never heard *Soundtracks for the Blind* before yesterday, but I have listened to their 2012 album *The Seer* which is the only thing comparable. I’m a big fan of the tension builds, and how much this album rewards patience. I do think this album is overlong though, and some tracks could be shorter.

“Helpless Child” is the masterpiece of this album and the best work from either Swans album I’ve heard. It starts unassuming, with an ambient intro, but when the guitars finally come in, you know you’re in for a ride. Michael Gira comes in with his gravelly voice to sing a horrifying story of an abusive mother. This part of the song does a great job at building tension, but it is absolutely nothing compared to what is about to come. I don’t really know what I can say about the instrumental section at the end. 7 minutes of build from pulsing guitars all the way to a cacophony of cymbal crashes, layered piece by piece. All of it is glued together by this melancholic, floating synth line. And then it all just stops. What a trip.

“Animus” is my second favorite of the long tracks. The “Screaming out your name/Screaming out” parts are my favorite, the song just completely implodes, the violin (?) strings are frantic, it’s like all the elements that have been holding the track together come unglued and make themselves known individually. Just total chaos at the end in the best way.

“The Sound” and “The Final Sacrifice” are both great, but the last two I discussed I think are a little better. “The Sound” seems to continue the story from “Helpless Child”, with the appeals to a mother who despises all. At first the closing instrumental seems like it will build in the same way as “Helpless Child”, but then you get these rumbling drums and this urgency from the guitars that sets it apart. “The Final Sacrifice” is the darkest of the epics; I appreciate the dark, plodding intro. However, it’s certainly the least dynamic.

The following are the other tracks that stand out to me and a brief reason why. I’m a big fan of Jarboe’s screaming on “Yum-Yab Killers”. “Volcano” is pretty much a precursor to vaporwave, with a parody of 90s dance music turned sinister, with the reversed drum beat being particularly cool. “All Lined Up” is one of the most disturbing songs I’ve ever heard in my entire life, and the intense wall of sound after each chorus blows me away. “How They Suffer” freaks me out because I’m scared of my parents getting old. “Blood Section” is the only track that can be described as upbeat, but something about it feels so hollow. Jarboe freaks me the fuck out on “Hypogirl”, but it also has one of my favorite melodies. ”Empathy” is the catchiest song on the album, and one of the most enjoyable.

Ultimately, this was one of the coolest listening experiences of my life. However, I’m going to give it a slightly lower rating than the last two albums because I don’t see myself going back to it as much. Let’s say 4 points for “Helpless Child” alone, 2 points for the other three epics, and 2 points for the atmosphere and ideas throughout the rest of the album.

Favorite Tracks: Helpless Child, Yum-Yab Killers, Volcano, All Lined Up, Animus, The Sound, Hypogirl, Empathy

Least Favorite Track: I Love You This Much

8/10
