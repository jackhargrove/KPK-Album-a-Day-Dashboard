# Write-up

Talking Heads were a New Wave band formed in 1975 consisting of vocalist and guitarist David Byrne, bassist Tina Weymouth, drummer Chris Frantz (Weymouth and Frantz are married), and keyboardist and guitarist Jerry Harrison. Byrne, Weymouth, and Frantz all met at the Rhode Island School of Design (RISD), formed a band, and moved to New York, where they picked up Jerry Harrison from the Modern Lovers. They became a staple at the CBGB nightclub in downtown Manhattan, where the American punk scene developed and flourished. Talking Heads always had more of a dance bent than their peers, however, as can be seen in the hit single “Psycho Killer” from their 1977 debut album. In 1978, they teamed up with legendary producer Brian Eno to make their first truly good album, *More Songs About Buildings and Food*, which had the minor hit “Take Me to the River”. *Fear of Music*, released in 1979 and again with production from Eno, is perhaps their first great album, as well as their first real experimentation with Afrobeat; “Life During Wartime” was the hit from this album. In 1980, they released their final album with Brian Eno, the masterpiece *Remain in Light*.

After *Remain in Light*, the band was burnt out, and had no real idea what to do next. David Byrne made a solo record with Brian Eno, *My Life in the Bush of Ghosts*, which is on this list. In turn, Tina Weymouth and Chris Frantz pursued their own side project, the group Tom Tom Club with guitarist Adrian Belew. They are best known for their song “Genius of Love” which you’ve all probably heard (it was notably featured in an episode of *Always Sunny*, Carms). Byrne’s relationship with Frantz and Weymouth had grown strained, and these side projects did not help matters. Eno chose to end his partnership with Talking Heads, their three records together matching the number he did with Bowie. The band pulled themselves together and made *Speaking in Tongues* in 1983, which would prove to be their commercial breakthrough with the top 10 hit “Burning Down the House”. In 1984, they made the concert film *Stop Making Sense* with director Jonathan Demme, considered by many the best concert film of all time. Having reached their artistic peak, and with growing tensions setting an expiration date, Talking Heads decided to milk their success while they could. They released the boring but massively successful records *Little Creatures*, *True Stories*, and *Naked* from 1985 to 1988 before breaking up in 1991. Frantz and Weymouth still hate Byrne, and they refused to reunite at Coachella for $80 million in 2023.

*Speaking in Tongues* is the last great Talking Heads album, and proved they still had it even without Brian Eno. “Burning Down the House” was the most commercially successful Talking Heads song from their original run. Its title comes from a common audience chant at Parliament-Funkadelic shows. “This Must Be the Place (Naive Melody)” was also a big hit, and is often cited as one of the most beautiful rock songs of all time. This song notably features Weymouth on lead guitar and Byrne on bass, switching their typical instruments. Because both were uncomfortable with the new instruments, the song is very simple, resulting in the “Naive Melody” subtitle. Both songs have been featured in infinity movies and TV shows.

# Hargrove's review

Similar to *Remain in Light*, I was familiar with the big hits on this album but had never actually listened to it. “Girlfriend Is Better” is a song that I really loved when I was 11 or 12, so I will always have special feelings toward it. This is a difficult record to review, though, if I’m being honest. It has 5 absolute bangers that are on the same level as any of *Remain in Light*’s best: “Burning Down the House”, “Making Flippy Floppy”, “Girlfriend Is Better”, “Pull Up the Roots”, and “This Must Be the Place (Naive Melody)”. Unfortunately, the other 4 songs are kind of mid. I’ve given a ton of listens to this now because I really want to like them, but nothing in them grips me. They’re not bad; they have a lot of the funky and fun elements that makes the good songs great. But I think it’s an issue of songwriting. None of those 4 songs have the fundamentals, the infectiousness, the transcendent qualities deep in the DNA of the 5 great tracks. It makes for a really weird listening experience too because the 4 mid tracks are all in a row in the middle of the album. What makes it really tough is that those 5 songs are just so, so good that it makes it hard to know where to rank this record. “Burning Down the House” might just be the best pure dance song to come downstream of the entire punk supergenre. It is just body-moving, contagiously funky, wildly entertaining fun. “Making Flippy Floppy” goes hard, and I get in a groove every time it comes on. “Girlfriend Is Better” is an all-time vocal performance from David Byrne (his post-chorus ad-libs make the track for me) with some incredible bass lines and melodies. “Pull Up the Roots” is similar to “Making Flippy Floppy”, a banger where I put down whatever else I’m doing and perk up my ears when it comes on. And “This Must Be the Place (Naive Melody)” is just as gorgeous every time I hear it as it was the first time. Simple and beautiful. Heartrending. But man is it a slog getting through those middle 4 tracks. I am determined to spend more time with them in the future in the hopes that I’ll make better peace with them, but after 5 or so listens I’ve made only limited progress. I even found them to be the least interesting parts of *Stop Making Sense*. Still, 5 transcendent tracks is enough for B Tier. And it’s not like I hate those other 4, I just find them genuinely mid.

Favorite tracks: Burning Down the House, Making Flippy Floppy, Girlfriend Is Better, Pull Up the Roots, This Must Be the Place (Naive Melody)

Least favorite track: I Get Wild / Wild Gravity
