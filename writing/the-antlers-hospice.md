# Write-up

Hospice is the third album by the Brooklyn based indie rock band The Antlers. The Antlers started as a solo project of Peter Silberman while he was attending NYU. It was heavy inspired by The Microphones, to the point that the band's name is taken from a Microphones single. The first two albums he put out, Uprooted and In The Attic Of The Universe, were self released projects. He followed it up with two EPs, which featured Sharon Van Etten and Micheal Lerner, the latter of whom would go on to join The Antlers. 2009's Hospice saw The Antlers grow into a full fledged band, with Lerner on drums, Darby Cicci on trumpet/banjo, Justin Stivers on bass and Van Etten singing vocals on four tracks. It was a breakout hit, garnering "Best New Music" from Pitchfork. In those days, that was enough to make a career, and the album was included on several "best of 2009" lists. The band released 2 more well received full albums and 2 more EPs before going on hiatus due to Silberman developing tinnitus. The band has since then switched to a more acoustic sound and released two more albums. Hospice is their best known work. It is a concept album centering around a toxic relationship between a hospice worker and a terminal bone cancer patient and Memorial Sloan Kettering (hence the song title) in New York City. Sharon Van Etten, who sung guest vocals on this album, has gone on to have a successful solo career of her own singing slightly less sad indie rock.

# Hargrove's review

This was kind of an album that I’ve been dreading listening to because it was foundational in crafting my taste. It was the first album I listened to at the recommendation of Pitchfork, r/indieheads, et al. in 2017 that I really did not like. I went back to look at my streaming data and I got through one full listen and half of a second before turning it off forever. As important as it is to learn what you like, it’s quite important to learn what you dislike as well, and I’ve named this record one of my all-time least favorites ever sent then. However, over the course of those nine years, I had completely forgotten what this album sounded like. I couldn’t have hummed a single melody, let alone named a song. I became worried. Would I like it? Would my entire foundation be shaken? Well, after five or so listens this week, I can say that it is…a little subpar. It is definitely not my thing, but it’s also kind of funny to me that I ever outright hated it. The album is basically a few really nice, really catchy melodies bogged down by the most annoying voice you’ve ever heard singing a clumsy metaphor over inconsistent indie pop/post-rock fusion instrumentation. I’ll go over those charges one by one. First, the voice. Look, I am famously a sucker for weird voices, and Silberman’s isn’t even that weird, but I just do not like it. Another indie band that is foundational for me with regard to how much I hate the singer’s voice is Future Islands. I fucking HATE that guy’s deep voice. Silberman sounds like a cross between his croon and the feebleness of Talk Talk’s Mark Hollis. Not for me. Speaking of Talk Talk, I’ll move into my next point: the instrumentation. It sounds like those sprawling, varied Talk Talk compositions tightened up and sanded off to create pop songs. There’s like a quiet/loud dynamic going on that hard shifts between the dreamy and droning music and the more rocking, together music. I call it “inconsistent” because there are some truly nice moments on the album, but overall it gets a little tiring and insipid. Finally, point three: the clumsiness of the metaphor. I do genuinely respect the big swing that Silberman took with this semi-serialized concept album. It’s quite ambitious. But there are just too many times where the cracks show and it pulls me out. The most glaring for me is “Bear”, which drops any mention of the hospice setting whatsoever. It sounds like it’s off an entirely different album. More generally, while I appreciate the sharp parallels between a self-pitying, self-victimizing abusive partner and a dying partner, the disanalogies build up quickly because of how much more sympathy you feel for the latter over the former. Overall, I found this record middling with some bright spots.

“Prologue” is kind of a formless, aimless instrumental opener, but I do appreciate the very loud noise near the beginning. “Kettering” is one of the album’s best tracks, but wow do I need to readjust to Silberman’s voice with every listen. He doesn’t even sound human at points. The extended instrumental outro is quite nice though, and easily the song’s highlight. The album reaches its peak early with “Sylvia” and its fantastic chorus. It’s catchy, fun to sing along to, and contains one of the album’s best lines with Sylvia, get your head out of the oven/Go back to screaming and cursing/Remind me again how everyone betrayed you”. Another great line: “And I hate my voice 'cause it only makes you angry”. Now, that’s a rare thing that Sylvia, the Nurse, and I can all agree on. Unfortunately, after two pretty decent tracks, the wight minute slog of “Atrophy” sucks the wind out of the album’s sails. The track starts as adult contemporary sad piano, shifts to abstract sound collage, and ends with adult contemporary sad guitar. All the while, Silberman curates the finest of Tumblr captions like “I'd happily take all those bullets inside you and put them inside of myself”. Awful track. I’m a little torn on “Bear” because of how jarring I find its departure from the narrative, but I’m including it in my favorites because musically it’s pretty much a perfect annoying indie pop song. The drums during the chorus are just, man. Even outside of the hospice narrative, I don’t find the lyrics particularly moving; I have a high bar for abortion tracks given how much of Joanna Newsom’s first three albums they take up. The title metaphor of “The Sprout and the Bean” is infinitely more clever than just calling the fetus a bear and refusing to elaborate. I won’t even mention “Baby Birch”. I like the reprieve of a woman’s voice on “Thirteen”, but it’s a deeply insubstantial and formless track. “Two” unfortunately delivers the album’s fourth banger melody. It’s still not my style aesthetically, but I do get that vocal line stuck in my head, and the lyrics are decently well-written. “Shiva” mostly goes in one ear and out the other for me. I don’t hate “Wake” overall, but at this point the album just sounds so smoothed out and sanded off to me. There’s nothing for me to grab onto. The sloganeering of “Don’t ever let anyone tell you you deserve that” falls flat to me as well. “Epilogue” is nice because it puts the melody of “Bear” to use on the actual hospice narrative finally, but I’m just not super impressed.

This album is much more low-mid than it is actually bad. I really only see myself listening to “Sylvia” again on its own. But it’s fine. It’s alright. It’s fine. It’s kind of in the same zone as cLOUDDEAD and Quasimoto for me.

Favorite tracks: Kettering, Sylvia, Bear, Two

Least favorite track: Atrophy
