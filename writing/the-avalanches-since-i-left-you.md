# Hargrove's review

Before this list, I had already listened to all three of the canonical plunderphonics albums: J Dilla’s *Donuts*, DJ Shadow’s *Endtroducing…*, and this album, the Avalanches’ *Since I Left You*. For whatever reason, *Donuts* has always been one of my all-time favorite albums. As for the other two, I enjoyed both and took 2-3 songs from each for my playlists to listen to a million times, but on the whole neither resonated with me in the same way that *Donuts* did. Historically, I’ve placed *Endtroducing…* above *Since I Left You* because it was more hip-hop, a genre I prefer to dance, and because DJ Shadow is from Sacramento. Sue me. Anyway, I had fun rediscovering *Endtroducing…* as an album for this list, and I just had the same pleasure with *Since I Left You*. For 40 minutes, it works so beautifully as an album. The flow between tracks is insane. It’s a real experience to listen to the samples recur between songs, taking the foreground in one before popping up in the background in another. The vibes on this album are also immaculate. Every last sound screams sunny summer days. It’s a real treat, a total delight. There are of course standout tracks as well: the instant mood-lifter that is “Since I Left You”, the easy breezy beautiful “Two Hearts in 3/4 Time” (though it’s definitely in 6/8 time lol), the operatically grand “Electricity”, and, my personal favorite, the oddball banger “Frontier Psychiatrist”. But I have to give credit to the shorter, less distinctive glue tracks like “Stay Another Season” or “Avalanche Rock” that make it such a stunning 40 minute experience. 40 minutes…but the album is an hour. After “Frontier Psychiatrist”, the album falls off fast and hard. Right away, you have two 5+ minute indistinctive tracks that halt all momentum. What remains after never picks up steam again. It’s really too bad that this album is 20 minutes long, but it is what it is. For what it’s worth, *Endtroducing…* is also probably around 20 minutes too long, but it distributes the dead weight through the album as interludes or good tracks that are a few minutes too long. I’m really not sure which is better, that or *Since I Left You*’s firm divide. Oh well. This is still a banger album that I had a lot of fun with and I imagine it will end up in A Tier.

The album starts with one of the greatest openers of all-time, as well as one of the greatest title tracks of all-time, in “Since I Left You”. It is a super mega banger, a sunny bop built off beautiful backing vocals, twinkling samples, a great bass line, and a perfectly chopped vocal sample. “Stay Another Season” doesn’t have much of an identity on its own, but it’s a fantastic transitional track. “Radio” intros a throbbing dance kick drum and an interesting rhythm from its vocal samples. It’s a good track. I adore the stuttery breakbeat drums, smooth bass line, and jazzy keyboard of “Two Hearts in 3/4 Time”. The Raekwon sample at the end steals the show as well in the best way. Easily one of my favorite tracks. “Avalanche” continues the Raekwon sample and introduces a De La Soul sample. I love the driving “She wicked, she wicked, she wicked” sample in “Flight Tonight”, as well as the bleep bloop computer noises throughout. “Close to You” and “Diners Only” are both decent tracks that I never much think about, but that are integral to the album’s tapestry. “A Different Feeling” introduces the most pounding dance beat since “Radio”. I like it a lot. It executes the quiet string outro nicely. It’s a real joy listening to “Electricity”. The grandiose opera vocals and the disgusting bass line mix so perfectly. The vinyl crackle and gorgeous piano of “Tonight May Have to Last Me All My Life” provide a nice relaxing atmosphere. “Pablo’s Cruise” exists as an interlude to aura farm for my GOAT, “Frontier Psychiatrist”. I cannot gush about this track enough. It’s so formally weird even compared to the other tracks on this album. The other songs mostly use the vocal samples as vocals, but here it uses the vocal samples as instruments. Along with the weird backing instrumentation, the chopped up parrot sample, and the earworm of “You’re crazy in the coconut!”, this song rules. “ETOH” and “Summer Crane” are both excruciatingly long pieces of musical wallpaper. There’s like 10 minutes between them. At least it’s a nice wallpaper, but I just want to get back to something more interesting. “Little Journey” is an interlude that sounds nice but isn’t enough to kick start the album again. “Live at Dominoes” is another super long track but at least it has some action in it. If it were shorter or earlier in the album, it would probably be a favorite, but as is I just want it to get on with things. I enjoy the thematic touches on the closer “Extra Kings”, like bringing back some previous samples and ending it with “Ever since the day I left you”. Still, we’re noticeably at a lower point here than we were 25 minutes ago.

I don’t want to go too hard on the negativity: this album truly bangs. It’s genuinely impressive what these guys were able to do with a few (thousand) vinyl records. I can’t wait until I can listen to it again when the weather is warmer. I’m putting it just a few places above *Endtroducing…*, but really there on the same level: very good but no J Dilla.

Favorite tracks: Since I Left You, Radio, Two Hearts in 3/4 Time, Flight Tonight, A Different Feeling, Electricity, Tonight May Have to Last Me All My Life, Frontier Psychiatrist

Least favorite track: Summer Crane
