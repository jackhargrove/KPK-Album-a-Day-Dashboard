\*\*\*\*\*DISCLAIMER\*\*\*\*\*  
The write-up will get more into the story, but this album is basically a reconstruction of an album Brian Wilson began recording shortly after *Pet Sounds* in the late 60s before he went permanently insane. *The Smile Sessions* includes a 48 minute reconstruction of that album, followed by nearly 2 hours of outtakes and random studio recordings. We need only listen to the reconstructed album, or tracks 1-19 of *The Smile Sessions* (ending with “Good Vibrations”).

# Write-up

I already gave some background here when we did *Pet Sounds*, but it’s necessary for this album, so here’s a brief refresher: Beach Boys become a super popular surf band in the early 60s, songwriter Brian Wilson gets really into LSD and weed in 1965 and writes the progressive, avant-pop opus *Pet Sounds* in 1966, but a combination of pre-existing mental illness and the drugs makes him begin to lose his mind. *Pet Sounds* confused American audiences, but it was a hit in the UK, and this success gave the label confidence in Brian Wilson to have full creative control of the next album. Big, big mistake. They began the “Brian Wilson is a genius” advertising campaign, which played up Wilson’s image as a mad scientist. And Wilson met Van Dyke Parks, a young classical musician who would become his primary creative collaborator. Wilson and Parks began work on *Smile*, an album Wilson intended to be an American opus that would put an end to the British invasion of the Beatles and the Rolling Stones.

Wilson and Parks began work on *Smile* in early 1966 based off of “Good Vibrations”, a track Wilson had written during the *Pet Sounds* session but couldn’t make work. They employed a very novel method of production; instead of doing everything in one take, they would splice tapes together to create more complex and grandiose tracks. As Wilson’s drug use intensified and his mental state deteriorated, production became much more difficult and expensive. Entire orchestras were brought in, and the songs became more and more elaborate. Wilson began to surround himself with ass-kissers and opportunists looking to exploit the crazed genius. Wilson began to piss off the rest of the Beach Boys; Mike Love famously told him “don’t fuck with the formula”, a quote that Love denies but that has come to define his entire career. Despite increasingly outrageous production costs, elaborate experimentation, and writer’s block, Wilson managed to complete the new version of “Good Vibrations” for release as a single by October 1966. “Good Vibrations” was a hit both commercially and critically, considered now one of the greatest songs of all time, but that put tremendous pressure on Wilson. In late 1966, production really began to fall apart. Wilson began to doubt his own ability, and he informed Capitol Records that the album would be late. In March 1967, production reached a breaking point, and Van Dyke Parks left; after 50 hours of tape was recorded across 80 sessions, this marked the end of the original *Smile* recordings.

After the failure of *Smile*, things took a serious turn for the worse for Brian Wilson. The Beatles released *Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band* in May of 1967, taking the critical acclaim Wilson thought *Smile* would bring him. He became paranoid, believing he was the victim of a Jewish conspiracy. Wilson would not regain control of his mind until 35 years later in the early 2000s. The band and record label scraped together some of the *Smile* recordings to release the album *Smiley Smile* in September 1967, to very disappointed reviews. Van Dyke Parks went on to be an in-house composer for Warner Bros. and eventually the arranger for Joanna Newsom on her album *Ys*. *Smile* became the greatest what-if in all of rock music, a lost masterpiece for the ages that only grew in stature with each passing decade. In 2004, after Brian Wilson finally regained control of himself, he completed the album and released it as *Brian Wilson Presents Smile*. While it was well-received, it didn’t completely scratch the itch of music fans who wanted to hear what the original record would have sounded like. In 2011, *The Smile Sessions* was released, a box set compiled in consultation with the Beach Boys with hours of recordings from the original *Smile* sessions. This included a 48 minute approximation of what the album would have sounded like. The box set received critical acclaim, with many finally feeling closure over the entire *Smile* debacle.

# Hargrove's review

This album is an electron: the second you observe where it is, its speed has changed, and if you try to sit back and let it wash over you, you’ll lose track of where it is in an instant. I’ve been aware of *Smile* for some time, and I think it’s such a crazy interesting story. I’m fascinated by tales of elaborate, over-the-top productions like this, or *Apocalypse Now*, or *Fitzcarraldo*, or *Loveless*. I’m also fascinated by addiction and mental illness, as you see in Brian Wilson or Syd Barrett. And, more than anything else, I love being in the know on the music lore. For whatever reason though, I never gave any of the material a listen. I fixed that today by not only listening to the reconstructed portion of *The Smile Sessions* but the entire 2:20:00 runtime with the outtakes as well, plus I also listened to *Brian Wilson Presents Smile* from 2004. It was super cool to compare the different versions, and to hear Wilson’s process at work. The issue was this: I don’t love the end result of this album. It’s just completely all over the place, never giving you enough time to get into any sort of groove or appreciate any particularly beautiful melody. I appreciate albums with lots of diversity, albums that force the listener to run if he wants to keep up, but this one doesn’t give any of the material any room to breathe. I think the track “Heroes and Villains”, which I just did not love, exemplifies this. It’s a genuinely impressive pop track with real dynamics and so many movements crammed into just 5 minutes, and I respect it a lot. But none of it sticks in my head like “Wouldn’t It Be Nice” or “God Only Knows” or “Good Vibrations” because you blink and it’s onto something else. I mean for Christ’s sake, there are only like 6 full songs on this thing! The rest is just noodles of harmony that fall off your fork before you can get them in your mouth. Just a mess that I didn’t have the stomach or the patience for, even if the scattered pieces are all so beautiful.

There were some tracks I really did like. “Do You Like Worms (Roll Plymouth Rock)” is quite good. I love that it’s slowed down, the vocals are transcendent, and the harpsichord (?) is cool. I thought the use of backing vocals was inventive even for the Beach Boys, and the bass line is just fantastic. It’s also one of the clearer pieces of Americana on the album, and it’s diverse without being overwhelming. I also enjoyed “Wonderful”, although that may be a result of it feeling like a reprieve after the complete aimlessness of everything after “Do You Like Worms”. It follows the *Pet Sounds* formula well: take a beautiful vocal melody, add gorgeous harmonies and baroque instrumentation behind it, profit. Don’t fuck with the formula. “Surf’s Up” is the first truly great song of the record. The chord progression builds tension masterfully and the melody is captivating. The middle section is a bit weaker but at least it’s beautiful, and the harmonies at the end are mesmerizing. I was surprised at how much I liked “Vega-Tables”. It’s honestly just fun and silly, really a lovable track. It’s also the longest part of the album where things settle down into one groove. There’s not much I can say about “Good Vibrations” that hasn’t already been said; it is an absolute, unadulterated banger completely deserving of its stature. It is the perfect balance between diversity and space for a single melody to take hold. Should have served as a model for the rest of the album. I also really liked “Our Prayer” and “Holidays”, but to a lesser extent than the other songs in this paragraph. I respect the former for giving a warning about what the rest of the album would sound like, and the latter reminds me of the two instrumentals on *Pet Sounds*.

There are some great songs on this album, but so much of it is just a mess. As I said previously, I just didn’t care for “Heroes and Villains”, and so much of the first movement is just noodling around the ideas from that song. Movement 1 ends with “Cabin Essence” which I found super annoying. “Doing doing doing doing doing” shut the fuck up. I liked “Wonderful”, but everything in movement 2 leading up to “Surf’s Up” is so aimless and shapeless. Listening to it is like trying to grab hold of fog. Movement 3 starts off fine enough, but “The Elements: Fire (Mrs. O’Leary’s Cow)” and “Love to Say Dada” are just such a waste of my time right at the end of the album. The former I particularly despise, it’s like a carnival ride that I cannot wait to exit.

Overall, I appreciate and respect this album for what it is. But man, is it just so *frustrating* at times. There are really these long stretches where I found myself getting kind of angry at the album. I will say, I did find the outtakes on the rest of *The Smile Sessions* pretty cool. I also thought this version was better than *Brian Wilson Presents Smile* because these songs don’t work with an old man singing lead vocals. It’s just unfortunate because actually hearing the album kills the mystique of it all somewhat. This album could have lived in legend forever, reminding us that in art, you don’t always get what you want.

Favorite tracks: Our Prayer, Do You Like Worms (Roll Plymouth Rock), Wonderful, Surf’s Up, Vega-Tables, Holidays, Good Vibrations

Least favorite track: Elements: Fire (Mrs. O’Leary’s Cow)

# Aidan's review

Saying that an album "makes you feel like you're on drugs" is a tired cliche at this point. But I don't know really how else to describe this album. It's not the fun fantasies of LSD that psychedelic rock purports to seek after. Rather it's the feeling of being slightly too high on weed and drifting in and out of focus. You blink for one moment, and you're in a completely different song, the melody and the lyrics have changed. The sonic texture is overall pretty similar, so it's easy to think you've got a handle on things before you're questioning if you shifted to a whole new song. Is that Surf's Up on there? My Only Sunshine? It's incredibly disorienting, and there's just enough recognizable structure to draw you back in with the catchy choruses of Good Vibrations and Heroes and Villains. It's the incredible diversity of ideas in Pet Sounds taken to the extreme. Why is there a song that just samples a woodshop? Why is there a song out of a 70s PSA to encourage healthy eating? Who knows. Brian Wilson's lost his mind and wants to you to be there with him, and has just enough incredibly catchy melodies to make up for the insanity of the album.
