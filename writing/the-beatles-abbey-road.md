# Write-up

The Beatles were a British pop/rock outfit formed in Liverpool in 1960 consisting of rhythm guitarist John Lennon, bassist Paul McCartney, lead guitarist George Harrison, and drummer Ringo Starr (real name Richard Starkey). Lead vocals on any given song were typically delivered by whoever wrote the song, with the exception that Ringo was generally given one song to sing per album despite the fact that he wasn’t much of a songwriter. Beatles history crash course: 16 year-old John forms skiffle (British version of rock n roll) group called The Quarrymen in 1956. 15 year-old Paul joins in 1957. 15 year-old George joins in 1958 as Paul’s friend despite John’s objections. Everyone else leaves the group, and our 3 boys look for new members. John’s friend Stu Sutcliffe joins in 1960 and suggests the name “Beatals” as a tribute to Buddy Holly’s band The Crickets. After a few minor changes, they land on The Beatles in 1960. Drummer Pete Best joins the band in 1960 and they leave Liverpool for a residency at a club in Hamburg, Germany, where they all get addicted to meth so they can play 6 hours a day, 7 days a week. They stay in Hamburg on-and-off for almost 2 years. In late 1961, the Beatles return to Liverpool, but Stu stays in Hamburg for a girl; he’ll be dead within 6 months. Now expert live performers, the 4 Beatles become regulars at the Cavern Club in Liverpool. There, music manager Brian Epstein discovers them and becomes their manager. Epstein gets them auditions with various labels; after getting rejected by Decca (shame they will never live down), they catch the eye of producer George Martin at EMI Parlophone. The Beatles sign with EMI and record for the first time in June 1962. Martin thinks Pete Best is a terrible drummer and convinces The Beatles to fire him. They recruit well-known local Liverpool drummer Ringo Starr as his replacement, and the final lineup is set.

The Beatles released their first UK album (at the time, different countries would get different albums), *Please Please Me*, in early 1963. The style was Merseybeat, a melodic form of guitar pop played in Liverpool in the early 60s. John and Paul, the group’s primary songwriters, begin the extremely fruitful Lennon-McCartney partnership: every song that either of them writes, even if done completely alone (as was usually the case), would be credited to both as Lennon-McCartney. Bolstered by lead single “Love Me Do”, *Please Please Me* reached #1 on the UK charts. Their non-album single “She Loves You” became the fastest selling single of all time in the UK. They became a sensation in Britain that was dubbed “Beatlemania” by the end of 1963, intensified by another smash hit non-album single “I Want to hold Your Hand”. Before the year was out, they released their second UK album, *With The Beatles*, which contained the first song ever written by George. In early 1964, Beatlemania finally made it to America: their first North American album (these are now considered non-canon, as it were; the UK releases are considered the real albums) released in January, and in February they flew to NYC to play the popular TV program *The Ed Sullivan Show*. They were now as massive in the US as they were in the UK. In July 1964, they released their third album, *A Hard Day’s Night*, which accompanied a critically acclaimed film of the same name. It was their first album consisting entirely of original songs. In August, they met Bob Dylan, who introduced them to three things: the American folk movement, what it was like to be critically acclaimed, and, most importantly, marijuana. They quickly put out their fourth (and worst) album *Beatles for Sale* at the end of 1964, but they became determined to make music that was critically acclaimed, not just commercially successful. Around the same time, in early 1965, George’s dentist secretly dosed him and John with LSD; the pair soon after became obsessed with acid and introduced Ringo to the stuff as well, but Paul refused to take it. They released their 5th album, *Help!*, in summer 1965, which was a good album but a little too obvious in its Dylan imitation. The Beatles were finally successful in being taken serious critically with their 6th album *Rubber Soul*, released in December 1965. *Rubber Soul* was the first great Beatles album from front-to-back, and one of the first albums to be more than just a random group of songs put together for commercial release. It marked the end of their boy band period, and the beginning of their shift into weirder, more interesting stuff. It also lit a fire under Brian Wilson’s ass, inspiring him to write *Pet Sounds* for The Beach Boys as a response.

As the most famous band in the world, and with their profile growing as a “serious” group, the Beatles began to face controversies and backlash in the mid 60s. The most famous of these was when John claimed they were “bigger than Jesus”, which got them heavily protested in the US. The Beatles also grew very tired of touring; they had been playing concerts nonstop since 1962, and their popularity was such that the crowds at their concerts were so loud that no one could hear the music. In summer of 1966 they released *Revolver*, a common pick for the best Beatles album, containing classics like Paul’s “Eleanor Rigby” and their most experimental song to date, John’s “Tomorrow Never Knows”. The tour they did after *Revolver* would prove to be their last; after playing the final date in San Francisco, the Beatles retired from live performance, becoming a studio band full time. Their newfound free time allowed them to spend more time writing, but it also imposed high pressure on them to release more music. Their first sessions for a new album yielded the songs “Strawberry Fields Forever” (John), “Penny Lane”, and “When I’m 64” (both Paul). The studio forced them to release the former two as singles, meaning they couldn’t be on the album, but they were extremely critically acclaimed and created much anticipation for the forthcoming album. Additionally, Paul finally decided to try to LSD with the rest of the band, and he became the first to talk about his drug use with the press not long after. And finally, *Pet Sounds*, which itself had been designed to compete with *Rubber Soul*, became the new best album to beat in the competition between The Beatles and the Beach Boys. *Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band* came out in May 1967 to unprecedented critical and commercial success. They became hippie icons, and helped to kick off the Summer of Love in 1967. They were quickly disillusioned with the hippie movement, however, and sought some deeper meaning in life. In August, they met the Maharishi Mahesh Yogi, an Indian spiritual leader (huge grifter) and became enamored with India. A few days later, their manager Brian Epstein died of a possibly intentional drug overdose. Brian Epstein had kept the Beatles together with an iron fist for 5 years, and they were completely rudderless without him. For the first time, the Beatles had no direction, no idea what to do next.

Paul had dominated the sessions for *Sgt. Pepper’s* and saw himself as the natural leader in Epstein’s absence. The first thing they did without Epstein was the psychedelic TV movie *Magical Mystery Tour*, a complete and total mess that was ridiculed and was the first real misstep the Beatles ever took. That being said, the resulting LP contained new great songs like John’s “I Am the Walrus” and Paul’s “The Fool on the Hill”. Hoping to shake off the failure and find themselves, all four Beatles went on a now legendary trip to India in early 1968. They went to an ashram led by the Maharishi Mahesh Yogi with a group of other celebrities including members of The Beach Boys and actress Mia Farrow. They primarily went at the behest of George, who was somewhat of a weeaboo for India. John was the second most enthusiastic, as he had just begun cheating on his wife with Japanese artist Yoko Ono and genuinely sought spiritual clarity. Paul went because he thought the whole India thing would be good for his image. Ringo went because everyone else was going, but he went home after less than 2 weeks because he was a very picky eater. Paul left a month later when he got bored. John began to grow disillusioned as he didn’t find the clarity that he sought, and he became convinced that the Maharishi was a fraud. John convinced George, who was a true believer, that the Maharishi was trying to sleep with women on the trip, and they left after 2 months. The Beatles reconvened in London in May of 1968 to begin work on their ninth studio album. These sessions were extremely tense: Paul was becoming more and more controlling and his relationship with John was strained, John began a heroin addiction and started bringing Yoko Ono to rehearsals, George was becoming a prolific songwriter but neither John nor Paul would take him seriously, and Ringo hated how negative the atmosphere was that he quit the band for 2 weeks. They had also just started their own business venture, Apple Corps, which was a ticking time bomb disaster. The sessions yielded the single “Hey Jude” (Paul) and their first ever double album, *The Beatles* (typically referred to as “The White Album”). This record was extremely scattershot, as it became abundantly clear that there was almost no collaboration between the members on the songwriting. There were also some highly experimental pieces, severe genre shifts, and the first ever song written by Ringo (“Don’t Pass Me By”)! It was released in late 1968 as the first release on Apple Records.

With The Beatles on the verge of collapse, Paul took it upon himself to orchestrate a brand new project in early 1969 with the hopes of bringing everyone back together. So began the *Get Back* sessions, named after the first song they (Paul) wrote as part of the project. The idea was to make a back-to-basics blues rock record and film the entire recording process. Unfortunately, many of the problems from the White Album sessions persisted: John hated being managed by Paul and they were barely on speaking terms, John missed half of the sessions because he was high on smack with Yoko, and George began to hate Paul because he kept shooting down his songs, causing George to quit the band for a weekend. A fun added element to the drama was that John, George, and Ringo, in an effort to subvert Paul’s new authority, voted to make Allen Klein their new manager. Klein was a sleazy crook who had previously managed The Rolling Stones, and those three bozos put him in charge of all of their money just to spite Paul. Probably the biggest mistake made at any point in The Beatles career, and the resultant money problems stretched tensions to their breaking point. Nevertheless, the *Get Back* sessions were fruitful, and The Beatles played their final ever live performance on January 30, 1969: a surprise performance of their new songs on the roof of their building in central London at lunch time. People on their lunch break from work were treated to the first Beatles concert in 3 years, and this performance has become legendary. Afterward, The Beatles gave their recordings from the *Get Back* sessions to engineer Glyn Johns to fashion into an album, and then they kinda forgot about it for a while.

By summer of 1969, Paul hated John, John hated Paul, George hated Paul, Paul was sad that George hated him, John was ambivalent toward George, George was desperate for John’s approval, and Ringo felt like the child of a divorce (even though he was the oldest). The end was near. Thankfully, they had the presence of mind to realize that they were the fucking Beatles, and they decided to make one last album before calling it quits for good. They began recording at EMI Recording Studio on Abbey Road in London. Producer George Martin wanted the album to be one continuous composition, but John refused because he didn’t want to have to collaborate with Paul at all; they compromised, and side 1 was made up of solo contributions from all four Beatles (including Ringo!), and side 2 being a medley. The last time all four were in a studio together was August 20, 1969. Exactly a month later, John told the rest that he was leaving The Beatles, but he agreed to keep that private as they were still releasing their remaining records. *Abbey Road*, the final album recorded by The Beatles, was released in September 1969. And that was that…except, can’t forget those *Get Back* sessions, which still hadn’t been turned into an album. They somehow found their way into the hands of famous producer Phil Spector, who added his Wall of Sound technique to the songs, including entire orchestras for some tracks. This pissed off Paul, who thought Spector had ruined the album. Klein ignored Paul’s objections and Paul was so pissed off that he stole John’s thunder and publicly announced that he was leaving The Beatles in April 1970. One month later, they released *Let It Be*, the final Beatles album, although not the last one recorded. After 10 very productive years, the Beatles were officially finished. George was 26, Paul was 27, John was 28, and Ringo was 29.

Where are they now? After the breakup, Paul, John, and George raced each other to set up solo careers (Ringo also went solo, but he was less concerned with the competition aspect). John had a head start, having already released material with his now-wife Yoko Ono. He had come to fashion himself a left-wing political icon, releasing preachy songs about world peace and prejudice. His most notable solo album was 1971’s *Imagine*, featuring the eponymous hit song. Paul began to record with his wife Linda McCartney, releasing *Ram* (my personal favorite Beatles solo album) together in 1971. It was recorded on Paul’s farm and contained a lo-fi but melodic sound that would become very influential on the development of indie pop, with some going so far as calling it the first indie pop album (which is, to be fair, ridiculous). Paul and Linda would recruit a few more musicians and formed the band Wings, which became quite successful. George was sitting on a tremendous backlog of songs he had written while with The Beatles but that John and Paul had rejected. He turned these songs into a triple (!) album called *All Things Must Pass* in 1970, which is probably the consensus best Beatles solo album. Ringo played drums for some of John’s solo work, and also released his only two solo hits in the early 70s: “Photograph” in 1973 and “You’re Sixteen” (ew) in 1974. There were still major tensions between the members even after the breakup. The opening song on Paul’s *Ram* included some veiled insults toward John; in response, he wrote the song “How Do You Sleep?” that was a far more direct attack on Paul (more or less a diss track), and that included George on slide guitar and Ringo on drums. Brutal. They never forgave him for trying to save The Beatles. All four Beatles continued their solo careers through the rest of their lives but, as time went on, their new music became less and less relevant. John was the first to die when he was murdered by a crazed fan outside of his UWS apartment in New York in December 1980. George was the next to go, dying from lung cancer in 2001. Paul and Ringo are both still kicking, but both are by now well into their 80s. In the not-too-distant future, there won’t be any Beatles left, and that’s sad. But what a legacy they’ve left behind.

Condensed version:

The Beatles were a British pop/rock outfit formed in Liverpool in 1960 consisting of rhythm guitarist John Lennon, bassist Paul McCartney, lead George Harrison, and drummer Ringo Starr (real name Richard Starkey). Lead vocals on any given song were typically delivered by whoever wrote the song, with the exception that Ringo was generally given one song to sing per album despite the fact that he wasn’t much of a songwriter. Beatles history crash course: 4 working class boys from Liverpool, became the biggest band in the world in 1964 playing guitar pop, started making more serious music and became critical darlings with their sixth album *Rubber Soul* in 1965, got really into weed and acid, released pop masterpiece *Revolver* in 1966 (on the list), stopped touring, released psychedelic masterpiece *Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band* in 1967 (album we’ve already listened to), released one of the most bizarre albums of all time with 1968’s *The Beatles* aka The White Album (my personal favorite but not on the list), released their swan song and pop rock masterpiece *Abbey Road* in 1969 (today’s album), broke up in 1970, pursued successful solo careers, two of them are now dead. Consult my longer piece above if you’d like more info. It is long, but I am a very dedicated Beatles historian, and my spin on events will be more entertaining (and accurate) than anything else you read. Please do consult me if you have questions about why they broke up, or if you’d like more detail on their trip to India, as those are the two subjects on which I consider myself one of the world’s foremost experts. But really, what else is there to say about The Beatles? I think RYM’s band page puts it better than I ever could: “*They're the Beatles man. Nothing new can be said anymore. If you go by popular opinion they will alway be the one group regarded as the greatest of all time. There'll be people who disagree with that result, but it's still an undeniably powerful statistic. In all likelihood, there will never be another band as popular and critically acclaimed. They win at music.*”

*Abbey Road* was not the final album The Beatles released (that would be 1970’s *Let It Be*), but it was the final album they recorded. As a result, it was written as a farewell to the world from The Beatles. After the immense tensions in 1968’s sessions for *The White Album* and the disastrous Get Back sessions in early 1969 that would be turned into *Let It Be*, all 4 members of The Beatles realized that it was time to call it quits. John in particular was one foot out the door. Paul decided that they should make one last album in the same style as their albums before Brian Epstein’s death and *Sgt. Pepper’s*, free of tensions (lol) and with George Martin’s production. After multiple false starts in February and May of 1969, the four Beatles came together to record in earnest in July and August at EMI Recording Studios on Abbey Road in London. The sessions were still tense: Paul and John still bickered, George still felt left out, and John’s girlfriend Yoko Ono was omnipresent. John wanted to make a traditional record of normal songs with his on one side and Paul’s on the other, while Paul wanted to make something artsy and thematic like *Sgt. Pepper’s*. They compromised, and the final result of *Abbey Road* was a traditional side 1 featuring songs from all four Beatles, and a side 2 dominated by a continuous medley of short unfinished songs running one into the other. The recording process made use of all the new-fangled technology of the late 60s, making it their most modern-sounding record. They also made heavy use of the Moog synthesizer at George’s behest. The album cover is among the most famous of all time, featuring the four Beatles crossing Abbey Road outside the studio in four completely distinct outfits.

The opener, “Come Together”, is a John song in the bluesy style he preferred at the time. It was originally written to be a campaign song for the opponent of California gubernatorial candidate Ronald Reagan. This has never been confirmed, but a popular fan theory postulates that the four verses are all cryptic descriptions of the four Beatles (in order: Ringo, George, John, Paul). The opening line, “Here come old flat-top”, was “borrowed” from a classic Chuck Berry song, who successfully sued over it in the 70s. It’s become one of the most recognizable Beatles songs, and was released as the lead single along with the next track. “Something” is a love song written by George, one of his two contributions to the album. It is in my opinion, and the opinion of many others, his finest song written for The Beatles. Frank Sinatra famously commented that it was “the greatest love song ever written”. “Maxwell’s Silver Hammer” is Paul’s first song on the record, and indicative of the theatrical music hall stuff (or as John calls it, “granny music”) he was into at the time. Paul wrote all the way back during The Beatles trip to India in early 1968, and had unsuccessfully attempted to record it for both *The White Album* and *Let It Be*. The biggest impediment to recording was that the other three Beatles fucking hated it. I think it’s pretty fun though. “Oh! Darling” is another Paul song, this time more to John’s liking (John even wanted to sing it instead of Paul). “Octopus’ Garden” was written by Ringo, the second and final song he ever wrote for The Beatles after “Don’t Pass Me By” on *The White Album*”. It’s a fun and silly song he wrote the melody for while on vacation, and George filled in the rest of the song but refused to take any credit (good guy George). “I Want You (She’s So Heavy)” is a John song that closes side 1. It expresses the depths of his adoration for Yoko. It’s the second longest track released by The Beatles. It was influenced by progressive rock, and in turn it proved influential on doom metal.

“Here Comes the Sun” is a George song that opens side 2 and is by far the most streamed Beatles song on Spotify. It’s an upbeat, hopeful track that people love. “Because” is a John song that’s the final standalone track on the album. It was inspired by the Beethoven’s “Moonlight Sonata”. The medley, also known as “The Long One”, begins with “You Never Give Me Your Money” and ends with the penultimate track, “The End”. It’s one continuous composition made up of 5 Paul songs (“You Never Give Me Your Money”, “She Came in Through the Bathroom Window”, “Golden Slumbers”, “Carry That Weight”, and “The End”) and 3 John songs (“Sun King”, “Mean Mr. Mustard”, and “Polythene Pam”). This inventive style of composition in rock music proved very influential on art rock. “The End” was intended to be the final track on the final Beatles album. It starts with the only drum solo Ringo did in his entire time with The Beatles, followed by Paul, George, and John trading guitar solos back and forth cyclically in that order. It ends with the immortal line “And in the end, the love you take is equal to the love you make”. While this was intended to be the closer, there was a leftover track from the medley, the 24 second joke Paul composition “Her Majesty”, which one of the engineers tacked on the end of the album without telling anyone. The Beatles heard it before release and approved it, liking the idea of ending things on a joke.

*Abbey Road* released on September 26, 1969. Six days earlier, John had informed the rest of the group that he would be leaving, and that is historically the official date The Beatles broke up. However, they all agreed to keep this private until after *Abbey Road* and *Let It Be* released. Paul was the one to officially break the news to the press the next year. *Abbey Road* was a massive commercial success, staying at number 1 in the album charts for 11 weeks. The critics, however, were unimpressed, with many considering the side 2 medley a gimmick. Once the news that The Beatles had broken up became public, however, critical reception increased, and today *Abbey Road* is the album most commonly cited as the group’s finest. It’s not my personal favorite (though I do love it), but it’s an album of banger after banger and a fitting final testament of the most successful band in history.

# Hargrove's review

I love The Beatles. I really, really do. Very few things make me feel so happy as putting on a Beatles album. Very few historical figures represent my personality as well as Paul McCartney (Guy takes leadership of a troubled organization he loves and valiantly does everything in his power to make it work while his best friends in the world couldn’t be bothered and denounce him as bossy, only for Paul to alienate them further when the stress induces emotional overreaction and spiteful confrontations? Get better writers!). I get so warm and fuzzy every time I put on *Help!*, or *Rubber Soul*, or *Revolver*, or *Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band*, or *Magical Mystery Tour*, or *The White Album*, or *Let It Be*, or *Abbey Road*. *The White Album* is one of my favorite albums of all time, but *Abbey Road* is pretty damn good too. Side 1 is full of bangers. “Something” is the best song on the album, but I can’t help but sing along to “Come Together”, laugh along with Paul through “Maxwell’s Silver Hammer”, get lost in the hypnotic guitar of “I Want You (She’s So Heavy)”, or enjoy Paul stretching his voice on “Oh Darling”. Hell, I even get nothing but good feelings from “Octopus’s Garden”. Good job, Ringo. You may not be a genius songwriter, but you played to your strengths. All of side 1 makes it onto my favorite tracks. Side 2 is a different beast, but one that I’ve come to love all the same. I’ll never understand exactly why “Here Comes the Sun” has become the single most enduring Beatles song, but George’s joie de vivre does win me over on occasion. “Because” is a tad insignificant, but really, really pretty. That synth is used so gorgeously. Then there’s the medley. I am a medley-defender. I think it was a really brilliant way to take the remaining throwaways and work them into something much greater than they were. Each track has characteristically catchy and brilliant melodies, but only “You Never Give Me Your Money” and “She Came in Through the Bathroom Window” would stand on their own as songs. The latter is a personal favorite Paul song of mine, and one that I come back to very frequently. I like “Mean Mr. Mustard” and “Polythene Pam” quite a bit. It’s nice to hear the lighter side of John after his side 1 contributions. “Sun King”, on the other hand, is the only real dud on the entire album. “Golden Slumbers” kicks off the race to the end with striking grandeur, and “Carry that Weight” finds my boys confronting the fact that no matter what they do, they’ll always bear the weight of being The Beatles. “The End” lets the personality of all four Beatles shine through their instrument in a way that I’ve never seen any other band do effectively. And the lone lyric, while simple, is a profound statement that wraps up their careers. But don’t forget “Her Majesty”! A fun ditty about falling in love with the Queen that I used to sing as a kid and I still love now. I’ve written much longer reviews for albums I’ve liked less, but I don’t feel the urge to write more, though I certainly could. Very few other albums make me feel this happy when I put them on, this excited to listen to them. And many of the others are also Beatles albums! In terms of ranking Beatles albums, I would put this on the second tier along with *Sgt. Pepper’s*. Right now I’m feeling *Abbey Road* slightly more, but that’s always subject to change. *The White Album* stands alone on my top tier, and *Revolver*, *Rubber Soul*, and *Magical Mystery Tour* come in tier 3. Gorgeous, gorgeous music.

Favorite tracks: Come Together, Something, Maxwell’s Silver Hammer, Oh! Darling, Octopus’s Garden, I Want You (She’s So Heavy), Because, You Never Give Me Your Money, Mean Mr. Mustard, Polythene Pam, She Came in Through the Bathroom Window, Golden Slumbers, Carry That Weight, The End, Her Majesty

Least favorite track: Sun King
