# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: The Cure are a band that formed in West Sussex, England, in 1976, fronted by vocalist and guitarist Robert Smith. Other members of the band during the making of *Pornography* include Simon Gallup on bass and keyboard and Lol Tolhurst on drums. The Cure have been continuously together and active since 1976, releasing a well-received album just last year. Robert Smith grew up in England with a musical family and attended progressive, experimental schools (not an uncommon start for many artists on this list). One day in middle school, Smith wore a black velvet dress to school; his teachers were concerned but allowed it, while hi classmates beat the shit out of him after school. Smith was expelled from high school after his first band, Malice, incited a riot during a performance. Smith founded The Cure in 1976. Although he wasn’t originally supposed to be the lead vocalist or songwriter, he eventually fell into these roles. The Cure won a talent show in 1977, which got them signed to their first label.

The Cure released their debut album *Three Imaginary Boys* in May 1979, a relatively standard New Wave and post-punk album, the style that was in vogue at the time. Shortly afterward, they released “Boys Don’t Cry” as a single, which is still one of their most popular songs and I’m certain that most of you would recognize it. Now popular, The Cure went on tour with Siouxsie and the Banshees (more on them later); this collaboration convinced The Cure to abandon their standard punk aesthetic and instead join the brand new goth subculture (yes, the kind of “goth” you’re thinking of). Smith began to dress in all black, wear makeup including red lipstick and black eyeliner, allow his hair to grow into a wiry mess, and consume immense amounts of psychedelic drugs. The Cure released a trilogy of gothic rock records, culminating in 1982’s *Pornography*, an album that combined crushing and hopeless lyrics (the first line being “It doesn’t matter if we all die”) with cold and atmospheric music. After*Pornography*, The Cure made their style more commercial, and by the mid-80s they had achieved superstardom; their 1987 song “Just Like Heaven” is another that you probably all know. The Cure returned to the darkness of their early gothic trilogy with 1989’s *Disintegration*, their critical and commercial peak with hits like “Lovesong” and “Lullaby”. The Cure have released 6 more albums since 1989, with “Friday I’m In Love” (yet another song most of you have probably heard) being the most popular in this period.

The term “gothic rock” was originally used to describe The Doors in the 1960s given their dark and macabre aesthetic. “All Tomorrow’s Parties” from *The Velvet Underground & Nico* was also very influential on the development of the goth subculture. Joy Division was the first group to solidify the goth aesthetic and associate it with a particular style of post-punk music with their 1980 album *Closer* (released shortly after lead singer Ian Curtis committed suicide). Bauhaus, Siouxsie and the Banshees, and The Cure further developed and popularized the subculture after Joy Division’s dissolution. Goth has proven very influential and is still popular among disaffected youths today. Robert Smith was the inspiration for the movie *Edward Scissorhands* and The Cure very influential on Tim Burton’s style more broadly. The Cure were very instrumental in bringing a darker vibe to alternative music; for example, The Cure are one of Trent Reznor’s (Nine Inch Nails) favorite bands. They also had an impact on popular music, with Adele famously covering “Lovesong” on her second album *21* and Billie Eilish’s early style taking major cues from goth.

# Hargrove's review

It sincerely pains me how much I loved this record. I have been a Cure hater for some time, mostly because I found *Disintegration* annoying as a teenager. I was overexposed thanks to my family being such huge fans (we have a black cat named Robert Smith), and *Disintegration* was frankly too poppy for what I was looking for at that time. This was my first time giving any other The Cure record a spin, and it blew. My. Mind. This record creates an atmosphere more intense than almost any album I’ve ever heard. The guitar feedback drone is oppressive and claustrophobic, Smith’s voice is chilling and he constantly sounds like he’s on the verge of suicide, the drums are relentless and militaristic, and the lyrics are bleak to the point of absurdity. Philosophically, I have always found nihilism to be feckless, cowardly, and lazy; artistically, however, it scratches an itch for me that almost nothing else can. I believe this stems from my view of philosophy as a means of solving problems, and nihilism’s solution to problems is to just give up. My view of art is as a vessel for emotions, and despair is one of the most potent emotions there is. This record is tortured and horrifying from beginning to end, and I cannot get enough of it.

“One Hundred Years” is a masterful opener, it sets the mood both musically and lyrically for what the album will be right off the bat: the guitar feedback is droning, the drums are rote and mechanical (kind of a proto-Depeche Mode), the keyboard drops the temperature below freezing, and the lyrics are dark to the point of absurdity (“It doesn’t matter if we all die”). I’m also a big fan of the song structure, where there is heavy repetition at the end of each verse. This song (much like the rest of the album, but this song in particular) feels to me like the 80s-ification of “Venus in Furs”. The drums in “A Short Term Effect” place the downbeat on 3, one of the most prominent examples of oddly off-kilter drumming on this album. I don’t know what I expected from the drums exactly, but it was not rhythmic complexity. The guitar feedback drone carries the atmosphere nicely from the previous track. “The Hanging Garden” is an early climax in terms of the intensity of the record, with the most dynamic bass line of the record and the drums really taking up a lot of space. The chorus is also quite catchy. “Siamese Twins” is the first real break from the intensity of the first few tracks, and it sets the record on a path to end much more quietly than it began. While I think going quiet was ultimately a good choice for the album, I think this song loses too much momentum and it really ends with a whimper. Regardless, it has some great lines like “I chose an eternity of this” and the pleading of “Is it always like this?”.

“The Figurehead”, which might be my favorite track, continues the lethargy of the previous trac but it imbues it with a new heaviness. The guitar is just crushing, and this time the drum downbeat is on the and of 2, continuing to deny the rhythm you expect. Great lines include “I laughed in the mirror for the first time in a year”, “I can lose myself in Chinese art and American girls”, and “I will never be clean again”. “A Strange Day” has this really atmospheric and droning intro and just stellar keyboard work. Otherwise, the music is largely unremarkable, except for during the bridge and the guitar near the end. The lyrics are positively apocalyptic, with the bridge being a lyrical high point of the whole record: “My head falls back and the walls crash down/And the sky and the impossible explode/Held for one moment, I remember a song/An impression of sound/Then everything is gone forever”.  “Cold” begins with a cello, and orchestral strings are just inherently chilling. Great lyrics include “Your name like ice into my heart” and “A shallow grave, a monument to the ruined age”. The opening montage of “Pornography” is very creepy, but it goes on a little too long. I also don’t love the song’s mix, I think the vocals are just a bit too low. The atmosphere is great though, everything is chaotic and falls apart at the end of the record. However, the very final lines of the album provide the only sliver of hope: “I must fight this sickness/Find a cure/I must fight this sickness”. Smith knows that he’s succumbed to his basest instincts, and he knows he has to find a way out. Presents some hope, but not too much; he knows that he must get out, but he doesn’t know how. Really cool ending.

I think *Pornography* might have the clearest aesthetic focus of any album we’ve listened to so far. It knows exactly what it wants to be, and it achieves it every last second of this record. Everything from the vocals to the guitar to the drums to the keyboard to the lyrics work together to capture a crushing, boundless emptiness. And it does it with a particularly appealing post-punk style of music. I really really liked this record, and I went in wanting to hate it. Fuck, man.

Favorite tracks: One Hundred Years, A Short Term Effect, The Hanging Garden, The Figurehead, A Strange Day, Cold

Least favorite track: Siamese Twins
