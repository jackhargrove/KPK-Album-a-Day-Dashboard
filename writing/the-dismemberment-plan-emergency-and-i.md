# Write-up

The Dismemberment Plan (hereafter referred to as “The Plan”) was an indie rock band formed in Washington, DC in 1993 consisting of vocalist and guitarist Travis Morrison, guitarist and keyboardist Jason Caddell, bass guitarist and keyboardist Eric Axelson, and drummer Joe Easley. They took their name from a throwaway line in the movie *Groundhog’s Day*. Within the DC punk scene of the mid-to-late 90s, The Plan developed a reputation for being eccentric and artsy among their more hardcore-inclined peers (DC was the birthplace of hardcore after all). Morrison in particular was noted for his unique vocals, sounding more like an MC than a singer. They also took heavy influence from emo, another genre that had gotten its start in DC. They released their first album *!* in 1995 and their second album *The Dismemberment Plan Is Terrified* in 1997. Both were lo-fi and unrestrained, earning some local success but failing to get big outside of that. In 1998, they began recording their third record at the tiny and decrepit Inner Ear studios in Arlington, Virginia. By chance, they were discovered at a show by an A&R representative (who was the bassist of Blondie) from Interscope and were signed to a major label 2 record deal.

Recording for this third record moved to Water Music in Hoboken, New Jersey with a major label budget. For the first time, The Plan was able to record with good equipment and without beingrushed. Despite the increased budget, The Plan refused to used suggested elements like string ensembles, instead choosing to stick with a shitty casio keyboard. Many of the songs have off-kilter rhythms with weird time signatures; for example, on “Spider in the Snow”, they chopped off the last beat of every other bar, rendering the song an abrupt 7/4. Most of the songs on the record dealt with the angst of your mid-20s and dealing with change. While recording the vocals for “Spider in the Snow”, Morrison received a call from his girlfriend to break up with him, causing him to break down crying with the recording still going. Producer Robbins described this as a “microcosm” of the record. Morisson designed the album’s cover himself on a computer. After they finished recording, The Plan returned to DC. While there, Morrison came up with the name *Emergency & I*.

Due to some weird business circumstances, Interscope dropped The Plan without releasing *Emergency & I*, essentially meaning The Plan got to record at major label quality without having to pay the label back. The record was released by their old local label DeSoto in 1999. It was their first record to get attention outside of DC (thanks in small part to a 9.6/10 from indie tastemakers Pitchfork), and they became the openers for the European leg of Pearl Jam’s world tour. They released their follow up, *Change*, in 2001, and went on tour with Death Cab for Cutie in 2002. Although they were peaking in popularity, the band abruptly broke up in 2003 after a final show. Morrison released a solo record, *Travistan*, in 2004; it received a rare 0.0/10 from Pitchfork, effectively destroying his career overnight (Seriously. He said the day after the review, his crowds dwindled from 50-100 people to fewer than 10). The Plan reunited in 2010 and released a shitty comeback record, and reunited again last year, but they’ve never found that original spark again. Still, *Emergency & I* remains one of the best indie releases of the late 90s.

# Hargrove's review

I love this album. In many ways, it feels as if it was made for me specifically. Its general style is poppy post-hardcore with a touch of emo, which is the genre I feel most at home in. It uses weird time signatures on multiple songs, which allows me to condescend about the one music theory thing I know, but it also uses them to actual artistic effect, like with the eighth note dropped from every other bar in “Gyroscope” making it feel like we could stop spinning at any moment. The rhythms in general are very funky for this style of music, which I adore. Morrison sounds unlike any other singer I’ve heard, mixing 20s white boy angst with quasi-rap delivery. Once again, my shit. The lyrics, which I’ve sent in the chat too many times to count, deal with urban isolation, relationship melodrama, existential angst, and general ennui. The sound gets pretty out there, but it never abandons general pop structures or sensibilities. Simply put, I don’t know how you could tailor a record more to my taste unless you took an elven woman from the Sacramento area with a unique voice and had her play sprawling 15 minute folk compositions.

“A Life of Possibilities” throws you into the weirdness right away, mid-vocal line for Travis, with a hefty bass synth doing its own thing. I love the dual guitar line, it’s a great melody. And the whole track just has this super tense and uneasy feel thanks to Morrison’s delivery. The climax is AWESOME, just perfect guitars and an amazing vocal line. GOAT opener. “Memory Machine” is one of the more hardcore songs on the record, and is my favorite of that style. The lyrics to this song are simply amazing, even if I vehemently disagree with what Travis is saying I totally get where he’s coming from. I love the “black wire, red wire” freakout, the icy synth in the chorus, the just-too-long lines in the verses over the 5/4 beat, the skitteriness of the drums, amazing track. “What Do You Want Me to Say?” continues the run of great tracks with some of the most spiteful lyrics I’ve ever heard in a relationship song. “There was a time when you could make me laugh at will/And you can do it still/But never is it for the right reasons, yeah”. Brutal. That quarter note guitar in the verses is so off-putting, and the almost-beatboxing Travis does in the bridge is awesome. Somehow, after all that, “Spider in the Snow” might still be my favorite song on the first half. I have never heard a better capturing of mid-20s angst. I listened to this as a teenager and was like, “I’m sure I’ll relate to that someday”. And now I do! “How can a body move at the speed of light/And still find itself in such a rut?”. I’ve never heard a better meditation on settling into routine, something we’re all dealing with right now. The music is perfect too. The verses have a quarter note chopped off in every second measure (they’ll one up this with an eighth note in “Gyroscope” later), making for a guitar line that can’t quite find its groove. The synth is used masterfully, and the vocals in the chorus are so yearning. I love this song. “The Jitters”, though still great, is the first small dip in quality. That being said, hard to think of a song that creates an atmosphere so visceral. It paints a portrait of depression that you can reach out and touch: “I’ve been good, I’ve been busy/I’ve realized my friends' true intentions, cut all ties/I’ve been doing ten thousand push-ups a day”, “When the days bring nothing new/And the sound of laughter makes you sick and snide”, “Nothing’s wrong, I’m just fine/I’ve realized I just don’t like jokes”, “You can tell every time they lean away/When you just want to talk/You couldn’t buy their interest now”, it’s brutal. The instrumental is so stripped back, so quiet, even the vocal “explosion” in the chorus feels held back. Cool song. The first half closes with my least favorite track, the very hardcore-adjacent “I Love a Magician”. I like The Plan’s take on hardcore more than I like most, but I’ve always felt this song lacks the delicate touch every other track does. It’s not bad, but it’s not great. That’s the last song I’ll say that about.

“You Are Invited” kicks off the second half with one of the most unique sounds I’ve ever heard. A drum machine? In my post-hardcore indie rock with emo characteristics? It’s more likely than you think! This is a heavily vocals-focused track that tells the surreal story of a universal invitation. I love the way the narrative progresses, with the invitation not really doing anything for his mental state; the parties still suck, because he’s still himself. I do think him giving it to someone else is sweet. The growing cacophony is done really well, and when it finally does explode, it’s the most cathartic moment of the album. “Gyroscope” is my personal favorite track, and one of my favorites of all time. It’s the perfect counterexample to gimmicky use of time signatures in math rock. No one can quite figure out what it is (I don’t think the band members themselves know), but the best approximation is 15/8. It sounds so weird, like the song resets every two bars. The lyrics are pure melodrama in the best way: “His eyes on fire and his hands kind of shake/Like his voice is ready to break/You kind of wonder how long this boy’s been awake/Or how much less sense one person can make” is poetry. I love relationship stuff that gives both sides, and it happens here. Similar to the gyroscope losing balance, it constantly feels like this song is finally going to lose momentum and fall over. And the chorus, man. The chorus. One of the greatest songs ever written. Followed by “The City”, another one of the greatest songs ever written. That synth line is something other bands search for album after album after album. It’s so beautiful, so wistful, so melancholic. The drums are skittery and amazing. Travis’ vocals in the chorus build perfectly into something heart-wrenching. And the city being sung about is DC. “Girl O’Clock” is fucking crazy. I don’t even really know how to describe it. The sense of urgency it creates is insane. Travis sounds genuinely deranged, more than any other metal or punk singer we’ve heard. It’s the vocal performance of a lifetime. The other instruments meet his vibe too. My second favorite of three more hardcore songs, after “Memory Machine” and above “I Love a Magician”. “8 1/2 Minutes” keeps the urgency, but channels it from sex into the apocalypse. Once again, Travis sounds cuckoo, but this time it’s more understandable given the subject matter. The chorus ties everything together beautifully though, and it’s a cool concept. Yet another good song. The closer is “Back and Forth”, where Travis finally meets his potential as a hip hop MC. Or Bob Dylan. The melody really does go back and forth again and again, both in the vocals and the guitar. I don’t like it as much as some do, but it’s a damn good closer.

What else can I say. I love this record. Hard to think of something more designed to please me specifically. And I felt that way as a teenager; imagine how I feel now as someone in his mod-20s in DC!

Favorite tracks: A Life of Possibilities, Memory Machine, What Do You Want Me to Say?, Spider in the Snow, The Jitters, You Are Invited, Gyrsocope, The City, Girl O’Clock, 8 1/2 Minutes, Back and Forth

Least favorite track: I Love a Magician
