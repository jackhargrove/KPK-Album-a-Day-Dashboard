# Hargrove's review

I have had an instinctive aversion to the Doors for no legitimate reason whatsoever. I think I found the idea of them annoying because their instantly recognizable style sounds like self-parody if you’re a lazy dickhead and refuse to dig any deeper than “druggy yells over a dated keyboard”, and also because my dad is a superfan who tried to force the Doors on me at a time when I was a lazy dickhead. Now that I’ve finally given a real listen to the Doors, I’m pretty annoyed at myself for writing them off for such stupid reasons. Though I’ve known “Light My Fire” for over a decade, never once have I given it enough of a shot to realize how orgasmic those solos right in the middle of it are. I of course also knew the ubiquitous “Break on Through (To the Other Side)” but never realized how fun the vocals could be if I’d just wipe the sneer off my face. On top of that, deep cuts like “Soul Kitchen”, “The Crystal Ship”, and “Take It as It Comes” deliver some of the finest 60s pop rock that I just never cared to look into. And “Alabama Song (Whisky Bar)” has to be one of the most bizarre cover choices I’ve ever heard on a major album. While there’s no real weak link in the quartet, Manzarek consistently steals the show for me. That dude can fucking SHRED on the keys. Plus, that particular texture just sets the Doors apart from any of their contemporaries. Krieger holds his own on guitar while Densmore has this swinging jazz sensibility that keeps the music light and refreshing. As for Morrison, though I don’t know that his poetry always lives up to my expectations, he’s one hell of a charismatic frontman. Despite all of that praise, however, *The Doors* as an album falls prey to the same issue as many classic rock albums, especially debuts: it’s just not consistently good throughout! Side one would have been immaculate were it not for the eye roll-inducing “Twentieth Century Fox”; by contrast, boy, are there some duds on side 2. The run from “Back Door Man” to “End of the Night” is just a brutal stretch for any album. And I don’t know that I can get through any classic rock review without a blazing hot take, so here goes: “The End” is only okay. It’s fine. It’s a good track. It’s alright. It does not have nearly the payoff I’d like from an eleven-minute slow burn, let alone an eleven-minute slow burn that concludes the iconic debut album *The Doors* from iconic band the Doors. Still, I could never in good conscience put an album with a moment so enrapturing as the solo section of “Light My Fire” end up below B Tier, allowing the Doors to leapfrog the classic rock wall at the top of C Tier.

I don’t know what it means at all, but, fuck, “You know the day destroys the night/Night divides the day” is an amazing opening line for an album. I just like the way those words sound together. “Break on Through (To the Other Side)” is a serious banger, bringing together searing keys, a fun jazz rhythm, and an intense vocals. It’s hard for me to believe that this wasn’t initially a hit. Manzarek flexes his melodic muscles with a sharp-as-nails riff on “Soul Kitchen”. Morisson further blesses the track with vivid imagery like “Turn me out and I'll wander, baby/Stumblin' in the neon groves”. This is the ideal Doors album cut. I don’t mind at all the slow down on “The Crystal Ship” after the energy of the first two tracks. I love the vocal melody, hitting hardest when Morisson raises his voice at the start of the final chorus. The keys also shine as Manzarek brings in his most complex playing yet. I don’t care for “Twentieth Century Fox” at all. I like the jazzy drums, but it’s just not a strong enough pun to build a song out of. And there’s really little more to it than that. “Alabama Song (Whisky Bar)” fits the weird, dark, druggy vibe of this record so well. It’s an oddball track but it just works. “Light My Fire” is far and away the best track on the album. If it were just the verses and choruses, it would be a decent but unremarkable classic pop rock song, but holy FUCK those solos are positively transcendent. Manzarek goes INSANE and Krieger impressively holds his own in response. I also have to respect the bass vamping too, though I’m not sure whether that’s Manzarek or the session guy. The entire midsection of this track is pure bliss. I mean, it’s as good or better than any jazz solo I’ve ever heard.

“Back Door Man” is a more conventional cover than “Alabama Song”; given that, I’m disappointed they played it so straight. I would’ve preferred they made it their own as Led Zeppelin did with “When the Levee Breaks”. “I Looked at You” is almost comically weak compared to the tracks on side one. It’s not terrible but it has nothing going for it whatsoever. “End of the Night” is similarly unimpressive but with added aimlessness. It lacks any of the energy I appreciate from the Doors. “Take It as It Comes” isn’t quite as strong as anything on side one, but it’s a damn gem within this back half. A great chorus and a stellar keyboard solo? I’ll take it. There’s so much to appreciate about “The End”: the way it builds, the way the guitar and keys both play so delicately under the first verse, the lyrics and delivery from Morrison on that same verse, the ay the drums keep the intensity simmering. It’s undeniably a good track. But eleven minutes at this slow of a build require a lot of buy-in and a lot of pay-off, and it never reaches a moment worthy of its grand presentation. There are moments of pure ecstasy in “Light My Fire” for only half the length. The climax that does exist is decent, but that’s all it is. In a vacuum, this would be on my favorite tracks list, but that would feel wrong given my disappointment. That’s probably unfair, but what are you gonna do.

While *The Doors* escapes the classic rock corridor, I think the perfect place for it in B Tier, is next to *The Stone Roses*, *Red*, and *Power, Corruption & Lies*, all albums with moments of pure inspiration alongside the unremarkable.

Favorite tracks: Break on Through (To the Other Side), Soul Kitchen, The Crystal Ship, Alabama Song (Whisky Bar), Light My Fire, Take It as It Comes

Least favorite track: End of the Night
