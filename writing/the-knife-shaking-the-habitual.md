# Write-up

Story/Fun Facts: The Knife is a brother/sister electronic music duo from Gothenburg, Sweden formed in 1999. The duo consists of Karin and Olof Dreijer; the two refuse to credit each other separately, with all aspects of production just being credited to The Knife, but Karin provides all the vocals. Karin Dreijer is also known for their (yes, Karin uses they/them pronouns. Just wait until the next paragraph) solo work as Fever Ray, in particular their highly acclaimed 2017 album *Plunge*. They released their self-titled debut album in 2001 to little recognition or fanfare. In 2002, they released “Heartbeats”, as the lead single to their 2003 album *Deep Cuts*. “Hearbeats” receive massive critical and commercial success, launching The Knife into the mainstream. In 2006, they released their third album, *Silent Shout*, to new heights of critical acclaim; it appears later on this list in the electronic section. The Knife went on tour to support *Silent Shout* with a tour, marking the first time the duo had ever played live. Although they were commissioned for an opera with other collaborators that released in 2010, the duo otherwise dropped off the face of the Earth until 2011, when they announced a new album, *Shaking the Habitual*, which would prove to be their last before breaking up.

*Shaking the Habitual* is a long and varied album with less conventional song structures than its predecessors. It places significant focus on its percussion, above and beyond the other elements. It was recorded in Stockholm and Berlin over a period of 2 years. The album is conceptually extremely political and ideological; before the album’s recording, Olof Dreijer attended a gender studies class at Stockholm University and shared the reading list with his sister (yes, this is all real and according to the two of them), and they wanted the album to represent all of that. Even the title, *Shaking the Habitual*, comes from the work of Michel Foucault. The album cover comes from a comic book that the duo commissioned entitled *End Extreme Wealth*. Brooklyn-based musician Shannon Funchess aka Light Asylum provides vocals on “Stay Out Here”.

# Hargrove's review

*Silent Shout* is probably my second favorite electronic album of all time (LCD Soundsystem’s *This is Happening* being number 1) and I’ve listened to it dozens of times. I have also listened to *Shaking the Habitual* before, although certainly fewer times than *Silent Shout*. It’s just much harder to listen through. Let’s be frank: it’s a fucking mess. The songs are lengthy and sprawling, the sound changes completely from track to track, and there’s a 20 minute ambient piece smack dab in the middle of the record. Clearly, this album wasn’t intended to find wide success like their previous two. And yet, that’s also what gives it its value. It’s an extremely creative deconstruction of electronic and dance music as genres. In particular, I hear so much *Remain in Light* in here, specifically in the percussion. Like Talking Heads, The Knife keep a funky groove around which to fuck around. It’s not an ode to electronic music, it’s a dissection. And when it works, it fucking *works*. There are some tremendously high highs, and those make up the majority of the album. Unfortunately, there are some very low lows, with two tracks in particular that I have difficulty not skipping when I listen: “A Cherry on Top” and “Fracking Fluid Injection”. I prefer the structured dance of *Silent Shout*, but I can’t deny that *Shaking the Habitual* fascinates me, and I found it very worthwhile to revisit and dig in over the last two days.

“A Tooth for an Eye” starts the album out with just a drum beat, a message to the listener for what to focus on. The drums are layered, complex, tribal, and, more than anything, they make you want to dance. Karin Dreijer’s vocals come in sooner than you expect, and they are unique. She has the exact type of weird voice that most appeals to me: screechy woman (in a close second place is man with intonations never before considered in the English language. Looking at you, Bob Dylan). The refrains of “eyes eyes eyes eyes” is SO good to me. And the drums just come to a fever pitch in the second half. Such a strong start to the album. “Full of Fire” was the single (9 minute single lol) and it delivers as a banger. We leave the world/Afro beat of the last song’s percussion for a driving industrial drum beat. The song overall is loud, fast, and repetitive, the platonic ideal of post-industrial. Just the right amount of abrasive. Added to my playlist. “Without You My Life Would Be Boring” is a contender for my favorite song by The Knife. We get these Afro-electronic drums, a weird flute, and a frantic vocal performance that reaches periodic tension releases when she says the title before going straight back to the grind. This is just a perfect song. And right after, “Wrap Your Arms Around Me” comes hulking in, sounding bigger and badder than anything that came before. This song is just so massive, so towering, so unsettling, so oppressive. The synths strike fear deep into my heart. And it builds and it builds and it builds. Great track too.

“Old Dreams Waiting to Be Realized” is really cool as the centerpiece. It’s a true Brian Eno style ambient track, not like an Aphex Twin style ambient but still with a melody. I will say that every time I listened through this whole album, I never got the urge to skip it, which I think is a measure of success for this type of track. It’s more varied than you would expect, and it keeps me uneasy and on my toes every listen. “Raging Lung” starts the second half with just another banger, this time with a tribal bass drum. It probably has my favorite vocal melody on the album. I absolutely adore the post-chorus instrumental breakdowns; the high-pitched synth is interesting to follow, and the low-pitched synth keeps things sleazy. God it’s good, definitely the best on disc 2. “Networking” is the busiest song on the album, fast and full of these noisy synths, I don’t love it if I’m being honest. “Stay Out Here” is the most conventional club track; not the most interesting on the album, but look, it sounds good, I dig it. “Ready to Lose” is a fantastic closer, it starts slow and skeevy before the vocals build. I love the “ready to lose the privilege” part. It just keeps adding new instruments and new elements, I think it’s very emblematic of the album as a whole.

Unfortunately, this album does have problems. I do not care for “A Cherry on Top”, and it totally kills all the momentum on the first half of the album. You start with two absolute bangers in a row, and then there are two absolute bangers right after, but you have to get through an 8 and a half minute half-baked atmospheric piece first. It’s pretty obnoxious, not good on its own merits, and its placing on the album is just dogshit. “Fracking Fluid Injection” is also quite terrible, you can see the light at the end of the tunnel for this long-ass album but it’s gate kept by this super frustrating and uninteresting track. I love harsh metallic scraping sounds when they’re used well (an interesting example: Lorde’s “Hard Feelings/Loveless” uses a similar sound to great effect), but it’s just awful here. Very bad track. The two interludes “Oryx” and ”Crake” also feel quite insignificant, although they are mercifully short. If you drop these four tracks I think you have a much tighter album of much more reasonable length, and I think this begins to rival *Silent Shout* for me.

Ultimately, though, the bad parts can’t take away from the good, and the good is damn good. This album has 6 electronic bangers that are better than most artists entire careers, and they have an average length of like 7 minutes. So inventive, so cool, so unexpected at every turn. I’m usually against doing 2 days for an album, but I won’t lie, I enjoyed it here. Just had fun listening to it over and over again. I have very similar feelings for this album as I did for *Soundtracks for the Blind*.

Favorite tracks: A Tooth for an Eye, Full of Fire, Without You My Life Would Be Boring, Wrap Your Arms Around Me, Old Dreams Waiting to Be Realized, Raging Lung, Ready to Lose

Least favorite track: Fracking Fluid Injection
