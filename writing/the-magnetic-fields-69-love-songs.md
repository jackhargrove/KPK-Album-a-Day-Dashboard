# Hargrove's review

This album is just not my thing. I’ve felt this way for about eight years at this point. I double checked my Spotify listen history and in August 2017 I listened to the first few tracks, realized I would not be able to stand three hours of it, and turned it off. Coming back to it this week I truly gave it my all. I give every album my all, but especially here I know Aidan likes it and I wanted to give it as fair a shot as I could. Unfortunately, there are just too many ways in which this does not appeal to me. Academically, I find the concept of writing “love songs” as a genre rather than as just songs about love to be interesting, but I’m someone who would much rather listen to emotional music that comes from the writer’s personal experience. So many of these songs leave me thinking I’m supposed to be impressed by how clever they are instead of moved by the art. Now, it’s not impossible for me to enjoy something like that, but it’s never going to have a particularly high hit rate with me. And when you’re firing 69 shots, it’s going to end up with me sitting through literally hours of material that leaves me cold. And I really don’t think the unbelievable length plays into the concept at all beyond needing a quota for Merritt to “challenge” himself with. Listening to the songwriting on this album sometimes feels like listening to a guitar solo from one of those really technically gifted guitar guys like Peter Frampton or Steve Vai. Merritt is clearly very talented; every song is written well. So many feel like an exercise in masturbation. Still, there are some cool songs in here. To be specific, there were 16 I identified myself as liking, and probably about as many that I thought were decent but not quite my favorite. That still leaves half the album, about an hour and a half, of torture for me. Oh well.

“I Don’t Believe in the Sun” is one of the better tracks on the album. The piano line is stellar and the primary lyrical conceit is clever. I generally do not care for Merritt’s baritone at all, but the grandness of the instrumental makes it more bearable. “All My Little Words” is the consensus favorite but I don’t really get it. Admittedly the chorus is catchy but it’s not even the catchiest on the album and the lyrics are whatever. I like it, but I don’t get the level of acclaim. “The Book of Love”, on the other hand, deserves the acclaim it gets. On an album that’s just an exercise in executing love songs well, it’s by far the best written. It doesn’t need much more than that. I like “The One You Really Love” just for the harmony at the end. “Sweet-Lovin’ Man” is one of the few to be truly good because of the instrumental, a nice, (relatively) maximalist synth backing.

Disc 2 is in general better than the rest. It’s the only disc where I would say the median song is good. “No One Will Ever Love You” is a Fleetwood Mac jam that sounds damn good and has some of the best lines of any song. “When things go wrong, I sing along/It is the nature of the business/But you’re not here to make my sad songs more sincere” is a great line and, given it’s relation to the act of songwriting, is a sentiment that seems truly felt. “If You Don’t Cry” is a bop with bouncy synths and great vocals. “(Crazy for You But) Not That Crazy” has great drumming and a super catchy chorus. “World Love” is by far the best of the genre parodies. “Love, music, wine, and revolution” is super funny and the guitar is a dead-on approximation of world music. I’m biased, but “Washington, D.C.” is super cleverly written and I love the upbeat, twee vibe. “Papa Was a Rodeo” is the best of the surprisingly numerous country songs on this record. It’s very well-written, well paced, funny, enjoyable, and “never stuck around long enough for a one-night stand” is a great line.

Disc 3 is the weakest, but still has a few standouts. “Busby Berkeley Dreams” is really pretty and moving, with great imagery as well. I can’t stop singing along to it. I will concede to Aidan that “The Death of Ferdinand de Saussure” is very lyrically clever, but it only works because the surrounding song is so good and catchy. “Blue You” has a dark fairy tale vibe to the melody and chords here that I really like. I always love twinkling forests. “I Can’t Touch You Anymore” has a bouncy beat and a catchy synth line and I honestly quite like it. “Strange Eyes” is the best song of the album’s final stretch. It’s the only one to break up the dreary monotony of the last 10 songs or so.

I would be lying if I said I hadn’t warmed up to this album at all compared to where I started. There’s so much to dig into, I truly believe just about anyone could find something they like in here. But the hit rate is just not there. Further, it’s not just that there are a lot of bad songs, but many of them are bad for the exact conceptual reason the album exists. To me, it’s not a case of “the artists went too far, but fundamentally the experiment worked”. So many of the misses are bad because they’re “clever” little pieces Merritt put together to satisfy some songwriting puzzle he had set out for himself. I would just rather listen to music that’s genuinely felt. I will always give props for taking a big swing though, and this album is undeniably a big swing.

I AM FINALLY FREE OF THIS ALBUM!

# Aidan's review

Actual Review: I love this album. I don't think I can really give a fair review of it just because I think the concept is so clever and I've listened to the songs I love so damn much. I think that listening to this straight through is just the wrong way to listen to it. There are plenty of bad songs on the way to 69. It's a gimmick of an album and some of the gimmicks don't land. But when it works, it's impressive, and as a concept album that cheapens the love song to a singularity, it's brilliant. I tend to pick out songs that I like from time to time, and that's where the variety shines and the duds are left behind. That is giving it leeway that I rarely give to other albums, but I don't care, it's impressive enough as an accomplishment to merit it. Merritt is an artist of the one-off, the best songs are those that take weird jokes and elevate them to 100ish seconds of beautiful cheap pop. The album certainly has weak sections, but the highs are so high and it’s massive enough that I can ignore the flaws. I’ve only listened to it straight through once before, so I reviewed each and every song to make sure I did it again.

69 love song reviews:

1. Absolutely Cuckoo - Great opener, good example of what this album does well. Simultaneously self criticizing while somehow also genuinely passionate. Not lyrically impressive, but fills the 94 seconds. 7/10
2. I Don't Believe in the Sun - Hard to believe that this is also Merritt. Tremendously clever song, which plays off of tired romantic metaphors. 7.1/10
3. All My Little Words - The first of the songs I think is overrated because of how sweet it is. Not as bad as an offender as some later songs, and the vocals are nice. 6/10
4. A Chicken With Its Head Cut Off - First real banger on the album. Love the vocals, love the absurdity, love the guitar. "We don't have to be stars exploding in the night/or electric eels under the covers" is such a great line. 9.5/10
5. Reno Dakota - First non Merritt song (I think). Showcase of the variety to come, pretty average for the simpler songs. The alcoholism is a nice touch. 7.5/10
6. I Don't Want To Get Over You - God I love Merritt's baritone. Prozac and Camus references are clever, and it takes the trope of not getting an old lover and highlights the self destruction and immaturity instead of making it into something normal. 9/10
7. Come Back From San Fransisco - Nice to see Aditya got a song about him. Heartfelt, quaint, and well written. Guitar is particularly good, as is the closing verse. 7.4/10
8. The Luckiest Guy on the Lower East Side - Bouncy, funny, lyrically brilliant, evocative and with a hilarious ending line. What's not to like? I have no clue what instrument is making the squish sound in the background. 9/10
9. Let's Pretend We're Bunny Rabbits - Delightfully dirty song. Takes the figure of speech for a nice quick spin, and ends the song before it gets old. 8/10
10. The Cactus Where Your Heart Should Be - Covers most of the same bases as the best songs on Side 1, but just doesn't put it together as well. Cool guitar melody, Merritt's baritone, and an analogy pushed to the limit result in a ok song. 7/10
11. I Think I Need A New Heart - Less clever than Lower East Side, but slightly better in nearly every other way, which is saying something. 9.3/10
12. The Book Of Love - Lyrically one of the strongest on the album, and the vocals are soothing, but they're not good enough to allow for instrumentation this sparse. 6/10
13. Fido Your Leash Is Too Long - First real miss in the album, the boppy sound is fine on better songs, but I never love it. The lyrics aren't clever, and the song has a tortured ending instrumental section. 3/10
14. How Fucking Romantic - The bad part of Disc 1 continues. It's clever on paper, but ends up coming across like middling slam poetry. 4/10
15. The One You Really Love - Back to form with nice duet on the vocals with just enough flourishes to keep it interesting. Lyrically competent, I like the final altered chorus but it's nothing to write home about. 6.9/10.
16. Punk Love - Minute of horseshit. One of several genres that Merritt just butchers 2/10
17. Parades Go By - Probably the best arranged piece so far, which carries it through. Nothing else going for it, and the synth in the background grates a bit, but it pulls through. 6.5/10
18. Boa Constrictor - Beautiful guitar, rhyming "convict her" with "constrictor" gets points from me. Not much else to say, good short song. 7.3/10
19. A Pretty Girl Is Like - Top 5 lyrical song for me, just hilarious in how straight Merritt plays it. Not much instrumentally going on, but it just keeps getting funnier and doesn't overstay its welcome. 9/10
20. My Sentimental Melody - I assume this is Lemony Snicket on the accordion. I'm generally not a fan of the slower and longer songs on this album, they detract from the joy and variety that makes it great. This could be a good song from another band, but it's clearly not Merritt's strong suit. 6/10
21. Nothing Matters When We're Dancing - Same issues as My Sentimental Melody, but gets points for the clever rhyme of "Paris" and "Lancing" and hamming it up a degree more. 6.1/10
22. Sweet-Lovin' Man - Starts off weak but once it gets going I'm a fan. The cowbell is a nice touch, and while it's not clever, it's heartfelt enough to work as a generic song. 6.9/10
23. The Things We Did and Didn't Do - Finishes off a weak second half of disc 1 with another forgettable song. Merritt just isn't able to muster the sincerity for this style of song. 5/10
24. Roses - Mildly funny gimmick song. Short enough to not be annoying but that's all it's got going for it. 3/10
25. Love is Like Jazz - Merritt's butchering of non-pop genres is my least favorite of the album and this is probably the worse offender at 3 minutes of awful "jazz". It's not even clever. Instant skip under most circumstances. 0/10.
26. When My Boy Walks Down the Street. - "he's going to be my wife" is a great line among a generally clever song. The generally solid side 2 begins in earnest. 6.9/10
27. Time Enough for Rocking When We're Old - Probably the best sentimental song on the album. 8/10
28. Very Funny - Somehow makes cuckolding sound beautiful. Not the most clever song, but I like the piano. 6.3/10
29. Grand Canyon - Another song whose central metaphor just isn't that clever. Merritt is very good at making these songs acceptable, but they never really get past that point. 5/10
30. No One Will Ever Love You - And we're back to the good stuff. Nice double entendres, love the guitars. Honestly never notices the synth in the background previously and it's a tad annoying, but still a great song with a killer title. 8.4/10
31. If You Don't Cry - This one usually blurs together with its slightly superior predecessor in my head due to the similar titles. I like the constant synth and vocal melodies, and the lyrics are good, it just doesn't come together for me. 8/10.
32. You're My Only Home - Another meh slow song. Nice synth, fine lyrics, but just lacks what I like about the album. The bridge is particularly nice though. 7/10
33. (Crazy for You But) Not That Crazy - While the previous song was a well executed song that shied away from the strengths of the album, this is the opposite, in that it's a lesser song that hits all the strengths of the album, with interesting rhymes, a unique take on conventional love songs and clever references (I did not know who Ganesh was prior to this). Is it particularly good? No. Still, 7.5/10
34. My Only Friend - Great song. Love the Billy Holiday tribute, love the piano, another rare good sentimental song. Feels like it ends a verse too soon, but too early than too late. 8/10
35. Promises of Eternity - Filler, but on the stronger end of filler, which is a general trait of disc 2. I like the structure and instrumentation, but it's still a meh song. 5/10
36. World Love - Far better than Merritt's takes on experimental music, jazz or punk, he shows genuine respect for world music, with a vaguely tribal drum and a plethora of random instruments forming a pleasant fog. Not a showstopper and a tad long, but not a bad song. 5.5/10
37. Washington D.C. - Nats suck, Commanders suck, Wizards suck and Hargrove lives there. Terrible city. I don't need the reminder how spell it, but the rest of the song is fine, especially when compared to everything else regarding the city. KIDDING CHILL Still hate the spelling part. 5/10
38. Long-Forgotten Fairytale - Love the beats, they have great variety and the little pauses are nice. Could take or leave the other elements and it's a tad long, but the overall result is still pretty good. 7.1/10
39. Kiss Me Like You Mean It - Good for variety, but not much else. 4/10
40. Papa Was A Rodeo - Wasn't a fan of this song previously, but after seeing that people gave it rave reviews, I gave it another change. It's pretty good, but I'm not ready to include it in my favorites. Does deserve its runtime though, which is pretty impressive for an album that works by flitting from song to song. 7.9/10
41. Epitaph for My Heart - The opening is dumb, but it then goes into a tremendously warm chorus. It's cheaper than Papa Was A Rodeo, but I like this album for its cheapness. 8/10
42. Asleep and Dreaming - I keep waiting for some kind of pace change, but it's still a fine song. I like the bit about being unable to judge beauty. 6.5/10
43. The Sun Goes Down and the World Goes Dancing - Bouncy, upbeat, convoluted. I had completely forgotten this song and I was happy to discover it. 8.8/10
44. The Way You Say Good-Night - Points off for preempting the far better Busby Berkley reference, but a good variety song, specifically the strings. Tad long. 6.7/10
45. Abigail, Belle of Kilronan - Wow another venture into another genre that Merritt does well? Wonder why this one turns out allhwite as opposed to the jazz and punk ones. I guess we'll never know. 7.4/10
46. I Shatter - Annoying and long. Waste of 3 minutes. 2/10
47. Underwear - Great beat. Feel like there was a "petite mort" pun missed out on here, but the French bit is still clever, and it's not shoving the bisexual agenda in my face. 7.9/10
48. It's a Crime - I love the Bentley line, and the verses are generally good with a nice melody. Chorus isn't great and it probably should be 1 verse shorter, but I still like the whole product. 7.1/10
49. Busby Berkley Dreams - What a great turn of phrase, and great song. I'm a sucker for the obscure references, and this is among the best on the album (and the clear cut winner is coming up soon). 8/10
50. I'm Sorry I love You - I'm not going to pretend like this song is a work of art, and I absolutely will forget it. 6/10.
51. Acoustic Guitar - I'll give it points for a clever name check and nice lyrics, but it's still yet another forgettable song. Still, by the lower standards of disc 3, it's probably above average. Guitar does deliver as advertised. 6.9/10
52. The Death of Ferdinand de Saussure - Best single name check in the album, great song, so clever and it's a good melody to boot. It gets a chuckle out of me every time and it's just a good song regardless. 9/10
53. Love in Shadows - I appreciate the theme, but it's just too listless to work. 4.7/10
54. Bitter Tears - The shift in production is a bit jarring, but it's a cool song. Can't quite place what it reminds me of. 6.4/10.
55. Wi' Nah Wee Bairn Ye'll Me Beget - I don't know why I like this song so much, but somehow I find the childlike escalation hilarious and the weird Scottish shit just works. 8.7/10
56. Yeah! Oh Yeah! - Not quite as delightfully dumb, but it's still solid. 7.7/10
57. Experimental Music Love - Another butchering of a genre. Cmon. 2/10
58. Meaningless - Back to the hits. Unpredictable rhymes, odd subject choice, all wrapped in a great song. Sounds a lot like Suburban Kids With Biblical Names, which is a completely meaningless reference. 8/10
59. Love is Like a Bottle of Gin - One of the best songs which leans heavily on a single metaphor. Might just seem better among the weak disc 3, but it works for me. 7.2/10
60. Queen of the Savages - Funny, dumb and catchy. Last real gasp of good songs on this album. 7.7/10
61. Blue You - Too slow, not interesting, and after a long album I struggle to acre. 4/10
62. I Can't Touch You Anymore - Far better, nice beat, interesting lyrics and just generally more going on. I appreciate the Paul Simon reference. 7.4/10
63. Two Kinds of People - Sounds a lot like Boa Constrictor, but not as good. Didn't love Boa Constrictor, really not a fan of this song. Organ is a nice touch though. 5/10
64. How To Say Goodbye - The verses sound a bit like a poor copy of the Beatles, but the chorus makes good use of Merritt's baritone. 6/10.
65. The Night You Can't Remember - For some reason this feels a lot like a Godard movie to me. I like the accordion here and the plucked? Guitar is nice. Yet another case of odd rhymes adding a little extra to a song 6.7/10.
66. For We Are the King of the Boudoir - Funny, but annoying. I'm probably more negative on this because of its late placement in the album, but it just doesn't work for me. 4/10
67. Strange Eyes - Really cool beat, and nice bridge, but nothing else going for it. Still, it's pleasant and doesn't overplay its hand. 6.3/10
68. Xylophone Track - I'm always frustrated by how poor this album ends. Long and unimaginative, it's such a contrast with the highs of this album to end on an unfunny drawn out track that doesn't hit on any of the strengths of the album. 3.5/10
69. Zebra - You get it? Zebra? Cause it's got a Z? And it's the end? Just a letdown of an ending. 2/10.
