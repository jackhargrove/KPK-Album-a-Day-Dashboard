# Hargrove's review

This album is just not my thing. I’ve felt this way for about eight years at this point. I double checked my Spotify listen history and in August 2017 I listened to the first few tracks, realized I would not be able to stand three hours of it, and turned it off. Coming back to it this week I truly gave it my all. I give every album my all, but especially here I know Aidan likes it and I wanted to give it as fair a shot as I could. Unfortunately, there are just too many ways in which this does not appeal to me. Academically, I find the concept of writing “love songs” as a genre rather than as just songs about love to be interesting, but I’m someone who would much rather listen to emotional music that comes from the writer’s personal experience. So many of these songs leave me thinking I’m supposed to be impressed by how clever they are instead of moved by the art. Now, it’s not impossible for me to enjoy something like that, but it’s never going to have a particularly high hit rate with me. And when you’re firing 69 shots, it’s going to end up with me sitting through literally hours of material that leaves me cold. And I really don’t think the unbelievable length plays into the concept at all beyond needing a quota for Merritt to “challenge” himself with. Listening to the songwriting on this album sometimes feels like listening to a guitar solo from one of those really technically gifted guitar guys like Peter Frampton or Steve Vai. Merritt is clearly very talented; every song is written well. So many feel like an exercise in masturbation. Still, there are some cool songs in here. To be specific, there were 16 I identified myself as liking, and probably about as many that I thought were decent but not quite my favorite. That still leaves half the album, about an hour and a half, of torture for me. Oh well.

“I Don’t Believe in the Sun” is one of the better tracks on the album. The piano line is stellar and the primary lyrical conceit is clever. I generally do not care for Merritt’s baritone at all, but the grandness of the instrumental makes it more bearable. “All My Little Words” is the consensus favorite but I don’t really get it. Admittedly the chorus is catchy but it’s not even the catchiest on the album and the lyrics are whatever. I like it, but I don’t get the level of acclaim. “The Book of Love”, on the other hand, deserves the acclaim it gets. On an album that’s just an exercise in executing love songs well, it’s by far the best written. It doesn’t need much more than that. I like “The One You Really Love” just for the harmony at the end. “Sweet-Lovin’ Man” is one of the few to be truly good because of the instrumental, a nice, (relatively) maximalist synth backing.

Disc 2 is in general better than the rest. It’s the only disc where I would say the median song is good. “No One Will Ever Love You” is a Fleetwood Mac jam that sounds damn good and has some of the best lines of any song. “When things go wrong, I sing along/It is the nature of the business/But you’re not here to make my sad songs more sincere” is a great line and, given it’s relation to the act of songwriting, is a sentiment that seems truly felt. “If You Don’t Cry” is a bop with bouncy synths and great vocals. “(Crazy for You But) Not That Crazy” has great drumming and a super catchy chorus. “World Love” is by far the best of the genre parodies. “Love, music, wine, and revolution” is super funny and the guitar is a dead-on approximation of world music. I’m biased, but “Washington, D.C.” is super cleverly written and I love the upbeat, twee vibe. “Papa Was a Rodeo” is the best of the surprisingly numerous country songs on this record. It’s very well-written, well paced, funny, enjoyable, and “never stuck around long enough for a one-night stand” is a great line.

Disc 3 is the weakest, but still has a few standouts. “Busby Berkeley Dreams” is really pretty and moving, with great imagery as well. I can’t stop singing along to it. I will concede to Aidan that “The Death of Ferdinand de Saussure” is very lyrically clever, but it only works because the surrounding song is so good and catchy. “Blue You” has a dark fairy tale vibe to the melody and chords here that I really like. I always love twinkling forests. “I Can’t Touch You Anymore” has a bouncy beat and a catchy synth line and I honestly quite like it. “Strange Eyes” is the best song of the album’s final stretch. It’s the only one to break up the dreary monotony of the last 10 songs or so.

I would be lying if I said I hadn’t warmed up to this album at all compared to where I started. There’s so much to dig into, I truly believe just about anyone could find something they like in here. But the hit rate is just not there. Further, it’s not just that there are a lot of bad songs, but many of them are bad for the exact conceptual reason the album exists. To me, it’s not a case of “the artists went too far, but fundamentally the experiment worked”. So many of the misses are bad because they’re “clever” little pieces Merritt put together to satisfy some songwriting puzzle he had set out for himself. I would just rather listen to music that’s genuinely felt. I will always give props for taking a big swing though, and this album is undeniably a big swing.

I AM FINALLY FREE OF THIS ALBUM!

Favorite tracks: I Don’t Believe in the Sun, All My Little Words, The Book of Love, The One You Really Love, Sweet-Lovin’ Man, No One Will Ever Love You, If You Don’t Cry, (Crazy for You But) Not That Crazy, World Love, Washington, D.C., Papa Was a Rodeo, Busby Berkeley Dreams, The Death of Ferdinand de Saussure, Blue You, I Can’t Touch You Anymore, Strange Eyes

Least favorite track: Love Is Like Jazz
