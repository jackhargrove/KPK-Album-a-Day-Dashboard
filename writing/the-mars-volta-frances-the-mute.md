# Write-up

The Mars Volta is a progressive rock band that formed in 2001 in El Paso, Texas, whose members at the time of this album’s recording included guitarist and primary composer Omar Rodriguez-López, lead vocalist Cedric Bixler-Zavala, drummer Jon Theodore, keyboardist Isaiah “Ikey” Owens, bassist Juan Alderete de la Peña, and percussionist and keyboardist Marcel Rodriguez-López (Omar’s younger brother). The band formed out of the ashes of post-hardcore/emo band At the Drive-In. After the shocking success of their brilliant, GOATed 2000 album *Relationship of Command*, At the Drive-In fell apart at the peak of their popularity due to creative differences between the members: guitarist Jim Ward, bassist Paul Hinojos, and drummer Tony Hajjar wanted to continue making music in the same style, while Bixler-Zavala and Rodriguez-López wanted to make weirder and more experimental music. The former three formed post-hardcore group Sparta, while the latter two formed the Mars Volta with Jim Ward’s cousin Jeremy Ward. While Bixler-Zavala was the clear leader of At the Drive-In, Rodriguez-López would be the dominant creative force in the Mars Volta

The Mars Volta released its first EP, *Tremulant*, in 2002. The prog rock sound was new and weird, but it was enough for them to record a full album, 2003’s *De-Loused in the Comatorium*. This album features Red Hot Chili Peppers member Flea on bass throughout, with some accounts indicating he was a full-fledged member of the band. It was produced by Rick Rubin. While most of the music was composed by Rodriguez-López, Bixler-Zavala composed the vocal melodies and lyrics, which detailed the internal struggle of a man in a drug-induced coma as a concept album. It received good reviews from every outlet except Pitchfork and sold half a million copies. Shortly before the album’s release, Jeremy Ward died of a heroin overdose, inspiring Rodriguez-López and Bixler-Zavala to quit drugs for good. Their second album, *Frances the Mute*, released in 2005. Rodriguez-López exerted even more control by taking over production duties, using a Miles Davis-inspired technique where he had each band member record separately so they couldn’t hear the other parts. Another concept album, Bixler-Zavala’s lyrics were based off of a diary discovered by a bandmate in a repossessed car that details the author’s search for his biological parents. The album was supposed to open with a 15-minute title track that explained many of the more cryptic lyrics, but it was removed due to time constraints and released independently as a single; hardcore fans still consider it the true album opener. Flea returns, this time playing trumpet instead of bass. The album again received rave reviews from every outlet except Pitchfork (who gave it a 2.0/10) and ultimately went platinum.

The Mars Volta would release four more albums until they went on extended hiatus in 2013 due to growing tensions between Rodriguez-López and Bixler-Zavala. Keyboardist Ikey Owens died in 2014. Despite their commercial success, their reputation in music snob circles suffered for a long time thanks to Pitchfork’s extremely negative reviews. However, in the last 5-10 years, their reputation has recovered significantly. After an At the Drive-In reunion in the late 2010s, the Mars Volta got back together in 2022 and have released two more albums. They are currently touring. Kanye West is a noted fan, posting a video of himself listening to “The Widow” from *Frances the Mute* on twitter in 2020.

# Hargrove's review

This album left an awful first impression on me and then each subsequent listen I’ve enjoyed it more and now I’m scared. I had never listened to the Mars Volta before but I’ve been aware of them since the 2010s and have always assumed I wouldn’t like them because they were prog (and, to be perfectly honest with myself, probably lingering bias from Pitchfork making them uncool). But then we listened to At the Drive-In’s *Relationship of Command*, which has become one of my all-time favorite records, and I learned that the Mars Volta was a successor group. So my preconceived biases going in were mixed. The first listen was pretty rough; it feels like it opens mid-bar, and it takes a few minutes before it gets into any kind of rhythmic groove I can settle into. From there, the cacophony  of different instruments and textures and effects and song structures on this way-too-long album really put me off. But as I’ve kept listening (9 listens now, more than 11 hours), I keep finding new things I like that make it easier to push through the (still plentiful) annoying bits. Cedric Bixler-Zavala’s (hereby “CBL”) vocals can get way over-the-top, sure, but the soaring choruses of “Cygnus…Vismund Cygnus” and “Cassandra Gemini” remind me of At the Drive-In’s finest moments. “L’Via L’Viaquez” is challenging, certainly, but the Latin-prog fusion sounds like nothing else I’ve ever heard and it’s become my favorite track. And yeah, the lyrics are opaque and ambiguous, but I got really into Cygnus’ story (particularly the parts involving L’Via and Miranda) and even when things didn’t add up, that feeling of desperation and urgency permeates every moment of the album. So is this now one of my top albums? No, its aesthetic sensibilities differ profoundly from my own. But I have come to sincerely respect it and find myself itching to listen to it more if only to become more confused? Absolutely.

As I said, “Cygnus…Vismund Cygnus” feels like it starts mid-song, which has made more sense as realized that “Cassandra Gemini” ends with the same music. However, it still throws me for a bit of a loop every time, denying me any foothold until the third section. The opening chorus goes crazy hard, though: Who do you trust?/Will they feed us the womb?/Chrome the fetal mirage/Will they feed us the womb? Yeah”. I involuntarily sing along to that every time. There’s a bit of a clusterfuck transition that I don’t care for, but I do like the funky section where he says “nicotine stains” quite a bit. The remainder of the song is built on a super rhythmically weird bass line but, what can I say, they execute the minutes-long build over it perfectly. The musique concrète section at the end maybe goes on a bit too long but it’s kind of interesting. This track took a long time for me to crack, but I’ve really come to appreciate it. I’ll give “The Widow” props for its catchy chorus, but this album works best when the cool unique moments outshine the annoying bits and this track does not bring much in the cool and unique department. It’s got a very conventional structure and sound. Its lyrics also leave me completely cold, mediocre on their own and even less interesting in how little they progress the album’s story. I do like the Neutral Milk Hotel-style trumpet and the chorus, but otherwise this is easily my least favorite track. “L’Via L’Viaquez”, by contrast, is easily my favorite. I love the Latin elements, whether the biting delivery of the Spanish lyrics over the fast section or the Latin musical groove of the slower English sections. It goes back and forth between those two sections for a long time, but I really don’t mind; each is great on its own and they only enhance each other when combined. It also contains one of the album’s catchiest moments with “And with every body that I find/And with every claymore that they mine/I won't forget who I'm lookin' for”. It’s a super badass track that really no other band in history could have made.

“Miranda That Ghost Just Isn’t Holy Anymore” has some serious issues. The ambient atmospheric opening section goes on way too long, and the later build in the musical section never reaches an adequate climax and leaves me wanting more. However, I’m including it with my favorites for two reasons. First, the trumpet melody rocks, I could listen to it forever. Second, I love the lyrics (as I did on the previous track). “And when Miranda sang, everyone turned away/Used to the noose, they obey“ goes so hard. “Cassandra Gemini” is an unwieldy behemoth lasting more than half an hour. The first two sections, comprising ~6 minutes and the first track on Spotify, would make my favorite track list on their own. The guitar line is one of the album’s best, the nearly spoken-word section has some great sleazy imagery, and the first chorus of “No, there's no light in the darkest of your furthest reaches” makes for a supreme highlight in both its catchiness and the relentless ride cymbal. It’s like a version of “The Widow” done better in every way. Unfortunately, the remaining 25 (!) minutes do not keep this intensity at all. The third section (roughly 7:00 - 20:00) picks up in the middle at the “Twenty-five wives in the lake tonight/Raw bark in the water of the marble shrine” but is otherwise kind of dull. Then, the fourth section (roughly 20:00 - 30:00) begins to lose me entirely as it descends into slow jazzy noodling. The last couple minutes bring it back with a reprise of the first chorus and finally the “Sarcophagi” section that opened the album, but man is this track just a mess. It doesn’t come close to justifying its run time. It’s certainly disappointing, but has enough good parts to keep it from my least favorite spot.

Still not really my thing, but I cannot deny that it’s an invigorating and alluring listen. I’m going to put it in B Tier because that’s where I put Tom Waits for similar reasoning. I find it very likely that I’ll get the urge to listen to this again sometime soon.

Favorite tracks: Cygnus…Vismund Cygnus, L’Via L’Viaquez, Miranda That Ghost Just Isn’t Holy Anymore

Least favorite track: The Widow
