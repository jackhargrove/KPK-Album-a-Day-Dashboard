# Write-up

The Microphones were an American indie rock band. The band is one of the two under which leader and sole consistent member Phil Elverum has released his music, with the other being the currently ongoing Mount Eerie. Elverum got his start in the music industry in the mid 1990s while working in a record store in his hometown of Anacortes, Washington, which was owned by Bret Lunsford. From 1982 to 1992, Lunsford had been a member of Beat Happening, a pioneering twee pop band. Lunsford gave Elverum the equipment to make music, and released two cassettes by him. He also recruited him play drums in a band, D+, where Elverum would meet Karl Blau. After graduating high school, Elverum moved to Olympia, Washington to go to college. There, he met another member of Beat Happening, Calvin Johnson, who also ran famed indie record label, K Records. Elverum soon dropped out of college and scraped by living in the K Record studio, playing in D+ and producing for K Records. He released his first full record under his own name, titled "Tests", followed by his first under The Microphones, "Don't Wake Me Up", which K Records put out. He began to get attention in the local area, and in 2000, released "It Was Hot, We Stayed in the Water", which saw him increase the complexity of his work, with more layered and intricate production. The album was inspired by a variety of sources including the Beach Boys, Will Oldham and the nature of the area, particularly the water. The record was seen as one of the best of the year and received further attention following Elverum's later success.

"The Glow Pt. 2" is titled as a sequel from the centerpiece of "It Was Hot, We Stayed in the Water, an 11-minute track titled "The Glow". Elverum described "The Glow" as referring to the white light of near death experiences. Thematically, the album is about fire (in contrast to the theme of water in "It Was Hot, We Stayed in the Water"). The album also showcased a further step forward in Elverum's production techniques, including increased use of stereo sound and a heavy layer of distortion achieved through various techniques. The drum machine was credited as "Karl Blau". Blau did not actually play drums, but he did help engineer the album and played saxophone on "The Moon". The album also incorporated influences from various styles of music including black metal and ambient music, in addition to the TV series Twin Peaks. The album was released on 9/11/2001 and received incredibly well and since then its stature has only grown among indie rock bands.

Elverum released one more album under the Microphones name, "Mount Eerie", which was themed around the mountain of the same name near his hometown and was themed around rock (in comparison to the water and fire of the previous two albums). The album was also very well received. After that, Elverum retired the Microphones name and started releasing music under "Mount Eerie". He also stopped releasing his music through the notoriously dysfunctional K Records, and switched to his own label. His music continued to be well received, but did not garner the same attention until 2017's "A Crow Looked A Me". Written in the wake of the death of his wife, Geneviève Castrée, the album featured a more raw sound and lyrics about her death and the aftermath in Elverum's life. Elverum would return to the Microphones project in 2020, in the well received "Microphones in 2020". He continues to release music, with the most recent being Mount Eerie's "Night Palace" in 2024.

# Hargrove's review

*“The Glow” Pt. 2* was one of those albums that the internet told me to listen to when I was 16. I did so once, enjoyed it (I think), and never returned to it. It did push me toward later Elverum projects that resonated more with me upon first listen. *A Crow Looked at Me* by his post-Microphones solo project Mount Eerie is a brutal listen; I found deep solace in the frankness of “Real Death” when my grandma died in 2019. I was also impressed by the Microphones quasi-comeback album *The Microphones in 2020*, which can be found at the start of my “Top 10 Albums of 2020” list in *The Dartmouth*. Despite this, I never revisited *“The Glow” Pt. 2* one time and didn’t count it as an album I had previously listened to when we started this list. After spending the last few days listening to it, I’ve found it to be one of the most difficult records to review up to this point. The highs are really damn high, almost definitely assuring it a place in A Tier. It starts with one of the best opening three-track runs I’ve ever heard in “I Want Wind to Blow” into “The Glow Pt. 2” into “The Moon”. All three of these tracks perfectly balance Elverum’s mopey sadboy inclinations with bursts of fiery, angry energy. Unfortunately, as the album goes on, he biases too much toward the mopey sadboy stuff. I’m not inherently against that kind of thing, but it’s frustrating to sit through “Headless Horseman”, “My Roots Are Strong and Deep”, “I’ll Not Contain You”, etc. when I know Elverum’s capacity to fight back. And while there are a fair few great lyrics on this thing, I find Elverum too clumsy of a wordsmith at times for the most despondent tracks to land properly. The high-energy, defiant sound does return in tracks like “Map”, “I Want to Be Cold”, and especially “Samurai Sword”, and I love those tracks, but these two aspects progressively separate like oil and vinegar instead of the perfect integration of the opening trio. Elverum definitively confirms which side of him wins out with the album’s closer, “My Warm Blood”, which dissipates into eight minutes of minimalist ambience. It’s a bit of a shame. I want him to fight back. I want him to try. But he succumbs to the darkness, to oblivion. Oh well. I’ll always have the blow-out distortion, bizarre guitar rhythms in stereo, and crisp drums of my favorite tracks to return to.

“I Want Wind to Blow” opens the album with truly amazing guitars, a mind-blowing use of stereo where the rhythms batter at each other like waves in the sea yet still sound so simple. Elverum mopes about for most of the track, but that feeling works best for me here at the beginning of the album. It helps that the chorus and even the verses are crazy catchy. The death march of the guitar/accordion/bass drum breakdown caught me off guard the first time I listened, pleasantly surprising me when the track gets loud after a second of hesitation. On top of all that, it includes some of my favorite lyrics, particularly “But there's no hope for me, I've been set free/There's no breeze/There's no ship on my sea”. It’s an amazing opener. “The Glow Pt. 2” starts loud, enticing me with its dynamic drumming, crashing cymbals, and distorted guitar. The out-of-sync strumming adds to the chaotic atmosphere. Elverum actually gets angry in the vocals themselves, something I wish there were more of. I faced death, I went in with my arms swinging/But I heard my own breath/And I had to face that I'm still living/I’m still flesh/I hold on to awful feelings/I’m not dead/There's no end/My face is red/My blood flows harshly/My heart beats loudly/My chest still draws breath/I hold it/I’m buoyant, ooh ooh/There's no end” is just an elite sequence. The banger opening trio finishes with “The Moon”, which starts with a driving motorik drum beat into a lamentful sax line in the chorus. I enjoy the lyricism, though the verse about the sun reflecting off the moon is the first to stand out to me as particularly clumsy. Thankfully, the chorus is comparably catchy to the opener’s.

After the stellar opening, “Headless Horseman” opens my least favorite section of the album. It loses all the oomph of the opening three, getting uncomfortably close to a ukulele ballad. There’s no fight to be found here or in the next few tracks, just limp whining over an acoustic. When an album starts to feel soft, “My Roots Are Strong and Deep” proves that the ideal next move is NOT a waltz. Elverum doesn’t make the piano sound as weird as he makes the guitar, though not for lack of trying. I can appreciate the atmospherics, but this track lacks any punch. I love what Elverum does with the stereo guitars of “Instrumental [1]”, ping-ponging back and forth in an almost swung pattern before growing into something fuller. It’s a strong interlude. I like “The Mansion” better than the other slow, quiet tracks that surround it. I appreciate the more ominous mood. I still would like Elverum to show more anger, but something about the complete and total resignation here also appeals to me. “[untitled]” sounds cinematic, but ends prematurely. “(Something)” contains the first hints of distortion since “The Moon”. I enjoy the industrial soundscape, but what I really need right now is some momentum. I don’t find it in “I’ll Not Contain You”, my least favorite track on the album. Pick up the pace, man! Do something for yourself! Try! Why tease me with the first three tracks that show you’re capable of getting loud and fast and angry, and then just veer off into this for 20 minutes? Get a move on! Plus, “Things are not concrete there and we fastly glide” is another clumsy line.

Power finally starts to return with “The Gleam Pt. 2” thanks to the pounding kick drums in the opening, as well as the dissonance of the steel drums. It’s short, but also an extremely important reintroduction of purpose. “Map” opens with even more dissonance. I love the simmering anger of the vocals, the female vocals (that have more power than Eleverum’s, even now), and especially the wonderful martial drum outro. It’s easily my favorite track since the opening three. Those drums are so crisp! It’s a little frustrating getting through the mopey stuff when you know Elverum has this in him, but it’s worth it when it pays off. I swear that “You’ll Be in the Air” has the same melody as the Beatle’s “When I’m Sixty-Four”. I like the delicate and pretty guitar arpeggios, but I’m torn on the vocals. I’ve been asking for Elverum to show some passion, but he stretches his voice too far here and it sounds silly. What is that moan? “I Want to Be Cold” starts off with DISTORTION and ANGER. FUCK YEAH! The title line is just brilliant, particularly in the construction “I hope your flames don't grow/I want to be buried in snow/I hope your flames don't grow/I just want to be cold”. This track packs an impressive amount of emotion into a minute-and-a-half. “I Am Bored” is kind of an interesting track because it’s definitely not distorted and loud, but it still has much more forward motion than the quietest tracks. I also find the lyrical concept more intriguing than any other on the album.

Much like “You’ll Be in the Air”, “I Felt My Size” is a monkey’s paw curl with the vocal intensity. Elverum needs to recognize his vocal limits a little better. I like the pretty melody and chords, btu I don’t love the execution. “Instrumental [2]” is another good interlude. I have to hand it to Elverum, these are the kinds of tracks I usually dismiss, but he does a great job at ensuring they add to the album. “I Felt Your Shape” reminds me of “Headless Horseman” in the way it comes uncomfortably close to a ukulele ballad. It’s the better of the two because of the guitar work, but I’m not in love with it. “Really all I felt was falsely strong” stands out to me as an awkward line. I fucking LOVE “Samurai Sword”. What a banger transition into maximum blow out in the first second. It’s full of thrashing energy from beginning to end. Elverum finally gets well and truly angry. The drums go insane, while the whispery vocals sound both determined and terrifying. “My Warm Blood” starts out fine. I like the only verse a good bit. But then, it goes into pure ambience for eight minutes. It’s one of those things where I respect the thematic idea to end the album, but first of all, I’d prefer that this album went out on a defiant note instead of a resigned one, and second of all, I don’t find it enjoyable to listen to.

To reiterate, this album has crazy high highs, enough for A Tier. But I can’t justify putting something with so many missed opportunities much higher than that. I’m sticking it between two jazz fusion albums that are similarly good but inconsistent.

Favorite tracks: I Want Wind to Blow, The Glow Pt. 2, The Moon, Instrumental [1], The Mansion, The Gleam Pt. 2, Map, I Want to Be Cold, I Am Bored, Instrumental [2], Samurai Sword

Least favorite track: I’ll Not Contain You

# Aidan's review

This is an all time favorite album, mostly due to how good the production and instrumentals are. "The Moon" might be my favorite track in this entire list, and is probably the one I've listened to the most thanks to a week in 2022 when I just played it nonstop on loop. The little patter starting with "and under swaying trees we had the same dream" is high up on my list of perfect moments in music. This album probably makes the best usage of stereo of any I've heard, with instrumentals constantly swirling between sides to create an enveloping atmosphere. There's ideas scattered all over the place on this album, but the production manages to tie them all together into something magical that just works for an hour. And a lot of the ideas are brilliant. The sudden transition of the hammering "I Want to Be Cold" followed by the acoustic ending and singsong outro is brilliant. "I Felt My Size" is such a good song that the Unicorns, a lesser indie band just ripped it off for an a pretty decent debut album. I can't think of any album that does a better job at making noise sound so warm and enjoyable, and that in turn makes the moments of tranquility feel magical. I have other albums I like less that I'll listen to far more because I feel like I can put them on and turn them off. The Glow, Pt. 2 only works a coherent whole, but it's so impressive as an entire album.
