# Write-up

The Necks are an Australian improvisational jazz trio composed of pianist Chris Abrahams, drummer Tony Buck and bassist Lloyd Swanton. The band's music is entirely roughly hour long, improvisational pieces, with their studio releases sometimes involving editing and guest musicians. All three members had played in previous bands before forming The Necks, and Sex was their debut album. Like most of their work, Sex is centered around single repeating two bar rhythm played by Swanton that all three then overdubbed their improvisations around. It is their best known work. Their live performances are similar, always improvisational. The band was once approached to play Sex live, but refused, seeing it as against their ethos. They have had collaborations with Swans and the electronic music group Underworld (the band behind the theme for Trainspotting). The Necks are still performing and touring, mostly in Europe and Australia.

# Hargrove's review

This review is going to be on the shorter side, but don’t mistake that as a sign that I didn’t like this album. It’s just that the one track format makes it impossible for me to follow my usual pattern of having a full paragraph dedicated to talking about individual tracks. Furthermore, the steadfastly minimalist approach that the Necks take to *Sex* reduces the raw material that I have to talk about. On this album, Lloyd Swanton lays down a simple three-note bass vamp that will be your home base for the next hour. Though this may sound monotonous, I never really found myself getting bored with this album even once in my seven listens. The bass vamp keeps a recognizable center that allows the piano and the drums freedom to move around. There’s motion in this album, but it’s glacial, slow deviations from the straight line path that add up over time. Chris Abrahams’ piano leads the charge here, playing in a style that’s soft, light, and impressionistic. My favorite part of the album comes at 19:45 when, after I’ve finally settled into the groove, a new recurring piano line begins that jolts me awake with each listen. Additionally, there are consistent sound effects throughout that I cannot for the life of me discern. The most prominent are these slow creaking sounds, as though from a rusty violin. Whatever they are, I love them. The atmosphere of this record is calm, serene, and soothing. While *Sex* is unique, there are four albums we’ve listened to in particular that it reminded me of. It starts with the compositional approach of Sleep’s *Dopesmoker*, adds the textural richness of Tim Hecker’s *Ravedeath, 1972*, and dresses all of that with the atmospheres of Talk Talk’s *Spirit of Eden* and *Laughing Stock*. Overall, I’m most impressed with the fact that this is one of the first ambient records to actually work for me…ambiently. Though I can appreciate the art of Eno or GAS or Biosphere, I don’t ever actually get the urge to listen to it in the background. I just end up tuning it out completely. *Sex*, however, made my day better when I listened while doing other things. I could check out of it for a bit without fully losing focus thanks to the consistency of the bass vamp, and then when I checked back in, the piano would be doing something new. This album has a lower ceiling for me because its uncompromising nature forces me to keep it at a distance. I just have to be realistic in saying that it can’t move me in the way my A Tier or higher albums can. But damn am I impressed. What a fascinating concept and what a pleasant execution. I’m putting it just above Weakling in B Tier as that was a record I had similar arms-length admiration for.

# Aidan's review

This record is breathtakingly simple in its concept and breathtakingly beautiful in its execution. It's a single three note, two bar, baseline, with the trio all improving over in, and the entire thing took just two takes to record. It's hypnotic without being dreary, and over the course of an hour, the energy slowly builds, never reaching any sort of tension, but just enough that you feel it. I've been fond of heavily repetative records between Ambient 1, Disintegration Loops and Hallogallo off Neu!, and this is just another great example. I've probably listened to this upwards of 10 times this week. It's not the single most compelling piece of music, but it's so easy to listen to and feel at ease. During my first listen, I was expecting to be bored, and I think that in the hands of lesser musicians this album could be a slog, but it's a good idea executed excellently. Best new album in a while.
