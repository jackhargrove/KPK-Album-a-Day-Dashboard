# Write-up

As befitting any proper British lefty band, the Pop Group was formed in a private school. In 1977 in Bristol Grammar School, Mark Steward, John Waddington and Simon Underwood decided to form a funk group. Another one of their fellow classmates, Nick Sheppard also went to play for the Clash's last album. The trio added Gaerth Sager and Bruce Smith to round out the band, and began to attract attention for their boisterous live performances. The group mixed an eclectic range of influences, from the current wave of punk to free jazz, dub to philosophers such as Jean Baudrillard to French poetry. Their debut single, "She Is Beyond Good and Evil" generated enough buzz and airplay to secure the group a contract with Radar Records, a punk label that also featured Elvis Costello and the Attractions, Pere Ubu, Richard Hell and the Vocaloids, and Nick Lowe. Politics were a key part of The Pop Group's music, their first tour's profits went to Amnesty International and their sophomore album was bluntly titled "For How Much Longer Will We Tolerate Mass Murder?"

To make their debut album, today's "Y", the group worked with Dennis Bovell, himself a noted dub musician, who managed to shape the group's chaos into a more dub inspired sound. The result was still incredibly messy, and as such received mixed reviews at the time, but has now grown into something of a cult classic. Sonic Youth, Minutemen, Nick Cave and Primal Scream have also cited it as an influence, and its distinct blend of dub, funk and punk would forerun the sound of groups such as Gang of Four and Public Image Ltd. After releasing their second album, the group split under the pressure of internal disagreements. Steward in particular went on to have a notable solo career, releasing several solo albums, collabs ranging from Nick Cave to Trent Reznor, as well as creating conceptual art. The band reunited for Matt Groening's edition of All Tomorrow's Parties, and managed to stay together after that, releasing two more albums to highly varied critical reception, as well as putting out a large amount of back catalog songs and remixes on Stewart's label, Freaks R Us. The band broke up once again following Stewart and Waddington's deaths in 2023.

# Hargrove's review

Unfortunate that our first album back is my least favorite album we’ve heard so far, but what are you gonna do. This album is so unbelievably annoying. It’s the fourth album I would call the *Trout Mask Replica* of a genre, and it’s by far my least favorite. The others are Sun City Girls (*TMR* of folk), Royal Trux (*TMR* of indie rock), and the original. This is the *TMR* of post-punk, but with one major difference: while the others were intentional deconstructions of an existing genre that at the very least forced you to analyze the component parts of said genre, this is an early, formative post-punk album! And it is atrocious. At no point does it make me think about post-punk in a different way. It’s just an annoying guy singing in an annoying voice over pseudo-free jazz noodling and admittedly good but wasted bass lines. I really like post-punk as a sound, and dance punk even moreso, but wow does the combination not work for me here. It uses dance elements, but at no point do you feel like dancing. Give me Minutemen, Talking Heads, or LCD Soundsystem over this any day.

“Thief of Fire” is by far the best track, mostly because it’s one of the only songs that actually bothers to be a song. The story of Prometheus has always been one of my favorite Greek myths, and it’s used well here. The bass and drums are both tight and the sax at the end actually works. The vocals are of course annoying, but this does work as a dance punk song until it falls apart at the end. I wish the rest of the album sounded like this. “Snowgirl” is awful immediately, and starts a nearly unlistenable section for me. I hate the bass line, I hate how out of sync it feels constantly, the saving grace is the piano but still just not good. “Blood Money” takes it to a new low, and is my least favorite until the closer. It’s not even a fucking composition; maybe there’s the occasional steady drum line, but this is torturous, torturous chaos. “Savage Sea” is better than the last two by virtue of the cool atmosphere created by the piano and the vocals, but it doesn’t do anything with it. Nothing is happening in this album! “We Are Time” is 4 minutes too long, but it’s the first time I’ve enjoyed anything since the opener. The bass and guitar lines both work, the drums are driving, and the vocals are the vocals but what are you gonna do. It goes on too long and gets worse, but I’ll put it in favorite tracks. “Words Disobey Me” is fine for a minute but falls into the same old shit. Another good bass line wasted. Just shut up man. The ascending part of the sax line in “Don’t Call Me Pain” sounds painfully like “Love Me Do”, another song I don’t like, and it kills me. There are fractured pieces of a bass line, but again, way too long. “The Boys from Brazil” feels like it has the rhythm section to an entirely different track. I do not like the call-and-response vocals. The closer “Don’t Sell Your Dreams” is one final spit in the face before the sweet release of the end. Awful, awful, awful nothingburger of a closer. It’s so goddamn annoying. No motion, no bitches, no nothing.

Did *not* enjoy this. Do *not* recommend. It’s annoying in the way the other *TMR* records are, but it doesn’t even expose anything interesting, and the personality of the album is unbearable. Never again.

Favorite tracks: Thief of Fire, We Are Time

Least favorite track: Don’t Sell Your Dreams

# Aidan's review

I'm hopelessly biased for this record because it's so similar in style to Gang of Four's Entertainment, one of my all time favorite records. And while it is neither as smart or as enjoyable as Entertainment, there's a lot I enjoy about this records. The guitars are great, the two very distinct stylings weave in and out of the groovy baselines. The songs are quick to find a groove and stick with it, and Stewart's raving fits really well with the overall stylings. There's some very smart and funny songs too, "The Boys From Brazil" complains about girls going for the "Boys from Brazil", which references a book and film about clones of Adolf Hitler (i.e. the boys from Brazil). While "She Is Beyond Good and Evil" may not technically be on the album, the idea of a Nietzschean superwoman girlfriend as the subject of a love song really amuses me. And most of the time you really don't end up listening to the lyrics, you just enjoy the great grooves. The album isn't free of sins, "Don't Sell Your Dreams" abandons the success previously found in keeping momentum through songs for a near 7 minute slog. Blood Money also floats around a bit much, and I can see being annoyed by some of the more cacophonous sections. Still, I enjoyed this album the same way I enjoyed a lot of the jazz albums we've done, the grooves are great, and overlooking the annoyingness that some of the more intricate sections have.
