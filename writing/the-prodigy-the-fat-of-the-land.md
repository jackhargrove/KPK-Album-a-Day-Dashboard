# Write-up

The Prodigy is a British big beat group formed in Braintree in 1990 whose best-known lineup consisted of producer and keyboardist Liam Howlett, vocalist and rapper Maxim Reality (real name Keith Palmer), dancer and vocalist Keith Flint, and dancer and keyboardist Leeroy Thornhill. The Prodigy began in 1990 when a 19-year-old Howlett began DJing hip hop and house music in his hometown of Braintree. He met dancers Flint and Thornhill at a nightclub, who requested he make them a mix tape. Howlett did so, naming the tape “Prodigy” because he had used a Moog Prodigy synth to make it. They formed a group in late 1990, and were joined at their first performance in 1991 by rapper Maxim Reality, who became the fourth member. The Prodigy released a debut EP in 1991 and a debut single, “Charly”, later that year; “Charly” became a massive hit with the rave scene, which had exploded across the country starting in 1989. The group used the money from the single to buy new equipment and released a second single, “Everybody in the Place (Fairground Edit)”, at the end of 1991. It reached even greater success, peaking at #2 on the UK singles chart. The Prodigy released its debut album *Experience* in 1992 to great success, receiving a platinum certification. The group moved beyond its rave origins with its second album, 1994’s *Music for the Jilted Generation*. It established a broader sound that was heavily focused on breakbeats and took aesthetic cues from alternative rock. It was another success, debuting at #1 on the UK album chart.

The Prodigy began its commercial peak in 1996 with the release of the controversial “Firestarter” single, which stayed at #1 on the UK singles chart for three weeks. The group changed its look from hip hop ravers to old-school punks. The Prodigy released its third album, *The Fat of the Land*, in 1997. The album heightened the punk influence in its electronic sound. The single “Smack My Bitch Up” did cause controversy, however. “Breathe” samples “Da Mystery of Chessboxin’” by Wu-Tang Clan, “Diesel Power” samples CAN, “Funky Shit” samples DJ Shadow, “Serial Thrilla” samples Nirvana and Autechre, “Firestarter” samples the Breeders, and “Climbatize” samples “Won’t Get Fooled Again” by the Who. Jonas Åkerlund of Swedish black metal band Bathory directed the music video for “Smack My Bitch Up”. It was a runaway success, becoming their first #1 album on the Billboard 200 in the US, where they headlined Lollapalooza. Thornhill left the group in 2000. They started going downhill fast with a flop single in 2002. Still, their 2004 fourth album was a commercial success. The group has continued to release records with declining relevance since. Flint committed suicide in 2019 at age 49. The Prodigy began touring again in the 2020s and intends to release an eighth album in 2026.

The Prodigy were influential on a style of 90s EDM in the UK known as big beat. Breakbeat is a large family of EDM genres that are built around syncopated breakbeats like hip hop. EDM first broke through in the UK when acid house kicked off the Second Summer of Love in 1988. Soon, rave became the dominant alternative youth culture, with a new style known as breakbeat hardcore taking over that made aggressive, driving tracks with breakbeats. The breakbeats were soon abandoned, leaving pure hardcore to guide the rave scene. Breakbeat instead moved toward the hip hop and alternative rock scenes. Big beat became a movement that combined the sounds and aesthetics of breakbeat EDM, UK hip hop, and punk-influenced alt rock. In addition to the Prodigy, the Chemical Brothers and Fatboy Slim were the most famous practitioners of the genre. Breakbeat hardcore had a revival in the 2010s underground hyperpop-adjacent scene with hardcore breaks, a genre exemplified by Machine Girl.

# Hargrove's review

Well this was definitely a fun album to listen to. It’s an hourlong burst of energy with lots of attitude and personality. I had never listened to the Prodigy before, but I was familiar with the big beat sound through Fatboy Slim singles I had heard on the radio a bunch of times (“Praise You”, “The Rockafeller Skank”) and an unsuccessful attempt to get into the Chemical Brothers half a decade ago. I can’t say I was the biggest fan of big beat before, but the Prodigy have made the most palatable and enjoyable form of it for me on *The Fat of the Land*. The overall vibe is like Massive Attack meets Nine Inch Nails, something I can definitely dig. Everything is high octane, with few even remotely slow or quiet sections within the songs. The breakbeats are funky as hell, losing none of their danceability amid the pounding synth and kick drum onslaught. The opening singles “Smack My Bitch Up” and “Breathe” are easily the highlights for me. I could see myself having a great time in the club with either one blasting (they would have made good basement music too). I don’t like “Firestarter” quite as much, though it’s still pretty decent. There are also some good album cuts, particularly “Serial Thrilla”, “Narayan”, and “Climbatize”. Impressively for an album of this type, the decent tracks are pretty evenly spread throughout the album. There’s no extended 20-20 minute period of mid music to fall asleep during. That being said, this album still does have its fair bit of filler. “Funky Shit” and “Mindfields” specifically come to mind for me, but the worst offender is “Fuel My Fire”, which closes the album on its lowest note. Still, the distribution makes the filler not too bad, and the energy remains high. This record doesn’t make me feel things that other records do, but I had a hell of a lot of fun, and that’s something, right?

What better way to start an album than a song titled “Smack My Bitch Up”? It’s quite the conversation starter. But seriously, it’s a banger and a super fun track. It feels ridiculous to say “Change my pitch up/Smack my bitch up”, and yet it’s been stuck in my head for days. It also uses the gritty midrange synths well and has a stellar breakbeat. “Breathe” is just as good as the first track, if not even better. The back-and-forth vocals between Keith Flint and Maxim Reality are super catchy and a joy to sing along to. Flint in particular exudes charisma here. The instrumental constantly sounds like a motor revving, keeping energy levels high. “Diesel Power” has a decent instrumental. The focus is on Kool Keith, though, who I have never liked as much as I’m supposed. His spits three passable but forgettable verses, and we move on. “Funky Shit” shifts the vibe of the album from 1990s rave to 2090s rave and it remains there for the rest of the album. It feels colder, more alienating, and certainly industrial. The sound is good, but this track is a little mid. Definitely filler. Thankfully, “Serial Thrilla” is not filla. We get a super strong breakbeat again, as well as great vocals from Flint. It’s obviously not quite single quality, but it’s pretty much exactly what I want out of an album track from this band. “Mindfields” is meh, leaving me unsatisfied and wanting more. The vocals fail to excite me, and the instrumental lacks motion too. “Narayan” is by far the album’s longest track, but it justifies its length. It starts with a chillingly cool synth arpeggio before the breakbeat comes in, creating an awesome soundscape. The breakdown section employs ritual chanting to great effect, taking the creepy atmosphere to the next level. “Firestarter” is a good track, but I don’t really get the hype. “Smack My Bitch Up” and “Breathe” are both streets ahead. I like the energy, but Flint’s vocals aren’t terribly memorable, and I have trouble keeping the track in my head. I prefer the instrumental that follows, “Climbatize”. It’s a great instrumental track with a super bouncy bass line and an entrancing Middle Eastern synth melody. I like it a lot. Sadly, the album ends on its weakest track, “Fuel My Fire”. On its own, it’s subpar filler and kind of annoying. As a closer without any sense of resolution, it’s downright irritating. Oh well.

There’s not a lot more for me to say. This was a fun album. Will definitely play a song or two in the basement next time I’m at the house. It’s a solid addition to my repertoire.

Favorite tracks: Smack My Bitch Up, Breathe, Serial Thrilla, Narayan, Firestarter, Climbatize

Least favorite track: Fuel My Fire

# Aidan's review

Smack My Bitch Up is nearly as good as its music video, which I am very glad to have seen in between waves of it being scrubbed from the internet. It's an adrenaline rush of an opener, and sets the stage nicely for what the rest of the album is going to be, high energy rave music that screams late 90s as much as Slick Willy or the Spice Girls. None of the songs areas good as the opener, but with exception of the annoying rap on "Diesel Power",  there's no real weak tracks here. My main gripe is that the album stretches out to a near hour and there's not enough hit singles in here to keep the energy going quite that long. I can see why people who love rave music adore it, but I found it merely pretty good.
