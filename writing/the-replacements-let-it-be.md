# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

This record is now the third we’ve listened to that has inspired me to order a band shirt. It totally kicks ass. I was very vaguely familiar with the Replacements because I know my dad had mentioned them before and also from when we listened to Hüsker Dü, but I was not prepared at all for how much I was going to like this. It has to be my second favorite 80s alt rock record I’ve heard after *Doolittle*. I think naming your album “Let It Be” is one of the most genuinely transgressive things we’ve seen from an artist yet. That takes serious balls. The Mats continue this middle finger spirit throughout, which would put me off if it didn’t feel so natural. What I relate to is their willingness to stick it to their fellow outsiders, whether through the piano balladry or the deeply unfashionable KISS cover. It reminds me of a song Fiona Apple released nearly 30 years later, “Periphery”, wherein she incisively tears apart the “peripheral idiots” within her own alternative scene. I get a sense from some punk bands that their contrarianism is in fact an attempt to fit in, just with the fringe instead of the mainstream. Not so here, however. These four losers could push anyone away, up to and including each other. It’s a commitment to Oppositional Defiant Disorder that hits close to home for me. What then takes it to yet another level is Westerberg’s unexpected tenderness. His songwriting on this album elevates it so far above what it could have (or, rather, should have) been. “Androgynous” comes out of nowhere as a touching but not fawning portrait of those who didn’t have a ton of people sticking up for them. “Sixteen Blue” is such a mature take on youth. But what takes the cake is “Unsatisfied”. Insofar as art’s worth can be measured by how strongly it conveys emotion, it’s one of the most artistically successful songs we’ve heard so far. It makes my eyes well up every time I hear it. And, finally, if all of that wasn’t enough for you, these guys just rock. They just make good songs. “I Will Dare” is just super catchy. On later albums, they’ll even top it with bangers like “Bastards of Young” and “Alex Chilton”, the latter of which I’m listening to on repeat as I write this on a plane. It just kicks ass. They just kick ass.

“I Will Dare” is a jangly power pop song that I cannot get enough of. The vocal melody is catchy and fun to sing along to, the guitar that highlights the chorus is perfect, and the boom chuk drums propel everything forward. It hooked me immediately on first listen. And as a lifelong, chronic nail-biter, as well as a now-and-again smoker, “Fingernails and cigarettes, a lousy dinner” is just like me fr. I love the almost country yodeling verses of “Favorite Thing”. The chord change into each chorus is musically one of my favorite parts of this album. “Yeah, I know, I look like hell/Smoke and I drink and I'm feeling swell” is a vibe. “We’re Comin’ Out” keeps the hardcore flavor of their earlier output. I appreciate the dynamics of the tempo change. “Tommy Gets His Tonsils Out” is an okay song, but I’m so glad it’s on the album because of the weirdo subject matter. It just adds something. Everything here adds something, even if it’s not stellar. And the chorus is catchy. Westerberg’s songwriting ability shines on “Androgynous”. I find it baffling that some random Midwestern punk loser can just whip out this kind of practiced, tight songwriting. “Kewpie dolls and urine stalls will be laughed at/The way you’re laughed at now” and “Now, something meets Boy, and something meets Girl/They both look the same, they’re overjoyed in this world/Same hair revolution, unisex evolution/Tomorrow, who’s gonna fuss?” jump out at me in particular. I also love how this track reinforces their refusal to be classified or pigeonholed, as does the next track “Black Diamond”. It has to be one of my favorite covers we’ve had on an album so far. I love how they commit to the bit and play it straight, hard, and loud. I’m also a fan of the clear contrast in songwriting style it has with everything else.

“Unsatisfied” is far and away the best track, however. It’s so impressive in both sentiment and intensity. In terms of sentiment,  it captures the exact feeling of wistfulness triggered by young adulthood and not feeling the away you thought you would. In terms of intensity, it conveys it to an almost punishing degree. Westerberg’s vocal performance obviously sells it but the instrumental also communicates this fairly complex emotion. I also adore the aimless twangs near the end especially. Really, really, phenomenally good track. “Seen Your Video” has a great instrumental opening that reminds me a lot of Minutemen, that kind of fun cowpunk feel to the instrumentation. Gary’s Got a Boner is another one that I think adds to the album just through its existence, but it’s easily my least favorite as a track. It’s got just a little bit too much of the hard rock stuff; the riffs aren’t particularly original nor do they complement the Mats much. That being said, I’m very glad that this album has a song called “Gary’s Got a Boner”. “Sixteen Blue” is another beautiful ballad almost on par with “Androgynous”. The guitar plays some seriously beautiful and melancholic chords and Westerberg sings some shockingly heartfelt lyrics. It brings back the twangs from “Unsatisfied” too. “Answering Machine” is the perfect closer. It’s got more stellar lyrical ideas and uncharacteristically sparse instrumentation. Westerberg’s voice and Stinson’s guitar have a similarly ragged timbre, which is cool.

Genuinely, this is a gorgeous album. I find it so, so pretty. I cannot recommend it enough. I foresee myself listening to a lot of Replacements for the rest of my life.

Favorite tracks: I Will Dare, Favorite Thing, Androgynous, Black Diamond, Unsatisfied, Seen Your Video, Sixteen Blue, Answering Machine

Least favorite track: Gary’s Got a Boner
