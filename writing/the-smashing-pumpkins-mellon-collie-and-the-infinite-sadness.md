# Write-up

Why does Billy Corgan look like that?

\*\*\*\*\*DISCLAIMER\*\*\*\*\*

Album is two hours long, so it’s a two-day album taking up the whole weekend.

The Smashing Pumpkins is an alternative rock band formed in Chicago in 1988 whose lineup consisted of lead guitarist and vocalist Billy Corgan, rhythm guitarist James Iha, bassist D’arcy Wretzky, and drummer Jimmy Chamberlin. Corgan is a Chicago native who was raised by an abusive father. He met Iha while working at a record store. They formed a duo in 1988 to make music inspired by the Cure and New Order. Corgan met Wretzky shortly afterward and she joined on bass. Chamberlin, a jazz drummer, was recommended by a friend; his ignorance of alt rock allowed the group to explore harder sounds. The quartet released its first single in 1990. It was a success, earning them a deal with a major label. Butch Vig recorded the group’s debut album *Gish* in 1991 shortly before producing Nirvana’s *Nevermind*. Though *Gish* was a modest success, the band immediately faced issues. Corgan was a control freak who insisted on playing every instrument in the studio except drums, freezing out Wretzky and Iha. Chamberlin started doing hard drugs, while Corgan became homeless.

By 1992, Seattle’s grunge dominated the mainstream music scene, earning the Pumpkins incessant comparisons to Nirvana and Pearl Jam. The group left Chicago for Georgia to record a second album with Vig in an attempt to force Chamberlin to quit drugs. This didn’t work, and the sessions went about as well as they did for the first album. Nevertheless 1993’s *Siamese Dream* was a smash success, hitting the top 10 of the Billboard 200 and selling 4 million copies. The success of *Siamese Dream* pissed off a lot of the group’s contemporaries, including jealous former tourmates Pavement, has-been sell-outs Hüsker Dü, and the ill-tempered Steve Albini. The album’s opener, “Cherub Rock”, was in fact about this strained relationship with the underground.

Corgan’s songwriting became prolific, and he set out to make an epic record inspired by Pink Floyd. The resulting album, 1995’s *Mellon Collie and the Infinite Sadness*, was a 2-hour double album that debuted at #1 on the Billboard 200, produced four top-40 singles, and went diamond (10x platinum). Alan Moulder was brought in as co-producer because of his work with my bloody valentine on *loveless*; he helped realize the signature Pumpkins sound with numerous layered guitar overdubs. The *Mellon Collie* sessions were run differently to the ones prior. Corgan was less of a dictator, with Iha and Wretzky allowed to record for the first time. Iha even wrote two songs. The record is separated into two discs, the first representing day and the second representing night. The band went on a world tour in 1996. Sadly, while on tour, Chamberlin and a touring keyboardist overdosed on heroin together, the former surviving while the latter didn’t. Chamberlin was fired from the band as a result. Still, they continued the tour, a decision that greatly harmed the group’s reputation.

The Pumpkins announced that *Mellon Collie* would be their last conventional album and that electronic music was the future. Their next album, *Adore*, was released in 1998 and largely abandoned guitars. Critics liked it, but audiences didn’t. Chamberlin, who had cleaned up his act, rejoined in 1999. Shortly after, Wretzky left the group. The following album, 2000’s *Machina*, returned to their classic sound, but was a massive commercial failure that some see as symbolic of the death of alternative rock with pop audiences. The band released one more album in 2000, played a farewell concert in the small Chicago club where they started, and broke up. Corgan and Chamberlin continued playing together, along with members of Slint and New Order. Wretzky dropped out of public life entirely. Corgan has since insulted her and Iha extensively to the press. The Smashing Pumpkins reformed in 2006 and have been together since. Currently, Chamberlin and Iha are in the band, but Wretzky and Corgan have not mended their relationship.

# Hargrove's review

“Today” by the Smashing Pumpkins from the album before this one was one of the *Rock Band 2* songs I most remember playing as an eight-year-old. Additionally, my dad and I used to watch a lot of *Whale Wars* around that time, which used “Bullet with Butterfly Wings” from this album as its theme song. As a result, both are pretty early, seminal musical memories for me. As an adult, it’s been funny to find out that my parents don’t actually like the Smashing Pumpkins all that much, that it was yet another example of my dad understanding that kids like melodies so he should at least make sure that the melodic music I listened to was good. I have also since gotten really into both “1979” and “Tonight, Tonight”, two absolutely brilliant singles from this album. However, I had never listened to a full album from the band. Given that background, I had two major misconceptions going into this record. The first was that, given the tameness of “1979”, “Tonight, Tonight”, and half of “Today”, the heaviness of “Bullet with Butterfly Wings” was an outlier in the Pumpkins sound. This was very much not the case. Around half of this record is just straight up metal. The other misconception was that this record would be wildly inconsistent. A band mostly known for its sprawling pop singles made an album with 28 tracks that’s over two hours long? Yeesh. Imagine what duds the deep cuts must be. Instead, this is one of the most startlingly consistent records I’ve ever heard. Sure, “1979” and “Tonight, Tonight” and “Bullet with Butterfly Wings” stand out as obvious high points, but otherwise there’s seldom variation in quality throughout the entire record. It’s consistently decent. Not every song is the greatest thing ever, but only one song on the album is bad (“We Only Come Out at Night”). For a record with 28 tracks and this level of mammoth, self-indulgent ambition, that’s pretty damn impressive. Unfortunately, it’s also this album’s greatest weakness. Yeah, there’s quite a bit of sonic diversity, from fiery metal to swirling orchestral rock to melancholic pop to acoustic ballads to hazy dream pop. But through it all, everything is just so…Pumpkins. It’s a blessing and a curse. On the one hand, it’s nearly two hours worth of good music, and the unified vision of the music makes up for the less coherent conceptual ideas underpinning the album. On the other, it can be a little punishing for a two hour listen straight through, especially after “X.Y.U.” when the album ends on a run of five slow songs in a row. It occasionally makes you yearn for something else. All in all, this is a brutal album to review. But to be honest, my 14 hours of Pumpkins this weekend (five listens to *Mellon Collie*, two to *Siamese Dream*) was pretty enjoyable. When Billy Corgan wants to make something delicately beautiful, well, sometimes he succeeds.

The opening piano instrumental of “Mellon Collie and the Infinite Sadness” feels sufficiently grand for this undertaking, though it’s maybe a bit long for its own good. The album kicks off in earnest with one of its best songs, “Tonight, Tonight”. I cannot get it out of my head! What a banger of a song with those gorgeous strings, swirling arpeggio guitar, and Corgan’s pleading deliver of lines like “We'll crucify the insincere tonight/We'll make things right, we'll feel it all tonight“. It’s the epitome of a beautiful track. It’s sincere hope almost feels hopeless. “Jellybelly” is the album’s first heavy song and I like its strong chorus, even though it doesn’t always stick with me after it’s finished. I like the guitar work on “Zero” a lot, but I can’t put it into my favorites because Corgan’s vocals do kind of annoy me. “Here Is No Why” slows things down again but brings great energy with its uneven guitar riff, catchy vocals, and awesome loud chorus. “Bullet with Butterfly Wings” might have become a bit of a cliché, but, fuck, how could a line as cathartic as “Despite all rage, I am still just a rat in a cage” not? It risks overdoing it with the angst, but in my opinion it works just because it’s so tight and well composed musically. It controls its dynamics very well. And every time I hear “The world is a vampire” a capella opening, I get a little excited. “To Forgive” slows the album down once more, its gorgeous strings in the bridge making me lament that the rest of the song is a bit bland. “An Ode to No One” brings back the aggression, which is pretty solid until the bridge/breakdown section that I don’t care for. “Love” is my least favorite track on disc 1; while I appreciate the various electronics and production techniques, it just feels too abrasive for little reward. “Cupid de Locke” returns to the highest level of beauty the Pumpkins can bring with its looped harp arpeggios and sublime vocal line. “Galapogos” follows it up with more lush, twinkling sounds that I just love. “Muzzle” returns to the ringing shoegaze guitars, though this track is a bit too mall alt rock for me. I really enjoy “Porcelina of the Vast Oceans”, a kind of pre-Radiohead prog alt rock track with spacey textures, an elevated quiet/loud dynamic, multiple great sections and buildup, and an awesome intro with spindly guitar and delicate drums. Disc 1 closes with “Take Me Down”, a track that’s just too soft and hazy for its own good, leaving it a touch formless.

“Where Boys Fear to Tread” starts disc two on a heavier note, but is a pretty forgettable track overall. “Bodies”, on the other hand, is the first purely heavy track to be a banger since “Bullet with Butterfly Wings” This track rules, from Chamberlin’s driving ride cymbals to Corgan’s delivery of banger lines like “The empty bodies stand at rest/Casualties of their own flesh”. “Thirty-Three” slows things down with an acoustic guitar and a pretty melody that reminds me of “Disarm” from *Siamese Dream*. However, it kind of approaches a mall alt pop thing again but this time with an emo inflection and it doesn’t fully work for me. “In the Arms of Sleep” is an eerie, haunting country ballad with an otherworldly drown hanging over it and a downbeat vocal melody tying it all together. “1979” is an all-time great masterpiece. It combines simply one of the greatest guitar riffs ever with an anthemic chorus and cool interplay between drums and a drum machine. The instrumental evokes pure nostalgia before even getting to the lyrics. It’s basically a perfect song. I do not love the lo-fi effect on Corgan’s vocals on “Tales of a Scorched Earth”, which I think would have fit better on a Nine Inch Nails or even black metal song. “Thru the Eyes of Ruby” is disc 2’s answer to “Porcelina of the Vast Oceans”, a long, spacey, and well-paced track with multiple good sections. There’s not a lot to “Stumbleine” but, I don’t know, I kinda like it. It’s catchy, man. “X.Y.U.” is the king of all non-single heavy tracks. Chamberlin is an absolute master of his craft, guiding the songs tempo from swampy sludge metal through an accelerando into blistering high speeds, all while Corgan channels his inner Black Francis. “We Only Come Out at Night”, just a super annoying vocal melody over instrumentation that’s somehow simplistic yet jarring. It also kicks off a five track run of slow songs to end the album that becomes a total slog. “Beautiful” has a weird R&B or hip hop inflection, but it’s overall meh. I like the theatrical, show tune quality to “Lily (My One and Only)”, but I just want more action here and it’s soooooo slooooooow. “By Starlight” is easily the best of these late slow songs, adding distant guitars and a sinister vocal melody to at least keep things simmering. “Farewell and Goodnight” closes the album fittingly, though I can’t help but feel that it would sound better if it came after a scorching rocker. Having all of the members sing was a nice touch, especially given how bad Wretzky is at singing. I haven’t heard a genuinely u skilled vocalist in a while and it was refreshing.

Oh man, can’t believe I’m finally done with this. That took forever. I’m glad I’ve finally unlocked some great songs that I haven’t been able to listen to in over a year. I’m putting it by both Television and Violent Femmes as those were also albums with at least one track that contends for greatest song ever, while the other tracks were decent to good.

Favorite tracks: Tonight, Tonight, Jellybelly, Here Is No Why, Bullet with Butterfly Wings, To Forgive, Cupid de Locke, Galapogos, Porcelina of the Vast Oceans, Bodies, In the Arms of Sleep, 1979, Thru the Eyes of Ruby, Stumbleine, X.Y.U., By Starlight

Least favorite track: We Only Come Out at Night
