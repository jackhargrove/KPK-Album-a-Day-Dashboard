# Hargrove's review

Another artist my dad looooooooves, although this time I have a lot more personal familiarity than I did with Neil Young. “There Is a Light that Never Goes Out” went fucking HARD for teenage me (and it still goes fucking HARD for 24 year old me). I have had various phases with The Smiths over the years where I binge their singles. However, I had never actually listened to their debut. Morrissey is obviously super distinctive and interesting, but Johnny Marr’s guitar has always been the main draw for me of The Smiths. He really does it like no other. I’ve heard the word “shimmering” used to describe his guitar work, and it’s really the only adjective that does it any justice. It sounds the way that a ray of light hitting a stained glass window looks. I find it just gorgeous. I don’t even know how it’s possible to make a guitar sound the way it does on a song like “How Soon Is Now?”. On *The Smiths*, you just get eleven tracks of pretty standard, if unpolished, Smiths songs. It’s remarkable how consistent they sounded across their entire career, although to be fair I guess they did only exist for 5 years. There are some very good songs (“This Charming Man”, “Hand In Glove”, “What Difference Does It Make?”), some not great songs (“Miserable Lie”, “The Hand that Rocks the Cradle”, “I Don’t Owe You Anything”), and several in between. But even with the variance in the quality of songwriting, every song is undeniably Smith-y (Smithsonian?), and that’s a style that’s always going to sound good to me in some way. I will say, the production on this album is a major problem. I’m down for lo-fi, but I think it impedes the quality of just about every song, even fully destroying “Miserable Lie” for me. I also would’ve liked to see more of an arc across the album. The Smiths are notorious for their messy, disorganized discography, and it sometimes feels like they just threw random songs onto the studio albums (even *The Queen Is Dead* tbh). These flaws do make *The Smiths* somewhat of an unsatisfying listen. But how can you criticize an album with “Hand on Glove”?

“Reel Around the Fountain” is a pretty decent opener. It’s kind of laid back and even “sunny”, as far as The Smiths go. I know the lyrics were a bit controversial but I think they’re sweet and heartfelt, and Marr’s guitar is tastefully restrained. “You’ve Got Everything Now” is also quite good, it’s punky and upbeat. It has a fantastic bass line and I love Morrissey’s vocal melody on the chorus. I think it’s cool how the lyrics are genuinely ambiguous; when Morrissey says “So who is rich and who is poor?”, I don’t think even he has an inkling of the right answer to that question. “Miserable Lie” unfortunately sucks. The opening is too low-key, then the double time starts too soon, and then the double time section is too abrasive with Morrissey’s falsetto vocalizations. I think this one was really hurt by the production struggles. The drums are WAY too high in the mix and the cymbals hurt my right ear. “Pretty Girls Make Graves” is fine, not quite one of my favorites but I do like Morrissey’s falsetto much better here. I read that “The Hand that Rocks the Cradle” was the first song Morrissey and Marr wrote together and it really shows. It just sounds like Morrissey dispassionately reading a poem. The vocals don’t feel musical at all and the lyrics are bland, Definitely my least favorite track. “This Charming Man” picks things back up again though. The story is obviously great, but it’s really made by that bouncy bass and Marr’s stunning guitar work. “Still Ill” has another great guitar line from Marr, I don’t quite know what Morrissey is yapping about here but the music is gorgeous. “Hand in Glove” is my favorite track on the album by a wide margin. The harmonica bit is just SO good. Morrissey also kills it on the vocal delivery, it’s theatrical in the best way. “What Difference Does It Make?” is also a banger. Just a very good song. “I Don’t Owe You Anything” sucks. The lyrics creep me out and it’s too slow. “Suffer Little Children” has some really nice moments and is genuinely moving, but it’s dragged out too long for my taste.

Is this consistently better than the numerous Smiths singles you’ve heard? No. Is it better than *The Queen Is Dead*? No. Is it worth your time if you like The Smiths? Yes, very much so. It’s got some bangers and it’s pleasant for almost all of its run time. It’s a little confused, a little muddled, a little raw (derogatory), but man does it have energy and heart.

Favorite tracks: Reel Around the Fountain, You’ve Got Everything Now, This Charming Man, Still Ill, Hand in Glove, What Difference Does It Make?

Least favorite track: The Hand That Rocks the Cradle
