# Write-up

The Stone Roses formed in Manchester or "Madchester" as the scene that grew up around them would come to be known. In 1980, four boys from Altrincham Grammar School for boys formed a band, the Patrol. The band was short lived, but later in 1983, the four members reunited under the name the Fireside Chaps (and later the Waterfront). Ian Brown sung, John Squire played guitar, Andy Couzens did both and Simon Wolstencroft played drums. To round out the lineup, they brought on Gary "Mani" Mounfield on bass. This band also broke up. Wolstencroft left to play with another Manchester band called freak party. When the band's other two members, Andy Rourke and Johnny Marr decided to bring in a cocky kid who went by Morrissey, Wolstencroft quit. Lucky for him, Couzens was putting the band back together (possibly on a mission from God). This iteration replaced Mani with Pete Garner and they called themselves the Stone Roses. Soon enough, Wolstencroft was out the door again, never to return and Alan "Reni" Wren was brought in to replace him. After missing out on playing with two of the biggest acts in Britain, Wolstencroft managed to wind up being one of the more long term members of The Fall (the lead singer, Mark E. Smith ran through some 60 members).

The band started to make waves when they backed Pete Townsend after sending him a demo. Within two months, they were signed and headlining magazines as the next big thing. Their first go at an album fell flat and was never released. Their rise to fame began to feel too slow for the band, who in response spray painted the band's name across Manchester. The band continued to play without putting out an album for 4 years, as their sound kept changing. During this time, Brown and Squire decided that as songwriters, they deserved a bigger share of the profits, which caused Couzens and Reni to leave the band. Both rejoined, but later on, Couzens and Garner would leave for other reasons. To replace them, they brought on their old friend Mani and the core lineup of the Stone Roses was set. The band began finding real success, including among their fans Liam and Noel Gallagher, who would be inspired to form Oasis.

Finally, the Stone Roses got into the studio. The band didn't really like the resulting self-titled album, saying it wasn't "fat or hard enough" but the public adored it. One journalist wrote that "The Stone Roses" was "simply the best debut LP I've heard in my record-buying lifetime. Forget everybody else. Forget work tomorrow". "Madchester" became the place to be as the Roses and their imitators created a scene centered around the club The Haçienda, which was owned by Factory Records and New Order. House music played a big part in the scene, as did ecstasy, which was was extremely cheap thanks to it being produced legally en masse in America until its scheduling in 1985. The house and rock music eventually merged to produce "baggy", of which "The Stone Roses" is the most famous album. The name of the genre came from the fashion that went with it, soccer jersey, "baggy" jeans, tie dye and bucket hats.

The year after the release of their debut, in the midst of a label dispute, the band decided to throw paint all over the office and employees of their label, earning fines, notoriety and a hearty congradualtions from Black Sabbath. As a result of the ensuing breakup with their label, the band was unable to put out an album until December of 1994. By then, the Madchester scene had died down. Despite spending a year of 10 hour days working on the album, the result was greeted with mixed reactions. Reni was the first to leave in May of 95, then Squire in April of 96, who broke his collarbone while mountain biking. The band struggled on for another half year as their performances grew worse and worse before calling it quits in 1996. The band reunited in 2011 before breaking up again in 2017. Mani died on November 20th of this year.

# Hargrove's review

This was an album I’ve been excited for because a major musical theme for me this year has been embracing more of the pre-90s jangle pop and alt rock that I used to shun to bug my parents. This was an album I had not heard at all, but that often gets ranked highly in the categories explained above. I’ve also gotten much more knowledgeable about electronic and dance genres this year, so I’ve been dying to hear what rock’s response to acid house. I had high expectation, which usually means I’ll either love the album or feel disappointed. Weirdly, though, I’m somewhere in between with *The Stone Roses*. “I Wanna Be Adored” and “I Am the Resurrection” both exceeded my expectations. I think both are brilliant. The rest of it though was neither incredible nor disappointing. “She Bangs the Drum” and “Made of Stone” are decent jangle pop bangers that will go on my playlists, but neither has reinvigorated a new love for the genre either. “Bye Bye Badman” and “(Song for My) Sugar Spun Sister” are both decent Beatles pastiche that make for great album tracks but without particularly remarkable songwriting. “Waterfall” and “This Is the One” are two of the better middle-album tracks  and also the two that feel least indebted to their influences. I’ll elaborate on the songs in the next paragraph, but overall I would describe the songwriting as “decent”, some really good, most just above average. The album itself is unfortunately not very well paced. The first three tracks are all bangers, and starting with “Made of Stone” the final run of this album works well, but the mid-section is a real, noticeable slump. “Don’t Stop” is some serious bullshit; reversing a song would have been a fun one minute interlude, but doing the whole five minutes is super annoying. It’s followed by two of the less interesting songs and a throwaway interlude, nearly 15 minutes of meh. *The Stone Roses* didn’t revolutionize my understanding of 80s alt rock, but it also gave me a few great songs to listen to and add to my DJ playlists. It’s an unexpected outcome, but I’m okay with it.

“I Wanna Be Adored” is just a ridiculous song. If there were an album with 45 minutes of this quality, it would be a contender for greatest album of all time. The slow fade-in is so perfect for the opening track of the debut album of a band who would never release anything further of note. The bass line lumbers in from the primordial ooze, readying you for one great ride. The guitar melody is gorgeous, the lyrics are simple but powerful, and I love the vocal up-tilt on “I wanna be adOOOoored”. This is a top tier alternative rock song. “She Bangs the Drum” shifts gears to jangle pop, and it’s quite good. However, it’s noticeable that the previous track was near the pinnacle of its genre, while this one is just pretty good. “Waterfall” is about as good as “She Bangs the Drums”, but it shifts once again into psychedelic rock territory. I like the outro a lot. “Don’t Stop” is bullshit, it just makes the album worse for only a little bit of personality in exchange. “Bye Bye Badman” shifts the sound into the Beatlesque direction that will define the second half of the album. It’s nice and upbeat but doesn’t do much to distinguish itself as a song. It is elite indie rock filler, though. “Elizabeth My Dear” is fine, much better interlude than “Don’t Stop”. I have more or less the exact same things to say about “(Song for My) Sugar Spun Sister” as I did for “Bye Bye Badman”. “Made of Stone” finally piques my interest again after the slump in the middle. It takes that Beatles feel but actually uses it for a Beatles-quality song. That chorus melody is just lovely, as are the jangling guitars around it. “Shoot You Down” is similar to “Bye Bye Badman” and “(Song for My) Sugar Spun Sister”, but strictly better than both. Weirdly, it reminds me of two Velvet Underground songs: the verse melody from “All Tomorrow’s Parties” and the chord change from “What Goes On” into the chorus. “This Is the One” has stellar songwriting, making it one of the best tracks. The production is unfortunately super muddy, but the anthemic chorus and the build both shine through. “I Am the Resurrection” starts with a Beatles vocal melody and ends with a five minute groovy instrumental breakdown. What more can you want out of a song? It’s a great track, particularly the vocal climax on the chorus and Reni’s drums throughout.

I just feel like I’m missing something. I’ll take the good tracks for now, but I hope someday to have a breakthrough where I see the album’s personality in the same way that so many others do. For now, I’ll just keep listening to “I Wanna Be Adored”, “I Am the Resurrection”, and “Fool’s Gold”.

Favorite tracks: I Wanna Be Adored, She Bangs the Drum, Waterfall, Made of Stone, This Is the One, I Am the Resurrection

Least favorite track: Don’t Stop

# Aidan's review

This album is a bit weird in that it is bookended by two songs that outshine anything in between them by miles. I Wanna Be Adored is a perfect song, and I Am The Resurrection is nearly as good until the second half, in which it turns into an awkward jam of a coda. Still, amidst the brightness of the two supernovas, there are pinpoint of light that are worth admiring. She Bangs the Drums contains my favorite lyrics from the album, "the past was yours but the future's mine". The instrumental section two thirds of the way through Waterfall, the melody of Bye Bye Bad Man right before the titular chorus, and the spaced out anthem of This Is The One are all worth listening to. My main issue with this band is that their lyrics and melodies are a weakness in comparison to their tremendous jangle-y wall of sound that makes every song sound pretty good. They only manage to round it out with the actual bones of a song a few times on the album, but when they do, it's so magical. It's such a fun album, the pairing of the satanism of the opener and the singer as Jesus on the closer is a nice touch, and the completely unironic egotism that probably sounds great on Molly just works to create an album I can listen to forever.
