# Hargrove's review

This was an album I’ve been excited for because a major musical theme for me this year has been embracing more of the pre-90s jangle pop and alt rock that I used to shun to bug my parents. This was an album I had not heard at all, but that often gets ranked highly in the categories explained above. I’ve also gotten much more knowledgeable about electronic and dance genres this year, so I’ve been dying to hear what rock’s response to acid house. I had high expectation, which usually means I’ll either love the album or feel disappointed. Weirdly, though, I’m somewhere in between with *The Stone Roses*. “I Wanna Be Adored” and “I Am the Resurrection” both exceeded my expectations. I think both are brilliant. The rest of it though was neither incredible nor disappointing. “She Bangs the Drum” and “Made of Stone” are decent jangle pop bangers that will go on my playlists, but neither has reinvigorated a new love for the genre either. “Bye Bye Badman” and “(Song for My) Sugar Spun Sister” are both decent Beatles pastiche that make for great album tracks but without particularly remarkable songwriting. “Waterfall” and “This Is the One” are two of the better middle-album tracks  and also the two that feel least indebted to their influences. I’ll elaborate on the songs in the next paragraph, but overall I would describe the songwriting as “decent”, some really good, most just above average. The album itself is unfortunately not very well paced. The first three tracks are all bangers, and starting with “Made of Stone” the final run of this album works well, but the mid-section is a real, noticeable slump. “Don’t Stop” is some serious bullshit; reversing a song would have been a fun one minute interlude, but doing the whole five minutes is super annoying. It’s followed by two of the less interesting songs and a throwaway interlude, nearly 15 minutes of meh. *The Stone Roses* didn’t revolutionize my understanding of 80s alt rock, but it also gave me a few great songs to listen to and add to my DJ playlists. It’s an unexpected outcome, but I’m okay with it.

“I Wanna Be Adored” is just a ridiculous song. If there were an album with 45 minutes of this quality, it would be a contender for greatest album of all time. The slow fade-in is so perfect for the opening track of the debut album of a band who would never release anything further of note. The bass line lumbers in from the primordial ooze, readying you for one great ride. The guitar melody is gorgeous, the lyrics are simple but powerful, and I love the vocal up-tilt on “I wanna be adOOOoored”. This is a top tier alternative rock song. “She Bangs the Drum” shifts gears to jangle pop, and it’s quite good. However, it’s noticeable that the previous track was near the pinnacle of its genre, while this one is just pretty good. “Waterfall” is about as good as “She Bangs the Drums”, but it shifts once again into psychedelic rock territory. I like the outro a lot. “Don’t Stop” is bullshit, it just makes the album worse for only a little bit of personality in exchange. “Bye Bye Badman” shifts the sound into the Beatlesque direction that will define the second half of the album. It’s nice and upbeat but doesn’t do much to distinguish itself as a song. It is elite indie rock filler, though. “Elizabeth My Dear” is fine, much better interlude than “Don’t Stop”. I have more or less the exact same things to say about “(Song for My) Sugar Spun Sister” as I did for “Bye Bye Badman”. “Made of Stone” finally piques my interest again after the slump in the middle. It takes that Beatles feel but actually uses it for a Beatles-quality song. That chorus melody is just lovely, as are the jangling guitars around it. “Shoot You Down” is similar to “Bye Bye Badman” and “(Song for My) Sugar Spun Sister”, but strictly better than both. Weirdly, it reminds me of two Velvet Underground songs: the verse melody from “All Tomorrow’s Parties” and the chord change from “What Goes On” into the chorus. “This Is the One” has stellar songwriting, making it one of the best tracks. The production is unfortunately super muddy, but the anthemic chorus and the build both shine through. “I Am the Resurrection” starts with a Beatles vocal melody and ends with a five minute groovy instrumental breakdown. What more can you want out of a song? It’s a great track, particularly the vocal climax on the chorus and Reni’s drums throughout.

I just feel like I’m missing something. I’ll take the good tracks for now, but I hope someday to have a breakthrough where I see the album’s personality in the same way that so many others do. For now, I’ll just keep listening to “I Wanna Be Adored”, “I Am the Resurrection”, and “Fool’s Gold”.

Favorite tracks: I Wanna Be Adored, She Bangs the Drum, Waterfall, Made of Stone, This Is the One, I Am the Resurrection

Least favorite track: Don’t Stop
