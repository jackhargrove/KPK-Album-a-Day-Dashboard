# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

Last time we took a break, we came back to what is still my least favorite album we’ve listened to thus far, The Pop Group’s *Y*. Dogshit album. This time, we get something way more fun. The Strokes are one of the first bands a teenager getting into indie gets directed to, so I first listened to this album in the summer of 2017 when I was really getting into music. I recognized both “Last Nite” and “Someday”, added them both to my playlists after a couple listens, and that was that. “Last Nite” is a fun banger, but I *really* like “Someday” and it’s grown into one of my favorite songs. But otherwise, I didn’t return to the album or listen to any Strokes stuff that came after. Both were on my basement playlist (Ale is a huge fan of “Last Nite”). Then, in Winterim 2022, I decided I wanted to give the full album another listen. I spent about a week straight just listening to *Is This It*, and I started to appreciate how consistently good it is. However, I’ve never considered it one of my favorite albums, nor the Strokes one of my favorite bands. But when I started listening to this again on Monday, I found myself floored by just how consistently good it is. I’ve always considered myself more of an Interpol guy than a Strokes guy, but when we listened to *Turn on the Bright Lights*, I found myself disappointed by some of the deep cuts that just don’t hold up like the singles. With *Is This It*, I’ve had the exact opposite experience. There is not a bad song on this album. It’s really remarkable. Maybe that’s a result of their simplicity, but every song here is a fun burst of energy and pop songwriting that runs exactly as long as it needs to and then stops. I could see this sound becoming same-y or repetitive, but they do such a good job at keeping things brief. It reminds me of *Doolittle* in that way. The guitar interplay is really well done, and I feel like I have a better appreciation for it after listening to Television. I think the decision to run Casablancas’ voice through a cheap amp was one of the greatest production decisions ever made, up there with the idea to record John Bonham’s drums from a stairwell on “When the Levee Breaks”. Casablancas just sounds so fucking cool. The staticky effect on his vocals complements how laid back he is so well, it’s just so good. I love it. Overall, I have very few complaints about this album. My biggest one is that I find the lyrics on many of the songs to be unrewarding when I dug into them. “Someday” has some great lines, but every other song reminded me a lot more of Tom Verlaine’s lyricism than Lou Reed’s, and I do not care for Verlaine’s lyrics. They just don’t hold up to scrutiny. Still, this is a tight, catchy, energetic, hipper than hip, and, most importantly, fun album. Everyone involved sounds like they’re having a great time. And even now, after thousands upon thousands of bands gave driven this sound into the ground in the intervening quarter century, it sounds so fresh and full of life. Everyone should listen to this album.

“Is This It” is a kind of unassuming opener, but I think it works really well. Starting with the most downbeat song makes all the excitement after feel like a successful quest for meaning. Casablancas sounds deeply resigned, but the guitar line is so expressive, and that chorus is catchy. “The Modern Age” is a fine song, but it’s surprisingly become my least favorite. I don’t know exactly why. I like the sunniness of the chorus quite a lot. But the verses just leave me cold. “Soma”, on the other hand, I really enjoy. I love the vocal melody in the verses, and i love the way the guitar in the left ear traces the vocals even more. The build into the outro is so explosive and cathartic. The delivery on “Well I ain’t listenin’ no more” is perfect. “Barely Legal” ups the ante once again. What a good track. The pre-chorus in particular is fantastic; I can’t get enough of the “Oh you ain’t never had nothing I wanted but/I want it all, I just can’t figure it out” part. I also love the delivery on “I just want to be your slave”. “Someday” is by far the greatest song the Strokes have ever made and one of the best pop songs from this century. I have listened to it regularly for a decade and I plan to for many decades more. It’s just so beautiful. The drum beat is crazy catchy, I find myself tapping it with my fingers all the time. The guitar is sunny sunny sunny. It has so many good lines, from “Maya says I’m lacking in depth/I will do my best” to “I see alone we stand, together we fall apart” to “I’m working so I won’t have to try so hard”. It’s the exact perfect pop song length and structure. It evokes the bittersweet feelings of nostalgia more than just about any song I know. A perfect track. “Alone, Together” goes in a more Interpol direction with some of the best guitar interplay on the album. And it has a great explosion in the outro too. Another strong track. “Last Nite” is just a banger. Not much more I can say. I had this in my basement playlist for good reason. It captures everything that makes this album so fun.

I acknowledge that “Hard to Explain” is a well-written song, but for some reason it just doesn’t click with me. When I’m listening to it it’s fine, but I never look forward to hearing it or play it in my head. The highlight is definitely the half-spoken chorus. “New York City Cops” has a direct quality to it that you don’t get on many of the other songs. It’s some straightforward punk, and it is fun. It has a hysterical chorus as well. I view the international release as the real version on this album, but I’ll touch on “When It Started” for completion’s sake. It doesn’t flow as well as “New York City Cops”, but it’s actually pretty good. I really like the weird baroque chord structure in the chorus. It’s restrained and almost smoldering. I feel like at this point in the album things should start slowing down, there should be some filler, but no. This follows through right until the end, “Trying Your Luck” has my favorite bass line on the entire album. I also love the rapid and dissonant guitar line that starts after “this life is on my side” in the chorus. It sounds like it’s firing laser beams. “Take It or Leave It” has not one but two catchy choruses to close things out. It’s a quite good song, but my one complaint I guess is that it doesn’t really distinguish itself as a closer, really. The album ends as quickly as it began, out of nothingness and back into nothingness. I wouldn’t have minded a bit more of an arc to it.

“Someday” notwithstanding, The Stroke’s have a limited ceiling in my estimation just because they don’t perfectly fit my preferred aesthetic in the way that my favorite artists of all time do. But I am honestly flabbergasted by the consistency here. So many good deep cuts. No bad tracks. And everything is so fun. I need to revisit this far more often.

Favorite tracks: Is This It, Soma, Barely Legal, Someday, Alone, Together, Last Nite, New York City Cops, Trying Your Luck, Take It or Leave It

Least favorite track: The Modern Age
