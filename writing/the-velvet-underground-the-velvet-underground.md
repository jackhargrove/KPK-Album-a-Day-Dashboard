# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

I’ve been looking forward to this one because of how much I enjoyed the last two, and also because I was curious of what the Velvet Underground pop record sounded like. The conclusion: it was pretty good. I did like it, but I feel comfortable saying *White Light/White Heat* > *The Velvet Underground & Nico* > *The Velvet Underground*. They’re remarkably consistent though. This album has some insanely high highs. “Candy Says” and “After Hours” are two of the most earnestly, unpretentiously beautiful songs I’ve ever heard in my life, which was unexpected going in. “What Goes On” is a classic Velvets rocker. “The Murder Mystery” keeps their experimentalism alive. And “Jesus” gets me for some reason, I don’t know why but I like it. That’s just half the album though. While none of it is bad, the rest get a little bland to me. A lot of those songs pool together in the middle of the album too, so there are times where I have to fight to keep my attention on the album. It’s high highs and medium lows, but they’re evenly distributed across the album.

“Candy Says” alone is reason to call this a good album. What an excellent, excellent, excellent, excellent, excellent track. Five “excellent”s. I mean it when it says it makes me sick to my stomach. Candy Darling is a fascinating figure, and this is such a tender and sympathetic portrait of her painted by someone who knew her well. Yule proves his worth to the Velvets with the aching beauty of his vocals alone. I have never heard a song this soft feel so immensely powerful. “Candy says, ‘I’ve come to hate my body’” breaks my heart without fail every time. Fuck, man. “What Goes On” feels like a perfect progression from the music they were making on their debut. They’ve gotten so good with drones that this rock track has an organ playing three extended chords the entire time and it feels like the track couldn’t exist without it. The chords are amazing, especially the one that begins the chorus. The guitar solo is also a highlight. “Some Kinda Love is only okay. The lyrics are interesting, but musically it’s a bit bare. I know that “Pale Blue Eyes” is the most popular track by the Velvets, and I had high expectations going in. Unfortunately, though I see the appeal, it just doesn’t do it for me. It’s a pretty track, but I think the issue is that “Candy Says” and “After Hours” are just so much more beautiful that it doesn’t leave a niche for “Pale Blue Eyes”. I also think it goes on a bit long. “Jesus” though is awesome. It feels so funny to hear anyone from this band appealing to Christianity. The twangy guitars are fantastic, the build within the verses is executed perfectly, and I love the quasi-yodeling in Reed’s vocals.

“Beginning to See the Light” is middle-of-the-pack. It almost sounds like it could have been on *White Light/White Heat*, but it lacks the edge that made that album so good. The best part of the song is the shorter, quieter sections where Reed’s voice suddenly becomes serious. “I’m Set Free” has a good melody on the last line of every chorus, but otherwise it’s insignificant. It’s my least favorite. “That’s the Story of My Life” sounds a bit hokey (how could it not with that title) in the instrumental, but it balances it out with the nihilism of the lyrics. It’s more basic than I’d like, but I can’t hate it. It’s fine. “The Murder Mystery” is a mess, but a beautiful mess. It wouldn’t be a Velvets album without something completely insane. I was yearning for the batshit by the time I got to the end of this album, and I got it. I think what makes it work is that there are some moments of genuine beauty, particularly the sung Tucker/Yule sections, that provide some respite. The keyboard work is great, it’s grimy and reminds me of The Doors. It’s a cool experiment and I’m glad it exists. “After Hours” ends the album on the same note of unbelievable beauty as it began. Tucker’s vocals are unironically a revelation. She sounds like the angel of a dead child. It’s also fitting that the solo lead for the drummer doesn’t have drums; instead, the song is complemented perfectly by just a soft guitar and bass. So fucking pretty, just an excellent closer.

Good album. Remarkable discography. I’m gonna go listen to “Candy Says”.

Favorite tracks: Candy Says, What Goes On, Jesus, The Murder Mystery, After Hours

Least favorite track: I’m Set Free
