# Write-up

We left off with the Velvets with the transgressive White Light/White Heat, which was even worse of a commercial failure than their debut. John Cale and Lou Reed were the two main creative drivers of the band, and while the tethering of Cale's experimentation to Reed's pop acumen would eventually produce critical acclaim, it wasn't selling records. Reed told Moe Tucker and Sterling Morrison that they had to kick out Cale or dissolve the whole band, and they agreed to kick out Cale. It fell to Morrison to tell Cale. To replace Cale, Reed brought in Doug Yule, who he met due to Yule renting a place from their road manager. Yule was brought in to fill the gaping hole that the multi-instrumental Cale has left and did an admirable job, playing bass and organ in addition to singing on "Candy Says". Their eponymous third album was entirely written Reed, as he jettisoned the experimentalism of Cale in favor of a laid back folk rock sound, with the notable exception of Murder Mystery. In contrast with the acrimony of their previous two album recordings, the process went smoothly beyond an alleged theft of instruments at JFK Airport. The album cover photo is a picture of the band taken by Reed's close personal friend Billy Name. The album has two mixes, one called the "closet mix" done by Reed in which the vocals are emphasized and the other more widely spread mix with a more balanced tone. As far as I can tell, the latter is the one on streaming services, but the difference is not significant to my ears. In contrast to the critical distain found by the previous two albums, their third effort was received well. Dartmouth alumnus and "Dead of American Rock Critics" Robert Christgau called it their best album while superfan Lester Bangs thought that it was good, although more suited for the casual fans.

Despite that, the album still did not sell, due to limited promotion and the lack of a successful single. MGM dropped the band after their third commercial failure, leaving them to sign with an Atlantic Records subsidy for their fourth record, "Loaded" (named such because it was intended to be "loaded" with hits). Reed hated that mix too (third time for those keeping track) and disliked the initial marketing of the album featuring Yule prominently and left the band as a result. Loaded was the band's most successful album, selling a quarter of a million copies. After Reed left, Morrison and Tucker followed, leaving the new addition Yule as the sole member of the band. He put out an album, Squeeze under the Velvet Underground name which has been widely ridiculed as an attempt to trade off the name of the band. In recent years, it's been reevaluated towards a more generally positive direction. When the band reunited in 72, Morrison pushed for Yule to be part of it, but Cale and Reed shot it down.

Where are they now? Lou Reed, Nico and John Cale all had acclaimed solo careers. Sterling Morrison taught Medival Literature at UT Austin, then worked as a tugboat captain. Moe Tucker started a family and had her own band which Morrison occasionally played in. She's allegedly very into QAnon now, although I haven't found any first hand info besides her Tea Party support. Doug Yule bounced around a few bands and played on two of Reed's albums before settling down in San Francisco to make violins and cabinets. Nico (real name Christa Päffgen) died in 1988 in a cycling accident. Morrison died of cancer in 1995. Reed died of liver disease in 2013. Cale and Tucker are still kicking as of this write-up.

# Hargrove's review

I’ve been looking forward to this one because of how much I enjoyed the last two, and also because I was curious of what the Velvet Underground pop record sounded like. The conclusion: it was pretty good. I did like it, but I feel comfortable saying *White Light/White Heat* > *The Velvet Underground & Nico* > *The Velvet Underground*. They’re remarkably consistent though. This album has some insanely high highs. “Candy Says” and “After Hours” are two of the most earnestly, unpretentiously beautiful songs I’ve ever heard in my life, which was unexpected going in. “What Goes On” is a classic Velvets rocker. “The Murder Mystery” keeps their experimentalism alive. And “Jesus” gets me for some reason, I don’t know why but I like it. That’s just half the album though. While none of it is bad, the rest get a little bland to me. A lot of those songs pool together in the middle of the album too, so there are times where I have to fight to keep my attention on the album. It’s high highs and medium lows, but they’re evenly distributed across the album.

“Candy Says” alone is reason to call this a good album. What an excellent, excellent, excellent, excellent, excellent track. Five “excellent”s. I mean it when it says it makes me sick to my stomach. Candy Darling is a fascinating figure, and this is such a tender and sympathetic portrait of her painted by someone who knew her well. Yule proves his worth to the Velvets with the aching beauty of his vocals alone. I have never heard a song this soft feel so immensely powerful. “Candy says, ‘I’ve come to hate my body’” breaks my heart without fail every time. Fuck, man. “What Goes On” feels like a perfect progression from the music they were making on their debut. They’ve gotten so good with drones that this rock track has an organ playing three extended chords the entire time and it feels like the track couldn’t exist without it. The chords are amazing, especially the one that begins the chorus. The guitar solo is also a highlight. “Some Kinda Love is only okay. The lyrics are interesting, but musically it’s a bit bare. I know that “Pale Blue Eyes” is the most popular track by the Velvets, and I had high expectations going in. Unfortunately, though I see the appeal, it just doesn’t do it for me. It’s a pretty track, but I think the issue is that “Candy Says” and “After Hours” are just so much more beautiful that it doesn’t leave a niche for “Pale Blue Eyes”. I also think it goes on a bit long. “Jesus” though is awesome. It feels so funny to hear anyone from this band appealing to Christianity. The twangy guitars are fantastic, the build within the verses is executed perfectly, and I love the quasi-yodeling in Reed’s vocals.

“Beginning to See the Light” is middle-of-the-pack. It almost sounds like it could have been on *White Light/White Heat*, but it lacks the edge that made that album so good. The best part of the song is the shorter, quieter sections where Reed’s voice suddenly becomes serious. “I’m Set Free” has a good melody on the last line of every chorus, but otherwise it’s insignificant. It’s my least favorite. “That’s the Story of My Life” sounds a bit hokey (how could it not with that title) in the instrumental, but it balances it out with the nihilism of the lyrics. It’s more basic than I’d like, but I can’t hate it. It’s fine. “The Murder Mystery” is a mess, but a beautiful mess. It wouldn’t be a Velvets album without something completely insane. I was yearning for the batshit by the time I got to the end of this album, and I got it. I think what makes it work is that there are some moments of genuine beauty, particularly the sung Tucker/Yule sections, that provide some respite. The keyboard work is great, it’s grimy and reminds me of The Doors. It’s a cool experiment and I’m glad it exists. “After Hours” ends the album on the same note of unbelievable beauty as it began. Tucker’s vocals are unironically a revelation. She sounds like the angel of a dead child. It’s also fitting that the solo lead for the drummer doesn’t have drums; instead, the song is complemented perfectly by just a soft guitar and bass. So fucking pretty, just an excellent closer.

Good album. Remarkable discography. I’m gonna go listen to “Candy Says”.

Favorite tracks: Candy Says, What Goes On, Jesus, The Murder Mystery, After Hours

Least favorite track: I’m Set Free

# Aidan's review

Don't believe the name on the cover, this is a Lou Reed album. A pretty good one at that, but it lacks most of the transgression and edge that I liked on the first two VU albums. Candy Says is an incredibly touching song, and while I hope that whatever TikTok that got Pale Blue Eyes to 170,000 streams wasn't racist, it's still a pretty good song. After Hours and Murder Mystery are ghosts of what the Velvets used to be, with the former forerunning indie twee by nearly 40 years and the latter pushing the boundaries of cacophony as much as anything from White Light/White Heat. The remaining songs are professional, but Cale's absence is sorely missed as the band falls into the same sort of trotting rhythm on song after song. It's black and white from the sheer variety that the band previously brought to the table. For the most part, it still works, it's just different and unfortunately never matches the heights of the previous two albums.
