# Hargrove's review

I used to listen to “The Gift” a lot as a little kid (I don’t remember it being so explicitly sexual!) but other than that I hadn’t heard any song from this album. I was really excited to listen to it though because of how cool *The Velvet Underground & Nico* was and because of its reputation as their most out-there record. I ended up listening to this around 10 times over the last three days and…I’m still not sure if it’s good! What I do know, however, is that it absolutely kicks ass. It’s awesome how much creativity there is, how much fun they’re having, and the moments of sweetness that seep through the noise. Man is it a complete fucking mess though. By far my biggest gripe is the sequencing. I think “The Gift” is so cool, but putting it as the second song, immediately after how hype the opener is, really kills momentum. I get 2 minutes of “fuck yeah!” from “White Light/White Heat”, and then 10 minutes of John Cale deadpanning. Further, “Here She Comes Now” is sandwiched between two much better songs and buried deep in the middle of the record, which ends up making me forget it. My proposal is the following: “White Light/White Heat” -> “Here She Comes Now” -> “Lady Godiva’s Operation” -> “The Gift” -> “I Heard Her Call My Name” -> “Sister Ray”. Still not perfect, but as good as you’re gonna get with this set of songs. My other issue has to do with the songwriting. Only two of the tracks (“White Light/White Heat” and “Lady Godiva’s Operation”) feel like fully-formed song ideas. “The Gift” is two ideas stapled together, “Here She Comes Now” is incredibly simple, “I Heard Her Call My Name” exists for the improvised guitar solos, and “Sister Ray” is “Sister Ray”. This is a little infuriating just because songs like “Sunday Morning”, “Femme Fatale”, “All Tomorrow’s Parties”, and “Heroin” off their debut, as well as “Perfect Day” and “Walk on the Wild Side” from Reed’s solo career, show that he can write a damn good song, music that can be covered for eternity.

But still, there’s something God damn alluring about this album. I couldn’t stop listening to it, and I was excited every time. “White Light/White Heat” is a bolt of electricity straight down your spinal column with great sing-along potential. “The Gift” is an amazingly unique idea and the short story itself is amazingly well-written and paced, though the instrumental in the right ear leaves a little to be desired. “Lady Godiva’s Operation”, my favorite track, has a catchy-as-hell melody with a perfect vocal delivery from Cale. I especially love the back half, where he and Reed sloppily trade lines back and forth and they have those DIY sound effects. Great song. “Here She Comes Now” would be my least favorite were it not for the frustrating position of “The Gift”, but still it’s a nice simple song with a good chorus. The guitar solos in “I Heard Her Call My Name” are genuinely head-splitting, adapting jazz solos to rock in a way I didn’t think was possible. And “Sister Ray” is both blistering and laugh-out-loud funny (I’m sorry Carms for ruining it for you :/). Despite its length, I don’t tire of it, and the interplay between Reed’s guitar and Cale’s organ is something to behold. And the lyrics throughout are wonderful, I love Reed’s fascination with the bizarre and the degenerate. I’m on my final listen of “Sister Ray” as I’m writing this and I’m sad to move on. When I listen, I feel a considerable mix of enjoyment and irritation, though thankfully the former is stronger. I’m putting this right below their debut, as I imagine that will have more tracks I’ll want to return to, and right above *Surfer Rosa*, which I have similar but less intense feelings on. B Tier feels weird given how high the highs are, but it reflects these mixed emotions.

Favorite tracks: White Light/White Heat, Lady Godiva’s Operation, I Heard Her Call My Name, Sister Ray

Least favorite track: The Gift
