# Write-up

The Who is a British rock band formed in London in 1964 consisting of vocalist Roger Daltrey, guitarist and primary songwriter Pete Townshend, bassist and secondary songwriter John Entwistle, and drummer Keith Moon. It is one of the most successful rock groups of all time, providing a bridge between early British Invasion artists like the Beatles and the Rolling Stones with the hard rock and power pop musical directions of the 70s. Daltrey, Townshend, and Entwistle were all schoolmates who ended up in a band called the Detours together as teenagers in 1961. In 1964, they changed their name to the Who and recruited insane drummer Keith Moon. Their manager convinced them to present as members of the mod subculture popular with young men at the time; they would forever be linked to the aesthetic. At a performance that year, Townshend accidentally broke his guitar and decided to smash it to pieces, which became a staple of Who live sets and eventually an iconic symbol of rock and roll. They had their first hit single in 1965 with “I Can’t Explain” and quickly followed it up with the debut album *My Generation*. After a lackluster 1966 follow-up *A Quick One*, the Who realized the mod subculture was dying and performed at the seminal early hippie Monterey Pop Festival in California in 1967. While in the US, the members began destroying hotel rooms, with Moon using cherry bombs to explode hotel toilets. They released their third album, *The Who Sell Out*, in 1967. It’s their first to be well regarded and shifted to the freakbeat genre, a style that mixed psychedelic influences with Beatles-esque beat tunes and garage rock. Their next album, 1969’s *Tommy*, invented the rock opera. It chronicled the life of a deaf, dumb, and blind young boy in one continuous narrative. It was a smash success that made the Who one of the most popular bands in the world.

Seeking to replicate the success of *Tommy,* Townshend planned an even more elaborate sci-fi rock opera entitled *Lifehouse*. However, the project was too ambitious, driving Townshend to a nervous breakdown. The group instead repurposed it into 1971’s *Who’s Next*. Every song was written by Townshend for *Lifehouse* except for the Entwistle track “My Wife”. The group brought on former Beatles engineer Glyn Johns to produce, who encouraged a stripped-back, no-nonsense sound. The cover, an homage to *2001: A Space Odyssey*, features the band standing next to a junk heap onto which they have just urinated. It was one of the first rock albums to integrate synthesizers into its fundamental sound. The album was a huge commercial and critical success at the time and is regarded as the Who’s finest in retrospect.

# Hargrove's review

Outside of playing “Pinball Wizard” from *Tommy* on Rock Band 2 as a kid, and general familiarity with “My Generation”, I have never really listened to much from the Who. Upon first listen, I was surprised to learn that I had heard both “Baba O’Riley” and “Behind Blue Eyes” before numerous times. I would not have been able to name those songs or tell you who the artist was, but I recognized them immediately. I have a complicated relationship with classic rock; some of it is bloated, overwrought, and overrated, while some of it is the greatest shit I’ve ever heard in my life. *Who’s Next* constantly flits back and forth across this line, amazing me one moment and driving me bonkers the next. Keith Moon’s drumming was quite as mind-blowing as I had hoped, but it’s pretty damn good and consistently a highlight. It’s so fluid and unconventional without forgetting its place as a backbone. John Entwistle’s bass playing was even better, exceeding my expectations even after I had read about the praise he receives. If anything, critics undersell how dynamic and melodic his bass lines are. I also rather like the song he wrote, “My Wife”. I found Roger Daltrey’s vocals hit or miss. Sometimes he sounds like a cool gruff rock guy, other times he’s stretching his voice way too hard and sounds lame. My biggest gripe with this band is Pete Townshend. He sings probably a solid third of the vocals on this record, and I hate it every time. He sounds so limp dick and feeble when he sings. His guitar parts are not my favorite, particularly the overdramatic beats on “Baba O’Riley”. He wrote a few banger songs for this album, but others are only middling. And overall, his dramatic, theatrical sensibility takes away from my enjoyment. Oh well. While this album’s aesthetic does not especially appeal to me, some of the tracks are just undeniable. In particular, “Bargain”, “My Wife”, “Behind Blue Eyes”, and “Won’t Get Fooled Again” are all great. That’s a solid half of the run time. It’s a decent result from a classic rock album I had never heard before.

I do not like “Baba O’Riley” one bit. It insists upon itself. I do not like Townshend’s vocals on the “teenage wasteland” bridge. I do not like the lyrics that refer back to a story that no longer exists. I do not like the simple arena rock “duh, DUH DUH” guitar bit. I do not like this song (the synth is kind of cool, I have to admit). Given this, I was pleasantly surprised by how much I liked “Bargain”. The lyrics are fantastic; ““To win you, I'd stand naked, stoned and stabbed/I’d call that a bargain/The best I ever had/The best I ever had” is just great writing. Daltrey growls them in a way I find most pleasant, Entwistle plays a hyperactive bass line, and Moon plays the best drums on the album in the outro. Townshend’s vocal section blows, but otherwise this is a great hard rocker. The lyrics of “Love Ain’t for Keeping” contain some nice imagery, but this nice breezy track is not terribly impressive overall. I really like “My Wife”. Entwistle’s vocals are great, providing a nice break from and contrast to Daltrey and Townshend. The lyrics crack me up, and the song is a fun listen. After three great lyrical tracks in a row, “The Song Is Over” returns to the annoyingness of “Baba O’Riley”. The music follows, with an arena rock chorus that drives me nuts. The more *Lifehouse* the songs sound, the worse they are. I do not care for the intro of “Getting in Tune” the rest of the track improves, but still overly theatrical for me. “Going Mobile” isn’t a particularly well-written song, but it actually has a few things going for it. Entwistle sounds fantastic on the bass, and the it has one of the best synth parts on the album. It’s alright. I really like “Behind Blue Eyes”. I like the edgy villain lyrics, like “But my dreams, they aren't as empty/As my conscience seems to be/I have hours, only lonely/My love is vengeance that's never free”. I like the super catchy melody. I like how the song changes 2/3rds of the through as the drums come in. I like the cathartic climax. It’s a classic as far as I’m concerned. Weintraub told me that “Won’t Get Fooled Again” might be the best rock song he’s ever heard, and though I don’t agree I can absolutely see and respect it. It’s a stellar hard rocker in the vein of “Bargain” that’s epic without going over the top. I love these anti-radical politics songs from big 60s groups that could only be the result of being forced to spend too much time around annoying hippies. Another example is the Beatles’ “Revolution”. But seriously, the lyrics are great: I'll tip my hat to the new Constitution/Take a bow for the new revolution/Smile and grin at the change all around”, “For I know that the hypnotized never lie”, and “Meet the new boss/Same as the old boss” are great lines.

Because of the Who’s ambition and commitment to their sound, I pretty much either really like or really dislike every song on here. There’s very little middle ground. When something pushes right up against the edge it’s great, but the second it goes over the edge it’s awful. There are three other albums on this list that fall into this same category of “classic rock album with a few amazingly good tracks and a few tracks I can’t stand”: *Rumours*, *Led Zeppelin IV*, and *Exile on Main St.* They’re all ranked in generally the same place, so I’ll stick this album there too, below Fleetwood Mac and Zep but above the Stones.

Favorite tracks: Bargain, My Wife, Behind Blue Eyes, Won’t Get Fooled Again

Least favorite track: Baba O’Riley
