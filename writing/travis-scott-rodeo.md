# Write-up

Travis Scott (real name Jacques Webster II) is a rapper and producer from Houston, Texas. He takes his stage name from his favorite uncle Travis and from the real first name of his hero, Kid Cudi (Scott Mescudi). As a small child, he lived with his grandmother in a very bad neighborhood, but he spent his adolescence in a more middle class area of Houston with his parents. He attended University of Texas San Antonio for a year before dropping out to pursue music. He began making music while in college, and moved to NYC in 2012 to pursue a rap career. He didn’t like the scene in NY though, so after 4 months he moved to LA. This began a tumultuous period of his life where his parents cut him off, he moved back in with them in Houston, they kicked him out of the house, and he moved back to LA. Somehow, his music made into the hands of rapper T.I., who would help him get his big break. Scott put together his first mixtape in 2012, which was eventually remade by his new mentor Kanye West. Later that year, Scott signed to Kanye’s label GOOD Music. He was a member of the XXL Freshman Class of 2013 and performed at South by Southwest in 2014. He generated a lot of buzz through internet singles, and released his second mixtape *Days Before Rodeo* in 2014. This mixtape garnered a lot of hype, but critics were not yet impressed.

Scott released his debut album *Rodeo* in September 2015. The album showcases his atmosphere and feeling based music, prioritizing unique soundscapes in the production over verses and lyricism. Scott himself has claimed that he does not consider himself to make hip hop music, instead aiming for an ambient and psychedelic sound. His listed influences include artsy musicians like Björk and Thom Yorke. However, the music clearly does belong to a hip hop tradition sonically. The beat style is quintessential trap, a Southern genre that was about to peak in popularity and would define the 2010s, in no small part thanks to Scott. The album has many featured artists, but the only ones to appear elsewhere on our list are Kanye West and Juicy J. The album received decent but not rave reviews from legacy publications; hip hop poptimism hadn’t quite broken through there yet. However, he was hailed as the next big thing by internet music nerds on 4chan, RYM, and by Anthony Fantano. Many were excited to see where his music would go next.

In the 10 years since *Rodeo* released, Scott has become one of the biggest stars in the world. He’s released number 1 albums, has multiple songs with over 1 billion streams, and even has two kids with Kylie Jenner. His greatest critical success since *Rodeo* has been his third album *Astroworld* in 2018. However, as he’s leaned more and more into the commercial superstardom, some of the people who originally championed him have been disappointed. He’s found a niche where he can churn out hit song after hit song and all he had to do was sacrifice the experimentation that defined his early career. Who can blame him? He’s also had quite a few controversies that have accompanied his rise to fame. Most notably, 10 people died in a tragic crowd crush at his Astroworld festival in 2021. He’s also been arrested for bad behavior numerous times. Still, he’s on top of the world and one of the most significant players in the hip hop game today.

# Hargrove's review

This is a fun album. Travis Scott makes fun music. I got to talk about it a bit last week with Three 6 Mafia, but I really like Southern hip hop. I think there’s often a greater focus on feeling that produces really interesting atmospheres. I also appreciate how well the genre mixes partying with darkness. I think trap is a great genre, even if its 2010s explosion meant not all of it was great. All of that describes what Scott does well on *Rodeo*. There is a *heavy* focus on feelings, vibes, and atmospheres. Travis is not a good lyricist, he’s not a particularly impressive rapper, and he’s really not even much of a songwriter. As his career went on, this became a serious issue; he’s been making the same songs but with more and more vapidity with each passing album. But on *Rodeo*, he uses this weakness to his advantage, creating all-consuming landscapes of hip hop. He also brought an air of experimentalism to a genre that was (and is) at risk of becoming stagnant. Every song is both party and gloom, often implying that the former causes the latter no matter how braggadocios the lyrics are. There are some serious standout songs: “90210 (feat. Kacy Hill)” has an argument for being one of the best songs of the 2010s, and “Pornography” and “Antidote” are both great. There are a few more that stand out to me, which I’ll get into later. Despite all this praise, this is far from a perfect album. For one, it suffers from a problem endemic to both hip hop and debuts: it’s too damn long. Why do these hip hop artists insist on adding more and more meh tracks to push albums over an hour. It’s so frustrating. The second issue, which isn’t even really an issue but can be grating, is how many songs are good but not great. As I mentioned, there are some great tracks, but so much of this album I listed in my notes as “borderline favorite track”. The flip side of focusing so heavily on vibes and atmosphere is that almost every song has some flaw that holds it back. But I think that’s part and parcel with the style here. For what it is, this is a very good album.

“Pornography” is a complete banger opener. It has one of the best hooks on the album, so catchy that I’ve been singing it to myself for the last few days. It establishes the dark and grimy atmosphere so well. The drum sample is one of my favorite I’ve ever heard in hip hop. I enjoy the use of guitar, and I also Travis’ flow in the second half. Awesome track. “Oh My Dis Side (feat. Quavo)” keeps things going nicely. I hate to admit how fucking catchy the “oh my, oh my” refrain is. The lyrics are so dumb that they wrap around to being kind of a vibe. The second half is worse because of how repetitive it is, but overall I really like this one. “3500 (feat. Future & 2 Chainz)” is a “borderline” track that I ended ip putting in my favorites. The hook is so dominant that it’s kind of bad that I think it’s kind of annoying, but it’s a well-constructed track. The vroom vroom synths are hype. “Wasted (feat. Juicy J)” is the first truly forgettable track. It’s got a very stereotypical trap flute and the beat is boring. Juicy J has a nice verse though. “90210” is transcendent. What a good song. Unmatched atmosphere, one of the most effective beat switches I’ve ever heard, and honestly just shockingly progressive (musically not politically) for a track on such a massive album. I love the tropical drum sample in the second half and Travis spits some of the best bars I’ve ever heard from him. The Kanye influence on this album is undeniable, but I think the song that most sounds like it could be on a Kanye album is “Pray 4 Love (feat. The Weekend)”. It’s fine, another borderline track. I hated the Weeknd verse at first but it really grew on me. I usually dislike Swae Lee, but I thought he fit really well on Nightcrawler (feat. Swae Lee & Chief Keef)”, another borderline track. The hook is meh but I like the industrial synths. Chief Keef’s verse is great.

“Piss On Your Grave (feat. Kanye West)” is vintage Yeezus-era Kanye. He sounds so angry for absolutely no reason. It’s a little uncomfortable knowing where that anger will lead, but I love this little middle finger right in the middle of the album. “Antidote” is a complete banger that just reminds me of fraternity basements. It’s so simple yet so catchy, the ad libs are completely braindead, it’s kind of a pure distillation of what makes Travis Scott good. “Impossible” lacks in other areas, but it does the atmosphere so well it has to be one of my favorites. It sounds like a musical transcription of what 5 AM feels like. “Maria I’m Drunk (feat. Justin Bieber & Young Thug)” is the last borderline track. Justin Bieber’s verse is surprisingly good. The beat is extremely trap-flavored. It just suffers from being so late on the album. The last three tracks could be cut and nothing of value would be lost. “Flying High (feat. Toro y Moi)” is my least favorite because I find the melody actively grating, but the other two are jsut nonentities. A disappointing way for the album to end.

If this album were a little bit tighter and the songs just a bit better, I could see myself listing this as a top hip hop album of the 2010s. As it is, I still think it’s really good, and it has a few tracks that are just amazing.
