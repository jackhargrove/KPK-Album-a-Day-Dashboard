# Write-up

Type O Negative was a metal band formed in Brooklyn in 1989 and originally consisting of lead singer and bassist Peter Steele, guitarist Kenny Hickey, keyboardist Josh Silver, and drummer Sal Abruscato. Peter Steele was a 6’7”, long haired, heavily-tattooed, baritone behemoth of a man who spent the 1980s as a member of various New York hardcore punk groups, most notably Carnivore. He formed a new group, called Repulsion, in 1989, but they soon changed their name to Sub-Zero because there was already another band named Repulsion. They soon realized Sub-Zero was also already taken, so they finally settled on Type O Negative (the logo they made for Sub-Zero was a 0 with a minus sign, and you can see how that turned into Type O Negative). They released their debut album *Slow, Deep and Hard* in 1991, introducing a new sound that combined the slow doomy riffs of Black Sabbath, the vocals and aggression of hardcore punk, and the atmosphere of gothic rock (more on the album later). Their second album, *The Origin of the Feces*, was a fake live album that featured Steele’s butthole as the cover. Their third album, 1993’s *Bloody Kisses*, was their commercial breakthrough, shedding much of the hardcore aggression of their early work in favor of the goth rock atmosphere. This album eventually went platinum, peaked at #42 on the Billboard 200 Albums chart, and spawned the group’s most recognizable track, “Black No. 1 (Little Miss Scare-All)”. In 1996, the group’s fourth record *October Rust* received significant critical acclaim, and is often today considered the finest work of gothic metal. Type O Negative would release three more albums between 1996 and 2007. Peter Steele died of natural causes in 2010 at age 48, and the band broke up as a result.

*Slow, Deep and Hard* was a transitional album for Steele’s sound, laying the foundation for the gothic rock that they would pioneer but still containing much influence from his hardcore punk roots. An album deeply obsessed with sex, the cover is a heavily pixellated photo of a penis entering a vagina. The album is a loose concept record about Steele’s breakup with his girlfriend, and his spiral into hatred, insanity, and ultimately suicide as a result. Although Steele obviously didn’t kill himself, he had seriously attempted to; in 1989, after breaking up with his girlfriend, Steele slit his wrists but survived. Not long after, he wrote all of the songs of this album in just one night. The title and lyrical content of “Der Untermensch” spawned many allegations of Nazism against Steele and Type O Negative (despite keyboardist Josh Silver being a proud Jew) that would follow them for their entire career, especially in Europe where the sarcasm of the lyrics didn’t translate. In an attempt to change this image, Type O Negative’s third album *Bloody Kisses* features a song titled “Kill All the White People”. If you enjoyed the dour atmosphere of this record and the slow doom-y riffs but didn’t care for the aggression, I recommend you check out their record *October Rust*.

Type O Negative pull from a broad range of influences. While the metal aspects of their sound come most directly from the loud and slow riffs of Black Sabbath, they also had an unexpected pop sensibility stemming from their love of The Beatles; the band was often referred to as “The Drab Four” by the press, in reference to The Beatles’ nickname “The Fab Four”. They also pulled heavily from The Doors, considering “Light My Fire” to be the best song of all time. And of course, gothic rock artists like The Cure and Danzig were the predecessors of their atmosphere. The band got their start in the late 80s and early 90s, a time when the high speed of thrash completely dominated the metal scene. Type O Negative were instrumental in making slow metal cool again, paving the way for scenes like stoner metal, drone metal, and the resurgence of doom metal that would define the 2000s.

# Hargrove's review

I thought this was a fun listen, didn’t love it didn’t hate it. One thing I really respect that we haven’t seen enough of is contempt for the audience. Calling a 7 minute ambient industrial track with Gregorian chants for vocals a “dance mix” is awesome, and then following it with a minute of silence even better. I love it when an artist gives me the middle finger. As for the music itself, I generally preferred the super slow, syrupy, lethargic, funeral dirge sections to the thrashier and more hardcore sections. Overall, I prefer the typical integrated mixture of hardcore and doom that you get from sludge metal to the bipolar back-and-forth that you get here. However, I did like the kind of progressive song structures. The opener “Unsuccessfully Coping with the Natural Beauty of Infidelity” is definitely my favorite track. The first half is decent and is a good introduction to the unique sound, but I found the entire “I Know You’re Fucking Someone Else” section to be really good. It’s surprisingly poppy, the melody has been stuck in my head all day. It’s also really funny, especially the call-and-response sections. Around 7:45, a very gothic atmospheric section kicks in that I enjoyed. I enjoy the glacial opening of “Der Untermensch” but I’m not crazy about the thrashier sections nor the vocal delivery. The keyboard is used to great effect in the “Waste of Life” section though. The keyboard is even better in “Xero Tolerance”, where around the 5:00 mark there’s an awesome synthpop section. The beginning of “Prelude to Agony” reminds me of *Dopesmoker* in how dynamic the guitar sounds despite how slowly it’s moving. This track is overlong though, and the lyrics in the “Jackhammerape” section are really unfortunate. “Glass Walls of Limbo - Dance Mix” is also too long, and Gregorian chants have never really been my thing. The start of “Gravitational Constant: G = 6.67 × 10−8 cm−3 gm−1 sec−2” is whatever, but I really enjoy the down and dark instrumental during the “unjustifiable existence” refrains. The section beginning around 6:50 has some of the finest keyboard work on the album. “Suicide is self-expression” is a fantastic closing line. I enjoyed this album in the net, but there were definitely parts that frustrated me and it’s much too long. I listened to *October Rust* as well and enjoyed that one more.

Favorite tracks: Unsuccessfully Coping with the Natural Beauty of Infidelity, Xero Tolerance, Gravitational Constant: G = 6.67 × 10−8 cm−3 gm−1 sec−2

Least favorite track: Glass Walls of Limbo - Dance Mix
