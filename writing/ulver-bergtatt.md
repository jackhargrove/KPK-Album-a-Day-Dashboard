# Write-up

Ulver (“Wolves” in Norwegian) are an experimental band formed in Norway in 1993 who have played a broad array of musical styles but who began as a black metal band. Ulver’s lineup at the time of this album consisted of vocalist Kristoffer Rygg, guitarist Håvard Jørgensen, guitarist Torbjørn Heimen Pedersen, bassist Hugh Mingay, and drummer Erik Olivier Lancelot. Ulver released its debut EP in late 1993. It was in a similar style to the black metal that was popular in Norway at the time, but had a broader range of influences from jazz, folk, and gothic rock. Ulver also had no association with any members of the second-wave of black metal/“Black Metal Inner Circle” in Norway like Varg Vikernes or other members of Mayhem (this is probably a good thing. Those people were fucked up. I highly recommend you read the write-up I did for Burzum that talks about all the crazy shit they did). Their debut EP did have many Pagan elements of Norwegian folklore, and the lyrics were in the archaic language of Dano-Norwegian, but they were not Satanists like many other Norwegian black metal artists.

Ulver released its debut album *Bergtatt — Et eeventyr i 5 capitler* in 1995. The title translates to *Spellbound — A Fairy Tale in 5 Chapters* from Dano-Norwegian, the language the lyrics are written in as well. It tells the story of a woman in medieval Norway who gets lost in a snowy mountain forest and is lured to her brutal death by trolls. It is heavily inspired by Norwegian folktales about Christians being tricked and killed by ancient Pagan creatures in the forest. The musical style is based around black metal, particularly in the vocals and the drums, but it also included many elements and interludes from folk music. This style would be extremely influential for later folk metal artists like Agalloch. It is also interesting to note that every member of Ulver was 20 years of age or younger when this album was released. It received acclaim at the time, and in retrospect is considered one of the finest black metal records of the 90s.

Ulver went fully folk and acoustic for their second album in 1996 *Kveldssanger*, and then went fully black metal and electric for their third album in 1997 *Nattens Madrigal*. These two albums, along with their debut, are considered a part of *The Trilogie*, or Ulver’s early black metal days. Since then, Ulver has largely left metal entirely, starting with an electronic and industrial album in 1998. Their 2000 album *Perdition City* was a very well received work of trip hop. Their 2007 album *Shadows of the Sun*, in the neoclassical darkwave style, was also acclaimed. More recently, Ulver has been making synthpop music. They have attained a level of legitimacy in the mainstream music scene in Norway that no other black metal artist was able to. Ulver is still making music today.

# Hargrove's review

With this album, we’ve listened to 5 of the top 10 black metal albums on RYM (well, different Agalloch album, but whatever) and I feel like I’m getting a pretty good handle on the genre. It’s much easier to rank them since I can simply compare them against each other. I was pretty excited for this one because of how much I liked Agalloch, and I knew Ulver was one of the biggest influences on Agalloch. I also love weird music that emulates the Medieval, and I had heard that this did something along those lines. Overall, I didn’t like it as much as I had hoped. I still definitely enjoyed it, but I found it inconsistent and some of the songs to be a bit lacking. Given this is such a short album with only five songs, that’s a major issue. On the positive side, the opening track is amazingly good, the lyrical concept of the album is tight and executed better than 90% of concept albums, I really enjoyed the soft chanting vocal mode (hereon: “Gregorian voice”) used about half the time, and overall the fusion of metal with folk sounds good. On the negative side, the sections with full on growling vocals/blastbeat drums/distorted guitar feel a bit generic and samey, the fourth track is a failed experiment, and, outside of the opener and the third track, the music doesn’t sell the concept as well as the lyrics do. I have very specific desires for what I want the music of a forest to sound like; I was so forgiving to the flaws of *Spirit They’re Gone, Spirit They’ve Vanished* because it nailed that twinkling, free but daunting vibe that forests give me. I didn’t get that all the way through here. Some of the tracks needed a bit more twinkling, and, no, acoustic guitar doesn’t count on its own. I don’t know, man. It’s hard to evaluate. I really do like the opener, the closer is quite good too, and the third track has a couple really cool moments. But it just doesn’t get me where I want to go every time.

On “I Troldskog Faren Vild”, immediately the Gregorian voice stands out to me as the most interesting part. It has a sort of Medieval melancholy that I can’t get enough of and, in a dark way, adds some of the twinkle I’m looking for. The guitar riff is also very good, especially the terrifying chord at the end of the first half of the riff. The first guitar solo is whatever but the second is quite good, and the one at the end slices straight through my eardrums. One of the best black metal songs so far. Unfortunately, “Soelen Gaaer Bag Aase Need” is not my favorite. The acoustic guitar and flute intro is okay, but then it shifts immediately into the harshest stereotypes of black metal. The Gregorian voice is gone for now, replaced by a snarl. The drums and the guitar are full speed ahead but without a good enough rhythm or riff to justify it. The Gregorian voice comes back briefly, but we return to the super aggressive part. The best part of this track are the lyrics, which really make me connect to and feel for the Maiden. I highly recommend finding a translation into Middle English instead of Modern English. “Graablick Blev Hun Vaer” starts almost identically to the previous track, which makes me roll my eyes. This track would be similarly unremarkable to the last one except for one thing: the field recordings are used masterfully. That crack of thunder has, no exaggeration, caught me by surprise and scared the shit out of me every time I’ve listened to it. And then the section where the Maiden is running through the forest and an experimental piano bit soundtracks her terror. It’s really well done. “Een Stemme Locker” has a kind of an interesting ritual ambient vibe, but overall I consider it a failed experiment. It’s too sparse, too bare, too slow, and too static. I like the idea of it, but I don’t care for the execution. “Bergtatt - Ind I Fjeldkamrene” is a strong closer and the only track to match up to the opener on music alone. It’s aggressive once more, but this time the guitar has so much propulsion it just makes it work. I also really like the section with the two acoustic guitars, one arpeggiated and one playing a heartbreaking melody. In different circumstances, I might criticize the album for going out on a light acoustic guitar melody. However, the first verse of this track is so horrifying to me that I appreciate the Maiden having a bit of a eulogy at the end. They just so immediately and matter-of-factly state that she died painfully, screaming. It’s scary, man.

There are a lot of good elements here, but it just did not take the kind of an album shape that I wanted it to. Lots of flaws typical of a debut album, I’d say. That first track really is great though.

Favorite tracks: I Troldskog Faren Vild, Graablick Blev Hun Vaer, Bergtatt - Ind I Fjeldkamrene

Least favorite track: Een Stemme Locker
