# Write-up

Unwound is a band formed in 1988 in Olympia, Washington whose classic lineup included guitarist and vocalist Justin Trosper, bassist Vern Rumsey, and drummer Sara Lund. They got their start playing covers of early post-hardcore groups like Hüsker Dü as high schoolers (I should note that “post-hardcore” is a retrospective term that wasn’t used at the time, back then those bands were just considered “hardcore”). Unwound recorded its first album in 1992, but it would be shelved until 1995 when it was released as *Unwound*. They were the first band signed to the Kill Rock Stars label which would go on to release records for many well-liked underground punk bands of the 90s Washington scene, particularly riot grrrl groups like Sleater-Kinney and Bikini Kill. Unwound released its official debut, *Fake Train*, in 1993. It received little critical attention at the time, but was lauded in retrospect upon its re-release as a box set in 2014. It showed heavy influence from fellow Washingtonians Nirvana, who in turn took influence from Unwound for their final album *In Utero* in 1994. From 1994 - 1998, Unwound released five albums, each further refining their pioneering of post-hardcore sound; 1996’s *Repetition* in particular from this stretch is very well liked. Unwound never saw major success, but they developed a reputation for their relentless touring schedule. They played live with bands like Bikini Kill, Sonic Youth, Fugazi, Deerhood, Blonde Redhead, and Mogwai. They were also known for avoiding age-restricted clubs (18+ or 21+) as they had liked to go to shows as teenagers and didn’t want them to be excluded from Unwound shows.

In 2001, Unwound released its final and most celebrated album, *Leaves Turn Inside You*. It wasn’t necessarily intended to be their final album, but problems in the band were mounting and it became clear that they wouldn’t stay together forever. Though it continued the post-hardcore sound the band had developed, it pivoted into a more post-rock direction. Trosper, who handled most production duties, became interested in techniques of using the studio as an instrument. He studied albums that had done this to great effect, particularly Bowie’s *Low*, Radiohead’s *Kid A*, Burzum’s more ambient metal work like *Filosofem*, The Cure’s *The Head on the Door*, and the Beach Boys’ *Smile*. Janet Weiss of Sleater-Kinney provided backing vocals on “Demons Sing Love Songs”.

Unwound went on tour after the release of *Leaves Turn Inside You*, but it was plagued with issues like lack of rehearsal, cancelled shows thanks to 9/11, and Rumsey’s worsening alcoholism. The latter became a persistent and serious issue, as it became difficult for him to even perform live. He suggested they become a studio-only band like The Beatles, but it became too difficult for Trosper and Lund to be around him, so Unwound broke up in April 2002. Over the next 20 years, there were many discussions for a reunion, especially as Unwound’s legacy continued to grow, but it was untenable so long as Rumsey couldn’t get his shit together. Ultimately, Rumsey died of undisclosed causes in 2021 at the age of 47. In 2022, Unwound announced they had reunited with a new bassist and played their first show back in 2023. They’ve been together since, but have not released a new album. Today, Unwound are considered one of the most influential bands of the 90s despite their lack of commercial success. The A.V. Club named Unwound the best band of the 90s in 2021. They have been cited as an influence by numerous indie rock and noise rock bands popular in the late 90s and early 2000s, with Modest Mouse in particular seen as a continuation of the Unwound sound. Though they have multiple acclaimed albums, *Leaves Turn Inside You* is generally considered their masterpiece.

# Hargrove's review

According to my Spotify data, I listened to this album all the way through once, in July 2017. The only thing I remembered about it was the 2 and a half minute intro drone, which I remember thinking was so dumb at the time. I must say, I liked it a lot better this go-round. As I’ve said before, post-hardcore (and particularly emo within post-hardcore) is a genre that just speaks to me, and this is one of the most highly regarded records in the genre. So I went in with very high expectations. When I gave it an introductory listen on Monday, I was nonplussed. The smoldering atmosphere is clear and intense from the get-go, but very few songs left a lasting impact on me. I was disappointed. When I gave it a second listen yesterday, I came away with much the same feeling except for “October All Over”, which just clicked for me. It’s the closest I’ve heard a song come to touching the sound that Slint had on *Spiderland*, but it does so in a way that feels fresh instead of derivative. Finally having some kind of hook, I wanted to see if I couldn’t crack the whole thing open. I went to RYM and picked 6 of their top rated songs on earlier albums, three off their debut and three of *Repetition*. These songs blew me away. They were perfect emo. I particularly liked the songs “For Your Entertainment” and “Corpse Pose” off of *Repetition*. It’s the exact sound that resonates with me, and I intend to listen to both albums in full when I have the time. This exercise was ultimately a blessing and a curse, however; it did make it easier for me to enjoy my third, fourth, and fifth listens of *Leaves Turn Inside You* but, at the same time, it made me wish they sounded more like they did on those earlier albums. *Leaves Turn Inside You* gets plaudits for its turn toward the post-rock sound, but honestly that was what made me like it less. So much of the rawness and the dissonance of their post-hardcore sounds had been ground down into a simmering whimper. Funnily enough, the two full-on post-rock epics on the album, “Terminus” and “Below the Salt”, are among my favorites from the album. When they fully committed, they pulled it off well. I found the negative effect to be on the more post-hardcore songs, which lost much of their immediacy and impact thanks to the post-rock sound. Still, I now like this album, and look forward to listening to it more and hopefully getting even more out of it.

I hated the drone that opens the album on “We Invent You” as a kid, but I’ve now come to appreciate it. I really like how the rest of the song surprises you when it springs out. The vocals are meh, but that Slint-y guitar is so uneven and amazing, and the drums are perfect. I also like the despondent tape stuff at the end. “Look a Ghost” is a tight, enjoyable indie rocker with the dictionary definition of angular guitars. I really like the faux-sunniness of the chorus. “Terminus” is the next song that I really like. I don’t love the opening few minutes, where the more conventionally-structured aspects lie; the guitar in the verses is tense in a way that I don’t find rewarding, but the fast vocal sections are cathartic releases. The real joy of this song though is the two sections of pure post-rock at the end. The first has these soaring strings that come out of nowhere, and the build and repeat cycles are executed masterfully. Then, there’s a sudden switch to this sullen section with a warbly Rhodes piano that sounds so different to anything else. It’s a great track. “Demons Sing Love Songs” follows right after and it’s smoldering done in the best possible way. You can really feel the aggression and anger of hardcore punk converted into suppressed longing on this one. The backing vocals are haunting, and the vocal melody is catchy in both the verses and the chorus. “One Lick Less” opens disc 2 with a totally oppressive and overbearing atmosphere. It’s my favorite of the slower songs. “October All Over” is still my favorite. The riff is both ominous and fragile, and the hi-hats are used to great effect. “Summer Freeze” is the most emo song on the album and I like it for that. The part of the chorus where he goes “Summer’s freeeEEeeEEEeeEEEezing me” captures that over-the-top emotional reaction that I love from emo. “Below the Salt” is the final post-rock suite. It starts off a bit slow for my taste but the bass line is stellar and I love the uneven 4 + 4 + 2 rhythm. It builds really well, with a consistent jolt at the beginning of each 10 beat repetition of the cycle.

Those are the songs that I was able to find great elements in over my listens. There are still quite a few that just don’t work for me quite yet. The stuttering chorus of “Treachery” is frustrating. I don’t care for the lyrics or chorus of “Off This Century”. “Decemeber” is just forgettable. I don’t understand the point of the sample in “Radio Gra”, though I do like the swirling guitars. “Scarlette” is my least favorite track, with those brutal vocals being mzxed so poorly making for an unenjoyable listen. I wish I hadn’t let my social responsibilities get me so behind this week becaus eI would have loved to spend more time with this album. As it is, I’ve gotten a lot out of it, but I want more.

Favorite tracks: We Invent You, Look a Ghost, Terminus, Demons Sing Love Songs, One Lick Less, October All Over, Summer Freeze, Below the Salt

Least favorite track: Scarlette
