# Write-up

Weakling was a black metal band from San Francisco consisting of guitarist and vocalist John Gossard, guitarist Josh Smith, bassist Sarah Weiner, keyboardist Casey Ward, and drummer Sam Foster. They took their name from a song off of Swans’ debut album *Filth*. They recorded one album together in 1998, which released two years later in 2000 as *Dead as Dreams*. The music was influenced by the style of Scandinavian black metal outfits like Burzum, Bathory, and Darkthrone, while the structure was influenced by 1970s progressive rock. There are a few weird myths and stories about the insane ways they planned to release *Dead as Dreams*, but none of these are true. What is true is that every member of Weakling either no longer works in music or just has completely moved on, so it’s insanely difficult to get a copy of the album today. The title of the album comes from the 1985 movie *Legend*, while the cover is an old Francisco Goya painting. The original release was only a few hundred copies. Weakling broke up in 1999, a year before the release of the album. That’s about all the information about the record that’s out there.

# Hargrove's review

This album fucks. This album fucks so hard. This is front to back loud wall of sound deep dark miserable metal for over an hour. It’s a really, really cool record. It took a lot of downloading and clipping MP3s to get this onto my Spotify, so I went into it annoyed and ready to dislike it. Nope. It kicks off at the exact same intensity it’ll keep all the way through the final note. The tremolo guitar is a near constant wash of noise that keeps you attentive at all times. There’s a synth/keyboard to play real melodies instead of everything being pure atmosphere. The drums keep things moving and pounding, but it feels like they’re lower in the mix than is typical for a metal album so you kind of get the aggressive beat but without the loud overbearing kick drum typical to metal. And the song structures are really cool. It takes a lot of the good qualities of progressive rock, the dynamics and the movements and the length, but replaces the schlocky aesthetics with pure, raw power. I do think the album is a little longer than it needs to be, especially the title track, but there’s rarely a moment where I’m like “alright, hurry up, let’s get going”. One of the best metal albums we’ve listened to so far, up there with *Dopesmoker*.

“Cut Their Grain and Place Fire Therein” is a great choice for the opener because it’s the shortest track. Even though you don’t get eased in sonically as the song starts loudly and aggressively, you do get eased in structurally which I think is important. I really like the final note of the main riff, which feels uncertain and unresolved. The section that begins around 4:00 is very powerful. “Dead as Dreams” is quite good, but it takes the spot for my least favorite track just because it’s the longest but without more memorable sections to show for that. I think this track definitely has some fat that could have been trimmed. It is consistently enjoyable, however. “This Entire Fucking Battlefield” is a candidate for my favorite track. It starts really strong with the most aggressive (and almost nautical) riff of the album so far. It then moves into a chugging riff that is a little less smooth than the others, but in a good way. It just keeps building and building until the new section around 11:00 that just goes unbelievably hard. It’s completely electric, yet also feels wistful and full of regret. “No One Can Be Called as a Man While He’ll Die” starts even more aggressively with the drums and the guitar. It’s more dynamic and loud than previous tracks. The riff that starts around 5:20 is AMAZING and it’s probably my favorite individual section of the entire album. “Desasters in the Sun” has a very slow opening but its a real wall of sound. The cymbal work in the beginning is really well-done. The section that starts around 8:30 is very nice. I particularly like the synth line here. This song is also a bit on the long side, but it’s fine.

This has been my favorite metal album so far, assuming you don’t count QotSA as metal (which you shouldn’t). I’m putting it just above *Dopesmoker*, which has been my gold standard until now. I actually think both are very similar albums, finding power in volume and repetition. I’, also putting it very near Matana Roberts’ *Coin Coin Chapter One*, as both impressed me similarly for artists I had never heard of.

Favorite tracks: Cut Their Grain and Place Fire Therein, This Entire Fucking Battlefield, No One Can Be Called as a Man While He’ll Die, Desasters in the Sun

Least favorite track: Dead as Dreams
