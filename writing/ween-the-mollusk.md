# Write-up

Ween is an alternative rock band from suburbs outside of Philadelphia whose primary members are vocalists and guitarists Gene Ween (Aaron Freeman) and Dean Ween (Mickey Melchiondo). They met in 1984 in a high school typing class, and settled on the name Ween as a portmanteau of “wuss” and “penis”. From 1984-1994, Ween consisted of just Gene and Dean, using a Digital Audio Tape to play pre-recorded backing music when they performed live. Their earliest work consisted of them getting very high together and making the most obnoxious music they possibly could. They eventually became mainstays at a venue in Trenton, New Jersey, where notably they opened for Henry Rollins of Black Flag (Rollins once told the audience to "start liking them now", for one day, "you will get down on your filthy knees and crawl to the altar that is Ween”). Ween released two albums on an independent label in 1990 and 1991 before making their major label debut with their third album, *Pure Guava*, in 1992. They gained some minor notoriety, and their music videos became a common target of the MTV shoe *Beavis and Butthead*. In 1994, they released the album *Chocolate and Cheese*, a fan-favorite to this day. At this point, their music was more listenable, but still retained many of the quirks and much of the humor of their early days. In 1997, Ween released the album *The Mollusk* which proved to be their most successful. Later that year, they did musical work for Trey Parker and Matt Stone, who had just created *South Park*. Ween would release 3 more albums between 2000 and 2007, with 2003’s *Quebec* being the most well-regarded, before breaking up in 2011 (primarily due to Gene Ween’s alcoholism). Gene Ween got sober and the band reunited in 2015, continuing to play live until disbanding again in 2024 due to Dean Ween’s mental health struggles. Ween played at the *South Park* 25th anniversary show in 2022.

*The Mollusk* is one of Ween’s best-remembered albums, and the favorite of both Gene and Dean Ween. It’s a concept album about the sea, with nearly every song containing some reference to the ocean or aquatic creatures, with one song (“The Blarney Stone”) even being a sea shanty. The album is a mish-mash of genres, taking influence from progressive rock, psychedelia, celtic punk, cowpunk, soft rock, surf rock, showtunes, even vaudeville. The track “Cold Blows the Wind” is an alteration of traditional English folk song “The Unquiet Grave”, while opener “I’m Dancing in the Show Tonight” is a reworking of 1953 Christmas song “Are My Ears on Straight”. The album’s cover was designed by Storm Thorgerson, who designed the famous cover of Pink Floyd’s *The Dark Side of the Moon*. The album proved very influential on Stephen Hillenburg, a marine biologists who was in the process of creating a children’s show about aquatic creatures that would become *SpongeBob SquarePants*. He was such a huge fan upon release in 1997 that he immediately got into contact with the band to commission a song; Ween wrote and performed “Loop de Loop”, a song about tying shoes that would be used in the first season of *SpongeBob SquarePants*. Hillenburg would also use the song “Ocean Man” from *The Mollusk* as the song playing over the credits of *The SpongeBob SquarePants Movie* in 2004. This album was also very influential on a young Kurt Vile, who would go on to found notable 2010s psychedelic rock group The war on Drugs. He has called “Mutilated Lips” his favorite song.

# Hargrove's review

Unlike the other albums that I’ve heard before and love, I did not discover this album as a teenager. Rather, I listened to it for the first time last year, shortly after moving to DC. It’s just such a fun album, it never takes itself seriously while being consistently musically enjoyable. Unlike The Lonely Island, Flight of the Conchords, even Weird Al Yankovic, it’s clear that the music comes first and the comedy comes second. “Ocean Man” is obviously a fantastic song, but it’s not even my favorite - that award goes to “Mutilated Lips”, one of the greatest bits of psychedelia I’ve ever heard. That chorus is so damn catchy, not a day goes by where I don’t think of “accepting only fresh brine”, the vocal effects are trippy as hell, and the guitar is just so nice. “The Mollusk” is a fantastic introduction to the album (better than the real opener), with the back and forth vocals of Dean and Gene, while that flute thing flutters about. Really good use of 6/8 time as well. I love how serious their voices sound as they sing in this song, and how it contrasts with the stupid lyrics (“Bring forth the mollusk”). “It’s Gonna Be Alright” is just a pretty piece of soft rock, a nice chill song that I come back to frequently. “The Golden Eel” is one of my favorites; it has such a sparse opening instrumentally with hand drums and distorted guitar, but the drama of the vocals tips you off that things will explode in the chorus, and they do. “Cold Blows the Wind” is a nice piece of Medieval folk that works so well with the overall vibe that Ween bring to the entire album. “Waving My Dick in the Wind” is shocking in how good it is, just fast-paced and frantic and catchy and funny. “Buckingham” is a prog rock song that doesn’t take itself seriously, and man is that refreshing. “She Wanted to Leave” is a good closer with some damn nice melodies. I’m always torn on “The Blarney Stone”, as though I do find it quite funny, it can get a little annoying right in the middle of the album on repeated listens. “Pink Eye - On My Leg” is far too long for how little it brings to the table. And the opener can easily turn listeners off if they’re worried that the rest of the album will sound like that. All in all, though, these imperfections have their place on this weird, wonderful album.

Favorite tracks: The Mollusk, Mutilated Lips, It’s Gonna Be (Alright), The Golden Eel, Cold Blows the Wind, Waving My Dick in the Wind, Buckingham Green, Ocean Man, She Wanted to Leave

Least favorite track: Pink Eye - On My Leg
