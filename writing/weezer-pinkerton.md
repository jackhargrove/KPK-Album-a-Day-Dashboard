# Write-up

See above for the write-up for Weezer’s 1994 debut album. After the release of *Weezer (The Blue Album)* in 1994, Weezer became national stars overnight. Sudden slingshots to stardom have fucked with the heads of many artists, and this was no different for lead singer Rivers Cuomo. Cuomo had mixed-feelings about the whole thing, and he also developed a severe inferiority complex. He had started listening to operas and classical music, and began to think that his rock music was less than. In particular, he became obsessed with the 1904 Puccini opera *Madama Butterfly*, which he would listen to in full after every Weezer concert. He decided he wanted to write his own opera, a science fiction rock opera set in the space in the distant future. Rivers would play the main character Jonas, the other members of Weezer would play the other characters, and they hired two female singers they were friends with to voice the women characters. It was to be called *Songs from the Black Hole*, and Cuomo wrote dozens of songs for it and recorded demos of all of them. However, they had trouble finding time to record it. In 1995, Cuomo enrolled in Harvard University (I know) to study classical composition. He was also born with one leg significantly longer than the other, and he finally had surgery to fix it around this time. The surgery and physical therapy were immensely painful, and Cuomo had to walk with a cane for over a year. The alienation he felt at Harvard combined with being bedridden from the surgery sent Cuomo into a deep depression, and he began to think *Songs from the Black Hole* was silly and stupid. Weezer cancelled its production, and though it has been pieced back together from demos, it’s considered one of the great lost albums in rock history.

In his depression, Cuomo decided he wanted to write songs that were raw, real, and reflected his mental state at the time. He picked some of his favorite songs from the cancelled rock opera and re-wrote the lyrics to be about his feelings of loneliness, his frustration with life, and, most importantly, his intense horniness. Earlier, I mentioned that Cuomo was obsessed with the opera *Madama Butterfly*. In that opera, the main character Pinkerton is an American sailor who lives in Japan for a while and begins a romance with a local Japanese girl. However, he ultimately leaves her for a white woman, destroying her spirit. Cuomo decided that the songs on his new album would reflect the “Pinkerton” side of his psyche, hence the title. The fifth track “Across the Sea” includes writing from a real letter Cuomo received from an 18 year old Japanes girl in a fan letter. When Weezer went to record, Cuomo refused to hire a producer. He also had the band record vocals live instead of overdubbing. The intention was for *Pinkerton* to be rough, raw, and short, like Cuomo’s feelings. Although the label was uncertain with the final project, they released it in 1996.

The reviews for *Pinkerton* were TERRIBLE. People liked Weezer for being the fun, nerdy power pop band of “Buddy Holly” and “Undone - The Sweater Song”. They were not expecting a deeply embarrassing and highly sexual exploration of a depressed psyche. Sales were abysmal. *Rolling Stone*’s year-end reader’s poll for 1996 voted *Pinkerton* the third-worst album of the year. It even made some critics re-evaluate how much they liked Weezer’s debut. This reception shattered Cuomo’s soul; he had poured his heart and all his feelings into this intimate and honest portrait of himself, and people completely rejected it. It sent him into a doom spiral of depression for the rest of the 90s, where at one point he painted all of the walls of his house black and sealed the windows so he could wallow in absolute darkness. Matt Sharp, the Weezer’s bassist and second-most important member, left shortly afterward to pursue his side project The Rentals (a band that at one point included future SNL star Maya Rudolph). Weezer was in dire straits. Eventually, Cuomo put himself back together, but he vowed to himself that, for as long as he lived, he would never put himself out there like that again. He would only make commercial pop music with vague, impersonal lyrics. In 2000, he went back to the studio and Weezer made their third album, *Weezer (The Green Album)*. Its cover echoed that of their debut, and the music was as polished as possible. The most famous song is “Island in the Sun”, which is actually not bad. But for the rest of Weezer’s career, they would only release music like this. It got really bad. They went from being one of the best bands of all-time to one of the worst.

However, as all this happened, people started to rehabilitate *Pinkerton*. It began in the late 90s on early internet forums, where people would discuss how much they related to what Cuomo was saying and how good the music was. In the early 2000s, third wave emo became the biggest genre in the world, and its main bands like Jimmy Eat World directly cited the emotional *Pinkerton* as their biggest influence (as a result, *Pinkerton* is often called “emo” in retrospect). In 2003, Pitchfork gave it a retrospective review of 10/10, one of the earliest major sources to do so. By the mid-2000s, a *Rolling Stone* reader’s poll ranked *Pinkerton* as the 16th best album of all time. Cuomo was reluctant to accept this at first, often saying that he thought *Pinkerton* was a piece of shit and that it brought up bad memories for him. Eventually, though, he embraced it. Now, *Pinkerton* is considered on par with *Weezer (The Blue Album)* as one of the best alternative albums of the 90s. It’s also my personal favorite Weezer album.

# Hargrove's review

*Pinkerton* is one of my favorite albums of all time. I really, really love this album. I can’t even remember when or why I first listened to it, but it was sometime in high school. I listened to it before I had even heard *The Blue Album*, although I was already familiar with “Buddy Holly”, “Say It Ain’t So”, and “Undone - The Sweater Song”. My dad *hates* Weezer, so I was drawn to it all on my own. If there’s one thing I look for in art, it’s extreme honesty. If there’s one thing I look for in music, it’s memorable melodies. This album has both in spades. I can’t even imagine what it would be like to put my deepest, darkest thoughts out into the world like this. Rivers Cuomo is either the bravest or the stupidest man to ever live. And yet, it was vital that he did so. Listening to *Pinkerton* is so, so, so cathartic. It can reliably stir up emotion in me when I’m feeling down. It’s really an album that reminds me of my own humanity, both as an individual and as part of a community. On the music front, this is just 10 bangers. All killer no filler. It’s actually insane that they did that twice in a row. There’s not a song I would cut off *Pinkerton* or *The Blue Album*.

I remember the first time I heard “Tired of Sex”, I was so impressed. I didn’t expect it to sound so roughshod, nor did I expect it to be so noisy. Despite this, it still belies the genius ear for pop songwriting of Cuomo. I think it also deserves credit for its lyricism; it’s nothing too complicated, but it gets across his alienation better than just about any song I’ve ever heard. “Getchoo” is one of the faster songs on the album, and its not one of the songs I connect too most. Still, it’s a hoot and a half to listen to. Even among the depression and isolation, including these hard rockers makes this album so much better. “No Other One” is my second-least favorite on the album, but that’s really not saying much. It’s a power ballad that just works. “Why Bother?” is one of my favorites. The chorus is not only anthemic, but a perfectly succinct explanation of the withdrawal that comes with depression. “Across the Sea” sounds like a single, but it’s shocking how anti-commercial the lyrics are. 18 year old girl? Narrating masturbation? She’s Japanese? Jesus, Rivers. This song lends so much authenticity to the entire album. “The Good Life” is a personal favorite (so many of those) of mine. I sing the chorus of it in so many situations; when I’m sick, when I’m depressed, when I’m sulking. This song was one of the things that got me out of my funk when I was unemployed after graduation. Such an important song to me. If I had to pick a least favorite track, it’s “El Scorcho”. I like this song, and I’m glad it’s on the album because of the flavor it adds, but there are some points that cross the line into annoying territory for me. “I’ll bring home the turkey if you bring home the bacon”? What are we doing here? “Goddamn you half-Japanese girls” is a legendary opening line though. “Pink Triangle” is yet another personal favorite. I use the “If everyone’s a little queer, why can’t she be a little straight?” and “I’m dumb she’s a lesbian” lines so much. The latter in particular I think is one of the funniest line drops in music history, and something being funny goes a long way with me. In general, I think that because Rivers is a funny guy and relentlessly makes fun of himself and his situation, it makes the harsher introspection much more palatable. “Falling for You” has always felt like a summation, a culmination of the sound of the entire album to me. It’s really quite progressive in structure, especially the chorus. It’s kind of the album’s arena banger. “Butterfly” is an aching and tender closer. I remember this song was the most controversial on 2010s internet discourse because some on Tumblr interpreted “I’m sorry for what I did/I did what my body told me to/I didn’t mean you harm” as like a rape thing, which I don’t get at all. Do not think that was the intention. It’s clearly a riff on Pinkerton leaving at the end of *Madama Butterfly.* It’s a heartbreaking rumination on what it’s like to let someone down. To be weak, for your courage to fail, and to find out what’s next. I find it one of the most moving songs of all time.

That’s all I have to say. This album is why I listen to music. This album is why I care about art. It is one of the few works that has actually tangibly changed my life, along with *In the Aeroplane Over the Sea* and *Ys* and *Emergency & I*. I just love it.

Favorite tracks: Tired of Sex, Getchoo, No Other One, Why Bother?, Across the Sea, The Good Life, Pink Triangle, Falling for You, Butterfly

Least favorite track (if I had to pick one): El Scorcho

# Aidan's review

I would quite happily inhabit a world where all the contemporary crisis were right and this album is a historic failure. I did not like the Blue Album, I found Weezer's shtick grating, I find the idea of an album that features rumination about 18 year old Japanese girls and getting tired of sex distasteful and more importantly, Hargrove would be wrong. Tragically, for once that is not the case. More annoyingly, it's still clearly a Weezer album, with the same soft-loud dynamic, steady guitars and unashamed nerdiness that I hated so much with the Blue Album. Here Rivers Cuomo has the sense to dial it down from an 11 to a strong 7 (incidentally my rating). There's still a lot of stuff that gets my goat, Across the Sea's weird opening phrasing irks me (I know it's quotations I don't care), and most of the most prominent lyrics and flourishes on songs like El Scorcho and Pink Triangle still irk me. But Cuomo's ability to write hooks is incredible, and the sentimentality on songs like Butterfly and No One Other is genuinely impressive. Against all my prior instincts, Cuomo manages to strike a likable and thoughtful figure, which in turn manages to sell the broader project.
