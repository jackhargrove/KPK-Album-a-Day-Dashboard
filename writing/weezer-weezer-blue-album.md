# Write-up

Weezer is a rock band from LA formed in 1992 whose classic lineup consisted of vocalist and guitarist Rivers Cuomo, bassist Matt Sharp, drummer Patrick Wilson, and rhythm guitarist Brian Bell. Cuomo was born in Manhattan in 1970 to hippie parents and his family lived in various places before settling in Mansfield, Connecticut when he was 10. Cuomo was always interested in music and completed courses at Berklee while he was in high school. He was originally interested in metal, specifically the hair metal of the 80s and even more specifically his favorite band KISS, and he formed the hair metal group Avant Garde in Connecticut in 1989. Avant Garde soon moved to Los Angeles and broke up in 1990. Now in LA, Cuomo entered the local music scene as a roadie and Tower Records employee. In 1991, Cuomo’s shifted his interests from hair metal to the alternative rock of Nirvana, Sonic Youth, and the Pixies (as did everyone else in America). He formed Weezer in 1992 with Sharp, Wilson, and guitarist Jason Cropper. The name came from a nickname that Cuomo’s father used for him as a toddler. They signed to a label in 1993 and began to record their debut album. During recording, they fired Cropper and replaced him with Bell. Cuomo wanted to self-produce the record, but the label forced him to get a real producer; they chose Ric Ocasek, who had been the frontman of The Cars a decade prior.

Weezer’s self-titled debut released in 1994. It took a lot of its sonic cues and structure from the alternative rock that was huge at the time, with the production of the guitar sounds specifically being modeled on the heavy guitars of Radiohead’s “Creep”. The music itself, however, was primarily power pop, a type of pop rock that had its heyday in the mid-70s. Power pop is defined by its use of power chords and loudness that are found in hard rock, but with melodies and vocal performances more typical of pop. Cheap Trick is the prototypical power pop band, and I have always loved their song “Surrender”. Added into this mixture was Cuomo’s own nerdy aesthetic, with songs referencing Dungeons & Dragons and shyness around girls. Finally, Cuomo was very influenced by the songwriting and melodies of the Beach Boys. The album produced three singles, “Undone - The Sweater Song”, “Buddy Holly”, and “Say It Ain’t So”. The latter two singles were immensely successful, with the Spike Jonze directed video for “Buddy Holly” getting endless play on MTV. The album itself received high praise from critics. This sudden commercial and critical success for the debut album of a band no one had ever heard of made many other bands in the alternative scene distrustful of Weezer and started an ongoing career-long fight for respect and credibility. Weezer has always been somewhat controversial and regarded as a joke, with many feeling annoyed that the undeniably great melodies and tight songwriting forced them to listen to a band of dorks.

Weezer would go on to have a successful but polarizing career that I’ll get into more when we listen to their second album, *Pinkerton*. To make a long story short, after the success of their debut, Cuomo enrolled in Harvard and had an expensive and painful surgery to lengthen one of his legs. His health struggles and difficulty grappling with his fame led him to make *Pinkerton* sad and deeply personal to the point of embarrassment. Critics ridiculed the album, and this essentially broke Cuomo’s spirit. He swore that for the rest of his career, he would only make basic, hyperpalatable, impersonal pop so that no one could make fun of him again. While their records since *Pinkerton* have spawned massive hits you all have heard like “Island in the Sun” and “Beverly Hills”, the last 25 years of Weezer has been universally hated by critics and fans. Still, their debut and *Pinkerton* are now regarded as two of the best records ever made.

# Hargrove's review

*Pinkerton* is and will always be my favorite Weezer album, but this album is damn good too. It’s pretty much just a perfect pop record. 10 tracks of animated, upbeat, loud, fun, catchy, and memorable power pop bangers in a tight 40 minute package. There is not a bad song on this thing. And though over the decade plus that I’ve been aware of Weezer I have sometimes memed myself into not knowing if I actually even like them or not, just one listen to the *Blue Album*, hell, just one listen to “Say It Ain’t So”, is enough to jolt me out of it. I just don’t know how they do it. “My Name Is Jonas” starts things off with a bang, although there is a pretty major plothole: his name is actually Rivers, not Jonas! It’s not my favorite song on the album, but it’s hard to imagine a better opener. It alerts you to exactly what the rest of the album is going to sound like without revealing its best melodies right off the bat. “No One Else” is probably my least favorite track if I absolutely had to pick one. In a vacuum, the lyrics just about cross the incel event horizon into feeling icky. Still, that chorus is pure sex, and our narrator receives his immediate comeuppance when we learn his girlfriend has left me in the very next song. It’s precisely this attitude that makes Weezer so enjoyable to me; Rivers explores the most embarrassing, unattractive, and just flat out wrong areas of his psyche with shicking self-awareness, never afraid to let himself be the loser when he’s being a loser. “The World Has Turned and Left Me Here” is one of the best non-singles on the album, the guitar is so loud and overbearing and it’s yet another catchy chorus. I also enjoy the off-balance feel of the track. “Buddy Holly” is the first single to appear in the tracklisting, and man was this osng deserving of its success. One of the best pure pop songs of the 90s. The verse starts with no hesitation, almost like you’re entering in the middle of a pre-chorus melodic phrase. The lyrics are stupid and silly and it’s just refreshing to hear a rock band not take themselves seriously at all. And that guitar solo, man. What an iconic song. “Undone - The Sweater Song” is a song I heard on Sacramento’s 94.7 radio station in the car before I knew who Weezer was, and it stuck with me for a long time. The imagery is over-the-top and funny, the spoken word skit is executed so well, and the chorus rips. “Surf Wax America” is a little weaker than some of the other songs, but it pulls off the Beach Boys homage really well, and I love the fast, metal-influenced kick drum. And though I sound like a broken record, that chorus is so catchy. I have 10 choruses simultaneously stuck in my head right now. “Say It Ain’t So” is the best song on the album, perhaps the best in Weezer’s discography, and up there with the best songs of all time. Its handling of alcoholism as a topic is surprisingly mature and effective for this record, and the bridge is pure catharsis, pure release. Close to a perfect song. “In the Garage” is the most obnoxious with its nerdiness, but I find the clearly personal lyrics to be touching, I can’t help but sing along to the chorus, and I love that wheezing harmonica. “Holiday” is yet another standard-issue banger, not much more to add to it than that. “Only In Dreams” is a phenomenal closer and the closest this album comes to *Pinkerton* in sound. It would in fact fit in perfectly on that album: the yearning, the build, the quiet-loud dynamics. And Matt Sharp’s bass line is amazing. This is maybe the perfect pop record. I prefer *Pinkerton* for its edgier sound, ambition, and tough to listen to lyrics, but for the vibe this album is going for, it’s hard to think of a way they could have executed it better. And though Weezer’s aesthetic and vibe isn’t for everyone, I love how unique they are. The cover alone, just four nerdy mildly unattractive guys awkwardly staring at the camera, violates every conception you have of what a rock band is. Amazing record.

Favorite tracks: My Name Is Jonas, The World Has Turned and Left Me Here, Buddy Holly, Undone - The Sweater Song, Say It Ain’t So, In the Garage, Holiday, Only in Dreams

Least favorite track: No One Else

# Aidan's review

I do not like Rivers Cuomo. He's obviously talented and is more well studied than most artists, but his idea of artistry is just so opposed to mine that I find his works incredibly grating. The fact that he decided to write an album inspired by a famous opera, and then picked the opera in which an American marries and has a kid with a 16 year old Japanese girl just kinda sums it up for me. I liked lot of the more restrained guitar work, especially on the intros, and I enjoyed a lot of the less overplayed songs but I find the quiet verse loud chorus just a lazy form of songwriting. Don't care for any of the hits, and "What's with these homies dissing my girl" might be the cringiest line of all time. I generally hate the "cringe" aesthetic, and Weezer proudly embraces it in defense of what has now become the cultural norm. I can see geek rock being something interesting in 1992 when you were trying to compete with Kim Gordon for MTV airtime, but now it just feels like the harbinger of the annoying present. The Blue Albums is still better than the plethora of wannabe Weezers and Rivers is somehow less of a sex pest than the average Cuomo, so that counts for something. Very similar in my mind to the Pixies in how I hated the frontman, although I felt that Rivers made it work on roughly half the album. If I ignore Buddy Holly and every single chorus, I liked it. Unfortunately the songs are like 60% chorus and Buddy Holly is legally allowed to be played on the radio.
