# Write-up

Whitehouse is an industrial music group formed in London in 1980 who at the time of this album consisted of vocalists and synth players William Bennett and Philip Best. The band’s best known lineup also includes Peter Sotos on synthesizer; though he does appear on this album’s title track, he left the group in 2002. Whitehouse is best known for pioneering the power electronics subgenre of industrial and noise music, which features screeching feedback, analogue synthesizers, and high frequency squeals. Bennett began his music career in the late 70s as the guitarist for a British post-punk band. He founded Whitehouse, named after both conservative activist Mary Whitehouse and the porno mag *Whitehouse*, in an effort to make music that would beat the audience into submission. Bennet released three solo albums under the Whitehouse name in 1980 and 1981, becoming an important part of the budding British industrial music scene. Other notable artists from that scene, like Steven Stapleton of Nurse With Wound, often collaborated and toured with him. While early industrial bands began experimenting with pop-adjacent sounds, Whitehouse desired to take the genre to the absolute limit of noise and aggression. Best, a 16-year-old runaway, joined Whitehouse in 1982.

Whitehouse went on hiatus for the second half of the 80s before being revived in the 90s at the behest of legendary American audio engineer Steve Albini. In this period, the lineup stabilized into a trio of Bennet, Best, and American author Peter Sotos, whose work *Buyer’s Market* we listened to yesterday. Sotos left the group in 2002. Best made a collaboration album with Merzbow in 1995 and completed a Ph.D. in English literature in 1998. *Bird Seed*, Whitehouse’s 17th album, was released in 2003. Bennet wrote every track except for “Bird Seed”, which was entirely handled by Sotos and produced by Albini. It received some acclaim in avant-garde electronic circles. The track “Why You Never Became a Dancer” went viral on YouTube in 2008 when it was paired with a super creepy edit of a Japanese serial killer victim. The video currently has 4.5 million views. Bennett ended Whitehouse in 2008 to focus on his other projects. However, in 2026, it was announced that Whitehouse would reunite as a duo of Bennett and Best at a musical festival in Japan in 2027. Whitehouse is one of the most influential noise groups of all time, inspiring many that came after.

# Hargrove's review

Writing this review is the last thing I need to do to escape the Sotos-verse, and boy am I excited. This review is a bit more complicated because all of Sotos’ contributions are limited to one track, though that track does take up about a third of the album’s run time. Outside of that, Whitehouse is a maximally abrasive industrial electronic group. And I like many of those things, so I was a little bit excited. There are a few banger instrumentals on this thing, specifically on “Why You Never Became a Dancer”, “Wriggle Like a Fucking Eel”, and “Munkisi Munkondi”. I really do love the searing feedback, that timbre of Merzbow but with just enough rhythm and repetition to avoid pure noise status. Unfortunately, the former two of those tracks also have vocals, and I do not care for the vocals on this album at all. Why would I want to hear a British man lecturing me on anything? The dominating manipulator persona gets old really quickly, which drags down a lot of this album’s more positive elements. On top of that, there’s no easier way to destroy any album than to drop a 15-minute Peter Sotos bomb into the middle of it for literally no reason. Still, there are a couple individual tracks that I liked enough to want to go back to on my own time, making this one of the tougher albums to rank.

I’m kind of a noise music sicko at this point so I really admire the instrumental of “Why You Never Became a Dancer”, but holy shit PLEASE shut the fuck up Mr. Bennett. I also haven’t seen *Why I Never Became a Dancer*, but this feels like kind of an outsized response against one specific person. The instrumental of “Wriggle Like a Fucking Eel” is also cooler than the last. I really, really like it. The vocals are still kind of annoying, but “wriggle like a fucking eel” is a funny phrase, and he sounds ridiculously British, so I can give it a bit of a pass. The instrumental of “Philosophy” reminds me a lot of “Black Smoke Rises” from the dälek album we listened to. The vocals are more subdued, but I don’t care for this muttering either. And get like a new bit in the lyrics, seriously. But fuck, I do respect the ringing, tearing, searing feedback in the instrumental. It just didn’t need to go on for 10 fucking minutes. “Bird Seed” nukes this album from orbit and turns what was probably going to be a C Tier record into a low D Tier record. I could see myself returning to this album in its entirety were it not for the existence of this track, but unfortunately that’s not the world we live in. It doesn’t even feel focused around a theme like some of the tracks on *Buyer’s Market*. Like, what does the title even refer to? I have now listened to over 6 hours of Peter Sotos this week between *Buyer’s Market* and this album and I just don’t want to listen to his work again. We return to the lecturing vocalist on “Cut Hands Has the Solution”. Yay! This seems to be the second track specifically about eating disorders. Can we please shake things up? It doesn’t even have a good instrumental to fall back on as it’s just a single drum pounding for six minutes. It’s my least favorite of the non-Sotos tracks. “Munkisi Munkondi” is the album’s only track that I can say without any reservations is a banger. I love the car engine type feedback loop with the chaotic, pounding drums. Perhaps more than that, I love that it’s only an instrumental. It’s genuinely a great track.

Holy fuck, I’m so glad to be done with these two albums. I’m going to stick it in D Tier right below *The Smile Sessions* that we listened to from the Beach Boys because both albums have small moments I like a lot surrounded by things I don’t.

Favorite tracks: Wriggle Like a Fucking Eel, Munkisi Munkondi

Least favorite track: Bird Seed
