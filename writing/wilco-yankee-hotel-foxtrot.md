# Hargrove's review

*Yankee Hotel Foxtrot* is the fourth of five great albums from last week. Spotify says I first listened to this album in early June of 2017, which lines up with my memories. It was one of the first albums I got into after I decided I wanted to get more into music and chose indie rock as my primary genre. My dad is a big fan of Wilco’s earlier alt-country work and an even bigger fan of Uncle Tupelo, but *Yankee Hotel Foxtrot* would never have been the album he would recommend. Instead, it was Pitchfork I have to thank for me trying this album out. I remember being blown away by “I Am Trying to Break Your Heart” on first listen. The sound collage elements were unlike anything I had ever heard before and the cryptic lyrics defied my attempts to interpret them (look, “aquarium drinker” is much easier to parse for a 24 year old than a 16 year old). I listened to it a lot that summer, sticking “Jesus, Etc.” and “Heavy Metal Drummer” onto my regular rotation playlists. “Heavy Metal Drummer” even made it onto my basement playlist as music chair in college. During COVID, I revisited the album and got really into “Pot Kettle Black”, still one of my favorite songs. More recently, I spent a lot of time with the record the week after the 2024 election, right after quitting my job; I became obsessed with “Ashes of American Flags” and added that to my GOATed *Yankee Hotel Foxtrot*s songs collection. While I like most of the songs on this album, those are the ones that have really stuck with me at different points in my life. An album having five songs that make it that far is a rare thing. As I continue to revisit and evolve my feelings on this album, I’m no longer as impressed by the noisiness of the musique concrète collages. I also grew out of a lot of indie rock a few years ago; I still love a lot of indie rock, but it’s far from my primary genre these days. And yet, every time I come back to this album I still find something to love. Tweedy’s lyricism is almost romantic in its self-deprecation and certain lines pack more folksy wisdom punch than entire country albums. The melodies are simple but infectious, the perfect complement to Tweedy’s stark sincerity. Though what remains of Wilco’s early country sound is largely vestigial by this album, its country soul remains. Tweedy is a singer whose home isn’t on the range, but rather in the Sears Tower. It’s an album that finds the roots growing out of the cracks in the sidewalk and wraps them around any disaffected yuppie with the heart to accept a little authenticity. It makes me feel seen.

“I Am Trying to Break Your Heart” is such a phenomenal album opener. It’s nearly 7 minutes long, but not one second feels wasted. Every time I hear “I am an American aquarium drinker/I assassin down the avenue”, I feel at home. There’s so much that I love about this song that I could talk about, but, to keep it brief, I love the way it chugs along constantly like a steam engine. No matter what is going on in the surroundings, that drum beat will connect each and every verse. And the dissonant piano is a personal favorite. And every time I hear “I’d be lying if I said it wasn’t easy”, fuck, what a line. “Kamera” has a great chorus but I’ve always been underwhelmed by it as a song. I just don’t love the verses. It’s a catchy ditty with nice psychedelic qualities, but it just feels insubstantial coming after the opener. “Radio Cure” feels like a song I should be iffy on for similar reasons, but I’ve always liked it. I love the cadence of the verses. I’m not quite sure what the lyrics mean, but they always make me sad. The slow burn is great and leads into such a restrained, tasteful, aching explosion at the end. “War on War” is a simple avant-pop bop. It’s nothing flashy, but man is it fun to hear. “Jesus, Etc.” takes the cake as best song on the album. It’s just so pretty, from the fiddle to the other country elements to the subdued vocals to the heartbreaking lyrics to the Nashville melody. “last cigarettes are all you can get” guts me every time.

“Ashes of American Flags” is also just fantastic. “All my lies are always wishes/I know I would die if I could come back new” might be the hardest any lyric on this album hits. I’m also partial to “I shake like a toothache”. Those unexpected strings on the final chorus give me shivers every time. “Heavy Metal Drummer” rounds out an immaculate three track run as this album’s best banger. It’s crazy that *Yankee Hotel Foxtrot* had a song that could be consistently played in the basement. The drums are a highlight, but overall it’s just a fun, sunny track. “I’m the Man Who Loves You” is unfortunately a pretty bad miss and the absolute low point on the album. I actively dislike this song. The verses sound like total word vomit that Tweedy can’t eject fast enough. The horns near the end are laughable. The Lou Reed “I Heard Her Call My Name’ guitar solo is not needed. I just don’t much care for it. “Pot Kettle Black”, though, is as good as any song here. “It’s become so obvious/You are so oblivious to yourself”. What a line. I love singing along to every word but the chorus in particular build so much energy out of nowhere. In addition to “I’m the Man Who Loves You”, this album’s biggest weakness is that it ends on kind of a whimper. Though I’m torn on “Poor Place”, it’s the better of the final two tracks. The chorus is so good, but it takes three minutes of a total slog to get there. I would be more willing to tolerate that slog if this were the closer, but no, you have “Reservations” to end things on 8 minutes of ambience. It’s not catastrophic, but it’s a bummer closer to an album that has been so good up to this point.

This album isn’t quite perfect, but man is it so endearing. It’s simply one of the best documents of pure, unadulterated indie rock from that decade’s heyday. I love its simplicity, its pop catchiness, and, frankly, the amount I relate to Jeff Tweedy. I hope to continue revisiting and rediscovering this album as I grow older.

Favorite tracks: I Am Trying to Break Your Heart, Radio Cure, War on War, Jesus, Etc., Ashes of American Flags, Heavy Metal Drummer, Pot Kettle Black

Least favorite track: I’m the Man Who Loves You
