# Write-up


# Hargrove's review

I thought *Pink Flag* was a pretty cool and certainly interesting conceptually, but the execution was hit-or-miss for me. It’s hard to pull off super short songs that align with my sensibilities. There were some really cool riffs (“Three Girl Rhumba”) and some really cool hooks (“Mannequin”), but so many of the songs left me feeling unsatisfied. Still, it was a cool record with a cool sound. *Chairs Missing* fixes a lot of the problems that I had with *Pink Flag*. It introduces fuller, fleshed-out song structures that allow the ideas some room to breathe. It takes the seething atmosphere and turns into something much thicker, and weirder, than the first record. It adds a keyboard and other frivolities that the rest of the British punk scene was too good for. It’s remarkable that this was released less than a year later because it’s quite the progression. It doesn’t change the idea of the music at all; instead, it executes better. It’s still got its flaws. One thing that I noted about a few songs was they had really strong verses but underwhelming or even downright bothersome choruses. Given the hooks they packed into *Pink Flag* (and even elsewhere on this album), that was a real downer. There are also some songs that don’t pack in the airy, spacey atmosphere that dominates on my favorite songs. Finally, the lyrics can get a bit repetitive. Despite all of this, I found myself consistently enjoying listening to this record all day. It’s just so distinctive. It knows what it is, it’s weird, and it doesn’t sound like anyone else. And the best tracks are very good. It’s all so alluring. It’s like a *Pink Flag* where I can enjoy the music viscerally instead of just cerebrally.

“Practice Makes Perfect” is a damn good opener. The on/off alternation of the droning distortion in the verses builds and releases tension in short, intense bursts. The bass is shockingly funky, the guitar slices quickly and lethally again and again, and the vocals reach a fever pitch by the end. It’s an extremely sharp track. The instrumental under the verses of “French Film Blurred” is my favorite part of this whole album. Some of the best atmosphere I’ve heard anywhere. The guitar is so creepy and spindly while the bass rumbles underneath. It gets me every time. Unfortunately, the chorus is deeply underwhelming, but I simply have to name this one of my favorite tracks for the verses alone. After two spacious, atmospheric tracks, “Another the Letter” brings everything back to 2D, which is a disappointment. I like dance punk a lot, but this doesn’t do it for me one bit. It feels almost claustrophobic. “Men 2nd” shares that issue but is a better track thanks to its bass line alone. “Marooned”, however, brings us back to the atmospheric glory of the first two tracks. The vocal performance is fantastic, the bass slinks along deviously, and the keyboard encases everything in cold, cold ice. I feel like I’m in the arctic. “Sand in My Joints” goes back to the *Pink Flag* style, but it almost pulls it off. It’s not bad at all, but it’s missing something. “Being Sucked in Again” has this great effect where the verses are hazy and spread out, but the chorus is sharp and focused. It makes me feel like I’m getting sucked into the chorus. The guitars are stellar and the keyboard feels like a hard ceiling looming over all. “Heartbeat” is a very slow burn, which is odd for these guys. It could have been a really cool track but it’s not executed properly. It doesn’t explode! The climax is entirely underwhelming. And the repetition of “like a heartbeat” gets dreary. I prefer the Big Black cover.

“Mercy” is a song that truly fucks. It starts off side 2 in style. The guitar sounds so loud without being too high in the mix; it’s all-consuming. There’s not one, not two, but three separate explosions packed into one five minute song. When it gets going, the guitar builds relentlessly and the cymbal crashes are ecstasy. “Outdoor Miner” is an unexpectedly pretty and sunny track. It’s bizarrely melody forward. Thankfully, that melody is so sweet and gorgeous. It also helps me form another mental bridge between post-punk and new wave. I am torn on “I Am the Fly”. It is so damn catchy (which I usually think of as a positive) that I know it’s going to be playing in my head for days. Unfortunately, it’s kind of annoying! That chorus drives me mad, especially when the hand claps and the choral voices join in. I respect a good hook, but it gets obnoxious, especially with the repetition in the second half. “I Feel Mysterious Today” is the nadir of the album. The verses are decent, but man that chorus is rancid. “From The Nursery” brings us back to replacement level. The songwriting is subpar, but the guitar rips. “Used To” is a well-done atmosphere piece. It’s perhaps a little insubstantial, but the whispery vocals and that vocal melody kick ass. And you have the layers of distortion and keyboard back. I just like it. “Too Late” ends things on a very high note. It starts with a fun, catchy hook, and then it builds into an insane punk jam. It builds a wall of sound out of the distortion and the driving drum beat. It reminds me of The Velvet Underground.

This album is certainly uneven. But I did thoroughly enjoy it as I was listening. I see myself revisiting a lot. When the execution is there, the atmospheres are second-to-none.

Favorite tracks: Practice Makes Perfect, French Film Blurred, Marooned, Being Sucked in Again, Mercy, Outdoor Miner, Used To, Too Late

Least favorite track: I Feel Mysterious Today
